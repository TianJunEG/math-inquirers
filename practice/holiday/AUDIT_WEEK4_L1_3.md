## Medium
**M1. P2 L7 4(a) variant "Add a/d + b/d" creates impossible questions (10 of 84 combinations).** vars d in 5..12, a in 1..3, b in 1..4, so the sum reaches or passes the whole: (d=5: 1+4, 2+3, 2+4, 3+2, 3+3, 3+4), (6: 2+4, 3+3, 3+4), (7: 3+4). For example "3/5 + 4/5" shows a bar of 5 parts and the hint "Colour 3 parts and then 4 more", answer 7/5 (an improper fraction, a P3 idea, and the bar cannot hold it); 2/5 + 3/5 = 5/5 is "1 whole" written as 5/5. Restrict to a + b < d. Same family: P2 L7 2(a) variant n = 5, k = 5 asks "what fraction is NOT shaded" and the answer is 0/5 (a degenerate card). Both oracles agree with the maths, so the build test did not see it.
**M2. P3 L7 2 (how many) hint gives the answer of 2(a) and the last item of 2 (lengths).** Hint: "A 5 m by 4 m garden and a 4 m by 5 m garden are the same garden turned round." 5 by 4 is the biggest-area garden (2(a): 5, 4) and the last length (5) and width (4) of the list. Use another pair of numbers or say only "a garden turned round is the same garden".
**M3. P3 L7 5(c) how much: "How many cm² does the area go up by?"** The stem tells the answer of 5(c) area ("The area goes up"). Say "What is the difference in area?" (the printed question is a single (c)).
**M4. P1 L7 T2 and its variants: "How many squares are in a grid 3 squares long and 2 squares high?" is ambiguous but marked 8.** A child who counts the 6 small squares is marked wrong; the sibling questions (4, 6, T3, G2) say "Count the small ones first, then the bigger ones" and T2 does not. The printed stem is the same, but online it auto-marks. Add "Count the bigger squares too." (the T2 hint only appears on request.) The variant family T2 (a 3 to 6, b 2 or 3) inherits it.
**M5. P1 L7 Q5 worked solution says something false.** Step 2: "Put them together along the longest side: they can make a rectangle. Slide one along that side and you get a parallelogram." Two congruent right triangles joined along their whole longest side can only make a rectangle (square when the legs are equal) or a kite; a parallelogram needs a short side (as the solution picture shows: the parallelogram is joined along a short side). Reword: "Along a short side you can also make a parallelogram." P1 L7 T1 draw step 2 ("a parallelogram (slide one up)") is the same loose wording ("turn one round", not "slide").
