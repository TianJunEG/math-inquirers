Turn any word problem into a bar model

Fractions, whole numbers and decimals, taught the same way every time: read it, say it in plain words, draw it, solve it. Children stop guessing which operation to use because the picture shows them.

Parsing

Four steps, every question

1

Read

Find who, what, and what is being asked.

2

Say it plain

Rewrite the problem as a short sentence: "3 of 5 equal parts is 24."

3

Draw

Build the bar: units, shaded parts, the gap.

4

Solve

Find 1 unit, then answer, then check against the model.

Example. Mei has some stickers. 3/5 of them are stars. She has 24 stars. How many stickers does she have?

Plain words: 3 units = 24, so 1 unit = 8, and 5 units = 40.

Each session teaches one or two model types, practises them together, then mixes them with earlier types so children learn to choose the right model, not just copy the last one. A short bridge lesson covers percentages as fractions for children who haven't met percentages yet.

FAQ

Which levels is this for?

Primary 4 to 6. Primary 3 children who are comfortable with multiplication and division facts can join the foundation sessions. Choose the level your child will be in next year.

Does my child need a calculator?

No. Primary 4 children do not use calculators, so the examples use numbers that can be worked out by hand. Older children can use a calculator for the harder decimal questions.

When are the live sessions?

Is it online?

Yes, live on video, and recorded step-by-step practice is available online. Live groups are kept small so every child gets their model checked.

My child doesn't know percentages yet.

That is fine. The class includes a short bridge lesson on turning common percentages (50%, 25%, 10%, 20%) into fractions, so the same bar models work.
