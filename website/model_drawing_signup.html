Parsing

Four steps, every question

Children learn to turn the words into a picture, so they stop guessing which operation to use. They draw on paper or a whiteboard while we work through it.

1

Read

Find who, what, and what is being asked.

2

Say it plain

Rewrite the problem as one short sentence: "3 of 5 equal parts is 24."

3

Draw

Build the bar: units, shaded parts, the gap.

4

Solve

Find 1 unit, answer, then check against the model.

Example

Mei had some stickers. 3/5 of them were stars. If she had 24 stars, how many stickers did she have altogether?

Plain words: 3 units = 24, so 1 unit = 8, and 5 units = 40.

Each session teaches one or two model types, practises them, then mixes them with earlier types so children learn to choose the right model, not just copy the last one. A short bridge lesson covers percentages as fractions for children who haven't met percentages yet.

Questions

Parents often ask

Which levels is this for?

Primary 4 to 6. Primary 3 children who are comfortable with multiplication and division facts can join the foundation sessions. Choose the level your child will be in next year.

Does my child need a calculator?

No. Primary 4 children do not use calculators, so the examples use numbers that can be worked out by hand.

When are the live sessions?

Is it online?

Yes, live on video. Recorded step-by-step practice is also available online. Live groups are kept small so every child gets their model checked.

Do children draw on the screen?

No. Children draw the bar models on paper or a whiteboard. The online practice checks each step and shows the model to compare with.

My child doesn't know percentages yet.

That is fine. The class includes a short bridge lesson on turning common percentages (50%, 25%, 10%, 20%) into fractions, so the same bar models work.
