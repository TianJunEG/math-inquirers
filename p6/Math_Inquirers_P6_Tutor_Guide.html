If you run late: the cut-first ladder

Every lesson has a flex buffer (1.5 to 4 minutes) right after the worksheet check. It is the first thing to lose. If the lesson is still late, cut in this order and never touch the fixed items:

1. Skip the Go further what-if (it is not a slide any more: it is in the notes of the explore slide and can be pasted in the chat).

2. Ask one quick question fewer in the warm-up, then one explore discussion question fewer.

3. Check together: explain only the starred question on each page (the one that carries the idea).

4. Move the second talk question to the opener of the next lesson.

5. Never cut: the sneak-peek puzzles, the exit slip, the looking back (2 minutes), the stamp, and in Lesson 10 the certificates and the last stamp.

Teaching habits that matter

  • Ask “How do you know?” after every answer, right or wrong.
  • Wait 10 seconds before helping. Let children struggle a little.
  • Praise the thinking (“You checked it two ways”), not just the answer.
  • Let children disagree with each other and settle it with objects or a drawing.
  • If a child finishes early, point to Think harder, Go further or the Investigation on the Finish page, not extra drill. Never say who is on which page, and never split a pair unless both children agree.

Preparing the materials

  • Each child needs a computer or tablet with Scratch 3 (free online). Python is optional (any online Python editor). Cut-outs used: unplugged code cards (Lessons 1, 4, 7), square grid (Lessons 7, 9).
  • Keep the answer key with the tutor only. Check answers together on screen.
  • Use the shared screen or a digital whiteboard for the mini-lesson table.

Setting up online

  • Send parents the workbook and materials list one week before. Needed: the printed workbook and cut-outs, scissors, a ruler, paper and a pencil, and a computer with Scratch.

How to use the scripts

Rules for every lesson: no public labels (never say advanced, top group, strong or early finisher), no announcing who is on which page, no cold-calling by name, no public tallies or rankings, and “pass” is always fine. Every lesson below is a minute-by-minute script. The slides match it: one slide or two for each part. You do not have to say the words exactly, but keep the questions, because the questions are what makes children think.

SAY
Words to say aloud, in plain language for 11- to 13-year-olds. Said in your own voice, not read from the page.
DO
What you do: set up, hand out, show a slide, or move around the room.
ASK / EXPECT
Questions to put to the group, with the answer you are listening for. Let children answer before you explain.
IF STUCK
A ladder of help. Give hint 1, wait ten seconds, then give hint 2. Do not give the answer.

Five habits that run through every script

  • Predict first. Before children test anything, ask for a guess and write it down. A wrong guess is useful because we can test it.
  • Wait ten seconds after every question. Count silently. Most children answer in the last four.
  • Ask “How do you know?” after right answers too, so that children do not read your face for the answer.
  • Praise the thinking, not the answer: “You checked it two ways” beats “Well done”.
  • When an answer is wrong, say “Let us test that,” and use objects, not “No”.

Recording the lesson

Every live lesson is recorded so that a child who misses it can still catch up. A child watching alone cannot ask you a question, so the recording needs spoken cues, visible timers and quiet working time.

How to teach so the recording works

  • Say the short REC line at the start of each part, once, to the camera, in a friendly “recording voice”. Keep it under ten seconds. The children in the live room will simply hear it as a routine.
  • Say the workbook page and question number aloud every time, and read each question out loud as well as showing it.
  • Put a countdown timer or a clock slide on screen while children work, and say “the timer is running” so the recording shows how long to pause.
  • Repeat a child’s answer in your own words before you respond, so it is clear on the recording without relying on their voice.
  • Do not talk through the working time. Silence is fine; the child watching later pauses the video anyway.
  • Show the answer slides clearly and read each answer aloud, so a child can mark their own work.

Privacy and consent

  • Only record on the approved platform, and only with parent consent given at registration. Do not make your own recordings or photos.
  • Record the tutor and the shared screen only. Turn off or exclude the other children’s video in the recording (use active-speaker or screen-only recording), and keep children’s names off the screen.
  • Do not read out a child’s name together with a right or wrong answer. Do not show a child’s page to the camera in the recording.
  • Share the recording only through a private link to enrolled families, and delete it when the program ends, or after the period the parents agreed to.
  • Tell the group at the start of each lesson that it is being recorded and what is recorded. A parent or child who does not wish to be in it can keep the camera off.

These are suggested working rules. Make sure the parent consent wording, the tutor agreement and the platform settings match them before the first recorded lesson.

After the lesson

  • Add a chapter marker at each time in the chapter list at the end of the lesson, so a child can jump to the part they need.
  • Turn on captions if the platform offers them.
  • Put the workbook page numbers and the materials list in the description of the recording.

Investigation The terms up to 100 are 4, 11, 18, 25, 32, 39, 46, 53, 60, 67, 74, 81, 88, 95 (14 terms).
