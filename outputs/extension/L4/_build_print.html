Learn it: Factors and multiples
My goal. I can list every factor of a number in order. I can find common factors and common multiples and use them to solve sharing and timetable puzzles.
A factor goes into a number exactly. A multiple is in a times table. Write factors in pairs, in order, and stop when the pairs meet.
Worked example 1
Find all the factors of 54.
  1. Try 1, 2, 3 in order. Write a pair each time the number goes in.
  2. 4 and 5 do not go into 54. 6 × 9 = 54 is a pair.
  3. 7 and 8 do not go in, and the next number, 9, is already used. The pairs have met, so stop.
Answer: The factors of 54 are 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18, 27, 54.
Worked example 2
Lamp A flashes every 4 seconds. Lamp B flashes every 10 seconds. They flash together now. After how many seconds do they next flash together?
  1. List the multiples of each number.
  2. Find the first number in both lists.
Answer: They next flash together after 20 seconds.
How to hunt
  1. Factors: try 1, 2, 3 in order. Stop when the pairs meet.
  2. Common factors: list both numbers, then pick the numbers in both lists.
  3. Common multiples: list the multiples of both. The first number in both lists is the least.
Good to know
  • A pair like 6 × 6 counts as one factor.
  • So 36 has 9 factors, an odd number.

Learn it: Numbers to 100 000
My goal. I can round numbers to the nearest 10, 100 and 1000. I can work backwards from a rounded number and solve digit puzzles.
To round a number, find the two neighbours it lies between. Halfway rounds up. To work backwards from a rounded number, find the smallest and the largest numbers that round to it.
Worked example 1
Round 46 382 to the nearest 1000.
  1. 46 382 is between 46 000 and 47 000.
  2. The halfway mark is 46 500. 46 382 is before it.
Answer: 46 382 rounds to 46 000.
Worked example 2
A number rounds to 8000 to the nearest 1000. What are the smallest and the largest it can be?
  1. 7500 is halfway, so it rounds up to 8000.
  2. 8500 rounds up to 9000, so the last number is 8499.
Answer: Smallest 7500 and largest 8499.
How to round
  1. Find the 2 neighbours, such as 46 000 and 47 000.
  2. Find the halfway number. Halfway rounds up.
  3. To work backwards, find where the rounding changes.
Place value
  • 35 206 = 30 000 + 5000 + 200 + 6
  • Halfway rounds up: 46 500 goes up to 47 000.

Level 4Unit 3 · Operation DetectivesName
Learn it: Four operations
My goal. I can solve multi-step problems with the four operations. I can use bar models for "times as many" and work backwards through a number machine.
A bar model shows a "times as many" story. Each equal part is one unit. To go back to the start of a number machine, undo each step in the opposite order.
Worked example 1
Mr Tan has 3 times as many red pens as blue pens. He has 156 pens in all. How many blue pens does he have?
  1. Blue is 1 unit and red is 3 units. That is 4 units.
  2. 4 units = 156, so 1 unit = 156 ÷ 4 = 39.
Answer: He has 39 blue pens.
Worked example 2
I think of a number. I add 9 and then multiply by 4. The answer is 68. What is my number?
  1. Undo the steps in the opposite order.
  2. Undo × 4 with ÷ 4: 68 ÷ 4 = 17. Undo + 9 with − 9: 17 − 9 = 8.
Answer: My number is 8. Check: 8 + 9 = 17 and 17 × 4 = 68.
How to solve it
  1. Bar models: draw one bar for each quantity. Count the units.
  2. Divide the total by the number of units.
  3. Machines: start from the end and undo each step. Check by going forward.
Opposites
  • + and − undo each other.
  • × and ÷ undo each other.
  • "4 times as many" is 4 units.

Level 4Unit 4 · Fraction FrontierName
Learn it: Fractions
My goal. I can change between improper fractions and mixed numbers. I can find a fraction of a set and compare fractions with different denominators.
A fraction bar shows equal parts of one whole. A fraction of a set uses a bar model: divide the total by the denominator to find 1 unit.
Worked example 1
Write 17/5 as a mixed number.
  1. 5 fifths make 1 whole. Divide: 17 ÷ 5 = 3 with 2 left.
  2. There are 3 wholes and 2 fifths.
Answer: 17/5 = 3 2/5.
Worked example 2
There are 48 stickers. 5/6 of them are gold. How many are gold?
  1. 6 units = 48, so 1 unit = 48 ÷ 6 = 8.
  2. Gold is 5 units: 5 × 8 = 40.
Answer: 40 stickers are gold.
How to do it
  1. Improper to mixed: divide the top by the bottom.
  2. Mixed to improper: whole × bottom + top, over the bottom.
  3. Fraction of a set: 1 unit is the total ÷ the bottom number.
Comparing
  • Same bottom: the greater top is more.
  • Same top: the smaller bottom is more.
  • Or compare with 1/2.
  • Equivalent: multiply or divide top and bottom by the same number.
  • Whole from a part: 3 units = 36, so 1 unit = 12.

Learn it: Tables and line graphs
My goal. I can read values and changes on line graphs. I can find missing numbers in tables and spot graphs with scales that mislead.
On a line graph, read the title and the scale first. The change between 2 neighbouring points is the difference of their values. A table can have a missing number: use the totals to find it.
Worked example 1
The graph shows the cups of barley water a stall sold. Each line is worth 10. How many cups were sold on Wednesday? When was the greatest drop?
  1. The Wednesday point is halfway between 50 and 60: 55 cups.
  2. Find each change. Thu to Fri is 75 − 30 = 45 down. That is the greatest drop.
Answer: 55 cups. The greatest drop was Thursday to Friday.
Worked example 2
Find the missing number. Each row and each column adds to its total.
  1. Use Ali's row: 14 + ? = 31, so ? = 31 − 14 = 17.
  2. Check the Week 2 column: 17 + 18 = 35.
Answer: The missing number is 17.
How to read data
  1. Read the title and the scale. What is one line worth?
  2. Change = the difference between 2 neighbours.
  3. Missing numbers: use the row or column total.

Learn it: Mixed puzzle heuristics
My goal. I can choose a way in to a puzzle: guess and check, suppose all are the same, work backwards, draw it or use a logic grid.
A puzzle needs a way in. Guess and check with a table: use each try to decide whether to go up or down. Suppose all are the same: then see how many swaps you need to fix the total.
Worked example 1
A shop has stools (3 legs) and chairs (4 legs). There are 11 of them with 38 legs in all. How many chairs are there?
  1. Each swap from a stool to a chair adds 1 leg.
  2. 5 legs short needs 5 swaps.
Answer: 5 chairs and 6 stools. Check: 5 × 4 + 6 × 3 = 38.
Worked example 2
Two next-door numbers multiply to 380. What are they?
  1. Try a pair. Is the product too big or too small?
  2. Use each try to choose the next one.
Answer: The numbers are 19 and 20.
Ways in
  1. Guess and check: keep a table of tries.
  2. Suppose all are the same. Count swaps.
  3. Work backwards: undo the last step first.
  4. Check your answer against every fact.
Good to know
  • Draw a queue or a bar model.
  • Check the answer against every clue.
  • A swap changes the total by a fixed amount.
  • Logic grid: ✓ for yes, ✗ for no. One ✓ in each row and column.
