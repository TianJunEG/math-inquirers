Learn it: Number patterns
My goal. I can find the rule of a number pattern and use it to write the next numbers.
A number pattern follows a rule. The rule tells you how to get from one number to the next. A frog hopping along a number strip makes a pattern.
Worked example
Kumar's frog starts on 6. It hops 4 each time. Write the first 6 numbers.
  1. Start on 6. Write 6.
  2. Hop 4. 6 and 4 more is 10.
  3. Hop 4 again. 10 and 4 more is 14.
  4. Keep going: 18, 22, 26. Each number is 4 more than the number before.
Answer: 6, 10, 14, 18, 22, 26. The rule is add 4.
How to find a rule
  1. Look at the first 2 numbers. How far is it from one to the other?
  2. Check the next 2 numbers. Is the hop the same?
  3. Going up? The rule is add. Going down? The rule is take away.
  4. Use the rule to write the next numbers.

Learn it: Number bonds
My goal. I can find the missing partner in a bond to 10 and to 20. I can tell when the 2 sides are equal.
A number bond is 2 numbers that make a whole. 6 and 4 make 10. So 6 + 4 = 10 and 10 = 6 + 4. Bonds to 10 help us find bonds to 20.
Worked example
Aisha has 20 beads. 14 beads are blue and the rest are white. How many beads are white?
  1. The whole is 20. One part is 14. We want the other part.
  2. 14 is 10 and 4 more. We need 4 and some more to make 10: 4 + 6 = 10.
  3. So 14 + 6 = 20. The other part is 6.
  4. Check: 14 + 6 = 20.
Answer: 6 beads are white. 20 = 14 + 6.
How to find the missing partner
  1. Find the whole (the greatest number).
  2. Look at the ones digit of the part you know. What makes 10 with it?
  3. Add the partners. Do they make the whole?

Learn it: Picture graphs
My goal. I can read a picture graph and use it to add and compare.
A picture graph uses pictures to show how many. The key tells what 1 picture means.
Worked example
The graph shows books borrowed in 1 week. How many more on Tuesday than on Thursday?
  1. Read the key. 1 picture is 1 book.
  2. Tuesday has 7 books. Thursday has 2 books.
  3. "How many more" asks for the gap. Subtract: 7 - 2 = 5. Check: 2 + 5 = 7.
Answer: 5 more books are borrowed on Tuesday. 7 - 2 = 5.
How to read a picture graph
  1. Read the title and the key.
  2. Count the pictures in each row you need.
  3. Add for "in all". Subtract for "how many more".

Learn it: Repeating and growing patterns
My goal. I can find the core of a pattern and use it to find a shape far along. I can follow a growing pattern and find the rule.
A repeating pattern has a core that goes round and round. Count in the size of the core to find a far place. A growing pattern gets larger by the same amount each time.
Worked example 1
The pattern keeps going. What is the 14th shape?
  1. Find the core. It is triangle, circle, circle: 3 shapes.
  2. Count in 3s: 3, 6, 9, 12. Place 12 is the end of a core.
  3. Places 13 and 14 start a new core. Place 13 is a triangle and place 14 is a circle.
Answer: The 14th shape is a circle.
Worked example 2
Ali has 4 marbles on day 1. Each day he has 5 more than the day before. How many marbles on day 5?
  1. Make a table. Write 4 under day 1.
  2. Each day has 5 more. The rule is add 5: 9, 14, 19, 24.
  3. Check: 4 hops of 5 is 20. 4 + 20 = 24.
Answer: On day 5 he has 24 marbles.
How to find a far place
  1. Find the core and count its shapes.
  2. Count in that size. Stop at or before your place.
  3. Count on from the last full core.
