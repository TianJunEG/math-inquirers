  1. Read the Learn it page first. It shows how the idea works.

  • Practice for the same ideas as the Holiday Program (our school-holiday course), with new numbers and stories. You do not need to have joined it: every unit starts with an example.
  • 3 short sessions a week, 20 to 25 minutes each, plus the weekend page Go further (see the pace plan).

A child who reads slowly may like a helper to read the Why with them. The answers start on page 79: please ask your child to try first.

  • You will need: a pencil, a rubber, a ruler and a paper corner.

Learn it: Numbers to 10 000
My goal. I can read, build and order 4-digit numbers. I can use clues to find a mystery number.
A 4-digit number has thousands, hundreds, tens and ones. The place decides what a digit is worth. A 0 can never be the first digit.
Worked example
Use the cards 7, 0, 4 and 9 once each. Make the greatest and the smallest 4-digit number.
  1. Greatest: put the greatest digit in the thousands place. Then the next greatest. This gives 9740.
  2. Smallest: the first digit cannot be 0. Use the smallest digit that is not 0: the 4.
  3. Put the other digits in order after it, smallest first: 0, 7, 9. This gives 4079.
Answer: Greatest 9740. Smallest 4079.
How to make a number from cards
  1. Th H T O: the place decides the worth. 1000 more changes the thousands digit.
  2. Odd or even? Choose the ones digit first.
  3. Making a list? Fix the first digit, then list the rest from smallest.

Learn it: Addition and subtraction
My goal. I can find missing digits in sums and take-aways. I can solve magic squares and number pyramids.
In a missing-digit sum, work one column at a time. Start with the ones. Remember to add the digit you carried.
Worked example
Find the 2 missing digits in this sum.
  1. Ones: 7 + 8 = 15. Write 5 and carry 1.
  2. Tens: ? + 6 + 1 carried must end in 9. So ? is 2.
  3. Hundreds: 5 + ? makes 8. So ? is 3.
  4. Check the thousands: 4 + 1 = 5. It matches.
Answer: 4527 + 1368 = 5895. The missing digits are 2 and 3.
How to find a missing digit
  1. Look at the ones column first.
  2. Write the carried 1 above the next column.
  3. For a take-away, turn it round and add to check.

Learn it: Tables of 6, 7, 8 and 9
My goal. I can use facts I know to find new times table facts. I can find patterns and solve number puzzles.
A times table fact you do not know can be built from facts you do know. Split a rectangle of dots into two smaller rectangles.
Worked example
There are 7 rows with 6 dots in each row. How many dots are there in all?
  1. The dashed line splits the dots into 5 rows and 2 rows.
  2. 5 × 6 = 30 and 2 × 6 = 12.
  3. Add the 2 parts: 30 + 12 = 42.
  4. So 7 × 6 = 42.
Answer: 7 × 6 = 42. There are 42 dots.
Ways to find a fact
  1. Split into facts you know: 5 ×, 2 ×, 10 ×.
  2. For 9 ×: take 1 group away from 10 groups.
  3. Check with the last digit.

Learn it: Word problems with bar models
My goal. I can draw a bar model for a word problem. I can use it to decide what to add, subtract or divide.
A bar model is a picture of a word problem. Each bar is a length that shows an amount. Longer bar, greater amount.
Worked example
Siti has 260 beads. Ravi has 135 more beads than Siti. How many beads do they have in all?
  1. Draw a bar for Siti. Draw a longer bar for Ravi. The extra part is 135.
  2. Ravi has 260 + 135 = 395 beads.
  3. Both children: 260 + 395 = 655 beads.
Answer: They have 655 beads in all.
How to draw a bar model
  1. Draw one bar for each amount.
  2. Write what you know on the bars. Put a ? on what you want to find.
  3. Look at the bars. Then add, subtract or divide.
More models
  • Times as many: draw 1 box, then 3 boxes. Count the boxes.
  • Total and difference: take away the extra, then share the rest.
  • Same after giving: half the gap moves.
