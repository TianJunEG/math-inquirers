Math Inquirers Level 6: skill additions (tutor sheet)

Extra items for early finishers and for tutors to set live. They are additions: nothing here is in the printed workbook. Slide items are on an optional slide in the lesson deck (just before Talk time). Bank items are the same skill with new numbers: read or write them for the pair. Children write the answer on their own paper and say it together on three. Nothing is held up to the camera. Never announce who is working on which item.

Lesson 1: Code the Pattern

SLIDETool: Draw itMeeting and catching up (L6-RATE)

(a) Town A and town B are 60 km apart. Ali cycles from A towards B at 15 km/h. At the same time Ben cycles from B towards A at 25 km/h. After how many hours do they meet? (b) Siti leaves a station at 9:00 and travels at 40 km/h. Kumar leaves the same station at 9:30 on the same road at 60 km/h. At what time does Kumar catch Siti?

Answer. (a) 1.5 hours. (b) 10:30.

Say. Draw the road as a line. In (a) both children close the gap, so add their speeds. In (b) Kumar closes the gap, so take the speeds away from each other.

Ask. How far apart are they at the start, and by how many km does the gap change each hour?

Listen for. (a) Closing speed 15 + 25 = 40 km/h, so 60 ÷ 40 = 1.5 hours. (b) Siti has a 20 km start, closing speed 60 − 40 = 20 km/h, so 1 hour after 9:30. Listen for "the gap" and "closing speed".

Two towns are 90 km apart. Mei cycles at 20 km/h and Ravi at 25 km/h towards each other. After how long do they meet?

Answer. 2 hours.

Say. Same skill, new numbers. Use the same tool and the same steps as the slide item (Meeting and catching up).

Ask. How far apart are they at the start, and by how many km does the gap change each hour?

Listen for. (a) Closing speed 15 + 25 = 40 km/h, so 60 ÷ 40 = 1.5 hours. (b) Siti has a 20 km start, closing speed 60 − 40 = 20 km/h, so 1 hour after 9:30. Listen for "the gap" and "closing speed".

SLIDETool: Spot the patternSums that cancel (L6-TELE)

(a) Work out 1/3 − 1/4. Compare it with 1/(3 × 4). (b) Find 1/(3 × 4) + 1/(4 × 5) + 1/(5 × 6) + ... + 1/(9 × 10).

Answer. (a) 1/12. It is the same as 1/(3 × 4). (b) 7/30.

Say. Write each fraction in (b) as a difference, like the one you found in (a). Then write the whole sum out in a line and look at what is left.

Ask. Which terms cancel? Which two terms are left at the end?

Listen for. Each term 1/(n x (n + 1)) = 1/n - 1/(n + 1). The sum is 1/3 − 1/4 + 1/4 − 1/5 + ... + 1/9 − 1/10, so only 1/3 − 1/10 is left. Listen for "the middle cancels". This is Try something simpler: start with two terms.

Lesson 2: Prime Hunters

SLIDETool: Spot the patternDigit sums for 3 and 9 (L6-D9)

(a) Use digit sums. Which of 6 372, 5 481 and 7 296 are multiples of 9? (b) Which of the three numbers are multiples of 3 but not of 9?

Answer. (a) 6 372 and 5 481. (b) 7 296.

Say. Add the digits of each number. If the digit sum is in the 9 times table the number is too. What about the 3 times table?

Ask. Why does a multiple of 9 always have a digit sum that is a multiple of 9? Is 7 296 in the 3 table?

Listen for. 6 + 3 + 7 + 2 = 18, 5 + 4 + 8 + 1 = 18, 7 + 2 + 9 + 6 = 24. 24 is in the 3 table but not the 9 table. Listen for the reason: 10, 100, 1 000 are each 1 more than a multiple of 9.

SLIDETool: Try something simplerZeros at the end (L6-ZERO)

How many zeros are at the end of the product 12 × 15 × 20 × 25 × 35? Do not multiply it all out.

Answer. 4 zeros.

Say. A zero at the end comes from one 2 and one 5 multiplied together, because 2 × 5 = 10. Break each number into 2s and 5s.

Ask. How many 2s are there in all? How many 5s? Which one limits the number of zeros?

Listen for. 2s: 12 has two, 20 has two, so 4. 5s: 15, 20, 25 (two), 35, so 5. There are 4 pairs of 2 and 5, so 4 zeros; the extra 5 does not make a zero without another 2. Check by multiplying: 3 150 000.

Lesson 4: Algebra Machines

SLIDETool: Draw itPercentage change, with units (L6-PCT)

(a) Ravi spends 25% of his money and $60 is left. Find the amount he began with. (b) Mei has 50 stickers. Ali has 20% more stickers than Mei. Ben has 20% fewer stickers than Mei. Find the difference between Ali's and Ben's stickers.

Answer. (a) $80. (b) 20 more stickers.

Say. Draw a bar for the money Ravi had at first. The part he spent is 25%, so 4 units make the whole and 1 unit is spent.

Ask. What percentage is left, and how many units is that? How many dollars is one unit? For (b), what are Ali's and Ben's stickers as percentages of Mei's?

Listen for. (a) 75% is left, 3 units = $60, 1 unit = $20, whole = 4 x $20 = $80. (b) Ali has 120% of 50 = 60 and Ben has 80% of 50 = 40, so 20 more; the gap is 40% of 50. Listen for "percent of what?": the 100% is Mei's stickers.

SLIDETool: Make a tableA rate as an equation (L6-PIPE)

A tank holds 360 litres. A tap fills it at 24 litres each minute. A drain at the bottom lets out 4 litres each minute. The tank starts empty and both are open. (a) How many litres does the tank gain each minute? (b) Find the time, in minutes, until the tank is full. (c) The tap is then closed and the drain stays open. Find the time in minutes until the tank is empty.

Answer. (a) 20 litres each minute. (b) 18 minutes. (c) 90 minutes.

Say. Make a table of minutes and litres in the tank. Then write the rule as an equation: litres = rate x time.

Ask. What is the rate of the tank filling when both are open? What equation can you write, and what operation undoes it?

Listen for. Net rate 24 − 4 = 20 litres per minute. 20 x t = 360, so t = 18. For (c) 4 x t = 360, so t = 90. Listen for "rate x time = amount" and for undoing it by dividing.

SLIDETool: Try something simplerShortcuts before calculating (L6-SHORT)

Find each answer without a long calculation. Say what you noticed. (a) 83 × 83 − 82 × 84 (b) 0.65 × 27 + 0.65 × 73

Answer. (a) 1. (b) 65.

Say. Try something simpler first: work out 5 × 5 − 4 × 6 and 6 × 6 − 5 × 7. What do you notice? For (b), look for a number that appears twice.

Ask. Why does the answer to (a) always come out the same? What can you take out of both parts in (b)?

Listen for. (a) n x n - (n - 1) × (n + 1) = 1 for every n. (b) Take out 0.65: 0.65 × (27 + 73) = 0.65 × 100 = 65. Listen for the small cases and the shared factor.

Lesson 5: Every Possible Way

SLIDETool: Act it outWorst case, the runner-up (L6-VOTES)

Ali, Mei and Ravi stand for class captain. The child with the most votes wins. There are 31 votes in all. After 20 votes are counted, Ali has 9, Mei has 7 and Ravi has 4. How many more of the remaining 11 votes must Ali get to be sure of winning, whatever happens to the other votes?

Answer. 5 more votes.

Say. Act it out as the worst case: the votes Ali does not get all go to Mei. Try Ali getting 3 more, then 4 more, then 5 more.

Ask. In the worst case who is Ali's rival? How do you know that 4 is not enough?

Listen for. If Ali gets x more, Mei could get all the other 11 - x. Ali needs 9 + x to be more than 7 + (11 - x). With x = 4: Ali 13 and Mei 14. With x = 5: Ali 14 and Mei 13. Listen for "give the worst case to the runner-up".

SLIDETool: Act it outWorst case, how many to be sure (L6-SOCKS)

A drawer has 8 red socks, 6 blue socks and 5 green socks. Aisha takes socks out without looking. (a) How many socks must she take to be sure of a pair of the same colour? (b) How many socks must she take to be sure of 3 socks of the same colour? (c) How many socks must she take to be sure of 2 blue socks?

Answer. (a) 4 socks. (b) 7 socks. (c) 15 socks.

Say. Act it out as bad luck: how many can she take with no pair? Then one more must make a pair. Use counters of three colours.

Ask. What is the worst that can happen in (c)? How many socks is that before the first blue sock arrives, and what then?

Listen for. (a) 1 of each colour is 3, so 4. (b) 2 of each is 6, so 7. (c) Worst case: all 8 red and all 5 green first (13), then 1 blue is not enough, so 2 blue: she needs 13 + 2 = 15. Listen for "one fewer than the target, in every colour".

Lesson 7: Turtle Geometry

SLIDETool: Try something simplerPerimeter of a staircase (L6-PERIM)

The figure is a staircase with 4 steps. Every step is 3 cm high and 3 cm wide. All the corners are right angles. (a) Find the perimeter of the figure. (b) A staircase has 10 steps. Every step is 2 cm high and 2 cm wide. Find its perimeter.

Answer. (a) 48 cm. (b) 80 cm.

Say. Try something simpler: a staircase with 1 step, then 2 steps. Can you slide the steps to the edges to make a rectangle?

Ask. What is the width of the whole figure, and the height? What stays the same when you slide a step sideways?

Listen for. Sliding the horizontal pieces to the bottom and the vertical pieces to the left makes a rectangle: the perimeter is the same as the rectangle around the whole figure. (a) 12 + 12 + 12 + 12 = 48. (b) 20 + 20 + 20 + 20 = 80.

Lesson 8: Secret Codes

SLIDETool: Guess and checkLetters stand for digits (L6-CR1)

Each letter stands for one digit. Different letters stand for different digits. A letter at the start of a number is not 0. Find D, E, C, A and B.

Answer. D = 5, E = 7, C = 3, A = 1, B = 4. Check: 573 + 771 = 1344.

Say. A cipher replaces letters with other symbols, and so does this puzzle. Start with the column that cannot be wrong: the answer has 4 digits.

Ask. What is the first letter of the answer? Which column do you do next, and what do you try?

Listen for. A = 1. The ones and tens digits of the answer are the same letter, B, which gives two equations to test. Make a table of tries for C and E. This is Guess and check, not Spot the pattern, even though it sits in the Secret Codes lesson.

Lesson 9: Data Detectives

SLIDETool: Make a tablePercentages from a table (L6-PCT2)

80 children each choose one favourite fruit. 36 choose apple, 20 choose banana, 16 choose mango and the rest choose orange. (a) How many children choose orange? (b) What percentage of the children choose apple? (c) What percentage of the children choose banana or orange?

Answer. (a) 8 children. (b) 45%. (c) 35%.

Say. Make a table of fruit, number of children and percentage. The total of the number column is 80.

Ask. How can you find the percentage for each row? What should the percentage column add up to?

Listen for. Percentage = children / 80 × 100, or 1 child is 1.25%. Apple 36 ÷ 80 = 45%. Banana 25% and orange 10% add to 35%. Listen for a check that the percentages add to 100%.

Lesson 10: Showcase Challenge

SLIDETool: Make a tableThe greatest total you cannot make (L6-PACK)

Stickers are sold in sheets of 4 and sheets of 9. Mei buys some sheets of each kind, or none of one kind. What is the greatest number of stickers that she cannot buy exactly?

Answer. 23 stickers.

Say. Write the numbers 1 to 40 in rows of 4. Mark every total you can make. Look for the first row where every number is marked.

Ask. Once you have 4 totals in a row, what happens next? How do you know 23 is the last one?

Listen for. Four in a row is enough because adding 4 reaches everything after. 24 to 27 are all buyable (24 = 4 × 6, 25 = 16 + 9, 26 = 8 + 18, 27 = 3 × 9), so nothing above 23 is missing, and 23 cannot be made. Listen for the argument.
