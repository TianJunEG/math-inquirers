Math Inquirers Level 5: skill additions (tutor sheet)

Extra items for early finishers and for tutors to set live. They are additions: nothing here is in the printed workbook. Slide items are on an optional slide in the lesson deck (just before Talk time). Bank items are the same skill with new numbers: read or write them for the pair. Children write the answer on their own paper and say it together on three. Nothing is held up to the camera. Never announce who is working on which item.

Ratio previews. Items marked PREVIEW teach ratio (a : b) from first principles. Ratio is a Primary 6 topic and is beyond Level 5: say "a first taste, not a test".

Lesson 1: Million Puzzles

SLIDETool: Guess and checkDigit sums and the greatest number (L5-DIGIT)

Wei has 6 digit cards: 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9. He chooses 5 different cards and makes a 5-digit number. (a) What is the digit sum of all 6 cards? (b) Find the greatest 5-digit multiple of 9 that Wei can make.

Answer. (a) 35. (b) 97542.

Say. A number is a multiple of 9 when its digit sum is in the 9 times table. Wei leaves one card out. Which card must he leave out?

Ask. How does the total of 35 help you choose the card to leave out? Why is the answer not 98754?

Listen for. 35 − 8 = 27, so the 8 is left out. Then the digits go in order from the largest: 9, 7, 5, 4, 2. Listen for "test both sides of the target" and a digit sum used as a check.

SLIDETool: Try something simplerRegroup first, then work out (L5-PARITY)

(a) Find 8 × 17 × 125. Group the numbers first. (b) Do not work out the answers. Which of these are odd? (i) 3 × 7 × 11 × 5 (ii) 13 × 14 + 15 (iii) 25 + 36 + 47 (iv) 1 001 × 2 002 − 3

Answer. (a) 17 000. (b) (i), (ii) and (iv) are odd. (iii) is even.

Say. Look for two numbers that make a friendly number. Then say whether each part is odd or even before you add.

Ask. Which two numbers in (a) make 1 000? What rule do you use for odd times odd, even times anything, and odd plus even?

Listen for. 8 × 125 = 1 000 first. For (b), listen for "odd x odd is odd", "even x anything is even", "even + odd is odd", "odd + odd is even".

SLIDETool: Guess and checkLetter sums (L5-CR1)

Each letter stands for one digit. Different letters stand for different digits. A letter at the start of a number is not 0. Find A, B, C, D and E.

Answer. A = 1, B = 0, C = 8, D = 9, E = 3. Check: 888 + 131 = 1019.

Say. Three digits plus three digits make 4 digits. What does that tell you about the first letter of the answer?

Ask. Which column will you look at second, and why?

Listen for. A = 1 from the size of the sum. Then the hundreds column (C + E + carry) gives B and a carry. Accept a table of tries.

SLIDETool: Guess and checkA multiplication in letters (L5-CR2)

Each letter stands for one digit. Different letters stand for different digits. A letter at the start of a number is not 0. Find K, M, N and P.

Answer. K = 8, M = 4, N = 3, P = 6. Check: 84 × 4 = 336.

Say. The answer has 3 digits and its first two digits are the same. Make a table: choose M, then try different K. What does 10 x M tell you about the smallest M that can work?

Ask. Which values of M can you rule out quickly? How do you know when to stop trying K for one M?

Listen for. M is at least 2 (a 2-digit number times 1 gives at most 2 digits), and the last digit P comes from M x M. Children list tries such as 14 × 4 = 56, 24 × 4 = 96, ... until the first two digits match. Accept an organised table.

Lesson 2: Fraction Feats

SLIDETool: Draw itPREVIEWRatio and fractions (L5-R4)

A bag has red and blue counters in the ratio 3 : 5. This means that for every 3 red counters there are 5 blue counters. (a) What fraction of the counters are red? (b) What fraction of the counters are blue? (c) There are 40 counters in the bag. How many are red?

Answer. (a) 3/8. (b) 5/8. (c) 15 red counters.

Say. Draw a bar with 3 units for red and 5 units for blue. The whole bar is the whole bag. How many units are in the whole bar?

Ask. What fraction of the whole bar is the red part? How many counters is one unit?

Listen for. 3 + 5 = 8 units, red is 3 of the 8, so 3/8. 40 ÷ 8 = 5 counters per unit, 3 × 5 = 15. Listen for the bar model and for the total as 8 units.

Lesson 3: Before and After

SLIDETool: Draw itPREVIEWWhat is a ratio? (L5-R1)

Ali mixes paint. For every 2 tins of blue he uses 3 tins of white. We write blue : white = 2 : 3 and say "2 to 3". The bar model shows 2 units of blue and 3 units of white. Every unit is the same size. (a) Ali uses 4 tins of blue. How many tins of white does he use? (b) Ali mixes 20 tins altogether in the ratio 2 : 3. How many tins of each colour?

Answer. (a) 6 tins of white, because 4 : 6 is the same ratio as 2 : 3. (b) 5 units altogether. One unit is 20 ÷ 5 = 4 tins. Blue 8 tins, white 12 tins.

Say. A ratio compares two amounts with "for every". 2 : 3 means 2 blue for every 3 white. Draw the bars first, and call each box a unit.

Ask. If Ali doubles the mix, what happens to both numbers? How many units are there in all in (b), and how big is one unit?

Listen for. 2 : 3 = 4 : 6 (multiply both numbers by the same number). For (b): 2 + 3 = 5 units, 20 ÷ 5 = 4 per unit, 2 × 4 = 8 and 3 × 4 = 12. Listen for "units" and "the same size". Do not teach more than this on the first meeting.

SLIDETool: Make a tablePREVIEWEqual ratios and simplest form (L5-R2)

A fruit punch uses 6 cups of juice for every 9 cups of water. (a) Write the ratio of juice to water. (b) Write the ratio in its simplest form. (c) How many cups of water go with 10 cups of juice? (d) Fill in the box: 2 : 3 = 12 : [ ]

Answer. (a) 6 : 9. (b) 2 : 3 (divide both numbers by 3). (c) 15 cups of water. (d) 18.

Say. Make a table. Juice in the top row, water in the bottom row. Divide both numbers by 3 to get the smallest whole numbers, then multiply up.

Ask. What do you do to both numbers to keep the ratio the same? How do you know 2 : 3 is simplest and cannot go smaller?

Listen for. The same number on both sides (multiply or divide). Simplest form means no whole number other than 1 divides both. 10 cups of juice is 5 groups of 2, so 5 × 3 = 15.

SLIDETool: Draw itPREVIEWSharing in a ratio (L5-R3)

Mei and Siti share 45 stickers in the ratio 4 : 5. Mei gets the 4 and Siti gets the 5. (a) How many equal parts are there? (b) How many stickers is one part? (c) How many stickers does each girl get? (d) How many more stickers does Siti get than Mei?

Answer. (a) 9 parts. (b) 5 stickers. (c) Mei 20 stickers and Siti 25 stickers. (d) 5 more stickers.

Say. Draw a bar for Mei with 4 boxes and a bar for Siti with 5 boxes, the same size. The 45 stickers fill all the boxes.

Ask. How can you check your answer? What do you notice about the difference between the two shares and one part?

Listen for. 4 + 5 = 9 parts, 45 ÷ 9 = 5. Mei 4 × 5 = 20, Siti 5 × 5 = 25, total 45. The difference 5 is 1 part because 5 − 4 = 1. Listen for the check: the two shares add up to 45.

Lesson 5: Volume Vault

SLIDETool: Act it outDice and hidden faces (L5-DICE)

A tower is made of 3 dice, one on top of another, standing on a table. Opposite faces of each die sum to 7. The upper face of the highest die shows 4. Find the total number of dots on the hidden faces: the face touching the table, plus each pair of faces that touch each other.

Answer. 17 dots.

Say. Take a real die if you have one. Turn it and look: which faces are on opposite sides? What do two opposite faces always add up to?

Ask. Which hidden faces form pairs that add up to 7? Which one hidden face is left over?

Listen for. The top and bottom of the bottom die add to 7. The top and bottom of the middle die add to 7. The bottom of the top die is 7 − 4 = 3. Listen for "pairs of opposite faces".

Lesson 6: Rate Racers

SLIDETool: Make a tableTotals you cannot make (L5-PACK)

Cookies are sold in packs of 5 and packs of 8. Aisha may buy several packs of each kind, or none of one kind. (a) Which totals from 1 to 20 can she buy? (b) Find the greatest total she cannot buy.

Answer. (a) 5, 8, 10, 13, 15, 16, 18, 20. (b) 27.

Say. Write the numbers 1 to 40 in rows of 5. Mark the multiples of 8 and then everything you can reach by adding 5s.

Ask. When you have 5 totals in a row that you can buy, what happens after that? Why?

Listen for. Five in a row can be bought (28 to 32), so adding 5 reaches every larger total. 27 is the last gap. Listen for the argument "five in a row means all the rest".

SLIDETool: Make a tableCatching up (L5-CATCH)

Ali walks 60 m each minute. Ben starts from the same place 5 minutes later and walks 80 m each minute in the same direction. (a) How far ahead is Ali when Ben starts? (b) How many metres does Ben gain on Ali each minute? (c) How many minutes after Ben starts does he catch Ali?

Answer. (a) 300 m. (b) 20 m. (c) 15 minutes.

Say. Make a table with the minutes since Ben started. Write how far each of them has walked, and the gap between them.

Ask. What happens to the gap each minute? How can you get from 300 m to no gap without listing every minute?

Listen for. The gap shrinks by 20 m every minute. 300 ÷ 20 = 15. Listen for "the gap closes by the difference in speeds" (this is a preview of an idea met again in later years).

SLIDETool: Make a tableThree workers (L5-WORK)

Ali lays 10 bricks each minute and Ben lays 14 bricks each minute. They start together. After 5 minutes Kumar joins them and lays 6 bricks each minute. (a) How many bricks do Ali and Ben lay in the first 5 minutes? (b) They must lay 450 bricks. How many minutes do they work altogether?

Answer. (a) 120 bricks. (b) 16 minutes.

Say. Make a table with two phases: before Kumar joins, and after. In each phase write how many bricks are laid each minute.

Ask. How many bricks are left after the first phase? How many bricks each minute do all three lay together?

Listen for. Phase 1: 24 bricks each minute for 5 minutes. Phase 2: 30 bricks each minute for the rest. 450 − 120 = 330, 330 ÷ 30 = 11. Listen for the two-phase table.

SLIDETool: Make a tablePREVIEWEqual ratios and scaling (L5-R5)

A rice recipe uses 2 cups of rice for every 5 cups of water. The ratio of rice to water is 2 : 5. (a) How many cups of water go with 6 cups of rice? (b) How many cups of rice go with 25 cups of water? (c) Wei uses 12 cups of rice. How many cups of rice and water does he use altogether?

Answer. (a) 15 cups of water. (b) 10 cups of rice. (c) 12 + 30 = 42 cups altogether.

Say. Make a table: each column is the same recipe made again. Multiply both numbers by the same number.

Ask. How many times bigger is 6 cups of rice than 2? What do you do to the water? How could you start from the water in (b)?

Listen for. Scale factor: 6 ÷ 2 = 3, so water 5 × 3 = 15. In (b) 25 ÷ 5 = 5 and rice 2 × 5 = 10. In (c) 12 ÷ 2 = 6, water 5 × 6 = 30. A rate is a ratio in disguise: one number is "for every" the other.

Lesson 7: Percentage Puzzles

SLIDETool: Draw itPREVIEWRatio and percentage (L5-R6)

In a class the ratio of boys to girls is 3 : 2. (a) What fraction of the class are boys? (b) What percentage of the class are girls? (c) There are 30 children in the class. How many boys are there?

Answer. (a) 3/5. (b) 40%. (c) 18 boys.

Say. Draw a bar of 5 equal units: 3 for boys and 2 for girls. The whole bar is 100%.

Ask. What percentage is one unit? How does that help with the girls?

Listen for. One unit is 100% / 5 = 20%, so girls are 2 × 20% = 40%. For (c) 30 ÷ 5 = 6 children per unit and 3 × 6 = 18. Listen for "the whole is 5 units".

Lesson 8: Angle Chasers

SLIDETool: Spot the patternWeekdays in a year (L5-YEAR)

A year has 365 days. In one year, 1 January is a Tuesday. (a) How many whole weeks are in 365 days, and how many days are left over? (b) How many Tuesdays are in the year? (c) What day of the week is 31 December? (d) What day of the week is 1 January of the next year?

Answer. (a) 52 weeks and 1 day. (b) 53 Tuesdays. (c) Tuesday. (d) Wednesday.

Say. Divide 365 by 7. The weeks use up every day of the week 52 times. Which day is the one extra day?

Ask. Which day of the week is day 365? Is any other day of the week in the year 53 times?

Listen for. 365 = 52 × 7 + 1. The extra day is the same weekday as 1 January, so only Tuesday occurs 53 times. Listen for the remainder idea.

Lesson 9: Pattern Generalisers

SLIDETool: Spot the patternLast digits of products (L5-LAST)

(a) Multiply 7 by itself 30 times: 7 × 7 × 7 x ... with 30 sevens. What is the last digit of the answer? (b) Multiply 4 by itself 25 times. What is the last digit of the answer?

Answer. (a) 9. (b) 4.

Say. Only the last digit matters, so keep only the last digit each time. Write the last digit after 1 seven, 2 sevens, 3 sevens, 4 sevens, and carry on.

Ask. When does the list of last digits start to repeat? How long is the cycle, and where does 30 land in it?

Listen for. Sevens: 7, 9, 3, 1, then repeats (cycle of 4). 30 = 7 × 4 + 2, so the 2nd in the cycle: 9. Fours: 4, 6, 4, 6 (cycle of 2); 25 is odd, so 4.

BANKTool: Spot the patternLast digits of products (L5-LAST-V)

Say. Same skill, new numbers. Use the same tool and the same steps as the slide item (Last digits of products).

Listen for. Sevens: 7, 9, 3, 1, then repeats (cycle of 4). 30 = 7 × 4 + 2, so the 2nd in the cycle: 9. Fours: 4, 6, 4, 6 (cycle of 2); 25 is odd, so 4.

Lesson 10: Challenge Day

SLIDETool: List every wayTriangles by size (L5-TRI)

The figure is a big triangle with a line from its top corner to each of 2 points on the base, and 1 line cutting across it, parallel to the base. Count all the triangles of every size.

Answer. 12 triangles.

Say. Count by size. First count the smallest triangles, then the ones made of 2 pieces, and so on. Keep a list.

Ask. Which lines do you need to make any triangle? What do all the triangles have in common?

Listen for. Every triangle uses the top corner and two of the 4 lines from it (6 ways to choose), and then is cut off either by the base or by the line across (2 ways): 6 × 2 = 12. Accept a size-by-size list that totals 12, for example 6 triangles that stop at the line across and 6 that reach the base.

SLIDETool: List every wayCounting numbers in bounds (L5-BOUNDS)

How many whole numbers between 300 and 600 have digits that add up to 6? Write them from smallest to greatest.

Answer. 9 numbers: 303, 312, 321, 330, 402, 411, 420, 501, 510.

Say. Split into cases by the first digit: 3, 4 or 5. For each case, what must the other two digits add up to?

Ask. Why is 600 not in the list? How many pairs of digits make 3? Make 2? Make 1?
