## Code words (owner decision of 23 Oct 2026: remove the ambiguous words)
This replaces the "kept on purpose" decision under M2 above. Rule now: no wrong placement of the answers may spell a real word, and the word must still be hard to guess.

**Rule 1, one arrangement only.** The letters of every code word have exactly one arrangement that is a word in the large list the build uses (`gen/words_en.txt.gz`, about 129 000 words; `common.anagrams`). New check V3d on every code-word page and on P1-04, P1-07 and P1-16. Also tested by me against a separate dictionary (web2, gcide, and the wordfreq words with Zipf frequency 2.5 or more, 285 736 words): 0 other arrangements for every word.

**Words found with a second arrangement, and replaced** (all in `BIG`): P1-02 CLOUD (COULD) now JUICE; P1-03 FRIEND (FINDER, REFIND, REDFIN) now SHADOW; P1-04 BELOW (BOWEL, ELBOW) now FRUIT; P1-07 SNAKE (SNEAK, SKEAN) now ENJOY; P1-10 STREAM (MASTER, MARTES, REMAST) now COUSIN; P1-16 MONDAY (DYNAMO) now FROZEN; P1-18 OCEAN (CANOE) now CLIMB; P1-19 ADULT (DULAT) now BUILD. Kept, because their letters have one arrangement: SQUARE (P1-05), SECOND (P1-06), FAMILY (P1-11), NUMBER (P1-13), PIANO (P1-15), LENGTH (P1-17). Words were chosen from the 6 000 most frequent English words, child-suitable, no category hint for the page, and with few one-letter neighbours so a code table with decoys can still be built (P1-03 and the clock and shape pages would not build with some candidates, for example FRESH, CHILD, ABOVE). The colour-by-code sheets (P1 14 sheets, P2 15 sheets) and the pack's colour pages P1-01, P1-12 and P1-14 have no code word (they use a colour key and a tally), so nothing there changed and the galleries were not rebuilt.

**Rule 2, guessing made hard by other means.**
- P1-04 and P1-07 now carry a decoy letter. P1-04 lists six numbers for five children ("Nobody has the number 23." is one clue); P1-07 shows six children for five places ("Kumar is not in the queue."). The left-out number or child has a letter that is not in the word. The solver tries every choice of 5 from 6 and every order (720): exactly one satisfies the clues, and all 719 wrong ones, including any that use the decoy letter, spell no word (decoy letters come from `common.decoy_options`, which allows only decoys where no ordered pick of 5 of the 6 letters spells another word). Greatest, smallest and second greatest mean the same with or without the unused number (check V1c). The left-out clue is always needed (V1b).
- The code pages keep their code table with 4 to 5 decoy letters and the V4 rule (at least 3 common words fit the table letters). P1-16 reads its sixth letter from a table of 4 letters (the true letter and 3 decoys); the old V4 (two common words from the six letters) cannot hold when the word has one arrangement, so V4 is now "the sixth letter is one of at least 4 table letters".
- Not done, said plainly: a person who can unscramble letters can still find the word from the six letters on P1-04 and P1-07 (FRUIT, ENJOY), because the only word that fits is the right one. The decoy and the 5-of-6 choice make that hard for Primary 1 pupils and mean a half-right queue or grid never "passes" by accident. Letting a second word fit would bring back the problem the owner removed.
- Double slips on code pages (two wrong answers) can still spell a word on a few pages in my dictionary (for example SQUARE to SQUADS, SECOND to RECORD, FAMILY to LAZILY, NUMBER to BUMMER, BUMPER, JUMPER, MEMBER, PIANO to PINNA, SHADOW to SHAPOO, CLIMB to BLIMP, CLICK, JUICE to DUNCE, COUSIN to MOULIN). This is the known limit L2 (95% rule met); it is not a wrong placement of the right answers.
