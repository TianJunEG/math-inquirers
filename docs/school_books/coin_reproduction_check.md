# Reproducing Singapore coins in the school puzzle pack: rules check (23 Oct 2026)

Status: read from the official MAS page on 23 Oct 2026 (the page opened; it is not legal advice, and the owner's lawyer should confirm before the pack is sold).

Source: Monetary Authority of Singapore, "Using Images of Singapore Currency", https://www.mas.gov.sg/currency/using-images-of-singapore-currency (opened 23 Oct 2026; the older address /currency/reproduction-of-singapore-currency returned 404). It rests on sections 20(1) and 20(2) of the Currency Act 1967 and a 2006 gazette notice (named by the page; the Act itself was not read).

## What the page says (our summary)
- Permission is NOT needed to distribute "a publication with a reproduction for educational purposes", or for news, factual articles and reports, if the general conditions are met.
- Permission IS needed for importing, making, selling, circulating or distributing merchandise or products that contain a reproduction, or a design used on a note or coin, and for an advertisement that does not meet the conditions.
- General conditions: keep the dignity, integrity and image of the note or coin; do not use images so that they could be mistaken as genuine; do not distort the President's image or any national symbol; nothing offensive; print on material clearly different from genuine coins and notes.
- Extra conditions for notes only: proportional scaling, "SPECIMEN" across a whole flat note, size at least 150% or at most 60% of the real note in both directions in books, one-sided only. The page gives no size rule for coins.
- Penalty without permission: a fine up to $2,000, up to 3 months in prison, or both.

## Is a paid photocopy-licence workbook an "educational publication" or "merchandise"?
Not settled by the page. A workbook sold to schools looks like an educational publication, but a seller could be read as selling "products". We could not settle this, so it is an owner and lawyer question (owner decision D1 below).

## What the pack does now (safe choice)
The pack and the colour-by-code sheets do NOT copy any real Singapore coin design or photograph. The coins are original line art: a plain ring with a ridged or smooth edge hint, an original motif per value (not the real motifs), the value large with "cents" or "$" under it, no "SINGAPORE" lettering and no national symbol. Only the relative sizes follow the real diameters (5c 16.2 mm, 10c 18.5 mm, 20c 21.4 mm, 50c 24.7 mm, $1 24.65 mm), which is a fact and not a design. The coins are drawn much larger than real size and only in black and white, so they cannot be mistaken for genuine money.

## If the owner wants the real designs
Ask MAS in writing (final design, reasons, contact number, through the feedback form on the page above) or get a lawyer's view that a sold, photocopiable workbook counts as an educational publication. Until then do not trace the real coins or use MAS photographs.
