## 1. Design principles

1. **Year arc: toolbox, deepen, apply, contest.** Term 1 builds the number toolbox with topics the holiday course did not teach (big-number operations, multiplication and division methods, divisibility, equivalent fractions, fractions and decimals, perpendicular and parallel lines, tables). Term 2 goes deep on number: written methods, remainders, fractions, decimals, bar models. Term 3 goes deep on shape, space and measure. Term 4 covers data, then names the problem-solving moves one at a time, then looks ahead to Primary 5. A child who did the holiday course starts at Term 1 and meets nothing twice; a child who continues meets each strand again at greater depth.
2. **Spiral by design, not by repeating.** Every lesson opens with a **6-minute remember warm-up**: three short questions that carry whatever the lesson uses from earlier (the previous lesson, last term, or the holiday course in Term 1). Each question is self-contained, with a worked example on the slide, so a child who joined this term can do it. The rest of the opening is the untaught problem. Strands return as new contexts: factor pairs (1.3) come back as remainder cycles (2.2), rectangles with a fixed area (3.3) and pairing patterns (4.3).
3. **One problem move per lesson is named out loud.** Each lesson has a key thinking skill (draw a model, make a list, work backwards, solve a smaller case, suppose, check against every fact). Terms 1 to 3 use the moves inside topics; Term 4 teaches them as moves in their own right.
4. **Inquiry before method.** Every lesson starts with an untaught problem (S tier) and the child notices a pattern, tests it, then explains why. Methods are named after the children have found a way, not before. Contest-style ideas (digit tests, pairing sums, cryptarithms, chance, counting by size) are taught at Think harder, with a gentler Start here lead-in that is not itself the contest idea.
5. **Three tiers in every lesson, with no tier hidden or ranked.** Same topic, three ways in. Go-further material includes contest-style problems that are written fresh (not copied) and kept in private tutor notes, not on public pages.
6. **Lesson 10 of every term is a Challenge Day plus the end-of-term check.** Lesson 9 is the added lesson (a single-topic lesson nothing later depends on). A school term is about 10 weeks, so there is no spare week: a missed lesson is made up from the recording, or in a holiday class if there is a seat. No dates are hard-coded.
7. **Every lesson stands alone.** Places open every term and the fee is prorated, so a child may start at any term and may join part-way through. Ideas used from an earlier lesson are reopened in the 6-minute warm-up, in the same term as well as from earlier terms.
