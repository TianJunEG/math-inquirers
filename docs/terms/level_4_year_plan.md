Tier key: **S** = Start here (core, approachable, untaught: a child can start without the method). **T** = Think harder (a taught idea in a new setting). **G** = Go further (mastery, then extension). Labels are neutral and chosen by the child; reports never rank children by tier.

## 1. Design principles

1. **Year arc: map, deepen, apply, contest.** Term 1 meets every Primary 4 strand once as puzzles (the adapted holiday lessons). Term 2 goes deep on number: written methods, remainders, fractions and decimals, bar models. Term 3 goes deep on shape, space and measure. Term 4 covers data, then names the problem-solving moves one at a time, then looks ahead to Primary 5. A child joining at Term 1 gets the whole map; a child who continues meets each strand again at greater depth.
2. **Spiral by design, not by repeating.** Every lesson opens with a 10-minute retrieval warm-up drawn from earlier lessons (one from last lesson, one from last term, one from two terms back). Strands return as new contexts: factor pairs (1.3) come back as remainder cycles (2.2), rectangles with a fixed area (3.3) and pairing patterns (4.3).
3. **One problem move per lesson is named out loud.** Each lesson has a key thinking skill (draw a model, make a list, work backwards, solve a smaller case, suppose, check against every fact). Terms 1 to 3 use the moves inside topics; Term 4 teaches them as moves in their own right.
4. **Inquiry before method.** Every lesson starts with an untaught problem (S tier) and the child notices a pattern, tests it, then explains why. Methods are named after the children have found a way, not before.
5. **Three tiers in every lesson, with no tier hidden or ranked.** Same topic, three ways in. Go-further material includes contest-style problems that are written fresh (not copied) and kept in private tutor notes, not on public pages.
6. **Lesson 9 of every term is a Challenge Day plus the end-of-term check** (Term 1 adapts the holiday Challenge Day). That is the buffer-friendly design: lessons 1 to 8 carry the content; a term of about 10 school weeks leaves one spare week for a missed lesson or a catch-up. No dates are hard-coded.

## 3. How the holiday lessons are reused

Term 1 adapts 9 of the 10 holiday lessons. **Cut: holiday L9 Data Detectives** (line graphs). Reason: the term-time group starts at the beginning of the school year when graphs are not yet taught, and a rebuilt version with pie charts (new to P4 in 2021, a gap in the holiday course) fits Term 4. Its best items (read a trend, compare two lines, misleading scale) return as the deliberate spiral in 4.1. **No lessons are merged**; nothing is dropped because it was weak. Changes carried over from the audits:

- L2: keep; move 2-decimal rounding items out.
- L3: remove algebra letters (T7, CH4) as the P4 SPEC bans them.
- L1, L4: keep; the unlike-denominator fraction sums (L4 CH2/5/6/7) are P4 (two denominators) and need no swap.
- L5: keep; rounding to 2 d.p. moved to Term 2 if used at all.
- L6: needs rework (page 2 packs four sub-skills). The 8-point compass is not in the 2021 syllabus [confirmed]; keep it as a short game labelled "not on the school syllabus". Clock-hand angles are optional Go-further.
- L7, L8: layout fixes (page flow) from the audits; content stays.
- L10: becomes Challenge Day 1 and the Term 1 check. Its heuristics reappear in Term 4 as full lessons, which is deliberate spiral, not duplication.

Terms 2 to 4: no holiday lesson is copied. Where a strand returns (fractions, decimals, area, symmetry, data) the content is new (operations, models, borders, mirrors, pie charts).

## 5. Assessment and progress

**End-of-term check (10 questions, about 30 minutes, inside lesson 9).** Four S questions (one per main topic of the term), three T, three G. Children choose where to start and may skip two. Marks are not published to other children; the aim is a picture, not a grade. Term focus: T1 place value, brackets, factor pairs, fraction of a set, decimal order, composite area, unknown angle, cube net, one heuristic, one open problem. T2 and T3 and T4 follow the same shape with each term's strands. Contest-type items (digit test, letter sum, pairing, two-event timetable) may appear as T or G questions in the term check, never as the S questions.

**Parent report line per term** (description, not ranking):
- T1: "[Name] met all the Primary 4 areas as puzzles; this term they were strongest at [area] and are building [skill]."
- T2: "[Name] worked on written methods, fractions and decimals; they can now [specific thing] and are practising [skill]."
- T3: "[Name] explored shapes, angles and measure; they can [specific thing] and are learning to [skill]."
- T4: "[Name] learned named problem-solving moves and previewed Primary 5; they used [move] most often and can [specific thing]."

Do not claim exam improvement; this is enrichment and we have no outcome data.
