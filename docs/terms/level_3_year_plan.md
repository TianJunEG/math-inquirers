## 1. Design principles

1. **A year in four arcs.** T1 Number, calculation and everyday tools (all new; one careful pass over the number, money, time and word-problem strands). T2 Measurement, shape, data and patterns. T3 Space, modelling and reasoning. T4 Structure, counting and contest thinking, then a showcase. T2 and T3 end with a project, T4 with a showcase, so the year rises from "learn the idea" to "use it on something open".
2. **Spiral on purpose, not by repeating.** Every strand returns at least twice in a new setting (fractions: T1 number line and simplest form, T3 fractions in measures). Every lesson opens with a 6-minute "Remember" warm-up from an earlier term (two items from the last term, one from two terms back). In T1 the warm-up revisits the holiday course; in T2-T4 it is also what lets a mid-year joiner start cold. Spiral reuse is listed in s3 and s4.
3. **Problem solving is taught as a ladder of named moves**: T1 draw it / list it / guess and check; T2 table, pattern, make a simpler problem; T3 bar model, work backwards, cut and rearrange; T4 cases, count systematically, argue by contradiction ("why can't it be...?"). Each lesson names one move and the tutor guide shows it.
4. **Inquiry grows term by term.** T1 children explain a result they found. T2 they design a small survey or shop. T3 they plan under constraints (budget, area). T4 they pose problems for others and defend a method. Each term's last lessons are open tasks with no single answer path.
5. **Tiers (owner principle, neutral labels).** Start here = core a child can approach before the lesson (objects, drawing, counting, guess-check, skills from the level below). Think harder = the idea taught in the lesson, in a new setting; **contest-style items live here at the least**. Go further = mastery plus extension (multi-step, generalise, justify, pose). Labels are never shown as ranks or "ability" groups; every child tries Start here, then picks. Worksheet slot is only about 15-18 working minutes (timing audit), so 5-6 questions per lesson, rest "if time".
6. **Honest scope.** Enrichment alongside school, not a replacement: it does not teach every school skill to speed (written-algorithm fluency, mental-arithmetic speed). No speed content at this level. No claim of "MOE-aligned", "GEP preparation" or SASMO preparation. Real contest papers are private-paper material: refer by tag only, never copy; problems here are original. Term-time pacing follows a typical school order only loosely (schools differ) [UNVERIFIED for any single school].
7. **Each term stands on its own.** A child may join at the start of any term (or mid-term, prorated). No lesson assumes an earlier term except through the warm-up; if a lesson needs one earlier idea, its Start here re-teaches it in under 5 minutes.

## 5. Assessment and progress

**End-of-term check (10 questions, one per lesson, about 25 minutes, run in the last 25 minutes of the term's final lesson, no ranking).** Question n tests the core idea of lesson n of that term, usually at Start here or Think harder level (never a contest item at Start here); two of the ten are Think harder items from the term's contest-style lessons. Each question is tagged by lesson and strand (and, from Term 2, by SASMO type such as spatial, divisibility or counting), so the tutor sees which strand needs another look. The result is shown as a strand profile, not a mark out of 10. A child who joined late or missed a lesson leaves those questions out; the profile covers only lessons attended, and it also tells a new joiner which catch-up pages to use. Retention of earlier terms is not in the check; the tutor logs it from the warm-up.

**Parent report line per term (observations, not outcome claims).**
- T1: "This term [Name] learned to [explain a written method and draw a strip for a two-step story]; next we will practise [one strand from the check] while we move to measures, shape and data."
- T2: "[Name] can now [read a graph's scale and measure area and perimeter]; a thing to practise at home is [one strand]."
- T3: "[Name] tried [spatial and modelling tasks] and [chose a bar model without prompting / needed hints]; the term project shows [one noticed strength]."
- T4: "Across the year [Name] [moved from ... to ...] in [one thinking move]; for next year, [one suggestion: join Level 4 / revisit one strand]."
