## 1. Design principles

- **The year has one arc: Number and Fractions (T1), Measure and Make (T2), Parts, Percent and Pattern (T3), Shape, Logic and Strategy (T4).** Each term ends with a Review and Challenge lesson (lesson 9) that mixes the term's ideas and is the end-of-term check. Terms 1-3 follow the usual school order of strands, a little ahead of or alongside school; Term 4 deliberately goes wider than school because P5 children are busy with year-end exams (check dates).
- **Spiral revisiting is planned, not accidental.** Each term has 2 or 3 "return visits": an old idea in a new setting (for example, bar models return in T3 with three quantities; triangle area returns in T2 as dissection; angle chasing returns in T4 with polygons). Each return is named in the Topics column as "spiral" so a new Term 2 child is never lost, and a returning child sees the idea again from a new side.
- **A new child can join at Term 1; a continuing child gets more, not the same.** Term 1 lessons need only P4 knowledge. Later terms assume only what Term 1 and school have taught, and each opening warm-up restates the needed fact, so joining mid-year is possible but not planned for.
- **Problem solving is built one tool at a time.** T1: draw a bar model, list in order, make a table. T2: dissect and rearrange, work with units and conversions, read graphs. T3: supposition and equal-share thinking, balance and "undo", systematic counting. T4: parity and remainders, logic elimination, working backwards, multi-step modelling. Every Review lesson asks the child to choose a move and say why.
- **Inquiry runs through every lesson** ("Notice it. Test it. Explain why."): an explore with objects or a picture, 30 seconds of silent thinking, pair talk, then a worked example. The Open Investigation page stays. In Term 4 the investigation grows from guided to child-posed (lessons 35 and 36).
- **Tiers stay neutral and are the same as the holiday course:** *Start here* = core, approachable, untaught-in-advance; *Think harder* = a taught idea in a new setting; *Go further* = mastery plus extension (P6 previews are labelled). Private-paper safeguarding is unchanged: nobody is ranked, answers are shown privately, no scores are recorded or read aloud.

**Gaps and where school outpaces us.** (a) Ratio is P6 in the 2021 syllabus (p. 43); Lesson 22 uses unit models without notation, which is correct for P5. (b) The syllabus has no division by a 2-digit number in any primary year (P4 stops at 4 digits by 1, p. 37), so there is no gap here; lesson 2 touches P4 written methods only. **Olympiad-style moves worked in** (taken from the SASMO G5 analysis; no contest problem copied): bar model with units; supposition (heads and legs); systematic listing; working backwards; parity and remainder reasoning; digit tests for 3 and 9; counting multiples in a range; dissect and rearrange; draw a smaller case; logic elimination with a grid; column-by-column cryptarithms; "extreme case" (largest or smallest possible, lessons 26 and 32); counting triangles by size; dice and painted cubes; adding rates of work; pairing numbers to sum a list; the cycle and remainder trick for calendars; a first pigeonhole example.

## 5. Assessment and progress

**End-of-term check (lesson 9, 30 minutes, private, nobody ranked):** 10 questions: 4 Start here (one skill each from the term), 3 Think harder (new setting), 2 Go further, and 1 "explain why" written in a sentence. Answers are never read aloud or compared. It is used to plan the next term and to decide what to revisit; it is not a grade. Two of the ten are always from an earlier term (spiral).

**Parent report line per term** (a sentence, not a score), examples:
- T1: "This term your child worked on big numbers, fractions and bar models, and is growing in explaining why a step works."
- T2: "Your child practised cutting and rearranging shapes, volume and rate, and read graphs."
- T3: "Your child explored percent, balance and patterns, and used a table to list cases."
- T4: "Your child worked with angles in shapes, logic, digit puzzles and parity, and wrote a problem of their own."
Do not state outcomes (PSLE results, competition prizes). This program does not promise them.
