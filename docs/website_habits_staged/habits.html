How children learn

The six inquiry habits

Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Every lesson practises six inquiry habits: noticing, wondering, trying, checking, explaining and looking back. Children practise how to think as well as what to write.

One set, all six levels

Six habits, practised in every lesson

Each lesson names one focus habit, shown as a badge on the slides and in the workbook, and children practise the other habits around it. The habits are the same at every level. What changes is how much the tutor models and how much the child leads.

Notice

“What do I see?”

Pause on the picture or the numbers and let the child say what they see before you name anything.

Ages 6-8
The tutor points to the picture and asks what we see; children say it in their own words.
Ages 8-10
Children list what stays the same and what changes, and name the pattern.
Ages 10-13
Children describe the structure first, then say which parts matter.

Wonder

“What could I ask?”

Invite a question or a what-if before any answer is given.

Ages 6-8
The tutor asks a "what if?" and children choose one thing to change.
Ages 8-10
Children write one question or "what if?" of their own before starting.
Ages 10-13
Children pose and refine their own question, and decide which is worth trying.

Try

“Let me test it”

Ask the child to test one example, or a smaller case, before deciding.

Ages 6-8
Children test one example with counters, coins or strips while the tutor models.
Ages 8-10
Children pick an example or a smaller case, test it and record what happened.
Ages 10-13
Children choose their own tests, including smaller cases, and keep a record.

Check

“Does it always work?”

Ask the child to test a second case before saying it works.

Ages 6-8
Children try one more number and see whether it still works, with the tutor.
Ages 8-10
Children test a second case and check by another route, such as going backwards.
Ages 10-13
Children look for a case that might break the idea, and check by estimating or going backwards.

Explain

“Here is why”

Ask for the reason in the child's own words, said or written.

Ages 6-8
Children say or draw why, as if teaching a friend; the tutor writes it down.
Ages 8-10
Children say or write a reason that uses the numbers or the picture.
Ages 10-13
Children write a short reason that covers every case, and listen to a different one.

Look back

“What did I learn? What would I change?”

Ask what was learnt and what they would change, after the work is done.

Ages 6-8
Children answer three look-back prompts, by saying, drawing or writing one word.
Ages 8-10
Children note what was hard, what helped and one thing to do differently.
Ages 10-13
Children lead the look-back, noting a tool used and a way to check differently.

How we use them

The tutor asks the prompt, waits, and gives a hint only if a child is stuck. A note after each lesson tells parents which habits we practised and one thing your child said or did. It records what happened in class. It is not a grade.

The six Math Inquirers habits are our own working set. They sit alongside frameworks that inspire our design, such as Pólya's problem-solving steps and mathematical habits of mind. We do not promise exam or competition results.
