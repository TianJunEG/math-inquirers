# Holiday program: what your child will be able to do (parent wording, draft)

Draft for the owner to edit. Written for Singapore parents, plain English, one short paragraph and an "I can" list per level. No prices, no exam or school-selection promises, no scores. Each list is traced to specific lessons in `docs/pedagogy/holiday_review.md` (section 6). These are what the lessons are designed to build; they have not yet been checked with children, so keep the wording as "your child will practise" until the beta shows otherwise.

Use once, near the top of each level page or in the welcome message:

> Math Inquirers is about how your child thinks, not about marks. Children are never ranked, and they can always say "pass". Our motto is: Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

---

## Level 1 (ages 6 to 8, builds on Primary 1 maths)

Over ten lessons your child plays with patterns, number bonds, coins, shapes and puzzles in a small group of up to eight. We care less about fast answers and more about how your child thinks: spotting a pattern, trying an idea with counters, and saying why it works. Mistakes are welcome here; they are how we learn.

**By the end, your child will practise being able to say:**

- I can find the rule in a counting pattern and use it to say what comes next, even far along.
- I can find all the ways to make a number or an amount of coins, and say how I know I have them all.
- I can work backwards to find a mystery number.
- I can use counters to check whether a number sentence is true, and say what the equals sign means.
- I can read a picture graph, and say what it can and cannot tell us.
- When I am stuck, I can choose something to try, like drawing it or using objects, and tell my tutor what helped.

---

## Level 2 (ages 7 to 9, builds on Primary 2 maths)

Your child will work through puzzles about numbers to 1000, money, fractions, shapes and measures. Each lesson starts with something hands-on, like cutting paper circles or hanging a simple balance, and then moves to puzzles with more than one answer. The last lesson is Puzzle Day, when children choose how to start: draw it, list it, or make a table.

**By the end, your child will practise being able to say:**

- I can make a list in order so I do not miss any answers.
- I can find a missing digit by starting at the ones, and check by adding back.
- I can solve "how many of each?" puzzles, like hens and goats, by making a table.
- I can use groups and times tables to find the 20th thing in a repeating pattern without drawing them all.
- I can run a number machine backwards to find the number I started with, and check by going forwards.
- I can compare fractions using bars, and explain my answer in my own words.

---

## Level 3 (ages 8 to 10, builds on Primary 3 maths)

Your child will explore four-digit numbers, times tables, bar models, remainders, fractions, area and angles through puzzles that have a clear way in and room to go further. Children are asked "how do you know?" often, and learn to test an idea before they trust it.

**By the end, your child will practise being able to say:**

- I can list every possibility in order and show I have them all.
- I can draw a bar model for a word problem and use it to check my answer.
- I can solve missing-digit puzzles by guessing, checking and improving.
- I can find a pattern in the times tables and say why it works.
- I can decide what a leftover means in a story, like how many cars are needed for 29 children.
- I can test a friend's claim with an example, such as "two gardens with the same fence must have the same area".

---

## Level 4 (ages 9 to 11, builds on Primary 4 maths)

Your child will hunt for factors, round and compare big numbers and decimals, work with fractions, cut and rearrange shapes, and read graphs carefully. Most of each lesson is spent working on problems in pairs and alone, and we finish by asking children to explain which way into a problem they chose and why.

**By the end, your child will practise being able to say:**

- I can find every factor pair by working in order, and use them to solve sharing and timetable puzzles.
- I can round a number and work back to the smallest and largest numbers that round to it, and I can read and order decimals.
- I can turn an improper fraction into a mixed number and find a fraction of a set using a bar model.
- I can cut and rearrange shapes to find area and perimeter, and test "bigger area means bigger perimeter" with examples.
- I can read a line graph, and spot a graph that gives the wrong impression.
- I can choose a way into a tricky puzzle, like guess and check, working backwards or a logic grid, and check my answer against every clue.

---

## Level 5 (ages 10 to 12, builds on Primary 5 maths)

Your child will work with very large numbers, fractions, triangles, volume, rates, percentages and angles. The lessons are about reasons: why a rule works, what stays the same, and how to check an answer against every clue. The last lesson is Challenge Day, when children pick their own way into unfamiliar puzzles.

**By the end, your child will practise being able to say:**

- I can test the closest number just below and just above a target, and estimate to check a big calculation.
- I can draw a bar model for a fraction story and say what stays the same.
- I can find the area of a triangle and explain why different-looking triangles can have the same area.
- I can find the amount for one unit, compare "best buys", and work out a discount and GST without falling for the usual traps.
- I can find unknown angles step by step, giving a reason for each step.
- I can start small, make a table, find the rule of a growing pattern, and explain it from a picture.

---

## Level 6 (ages 11 to 13, builds on Primary 6 maths, with a little coding)

Your child will take a first look at ideas such as negative numbers, simple algebra, prime numbers and probability, and use short Scratch programs to test their thinking. The maths always comes first and the code is a way of checking it. In the last lesson each child plans, builds, tests and explains a small program of their own.

**By the end, your child will practise being able to say:**

- I can write the rule for a number sequence, predict the 100th number, and test it with a short program.
- I can decide whether a number is prime and write it as a product of primes.
- I can add and subtract with negative numbers using a number line.
- I can write a machine rule with a letter, run it backwards to solve it, and check by going forwards.
- I can count every possibility once, and decide if a game is fair by listing all the outcomes.
- I can plan, build, test and explain a small program, and say why the maths works.

---

## At the end of the ten lessons

Your child's tutor sends a short note about what your child did well, one thing to try next, and ideas to practise at home. It does not contain scores or rankings. At home you can ask: "Which way into the problem did you pick today, and why?"
