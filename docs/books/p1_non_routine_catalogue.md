# Primary 1 non-routine question catalogue (from school test papers)

Status: analysis written 23 Oct 2026 from the owner's Drive folder "P1 Math". **No school question text is reproduced here.** The verbatim questions are in a private, gitignored file for the owner's own study (`docs/books/private_p1_papers/non_routine_questions.md`). Every example below is an original question written for this project and checked by a script (section 5). Nothing here is a claim about what any child will meet in any exam.

## 1. What was read, and what was not

- 107 files were listed. **73 distinct PDFs were read in full** (OCR text; page images checked where the picture decided the answer). They hold **78 separate tests, quizzes and reviews from 17 schools, dated 2018 to 2025** (2018: most; 2019 to 2025: a few per year). The earlier pass (`docs/books/p1_school_paper_patterns.md`) had read 32 PDFs.
- 28 files named `PastYr_...` are copies of papers already read (6 compared page by page; all identical), so they were not read twice. Five of the six year zips were opened and hold only the loose files already read. **The 2023 zip (17.7 MB) and the 14 MB MCQ worksheet book exceed the Drive download limit.** The book's text was returned up to Worksheet 19 and is a re-formatting of questions found in the papers above, so it added no new question. If the 2023 zip holds papers that are not also loose in the folder, they are not covered.
- Coverage of schools is uneven (Henry Park, MGS and Maha Bodhi supply half the items); counts below describe this set of papers, not the whole country. Many papers print no marks for the question, and a few answer keys were unreadable, so marks are partial.

## 2. What counts as non-routine here

Beyond a one-step recall or computation: multi-step stories; a heuristic (draw a model, guess and check, work backwards, find a pattern, list every case, table, compare by difference or sum, make equal, how many ways); reasoning (true or false, explain, which does not belong); an unfamiliar context (symbols, hidden facts, timetables); or a build-it-from-clues item. A plain "What comes next?" shape, a one-step "how many more" from a picture graph and a simple story sum were left out unless they carry a trap.

## 3. The types: what they look like, where they appear, how to use them

### LOG: Logical reasoning (who is where, ordering clues, number clues, order of events)

- **Count:** 52 questions in 38 tests from 15 schools.
- **Where found:** MGS (9), Tao Nan (7), Henry Park (5), Maha Bodhi (5), River Valley (4), Ai Tong (4). Years: 2018 (28), 2019 (5), 2020 (1), 2021 (8), 2022 (6), 2023 (1), 2024 (2), 2025 (1).
- **Marks and difficulty:** 14 at 1 mark; 20 at 2 marks; 18 not printed. Difficulty 1/2/3: 4 / 43 / 5. Picture matters in 37 of 52.
- **What it is:** The child must hold several facts at once and fit them together: a line or race order from two or three clues, who is taller than whom, which number satisfies two conditions at once, which clock is earliest, or what comes between two events in a day.
- **What makes it non-routine:** Nothing is computed. The answer comes from placing things in order, so a child who rushes to add or subtract gets it wrong. Many items are only solvable from the picture (queues, clocks, heights on steps).
- **Typical number ranges:** Positions 1st to 10th; lines of 3 to 9 children; numbers to 100 (two conditions such as between 35 and 55); clocks at o'clock and half past.
- **Typical wording (short fragments):** "is between ... and ..."; "taller than ... but shorter than ..."; "greater than 35 but smaller than 55"; "is last in the race"; "Circle the 3rd child from the right".
- **Where it belongs for us:** Think harder from Lesson 11 (Who Is Where?) and again in Lessons 27 (Fill the Grid) and 35 (Spot It). Number-clue puzzles belong to Lessons 1 and 3. Keep one clue per sentence and a picture or table to place children in.

### CMP: Comparison (difference or sum, more/fewer than, reversal traps)

- **Count:** 38 questions in 32 tests from 14 schools.
- **Where found:** Henry Park (6), Maha Bodhi (6), MGS (4), Tao Nan (3), Ai Tong (3), Nanyang (3). Years: 2018 (18), 2019 (4), 2020 (2), 2021 (6), 2022 (4), 2024 (2), 2025 (2).
- **Marks and difficulty:** 3 at 1 mark; 18 at 2 marks; 3 at 3 marks; 1 at 4 marks; 13 not printed. Difficulty 1/2/3: 10 / 21 / 7. Picture matters in 24 of 38.
- **What it is:** Two quantities are compared by how many more or fewer, or a total and a difference are given together. The trap is the direction of the words: "8 fewer than Fred" means add to find Fred.
- **What makes it non-routine:** The sentence names the smaller number first but asks for the larger, or the question asks for the one with fewer. A one-step sum becomes a thinking item because the child must decide whether to add or take away before calculating.
- **Typical number ranges:** Within 20 in the 2018 mini tests; within 100 from 2021 (47, 65, 93); tens differences (20 fewer, 25 fewer) in Raffles 2018 and Henry Park; ruler lengths in centimetres.
- **Typical wording (short fragments):** "... fewer than ..."; "How many more ... than ...?"; "has 6 more ... than ..."; "how many fewer ... than ...?".
- **Where it belongs for us:** Think harder in Lessons 10 and 12 (already written), repeated at Lessons 22 (time gaps) and 38 (graphs). Go further: three-person chains and sum-and-difference (Lesson 12, 38).

Original examples (written for this project; answers checked by script):

| ID | Tier | Lessons | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EX-CMP-1 | Think harder | 10, 29 | Wei has 26 stickers. He has 9 fewer than Jenny. How many stickers does Jenny have? | 35 (the one with more is Jenny, so add: 26 + 9). |
| EX-CMP-2 | Think harder | 12, 38 | Ben and Mei have 20 cards altogether. Ben has 6 more cards than Mei. How many cards does each child have? | Mei 7 and Ben 13. |
| EX-CMP-3 | Go further | 12, 38, 39 | Ali has 12 cards. Siti has 5 more than Ali. Ravi has 3 fewer than Siti. How many cards do the three children have altogether? | 43 (Siti 17, Ravi 14; 12 + 17 + 14). |
| EX-CMP-4 | Go further | 22 | A show starts at 2:50 and ends at 3:25. How many minutes does it last? | 35 minutes (10 minutes to 3:00, then 25 more). |

### BACK: Work backwards (missing start or missing part)

- **Count:** 35 questions in 26 tests from 13 schools.
- **Where found:** MGS (6), Henry Park (6), Ai Tong (5), Maha Bodhi (4), Catholic High (3), Tao Nan (2). Years: 2018 (19), 2019 (3), 2020 (2), 2021 (5), 2022 (1), 2024 (2), 2025 (3).
- **Marks and difficulty:** 4 at 1 mark; 18 at 2 marks; 2 at 3 marks; 1 at 4 marks; 10 not printed. Difficulty 1/2/3: 4 / 28 / 3. Picture matters in 10 of 35.
- **What it is:** The end result is known and the start (or a missing part) must be found: "gave some away and has 15 left", "paid $100 and got $25 back", "played half an hour and stopped at 6:00", or a missing digit in a column sum.
- **What makes it non-routine:** The story verb says "gave away" but the calculation is an addition, or the unknown is in the middle of the story. Children who read left to right and take away get it wrong. Time versions need counting back on the clock.
- **Typical number ranges:** Within 20 in 2018 tests; within 50 and 100 from 2021 (money to $100, 54 sold and 36 left); times in half hours and hours.
- **Typical wording (short fragments):** "How many ... did she have at first?"; "after giving some"; "He had 7 ... left"; "How much did the bag cost?".
- **Where it belongs for us:** Thin in Lessons 1 to 20 (only the undo-the-jumps idea in Lesson 6 and the "at first" strips in Lessons 12 and 18). Assign as a Think harder item to Lessons 23, 24, 28, 37, 38 and 39. Suggest to the Term 1-2 owners a retrofit in Lessons 7 and 10 (not done here; those lessons are built and audited).

Original examples (written for this project; answers checked by script):

| ID | Tier | Lessons | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EX-BACK-1 | Think harder | 7, 10 | Ravi's lift goes up 5 floors and then down 3 floors. Now it is on floor 12. Which floor did the lift start on? | Floor 10 (12 + 3 - 5 = 10). |
| EX-BACK-2 | Think harder | 24, 39 | Lily bought a book and a pen. She paid with a $50 note and got $14 change. The pen cost $9. How much did the book cost? | $27 (50 - 14 = 36 spent; 36 - 9 = 27). |
| EX-BACK-3 | Go further | 23, 28 | A film lasts one hour and a half. It ends at 4:00 p.m. When did it start? | 2:30 p.m. (16:00 minus 90 minutes is 14:30). |

### CTX: Unfamiliar context, hidden fact, symbol equations

- **Count:** 26 questions in 22 tests from 10 schools.
- **Where found:** Henry Park (10), MGS (4), Red Swastika (3), Tao Nan (2), Maha Bodhi (2), Rosyth (1). Years: 2018 (5), 2019 (5), 2021 (7), 2022 (3), 2023 (1), 2024 (3), 2025 (2).
- **Marks and difficulty:** 4 at 1 mark; 9 at 2 marks; 13 not printed. Difficulty 1/2/3: 1 / 18 / 7. Picture matters in 17 of 26.
- **What it is:** The wording or setting is unfamiliar: a symbol or picture stands for a number, a fact the question never states (a tricycle has 3 wheels, a week has 7 days, 2 tens 13 ones), a timetable to read, or a figure to cut up.
- **What makes it non-routine:** The child must supply knowledge or decode a notation before any arithmetic. Symbol items (a circle plus a circle plus a circle is 12) are early algebra in picture form.
- **Typical number ranges:** Within 40 for wheels, legs and groups; times to 5 minutes; money to $50.
- **Typical wording (short fragments):** "The symbol represents a number"; "For every 5 ... she packs 2"; "Write the correct time with am or pm"; "represent a number each".
- **Where it belongs for us:** Think harder in Lessons 17, 19 (picture equations), 20, 21, 22 and 30. Teach the hidden facts (wheels, legs, week, dozen) in the warm-up so a child who does not know them is not blocked.

Original examples (written for this project; answers checked by script):

| ID | Tier | Lessons | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EX-CTX-1 | Go further | 19 | A star plus a star plus a star makes 15. A star plus a moon makes 9. A moon plus a sun makes 10. How much is each picture worth? | Star 5, moon 4, sun 6. |
| EX-CTX-2 | Think harder | 20, 30 | A bicycle has 2 wheels and a tricycle has 3 wheels. At the park there are 4 bicycles and 3 tricycles. How many wheels are there? | 17 (4 x 2 = 8 and 3 x 3 = 9). |
| EX-CTX-3 | Think harder | 30 | Aisha practises the piano every day for 2 weeks. How many days does she practise? | 14 (a week is 7 days, so 2 x 7). |
| EX-CTX-4 | Think harder | 21 | The short hand of a clock points exactly halfway between the 4 and the 5. The long hand points to the 6. Is it 4:30 or 5:30? | 4:30 (at half past, the hour hand has moved half way from the 4 to the 5). |
| EX-CTX-5 | Think harder | 22, 23 | The park shows start at 9:45 (seals), 10:15 (parrots), 11:00 (otters) and 11:30 (turtles). Mei arrives at 10:40. Which show is on? How many minutes must she wait for the next show to start? | The parrot show is on (it began at 10:15). She waits 20 minutes for the otters at 11:00. |

### LIST: Systematic list or choose numbers to fit

- **Count:** 24 questions in 19 tests from 9 schools.
- **Where found:** Henry Park (8), Maha Bodhi (6), Red Swastika (3), Raffles (2), Ai Tong (1), SCGS (1). Years: 2018 (7), 2019 (3), 2020 (3), 2021 (3), 2022 (2), 2023 (1), 2024 (3), 2025 (2).
- **Marks and difficulty:** 3 at 1 mark; 14 at 2 marks; 7 not printed. Difficulty 1/2/3: 0 / 20 / 4. Picture matters in 12 of 24.
- **What it is:** The child chooses numbers or digits to hit a target, or lists every possibility: three numbers that add to 17, the smallest 2-digit number from four digit cards, a true number sentence from three given numbers.
- **What makes it non-routine:** There is no single calculation. The child tries pairs or orders and checks, and must stop when all cases are found. Digit-card items add the rule that the tens digit cannot be 0.
- **Typical number ranges:** Sums to 20 (three numbers) and to 100 (two numbers); 2-digit numbers from 4 cards; equations from three given numbers up to 100.
- **Typical wording (short fragments):** "Use three of the four numbers"; "Form the greatest number between 10 and 50"; "using only 2 number cards"; "Use each number card once only".
- **Where it belongs for us:** Think harder in Lessons 3 (written), 5, 8, 25 and 26. Digit-card and three-number equation items appear in about ten papers but only Lesson 3 teaches them; add the fact-family version to Lesson 8 or 25 as a Think harder item.

Original examples (written for this project; answers checked by script):

| ID | Tier | Lessons | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EX-LIST-1 | Think harder | 3 | Here are four digit cards: 3, 6, 8 and 1. Use two cards to make a 2-digit number that is more than 40 and less than 70. List every number you can make. | 61, 63 and 68. |
| EX-LIST-2 | Think harder | 25, 26 | Choose three numbers from 3, 5, 8, 9 and 12 that add up to 20. Find all the ways. | 3 + 5 + 12 and 3 + 8 + 9 (two ways). |
| EX-LIST-3 | Think harder | 8 | Use the numbers 4, 9 and 13 to make four true number sentences, two with + and two with -. | 4 + 9 = 13, 9 + 4 = 13, 13 - 4 = 9, 13 - 9 = 4. |

### EXP: Explain, true or false, odd one out

- **Count:** 20 questions in 15 tests from 6 schools.
- **Where found:** Henry Park (12), Catholic High (2), Tao Nan (2), Maha Bodhi (2), ACS (1), Maris Stella (1). Years: 2018 (4), 2021 (3), 2022 (4), 2023 (2), 2024 (4), 2025 (3).
- **Marks and difficulty:** 10 at 1 mark; 4 at 2 marks; 6 not printed. Difficulty 1/2/3: 2 / 14 / 4. Picture matters in 11 of 20.
- **What it is:** The child judges a statement or picks the odd one out: a set of true/false statements about packing or totals, "who is correct?", "which does NOT describe the picture".
- **What makes it non-routine:** The child must test each statement, not produce a number. Several wrong options are close to right (3+7 for 3 sevens, 9x9x9x9 for 4 nines).
- **Typical number ranges:** Multiplication facts within 40 (2 to 10 in a group); place value of 2-digit numbers.
- **Typical wording (short fragments):** "either true or false based on"; "Who is correct?"; "does not describe the pictures"; "Circle Yes or No".
- **Where it belongs for us:** Think harder in Lessons 14, 15, 17 and 29, and Go further in 35. Most school items are true/false statements about equal groups; add that format to Lesson 15 and 17 homework and to the Lesson 29 check.

Original examples (written for this project; answers checked by script):

| ID | Tier | Lessons | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EX-EXP-1 | Think harder | 14, 29 | Ali says: "38 is bigger than 41, because 8 is bigger than 1." Is Ali right? Say why. | No. Compare the tens first: 3 tens is less than 4 tens, so 38 is smaller than 41. |
| EX-EXP-2 | Think harder | 15, 17 | There are 14 cookies. True or false? (a) They can be packed equally into boxes of 2. (b) They can be shared equally among 4 friends. (c) They can be packed equally into boxes of 7. | (a) True. (b) False (14 is 3 fours and 2 left over). (c) True. |
| EX-EXP-3 | Go further | 15 | Mei says: "3 groups of 5 is the same as 5 groups of 3." Is she right? Show how you know. | Yes. Both make 15. Draw 3 rows of 5 dots and turn the page: it becomes 5 rows of 3. |
| EX-EXP-4 | Think harder | 35 | Which number does not belong: 35, 25, 15, 45, 52? Say why. | 52: the others all end in 5. |

### GC: Guess and check (money combinations, pairs to a total)

- **Count:** 18 questions in 15 tests from 11 schools.
- **Where found:** SCGS (3), Maris Stella (3), Catholic High (2), MGS (2), Henry Park (2), Tao Nan (1). Years: 2018 (11), 2019 (1), 2021 (2), 2022 (2), 2024 (2).
- **Marks and difficulty:** 2 at 1 mark; 8 at 2 marks; 8 not printed. Difficulty 1/2/3: 0 / 13 / 5. Picture matters in 13 of 18.
- **What it is:** Pick coins or items that make an exact amount, or two items that together cost a given total: 85 cents with eight coins on show, two toys for $40, four coins to show $1.
- **What makes it non-routine:** There is no formula. The child tries, adds, adjusts. Several papers print a Guess 1, Guess 2, Guess 3 frame.
- **Typical number ranges:** Amounts to $60 and to 100 cents; coins 5¢ to 50¢; notes $1 to $50; 2 to 4 items.
- **Typical wording (short fragments):** "Which 2 items did he buy?"; "Hint: Guess and check"; "Circle the 2 items below"; "Circle the coins he must use".
- **Where it belongs for us:** Think harder in Lessons 24, 25 and 26 (all new). Always include one item with two answers and one with no answer to teach checking. Write amounts with the cent sign (50¢) per owner decision D2.

### PAT: Look for a pattern

- **Count:** 15 questions in 14 tests from 9 schools.
- **Where found:** MGS (4), Tao Nan (2), Red Swastika (2), ACS (2), Rosyth (1), River Valley (1). Years: 2018 (8), 2019 (5), 2021 (2).
- **Marks and difficulty:** 5 at 1 mark; 4 at 2 marks; 1 at 3 marks; 5 not printed. Difficulty 1/2/3: 8 / 5 / 2. Picture matters in 12 of 15.
- **What it is:** Continue or complete a shape pattern, a number pattern with a gap, a growing pattern (matchsticks, daily totals), or find the letter or number at a given position.
- **What makes it non-routine:** The easy items (next shape) are routine; the hard ones need the rule behind a growing pattern or a position far along (the 9th flag).
- **Typical number ranges:** Counting steps of 2 to 10 within 100; matchstick patterns 3, 5, 7; positions to 9.
- **Typical wording (short fragments):** "What comes next in the pattern?"; "Complete the number pattern"; "Circle the shape that comes next".
- **Where it belongs for us:** Think harder in Lessons 4 and 28; Go further for the far term (Lesson 28, 30). Keep next-shape items at Start here, never counted as non-routine.

### DIAG: Draw a model or diagram

- **Count:** 13 questions in 12 tests from 7 schools.
- **Where found:** Henry Park (5), Tao Nan (2), Maha Bodhi (2), Rosyth (1), Ai Tong (1), Nanyang (1). Years: 2018 (3), 2019 (1), 2021 (2), 2022 (3), 2023 (1), 2024 (1), 2025 (2).
- **Marks and difficulty:** 3 at 1 mark; 4 at 2 marks; 6 not printed. Difficulty 1/2/3: 2 / 11 / 0. Picture matters in 11 of 13.
- **What it is:** Draw to solve: circle equal groups, draw a bar for each person, split a figure into its shapes with a ruler, draw the missing items.
- **What makes it non-routine:** The picture is the working. Items where the child must decide what to draw (the missing marbles, the line that splits a figure) are harder than reading a given picture.
- **Typical number ranges:** Within 20 for groups; figures made of 2 to 4 shapes.
- **Typical wording (short fragments):** "Circle to show"; "Draw ... in each box"; "Using a ruler, draw lines to show"; "Draw to show".
- **Where it belongs for us:** Think harder in Lessons 9, 11, 12, 18 and 32; Go further for a ribbon cut into pieces (Lesson 31). Already well used.

### MULTI: Multi-step problems

- **Count:** 12 questions in 10 tests from 8 schools.
- **Where found:** MGS (2), Tao Nan (2), Henry Park (2), Raffles (2), River Valley (1), Maha Bodhi (1). Years: 2018 (7), 2021 (2), 2022 (1), 2024 (2).
- **Marks and difficulty:** 2 at 1 mark; 4 at 2 marks; 2 at 4 marks; 4 not printed. Difficulty 1/2/3: 1 / 9 / 2. Picture matters in 9 of 12.
- **What it is:** Two or more operations in one story: add two kinds then share, find the group first then the total, a quantity hidden inside the story.
- **What makes it non-routine:** The child must plan the order of steps. The first step is not asked for in the question.
- **Typical number ranges:** Within 40, two operations; shares into 2 to 8 groups.
- **Typical wording (short fragments):** "puts them equally into"; "How many ... altogether?"; "is shared equally with".
- **Where it belongs for us:** Think harder in Lessons 10, 17 and 20; Go further in Lesson 18. Keep the hidden first step explicit in the tutor script.

|---|---|---|---|---|

| EX-MULTI-2 | Think harder | 17 | Jamie has 24 stickers. He shares them equally among himself and his 3 cousins. Then he gives 2 of his own stickers to Mei. How many stickers does Jamie have now? | 4 (4 people share, so 6 each; 6 - 2). |

### TAB: Make or read a table

- **Count:** 7 questions in 7 tests from 5 schools.
- **Where found:** ACS (2), Henry Park (2), Rosyth (1), Nanyang (1), Raffles (1). Years: 2018 (2), 2019 (1), 2021 (2), 2022 (1), 2024 (1).
- **Marks and difficulty:** 2 at 2 marks; 1 at 3 marks; 4 not printed. Difficulty 1/2/3: 1 / 6 / 0. Picture matters in 7 of 7.
- **What it is:** Make or read a table: a ratio ("for every 5 sweets, 2 chocolates"), a daily-total table, a shapes-count table, a table of scores with three questions.
- **What makes it non-routine:** The table is the method. The child extends a pattern in two rows together.
- **Typical number ranges:** Ratios 5:2 and 3:2; counts within 20.
- **Typical wording (short fragments):** "Complete the table"; "For every 5 ... she packs 2"; "Study the table below".
- **Where it belongs for us:** Lesson 28 is the only home today. Add a Think harder table to Lessons 32 (count each shape) and 36 (outfits, pairs), and a ratio-table item to Lesson 20.

### EQ: Make equal (transfer)

- **Count:** 5 questions in 5 tests from 3 schools.
- **Where found:** Henry Park (3), MGS (1), Maris Stella (1). Years: 2018 (1), 2021 (1), 2022 (1), 2023 (1), 2024 (1).
- **Marks and difficulty:** 1 at 1 mark; 4 at 2 marks. Difficulty 1/2/3: 0 / 1 / 4. Picture matters in 4 of 5.
- **What it is:** Make two or more amounts the same by moving some from one to another, or by adding to the smaller.
- **What makes it non-routine:** The child must see that giving 1 changes the difference by 2, and that the answer is half the difference. Items mix "give" and "buy" ideas.
- **Typical number ranges:** Totals to 40; transfers of 2 to 4.
- **Typical wording (short fragments):** "so that both ... have the same"; "How many more ... must Ken give"; "has the same number of".
- **Where it belongs for us:** Think harder in Lessons 8 and 12; Go further in 13, 18 and 39. Already planned.

## 5. How the examples were made and checked

- All 49 examples are original: new contexts, names from our cast and new numbers. Each one is the same kind of question as a school item, not a rewording of one. Sentences are short and use the plain words of the Level 1 wording guide. Money uses the cent sign (50¢) and a $2 note and $5 note exist; there is no $20 note question.
- A script (private, gitignored: `docs/books/private_p1_papers/verify_catalogue_examples.py`; run with `python3 -I`) re-computes every answer by brute force: it tries all orders for the line-up and height clues, all digit pairs for the number clue, all coin counts for the money items, all pairs and triples for the list items, all routes and all foldings for the counting items, and it checks that the stated number of answers is exactly right (for example, two pairs for $17, and no solution for the puzzle in EX-CHAL-3). All 49 passed on 23 Oct 2026.
- Before an item goes into a lesson, the author must write the lesson's own `verify/` check, as for all Level 1 items; the script here does not replace it.
- Tier labels are neutral (Think harder, Go further). Nothing here implies a result, a rank or a promise for any child.
