# Primary 5 non-routine question catalogue (from school test papers)

Status: analysis written 23 Oct 2026 from the owner's Drive folder "P5 Math". **No school question text is reproduced here.** The verbatim questions are in a private, gitignored file for the owner's own study (`docs/books/private_p5_papers/non_routine_questions.md`). Every example below is an original question written for this project and checked by a script (`docs/books/private_p5_papers/verify_catalogue_examples.py`, run with `python3 -I`; it is in the private folder because it lives beside the paper records, and contains no paper text). Nothing here is a claim about what any school will set. Counts describe this set of papers, not the country.

## 1. What was read, and what was not

- The folder holds **173 distinct PDFs** (no sub-folders, no zip files). **All 173 were read** from the Drive text layer (OCR text of the scans). Seven were copies of a paper already read under another file name (past-year sets filed under two schools, and two worked-solution files that repeat a paper's last questions), so there are **166 separate paper labels** from **19 schools, 2020 to 2025**.
- Years (paper labels): 2025 (37), 2024 (28), 2023 (11), 2022 (33), 2021 (29), 2020 (22), undated (6). 2023 is thin: only 11 labels, one label from each of 11 schools.
- Kinds: **75 end-of-year papers** (EOY, or "SA2" in 2020 to 2022 file names), 1 mid-year paper, **90 weighted assessments and term tests** (20 to 36 marks, 25 to 60 minutes).
- Schools (labels): Nan Hua 18, Tao Nan 15, Nanyang 13, ACS (Junior) 13, Maha Bodhi 12, Red Swastika 12, Ai Tong 11, CHIJ St Nicholas 11, Raffles Girls' 10, Rosyth 10, Henry Park 10, Catholic High 8, SCGS 6, Rulang 5, St Hilda's 3, Maris Stella 3, MGS 3, ACS (Primary) 2, Paya Lebar Methodist Girls' 1.
- **Not readable: none.** No file failed to open and no file was over the 10 MB download limit. **Pictures were not seen.** All 173 files were read as OCR text; none was rendered to page images, so figures, graphs, fractions typeset as stacked numerals and some handwritten answers are lost or garbled. Items whose answer depended on a lost figure carry "(verify)" or "?" in the private file, and the picture-dependent share below is a floor, not a count.
- Answers come from the printed answer keys where legible (most schools' keys were attached) and otherwise from my own working; both need a check before any reuse.

## 2. What counts as non-routine here

Beyond a one-step computation or a routine exercise on a taught method: multi-step stories, a heuristic (bar model with units, supposition, work backwards, pattern, list every case, table, make equal), reasoning (true, false or not possible to tell), an unfamiliar context (charges in steps, packs, a tank with a block in it), or build-from-clues (draw it). Routine questions (a bare computation, naming a shape, reading one bar) were mostly not recorded, but the Primary 5 papers hold so many multi-step items that **the record is a sample of the hard half**, not a count of every question.

**2279 non-routine questions were recorded.** The 16 type codes of Primary 4 are kept (EQ, GEO, LOG, CTX, LIST, MULTI, BACK, CHAL, EXP, TAB, GC, PAT, DIAG, CMP, WAYS, POSE). Two were widened: **WAYS** at Primary 5 is mostly an optimisation ("most", "least", "buy 3 get 1 free"), and **CMP** carries the average items.

Notes on the table.

- Difficulty is my own 1 to 3 judgement for a ten or eleven year old (1 one extra idea, 2 a trap or two steps, 3 hard for the age). **1821 of 2279 are difficulty 3** (80 percent): the typical recorded item is a stretch for the age, because the easier items were not recorded.
- Marks: **2 marks 1099, 4 marks 462, 3 marks 427, 5 marks 180**. 2-mark items are Paper 1 Booklet B and Paper 2 Questions 1 to 5 (one answer, with working shown); 3, 4 and 5 marks are Paper 2 Questions 6 to 17 with parts (a) and (b), where (b) uses (a). Weighted assessments are 2 to 4 marks per item.
- Items by paper year: 2025: 387; 2024: 372; 2023: 253; 2022: 394; 2021: 454; 2020: 354; undated: 65.
- **Two syllabus eras sit in the folder.** The 2020 to 2024 papers follow the older syllabus: ratio (174 items), average (87), speed (one item recorded), GST at 7 percent. The 2025 papers follow the new one: no ratio notation (the eight "ratio" hits in 2025 are "times as many" or "for every" wording), no average, no speed; they add true/false/not possible to tell more often, views and isometric drawing, GST at 9 percent and annual interest. Level 5 keeps ratio, average and speed as labelled previews, so the older papers are still useful evidence for lessons 17 and 24.
- **Paper shape.** 2020 to 2024 end-of-year papers: Paper 1 is 45 marks in 1 hour with no calculator (Booklet A: ten 1-mark and five 2-mark multiple-choice items; Booklet B: five 1-mark and ten 2-mark short answers); Paper 2 is 55 marks in 1 hour 30 minutes with a calculator (Questions 1 to 5 at 2 marks, Questions 6 to 17 with 2 to 5 marks each). **2025 changed to 50 + 50**: Paper 1 Section A 26 marks and Section B 24; Paper 2 five 2-mark items then ten longer questions for 40 marks.
- **965 of 2279 items (42 percent) were flagged as needing a picture**; the true share is higher because OCR dropped the figure silently. Digital and printed pages must carry the same kinds of drawing: angle diagrams, overlapping shapes on a grid, line and bar graphs, views of cubes, tanks with dimensions.
- There is no named challenge section. The last question of Paper 2 (4 or 5 marks, parts a and b) is the paper's hardest, and **the papers state no heuristic hint**: the child chooses the model.

## 3. The types, with original examples

Tier names: **Think harder** (the T tier) and **Go further** (the G tier). Never Start here. Each example has a scripted answer. Two examples are labelled P6 previews (ratio, average) because the 2021 syllabus puts them at Primary 6 and the papers before 2025 set them at Primary 5.

### EQ: Unit and equal-share models (492 questions)

The core Primary 5 problem sum: a total with "times as many", "more than" or "the same in the end", with whole numbers, money, mass and fractions of one quantity equal to fractions of another. In the 2020 to 2024 papers many are written with a ratio (3 : 5); the 2025 papers say "times as many" and "for every". Part (a) is often the unit, and part (b) uses it.

Frequent contexts in the papers: fractions (of remainder, whole from a part) (101), equal after a transfer or change (89), ratio (83), money and prices (59), average (25).

## 4. Number ranges and forms seen

- **Whole numbers:** totals to about 5 digits in bar-model stories (most 3-digit or 4-digit); 6- and 7-digit numbers appear in place-value and rounding items; multipliers 2 to 6 ("3 times as many"); differences of 10 to 500.
- **Fractions:** denominators 2 to 12, most often 3, 4, 5, 8; fraction of the remainder, fraction of a fraction, mixed numbers in measurement stories (ribbon, tank, paint), and "express your answer in the simplest form".
- **Decimals:** 1 and 2 decimal places (2 places in three of four decimal items); money to the cent; litres, kg, m with conversions to ml, g and cm; rounding "correct to the nearest tenth".
- **Percentages:** 5 to 90 percent, most often 10, 20, 25, 30, 40, 60, 75; GST 7 percent (2020 to 2024) and 9 percent (2025); discount 8 to 40 percent; annual interest 1 to 3 percent; one quantity as a percentage of another with a rounding instruction.
- **Money:** the middle item is about $16; one in ten is over $450; charges in steps use $0.20 to $3.70 per step, steps of 30 minutes, 400 m or 500 m, 50 g.
- **Geometry:** angles in whole degrees (55, 64, 68, 76, 106, 149); triangle areas with bases 4 to 40 cm; composite figures on grids of identical squares; cuboids with edges 3 to 60 cm; tank water depths of 2 to 30 cm.
- **Time:** 12-hour with a.m. and p.m. (some papers write "2.45 p.m." with a point), 24-hour clock written 2150; durations in hours and minutes; parking and taxi fares rounded up in whole steps.
- **Patterns:** repeating blocks of 4 to 8; 100th and 117th terms; linear figures (3n + 1); sums of the first 45 or 127 terms.

## 6. How the examples were made and checked

Each example was written for this project from the pattern of a question type, not from a paper question. `verify_catalogue_examples.py` recomputes every answer by arithmetic or brute force (all 49 pass). Authors write their own items of the same kind and their own `verify/` check; they never copy a school or contest question.
