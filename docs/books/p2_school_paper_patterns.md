Written 23 Oct 2026 for the Level 2 plan (`docs/terms/level_2_year_plan.md`). It is the Primary 2 twin of `docs/books/p1_school_paper_patterns.md`. **No school question text is reproduced.** Quoted phrases are 8 words or fewer. The verbatim questions are in the private, gitignored `docs/books/private_p2_papers/non_routine_questions.md`.

## 1. Headline findings that change the Level 2 plan

1. **Money is decimal notation, not "dollars only".** Papers write $3.05, $12.40, $0.55, $142.50 and also 55¢, 140¢. The Rosyth skills grid lists "read and write decimal notation", "convert dollars and cents to cents and vice versa" and "word problems in dollars only (or cents only)". The plan's T1.5 (a bill like $3.50, 350 cents) is right; it needs comparing prices to the cent ($14.05 vs $14.50), the $1 = 100¢ complement ("65¢ from $1"), unit-rate items ("4 for $3"), and notes up to $100 in a story.
2. **Time to the minute and durations are school work, not P3.** From 2022 the papers test 1 h 45 min = 105 min, 3:07 pm to 4:39 pm, 5:35 pm to 7:00 pm, 8:45 am to 11:23 am, a flight of 6 h 15 min, and ask for a drawn timeline ("Hint: Draw a timeline"). Finding the start from the end and a duration (7 pm end, 1 hour) is in nine papers from 2018. The plan labels "find the start time" P3 (MOE p. 36) and leaves clocks to T2.5; the school papers need all of it by the middle of the year.
3. **Picture graph keys are 2, 3, 4, 5 and 10; tally charts appear.** The plan's "never above 5" rule (T2.1) does not match the papers: a key of 10 is in at least eight papers (Red Swastika, ACS Primary, Rosyth, ACS Junior, CHIJ St Nicholas). Tally charts feed a picture graph in Maha Bodhi and Raffles. "Twice as many" and "3 times as many" are read from a graph (ACS Primary 2020).
4. **Fractions go to twelfths and are tested heavily in Terms 3 and 4.** Like fractions only: name, order, "make 1 whole", add and subtract within 1, "how many more parts must be shaded so that 5/8 is shaded" (about 12 papers, 2019 to 2025), "circle three fractions that add up to 1 whole" (4 papers). The plan teaches halves, thirds, quarters, sixths in T1.6 and like fractions on a line in T3.2; denominators 7 to 12 and the "more parts to shade" and "three that make a whole" items are not planned.
5. **"Hint" phrases name the heuristic at P2.** "(Hint: Make a Table)", "(Hint: Working backwards)", "(Hint: Draw a timeline)", "You may use models to help you", "You may draw pictures to help you" appear in the stems. At P1 there were none. A Level 2 lesson that names its tool in the question matches the paper style.
6. **The sharer is counted, again.** "Shares 24 stickers equally with her 3 cousins" means 4 people; "shared with her two friends" means 3. About 14 items (Pei Chun, Maha Bodhi, Tao Nan, SCGS, Raffles, CHIJ St Nicholas, Henry Park) rely on it. The plan covers it at T1.3 Think harder; it also needs a Start here exposure.
7. **Left-over situations appear without the word "remainder".** "What is the most number of friends she can give to?" (20 cookies, 3 each), "She has 1 sweet left; how many children?", "How many more must she buy?". The plan says "left over" only; that matches.
8. **Pointing to the P2 tables: only 2, 3, 4, 5 and 10.** No paper tests 6 to 9 as a table, so T4.2 (and lesson 32) stay a beyond-school preview; "n groups of m" statements (true or false, "which does not describe the pictures") are everywhere (Henry Park every year).
9. **Volume uses cups, glasses and bottles as a unit before litres; ml appears from 2020 (Tao Nan).** The plan mentions litres only.
10. **The reversal trap is the commonest comparison item**: "She has 49 more cards than Jane" with Jane to find (about 30 items). The plan places it at T1.9 Think harder; the papers put it at 3-mark Section C.

## 2. How the papers are built

| Feature | What the papers do |
|---|---|
| Length and marks | Topical quiz 30 marks, 30 to 55 minutes; semestral-style papers 40 to 50 marks, 1 h to 1 h 30; revision papers up to 100 marks (Red Swastika). |
| Sections | A multiple choice (options numbered (1) to (4), answer in brackets), 4 to 10 questions at 1 or 2 marks; B short answer (boxes or blanks), 1 or 2 marks, one or two mental-sum pages in Tao Nan; C problem sums, 3 or 4 marks, usually (a) and (b) with (b) built on (a). |
| Section instructions | "Choose the correct answer and write its number"; "Fill in the correct answers in the spaces provided"; "Show all your equations and workings clearly"; "You may use models to help you". |
| Answer frames | A full sentence with the noun after the blank ("She baked __ cupcakes altogether"); a number-sentence box "[ ] x [ ] = [ ]" in Rosyth, Nan Hua and Tao Nan; a "Working" column. |
| Model frames | "Fill in the blanks in the models" (Raffles); half-completed bars; "Look at the model below". |
| Skill grids | Rosyth, Pei Chun, Red Swastika, St Nicholas, MGS print the skill per question for parents; Rosyth adds "I have checked my work" ticks. |
| Marks by type | 1 mark: read a scale, name a shape, order, simple riddle. 2 marks: a two-step item, a model, a conversion, a timeline. 3 to 4 marks: story sums with (a) and (b). |
| Time of year | Term 1: numbers to 1000, +/-. Term 2: x and ÷ (tables 2, 3, 4, 5, 10), length (and graphs in some schools). Term 3: money, mass, volume, shapes. Term 4: fractions, time, graphs, volume, a general review. School order varies. |
| Dates | 2018 and 2019 use "a.m." with full stops and times as 9.30; 2022 to 2025 write "am" and "pm" and 9:30. |

## 3. Per topic

Quoted phrases are 8 words or fewer.

### 3.1 Numbers to 1000

- **Wording:** "In 580, which digit is in the ones place?"; "Write 598 in words"; "10 less than 209 is"; "Which odd number is greater than 300?"; "Arrange the numbers. Begin with the smallest"; "Complete the number pattern"; "Form the smallest 3-digit even number"; "Each card can be used once only"; "I am thinking of a 3-digit number" (clue riddles); "How many odd numbers are there between"; "3 hundreds and 11 tens is" (regrouped forms).
- **Number ranges:** 100 to 1000 (1000 itself in a few items); steps of 2, 3, 5, 10 and 100 in patterns; digit cards of three or four cards; clues use "even", "odd", "twice the", "3 more than", "between".
- **Pictures:** base-ten pictures (flats, sticks, units), place value charts, number-bond diagrams with 3-digit numbers, number lines.
- **Marks:** 1 mark for one-step place value; 2 marks for a card or clue puzzle; word form answers as one mark.
- **Level 2 plan:** T1.1 (number line, words, digit sums) matches. The clue and digit-card riddles (about 40 in the set) are not a planned item; add them to T1.1 Think harder.

### 3.2 Addition and subtraction within 1000 (with renaming)

- **Wording:** "Add 308 to 296"; "Subtract 68 from 908"; "Find the difference between 906 and 78"; "The sum of 649 and 123 is"; "Find the missing digit"; "Use the numbers to form a subtraction equation"; two-step stories "After selling 327 cookies, 173 were left"; "She has 49 more cards than Jane".
- **Ranges:** 3-digit with 3-digit, renaming across tens and hundreds; 900 - 235, 1000 - 437; story numbers 3-digit throughout.
- **Pictures:** vertical sums with boxes for missing digits; part-whole or comparison bars with a question mark; place value charts with counters (Raffles).
- **Marks:** bare sums 1 mark; word problems 2 marks; Section C 3 marks (two steps) or 4 marks (two parts of 2).
- **Level 2 plan:** T2.3 (jumps and columns) is for understanding; the papers also test the plain column and the missing digit. Fit is good if T2.3 includes a "missing digit" item and a "which equation matches the story" tick item.

### 3.3 Multiplication (tables 2, 3, 4, 5, 10)

- **Wording:** "5 groups of 4 is the same as"; "3 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 ="; "Write a multiplication equation for the picture"; "Which does not describe the pictures?"; "Tick the statements that describe the picture"; "5 for $1" (rate); "Multiply 8 by 5".
- **Ranges:** products to 50, 10 x 10 = 100; "7 sevens", "10 fours", 9 x 10; missing factor ("x 5 = 45"); "n times as many" (Catholic High, Pei Chun, ACS).
- **Pictures:** rows of dots or objects in boxes, bags and baskets, arrays; "Circle to show groups of 4".
- **Marks:** 1 or 2 marks; Section C items usually 2 or 4 marks with a division second part.
- **Level 2 plan:** T2.6 Table Tricks is the only multiplication lesson before T4.2; T1.3 is division. The Term 2 school unit (multiplication as groups and repeated addition, true or false statements, "which does not describe") has no lesson. See section 5.

### 3.4 Division (sharing and grouping)

- **Wording:** "shared equally among 5 friends"; "packs 6 in a bag"; "How many bags does she need?"; "Write a division equation for the picture"; "Divide 18 apples into 6 equal groups"; "What is the most number of friends she can give to?".
- **Ranges:** dividends to 50 (40 ÷ 5, 45 ÷ 5), divisors 2, 3, 4, 5, 10; "20 ÷ 2"; related facts (a multiplication and a division from one picture).
- **Pictures:** circled groups, baskets, plates, groups of dots; "Circle the balls to show the number in each box".
- **Marks:** 1 or 2 marks; the "sharer counted" and "left over" items 2 marks.
- **Level 2 plan:** T1.3 matches (sharing and grouping, "left over", "share with 2 friends is 3 people"). Add true-or-false equal-groups statements (Think harder) and the "most number of friends" item.

### 3.5 Fractions

- **Wording:** "What fraction of the figure is shaded?"; "and [ ] make 1 whole"; "Arrange the fractions. Begin with the smallest"; "Circle the greater fraction"; "How many more parts must be shaded?"; "Circle three fractions that add up to 1 whole"; "Which does not show 1/2 shaded?".
- **Ranges:** denominators 2 to 12 (6 and 10, 8, 12 most; 7, 9, 11 in "make 1 whole" sets); like fractions only; unit fractions compared (1/9 < 1/6 < 1/4).
- **Pictures:** equal parts in rectangles, circles, rows of triangles, fraction bars, pizzas and cakes cut into 6, 8, 10 or 12.
- **Marks:** 1 or 2 marks; the two-step "pizza left" story 2 marks.
- **Level 2 plan:** T1.6 and T3.2 and T4.3 cover this; denominators 7 to 12 and "parts to shade" are missing (section 5).

### 3.6 Money with $ and cents

- **Wording:** "How much money is shown?"; "Convert $26.30 into cents"; "325¢ is the same as"; "gave the cashier $50"; "How much change did he receive?"; "Which two items can she buy?"; "5 for $2. I can buy __"; "Arrange the amounts from greatest to smallest".
- **Ranges:** totals shown up to $252.50 (notes and coins); prices to $142.50 in ordering items; story prices usually whole dollars up to $100 and whole cents to 95¢; mixed notation in one item ("85¢" and "$0.20", "60¢ and $__ make $1").
- **Pictures:** specimen notes ($2, $5, $10, $50, $100) and coins (5¢, 10¢, 20¢, 50¢, $1) in a box; price tags on pictured items; a menu or price list with prices to the cent.
- **Marks:** 1 mark to count; 2 marks for a two-step purchase; 3 to 4 marks as Section C.
- **Level 2 plan:** T1.5 is the only money lesson (plus T3.7 and T3.8 contexts). Fit is good for counting and change; add comparison of 3 prices to the cent, "$1 = 100¢" complements, unit rate, "which two items for exactly".

### 3.7 Time and duration

- **Wording:** "What is the time shown on the clock?"; "Write the time using a.m. or p.m."; "How long did the movie last?"; "Fill in the blank with am or pm"; "1 h 53 min = __ min"; "Draw the minute hand to show 9:25"; "(Hint: Fill in the timeline below)".
- **Ranges:** to the minute on clocks (3:24, 12:10); 5-minute marks in Term 4 items; durations 30 min, 1 h, 1 h 25 min, 2 h 15 min, 6 h 15 min; conversions to 215 min.
- **Pictures:** analogue faces (two clocks side by side), timelines with the hour marks, TV guides, calendar page.
- **Marks:** 1 mark (read), 2 marks (duration with working), 2 marks (two-stage).
- **Level 2 plan:** T2.5 Clock Detectives covers the clock to 5 minutes and durations, with start-from-end at Go further. Papers need the minute, conversions, timeline, start-from-end and two-stage at Think harder or earlier.

### 3.8 Length, mass and volume

- **Wording:** "Use your ruler to measure the line"; "Draw a line 8 cm long"; "Fill in the blank with cm or m"; "The pencil is __ cm shorter"; "What is the mass of the apple?"; "heavier than / lighter than / as heavy as"; "How many litres of water can the pail hold?"; "Which two containers can fill the pail?".
- **Ranges:** length to 1 m in cm and metres to 1000 m in problems ("233 m, 487 m"); mass 5 g to 1000 g and 1 to 10 kg on dials and balances; volume 1 to 100 l and ml (100 ml, 150 ml, 700 ml in 2020+ Tao Nan).
- **Pictures:** a ruler under an object (not always starting at 0), kitchen scales (0 to 1000 g dial, 0 to 5 kg and 0 to 10 kg), balances with identical objects, glasses and jugs with a cup-count unit, litre pictures with a 1 l jar.
- **Marks:** 1 mark to read; 2 marks for a compare or two-step; Section C 3 to 4 marks.
- **Level 2 plan:** T1.8 (read the scale) and T3.4 (estimate) match; T1.8 also needs balance pictures with identical objects ("2 durians = 6 kg") and the cups unit.

### 3.9 Shapes, solids and patterns

- **Wording:** "Name the shapes that make up the figure"; "How many flat surfaces does it have?"; "I am a solid. What am I?"; "Copy the figure onto the dot grid"; "One of the shapes is wrong"; "Which shape is in the 12th position?".
- **Ranges:** circle, semicircle, quarter circle, square, rectangle, triangle; cube, cuboid, cone, cylinder, sphere; counts to 9 rectangles or 8 triangles in a figure.
- **Pictures:** figures made of 2 to 4 shapes, solids drawn in perspective, dot grids and square grids, a row of shapes with one wrong.
- **Marks:** 1 to 2 marks.
- **Level 2 plan:** T1.7 (shape splitters, counting triangles), T3.5 (solids) and T3.6 (tiles) cover the topics; "flat and curved surfaces", "spot the wrong shape", "which shape is in the 12th position" and the copy-on-a-grid skill need one line each.

### 3.10 Picture graphs, tally and charts

- **Wording:** "Each [icon] stands for 5 books"; "How many more children like football than tennis?"; "stands for 2"; "Complete the graph by drawing"; "A total of __ children"; "twice as many".
- **Ranges:** 4 to 6 rows; keys 2, 3, 4, 5, 10; total of 20 to 60; half icons are not used.
- **Pictures:** rows with labels at the left, title above, key below; tally chart with four-and-a-bar groups.
- **Marks:** 1 mark per blank in a 5 to 7 part block; 2 marks for complete-the-graph or give-so-equal.
- **Level 2 plan:** T2.1 covers reading and scales; add tally, key 10, and the "from a total" item. There is no bar graph in any P2 paper (a bar-like chart with a metre scale appears once).

## 6. Recommended changes to the Level 2 plan (for the owner to approve)

1. **T1.5 Shop Bills and Change:** write money both as $3.05 and 305¢; add "convert in both directions", "arrange three prices to the cent", "$1 = 100¢ complements", unit rate ("4 for $3"), and notes up to $100 in a story (Go further).
2. **T2.5 Clock Detectives:** teach the clock to the minute, hours and minutes to minutes and back, a drawn timeline, and "find the start from the end and the length". Re-level "find the start" from P3: it is school work for 30 min and 1 h at least.
3. **T2.1 Graphs That Talk:** allow a key of 10; add tally to graph; "twice as many"; "give __ so that they have the same number"; complete from a total. Drop "never above 5" or restrict it to Start here.
4. **T1.6, T3.2:** denominators to 12; add "how many more parts must be shaded so that 5/8 is shaded", "which does not show 1/2" and "circle three fractions that add up to 1 whole".
5. **T1.3 or T2.6:** add true/false and "which does not describe the picture" statements for equal groups; add the "most number of friends" item.
6. **T1.8 Read the Scale:** add the balance with identical objects (2 durians = 6 kg), cups as a unit for volume, and ml (a small Go further).
7. **T1.1:** add the clue and digit-card riddle ("I am a 3-digit number...") at Think harder.
8. **T3.6, T2.7:** add "one shape in the pattern is wrong" and "which shape is in the 12th place".
9. **T1.9, T3.7, T3.8:** name the three traps (reversal, at first, hidden people) and print the heuristic hint in the stem ("Hint: Draw a timeline") on at least one item.
10. **Wording changes on child pages** (section 7).

## 7. Paper-based wording changes for child pages

- **Section instructions:** "Choose the correct answer and write its number in the brackets"; options labelled (1) to (4), not A to D.
- **Frames:** a full sentence with the noun after the blank; "[ ] x [ ] = [ ]" boxes; "Working" column; "Show all your equations and workings clearly".
- **Hints in the stem:** "(Hint: Draw a timeline.)", "(Hint: Make a table.)", "(Hint: Working backwards.)"; "You may use models to help you".
- **Comparison words:** "more than", "fewer than", "heavier than", "lighter than", "as heavy as", "greater", "smaller" (never "bigger" for numbers).
- **Time:** "am" and "pm" without full stops, times as 9:30 with a colon; accept "a.m." and a full stop when marking.
- **Money:** "$3.05" and "55¢" both; "gave the cashier"; "How much change did he receive?".
- **Equal groups:** "n groups of m", "m + m + m", "n x m", "n ms".
- **Shapes:** "flat surface", "curved surface", "semicircle", "quarter circle".
- **Graphs:** "Each [icon] stands for 5 books"; keys 2, 3, 4, 5, 10.
