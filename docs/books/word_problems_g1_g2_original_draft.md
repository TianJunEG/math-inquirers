# Original practice problems for Levels 1 and 2 from the two 101 word-problem workbooks (draft)

**Draft, not yet in the program.** Written for the gaps and high-frequency types found in `word_problems_g1_g2_analysis.md`. Every problem is new: a new context, new numbers and wording written from the underlying structure, not from any problem in the two commercial workbooks (the workbooks are copyrighted and none of their problems, numbers or sentences is used). Every answer was computed by a short script, and every "find all" and "guess and check" answer was confirmed by checking all the possibilities. The problems have not been tried with children or reviewed by a tutor, so check reading load and difficulty before use. Tier labels are suggestions: S = Start here, T = Think harder, G = Go further.

Level 1 rules kept: whole numbers within 100; multiplication only with groups of 2, 3, 4, 5 and 10 and products within 40; division only within 20, written as sharing or grouping; no remainders; money in whole dollars or cents. Level 2 rules kept: numbers within 1000; multiplication and division only from the 2, 3, 4, 5 and 10 tables; no decimals (money in whole dollars or cents); fractions with denominators up to 12; leftovers said in words, never written as remainders. Names and contexts are Singapore-flavoured and change from the books on purpose.

Suggested homes name lessons in `docs/terms/level_1_year_plan.md` ("Lesson 17") and `level_2_year_plan.md` ("T3.7") as they stood when this was written; the Level 2 plan was being revised at the same time, so check the codes. Nothing here changes a lesson or a plan.

# Part A. Level 1 (ages 6 to 8), from the Grade 1 workbook's types

## A1. Join three groups (add by making tens)

Book type and share: 5 of 101 in Book 1, and a part of most others. Suggested home: Lesson 13 (make-a-ten adding), holiday L2 bonds.

**A1.1** [T] A school library puts 27 picture books, 13 comic books and 24 story books on a trolley. How many books are on the trolley?
Answer: 64 books. Solution: 27 + 13 = 40 (make a ten), then 40 + 24 = 64.

**A1.2** [T] On Saturday Ravi sold 38 tickets in the morning, 15 at noon and 22 in the evening. How many tickets did he sell?
Answer: 75 tickets. Solution: 38 + 22 = 60, then 60 + 15 = 75.

**A1.3** [T] Mei picked up 19 shells on Monday, 26 on Tuesday and 11 on Wednesday. How many shells did she pick up altogether?
Answer: 56 shells. Solution: 19 + 11 = 30, then 30 + 26 = 56.

## A2. Compare (more or fewer than), then total

Book type and share: Book 1 Q2, 6, 12, 13, 46, 55, 67, 68. Suggested home: Lesson 17 Bar strips, holiday L5.

**A2.1** [S] Siti has 36 beads. Dev has 19 more beads than Siti. How many beads do they have altogether?
Answer: 91 beads. Solution: Dev has 36 + 19 = 55; together 36 + 55 = 91.

**A2.2** [S] A red kite has 48 stars on it. A blue kite has 15 fewer stars than the red kite. How many stars are on the two kites altogether?
Answer: 81 stars. Solution: Blue has 48 - 15 = 33; together 48 + 33 = 81.

**A2.3** [T] Hui sold 22 cups of tea. She sold 18 fewer cups than Raj. How many cups did the two of them sell altogether?
Answer: 62 cups. Solution: Hui sold fewer, so Raj sold more: 22 + 18 = 40; together 22 + 40 = 62.

## A3. Compare chain of three, or a chain with a total

Book type and share: Book 1 Q8, 48, 65, 66. Suggested home: Lesson 17 Think harder and Go further, Lesson 35.

**A3.1** [T] A pencil costs 35 cents. A ruler costs 20 cents more than the pencil. A glue stick costs 15 cents less than the ruler. How much does the glue stick cost?
Answer: 40 cents. Solution: Ruler 35 + 20 = 55; glue stick 55 - 15 = 40.

**A3.2** [T] Ben has 14 cards. Mei has 9 more cards than Ben. Raj has 6 fewer cards than Mei. How many cards do the three children have altogether?
Answer: 54 cards. Solution: Mei 23, Raj 17; 14 + 23 + 17 = 54.

**A3.3** [G] Three friends have 90 stamps altogether. Siti has 31 stamps. Ravi has 14 more stamps than Siti. How many stamps does Ben have?
Answer: 14 stamps. Solution: Ravi has 31 + 14 = 45; Ben has 90 - 31 - 45 = 14.

## A4. Part-whole: take several parts from a total (the commonest type)

Book type and share: 15 of 101 in Book 1. Suggested home: Lesson 14 Take away, count up; Lesson 17.

**A4.1** [S] A pet shop has 60 animals. 24 are fish, 17 are birds and the rest are rabbits. How many rabbits are there?
Answer: 19 rabbits. Solution: 24 + 17 = 41, then 60 - 41 = 19.

**A4.2** [S] A bus has 45 seats. Girls sit on 18 seats and boys sit on 13 seats. The other seats are empty. How many seats are empty?
Answer: 14 seats. Solution: 18 + 13 = 31, then 45 - 31 = 14.

**A4.3** [T] Mei wants 80 beads for a necklace. She has 26 red beads and 30 blue beads. How many more beads does she need?
Answer: 24 beads. Solution: 26 + 30 = 56, then 80 - 56 = 24.

## A5. Group totals, then compare

Book type and share: 8 of 101 in Book 1. Suggested home: Lesson 17 Think harder.

**A5.1** [T] Box A holds 15 red pens and 21 blue pens. Box B holds 12 red pens and 14 blue pens. How many more pens are in Box A than in Box B?
Answer: 10 more pens. Solution: Box A 36, Box B 26; 36 - 26 = 10.

**A5.2** [T] Class 1A has 18 boys and 17 girls. Class 1B has 16 boys and 15 girls. How many fewer children are in Class 1B than in Class 1A?
Answer: 4 fewer children. Solution: 1A has 35, 1B has 31; 35 - 31 = 4.

**A5.3** [T] A zoo has 56 birds. 20 of them are parrots and the rest are ducks. How many more ducks than parrots are there?
Answer: 16 more ducks. Solution: Ducks: 56 - 20 = 36; 36 - 20 = 16.

## A6. Change story with a hidden start

Book type and share: 5 of 101 in Book 1. Suggested home: Lesson 4 Mystery Number, holiday L5.

**A6.1** [S] Dev had some stamps. He gave 14 to his sister and 9 to his brother. He has 25 stamps left. How many stamps did he have at first?
Answer: 48 stamps. Solution: Put back what he gave away: 14 + 9 = 23, then 23 + 25 = 48.

**A6.2** [T] Mei had some money. She earned $18 and then spent $7. Now she has $41. How much money did she have at first?
Answer: $30. Solution: Undo the last step first: 41 + 7 = 48, then undo the earning: 48 - 18 = 30. Check: 30 + 18 - 7 = 41.

**A6.3** [T] Ravi had some game tokens. He lost 11 tokens in one game and won 6 tokens in the next. Now he has 40 tokens. How many tokens did he have at first?
Answer: 45 tokens. Solution: Undo the win: 40 - 6 = 34; undo the loss: 34 + 11 = 45. Check: 45 - 11 + 6 = 40.

## A7. Abstract number puzzles in words

Book type and share: 4 of 101 in Book 1. Suggested home: Lesson 4, Lesson 15.

**A7.1** [S] The total of two numbers is 71. One of the numbers is 38. What is the other number?
Answer: 33. Solution: 71 - 38 = 33.

**A7.2** [S] A number is 17 less than 60. What is the number?
Answer: 43. Solution: 60 - 17 = 43.

**A7.3** [T] Add 15 to a number. The answer is 9 more than 50. What is the number?
Answer: 44. Solution: 9 more than 50 is 59; 59 - 15 = 44. Check: 44 + 15 = 59.

## A8. Equal groups, then add or take away

Book type and share: 14 of 101 in Book 1. Suggested home: Lesson 19, Lesson 22.

**A8.1** [S] There are 4 plates with 5 dumplings on each plate. Mum cooks 9 more dumplings. How many dumplings are there now?
Answer: 29 dumplings. Solution: 4 x 5 = 20, then 20 + 9 = 29.

**A8.2** [S] A shop has 3 boxes of 10 crayons. It sells 14 crayons. How many crayons are left?
Answer: 16 crayons. Solution: 3 x 10 = 30, then 30 - 14 = 16.

**A8.3** [T] Mei has 2 bags with 8 shells in each bag and 3 bags with 5 shells in each bag. How many shells does she have?
Answer: 31 shells. Solution: 2 x 8 = 16 and 3 x 5 = 15; 16 + 15 = 31.

## A9. Share or group, then one more step (and who counts)

Book type and share: 6 of 101 in Book 1. Suggested home: Lesson 21, Lesson 22.

**A9.1** [T] Ben has 20 biscuits. He shares them equally among himself and his 3 cousins. How many biscuits does each person get?
Answer: 5 biscuits. Solution: Ben and 3 cousins make 4 people; 20 / 4 = 5.

**A9.2** [T] A box has 12 red cups and 6 blue cups. All the cups are put equally into 3 stacks. How many cups are in each stack?
Answer: 6 cups. Solution: 12 + 6 = 18, then 18 / 3 = 6.

**A9.3** [T] 20 pencils are put into boxes of 5 pencils each. One box is sold. How many pencils are left?
Answer: 15 pencils. Solution: 20 / 5 = 4 boxes; 3 boxes are left, 3 x 5 = 15. (Or 20 - 5 = 15.)

## A10. Make two amounts equal by a transfer

Book type and share: 2 of 101 in Book 1. Suggested home: Lesson 17 Go further.

**A10.1** [T] Ali has 30 cards and Mei has 18 cards. How many cards must Ali give Mei so that they have the same number?
Answer: 6 cards. Solution: Ali has 30 - 18 = 12 more; half of 12 is 6. Check: 30 - 6 = 24 and 18 + 6 = 24.

**A10.2** [T] One tank has 25 fish and another tank has 11 fish. How many fish must be moved from the first tank to the second so that both tanks have the same number?
Answer: 7 fish. Solution: The gap is 25 - 11 = 14; move half of it, 7. Check: 25 - 7 = 18 and 11 + 7 = 18.

**A10.3** [G] Two bags have 24 balls altogether. Bag A has 4 more balls than Bag B. How many balls are in each bag?
Answer: Bag A 14, Bag B 10. Solution: Take away the extra 4: 24 - 4 = 20; share 20 / 2 = 10 for Bag B; Bag A has 10 + 4 = 14.

## A11. Money change and notes

Book type and share: 23 of 101 in Book 1 are money stories. Suggested home: Lesson 25, Lesson 26, holiday L6.

**A11.1** [G] A kite costs $29. Ravi pays with a $50 note. The cashier gives him one $5 note and some $2 notes as change. How many $2 notes does he get?
Answer: 8 notes. Solution: Change is 50 - 29 = 21; take out the $5 note: 21 - 5 = 16; 16 / 2 = 8.

**A11.2** [S] Mei has $40. She buys a book for $14 and a pen for $6. How much money does she have left?
Answer: $20. Solution: 14 + 6 = 20, then 40 - 20 = $20.

**A11.3** [T] Siti pays 100 cents for a toy that costs 65 cents. She gets one 20-cent coin and some 5-cent coins as change. How many 5-cent coins does she get?
Answer: 3 coins. Solution: Change is 100 - 65 = 35; minus the 20-cent coin leaves 15; 15 / 5 = 3.

## A12. Price or size of one from a group

Book type and share: 6 of 101 in Book 1. Suggested home: Lessons 19 to 22, Lesson 26.

**A12.1** [T] 2 pens cost $8. How much do 5 pens cost?
Answer: $20. Solution: 1 pen costs 8 / 2 = $4; 5 pens cost 5 x 4 = $20.

**A12.2** [T] 3 ride tickets cost $15. How much do 2 ride tickets cost?
Answer: $10. Solution: 1 ticket costs 15 / 3 = $5; 2 tickets cost $10.

**A12.3** [T] 2 mugs cost $14 and 5 spoons cost $10. Ali buys 1 mug and 1 spoon. How much does he pay?
Answer: $9. Solution: 1 mug costs 14 / 2 = $7; 1 spoon costs 10 / 5 = $2; together $9.

# Part B. Level 2 (ages 7 to 9), from the Grade 2 workbook's types

## B1. Change story, one hidden amount (3-digit)

Book type and share: 13 of 101 in Book 2. Suggested home: T1.5, T1.2, T2.3.

**B1.1** [T] A hall had some chairs. 128 chairs were taken out for a concert and then 75 chairs were brought in. Then there were 340 chairs. How many chairs were there at first?
Answer: 393 chairs. Solution: Undo the last step first: 340 - 75 = 265, then 265 + 128 = 393. Check: 393 - 128 + 75 = 340.

**B1.2** [T] A farm shop had 520 eggs. In the morning it sold some eggs. In the afternoon it sold 135 more eggs. Then 164 eggs were left. How many eggs did it sell in the morning?
Answer: 221 eggs. Solution: 520 - 164 = 356 eggs were sold in all; 356 - 135 = 221 in the morning.

**B1.3** [T] A train had 246 passengers. At the first stop 58 passengers got on. At the second stop some passengers got off and nobody got on. Then there were 197 passengers. How many passengers got off at the second stop?
Answer: 107 passengers. Solution: 246 + 58 = 304; 304 - 197 = 107.

## B2. Compare chain, with a total or a remainder

Book type and share: 12 of 101 in Book 2. Suggested home: T3.7, holiday L5.

**B2.1** [T] Mei read 85 pages. Ravi read 130 more pages than Mei. Siti read 45 fewer pages than Ravi. How many pages did the three children read altogether?
Answer: 470 pages. Solution: Ravi 215, Siti 170; 85 + 215 + 170 = 470.

**B2.2** [T] A stall had 240 red pens. It had 75 more blue pens than red pens. 190 pens were sold. How many pens were left?
Answer: 365 pens. Solution: Blue: 240 + 75 = 315; all pens 555; 555 - 190 = 365.

**B2.3** [G] Ali, Mei and Siti have 400 cards altogether. Ali has 120 cards. Mei has 45 more cards than Ali. How many cards does Siti have?
Answer: 115 cards. Solution: Mei has 120 + 45 = 165; Siti has 400 - 120 - 165 = 115.

## B3. Sum and difference; 'times as many' with a total

Book type and share: 5 of 101 in Book 2. Suggested home: T3.7 Go further, holiday L5.

**B3.1** [G] Two water tanks hold 90 litres altogether. The big tank holds 14 litres more than the small tank. How many litres does the small tank hold?
Answer: 38 litres (the big tank holds 52 litres). Solution: Take away the extra 14: 90 - 14 = 76; share 76 / 2 = 38. Check: 38 + 52 = 90.

**B3.2** [G] Raj has 3 times as many stamps as Dev. Together they have 84 stamps. How many stamps does Raj have?
Answer: 63 stamps. Solution: Dev is 1 part and Raj is 3 parts, 4 parts in all: 84 / 4 = 21 for Dev; Raj has 3 x 21 = 63.

**B3.3** [G] Ana, Ben and Cal have 160 shells altogether. Ben has twice as many shells as Ana. Cal has 25 shells. How many shells does Ben have?
Answer: 90 shells. Solution: Ana and Ben together have 160 - 25 = 135, which is 3 equal parts: 135 / 3 = 45 for Ana; Ben has 2 x 45 = 90.

## B4. Make equal by a transfer

Book type and share: 3 of 101 in Book 2. Suggested home: T3.7 Go further, holiday L5.

**B4.1** [T] Tank A holds 250 litres and Tank B holds 190 litres. How many litres must be poured from A into B so that both tanks hold the same?
Answer: 30 litres. Solution: The gap is 60; pour half of it, 30. Check: 250 - 30 = 220 and 190 + 30 = 220.

**B4.2** [G] Two baskets have 96 mangoes altogether. Mei moves 12 mangoes from basket 1 to basket 2 and now both baskets have the same number. How many mangoes were in basket 1 at first?
Answer: 60 mangoes. Solution: Each basket now has 96 / 2 = 48; basket 1 gave away 12, so it had 48 + 12 = 60.

**B4.3** [G] Ali and Ben have 185 badges altogether. Ali gives 25 badges to Ben. Now Ben has 15 more badges than Ali. How many badges did Ali have at first?
Answer: 110 badges. Solution: Now they have 185 in all with a difference of 15: 185 - 15 = 170, 170 / 2 = 85 for Ali, so Ben has 100. Ali gave away 25, so Ali had 85 + 25 = 110. Check: Ali 110, Ben 75; after the gift 85 and 100.

## B5. Share equally, then another step

Book type and share: 12 of 101 in Book 2. Suggested home: T1.3, holiday L3 and L5.

**B5.1** [T] 36 stamps are divided equally among Dina and her 2 friends. Dina then mounts her stamps on 3 album pages, with the same number on every page. How many stamps are on one page?
Answer: 4 stamps. Solution: Dina and 2 friends make 3 people: 36 / 3 = 12 each; Dina's 12 stamps on 3 pages is 12 / 3 = 4.

**B5.2** [S] A baker makes 250 rolls. He sells 130 rolls and packs the rest equally into 4 boxes. How many rolls are in each box?
Answer: 30 rolls. Solution: 250 - 130 = 120; 120 / 4 = 30.

**B5.3** [T] A teacher has 6 packs of 10 pencils. She keeps 12 pencils and shares the rest equally among 4 groups. How many pencils does each group get?
Answer: 12 pencils. Solution: 6 x 10 = 60; 60 - 12 = 48; 48 / 4 = 12.

## B6. Equal groups, then change

Book type and share: 12 of 101 in Book 2. Suggested home: T1.3, T1.5, T3.7.

**B6.1** [S] A school buys 5 boxes with 10 pencils in each box. 18 pencils are used, and then 25 more pencils are bought. How many pencils does the school have now?
Answer: 57 pencils. Solution: 5 x 10 = 50; 50 - 18 = 32; 32 + 25 = 57.

**B6.2** [T] Ana put 4 shells into each of 9 jars. She had 7 shells left over. How many shells did she have at first?
Answer: 43 shells. Solution: 9 x 4 = 36 shells in the jars; 36 + 7 = 43.

**B6.3** [T] Mrs Lim has 3 trays with 10 buns on each tray and 4 trays with 5 buns on each tray. She sells 26 buns. How many buns are left?
Answer: 24 buns. Solution: 3 x 10 = 30 and 4 x 5 = 20, so 50 buns; 50 - 26 = 24.

## B7. Unit price or unit amount, then many

Book type and share: 8 of 101 in Book 2. Suggested home: T2.6, T3.7 (money), T1.6.

**B7.1** [T] 4 notebooks cost $20. How much do 7 notebooks cost?
Answer: $35. Solution: 1 notebook costs 20 / 4 = $5; 7 notebooks cost 7 x 5 = $35.

**B7.2** [T] 3 kg of rice costs $12. How much does 5 kg of rice cost?
Answer: $20. Solution: 1 kg costs 12 / 3 = $4; 5 kg cost 5 x 4 = $20.

**B7.3** [G] 3 muffins cost $12 and 2 pies cost $14. Mei buys 5 muffins and 3 pies. How much does she pay?
Answer: $41. Solution: 1 muffin $4, so 5 muffins $20; 1 pie $7, so 3 pies $21; $20 + $21 = $41.

## B8. Change in notes

Book type and share: 5 of 101 in Book 2. Suggested home: T1.6 Market Day.

**B8.1** [T] A pencil case costs $27 and a ruler costs $13. Raj pays with a $50 note and gets his change in $2 notes only. How many $2 notes does he get?
Answer: 5 notes. Solution: 27 + 13 = 40; change 50 - 40 = 10; 10 / 2 = 5.

**B8.2** [G] A shirt costs $63. Mei pays with a $100 note. She gets one $10 note, one $5 note and some $2 notes as change. How many $2 notes does she get?
Answer: 11 notes. Solution: Change 100 - 63 = 37; take out $10 and $5: 37 - 15 = 22; 22 / 2 = 11.

**B8.3** [T] Ravi buys 4 books at $9 each. He pays with a $50 note and gets his change in $2 notes only. How many $2 notes does he get?
Answer: 7 notes. Solution: 4 x 9 = 36; change 50 - 36 = 14; 14 / 2 = 7.

## B9. Exchange: 'as much as' equivalences

Book type and share: 3 of 101 in Book 2. Suggested home: T3.3 Shape Equations.

**B9.1** [T] A melon costs as much as 3 pears. 2 melons cost $24. How much does 1 pear cost?
Answer: $4. Solution: 1 melon costs 24 / 2 = $12; 3 pears cost $12, so 1 pear costs 12 / 3 = $4.

**B9.2** [T] 2 scarves cost as much as 3 hats. A hat costs $6. How much does a scarf cost?
Answer: $9. Solution: 3 hats cost 3 x 6 = $18, which is the price of 2 scarves; 18 / 2 = $9.

**B9.3** [G] A bag of flour costs twice as much as a bag of rice. 1 bag of rice and 2 bags of flour cost $25 altogether. How much does a bag of flour cost?
Answer: $10. Solution: 2 bags of flour cost as much as 4 bags of rice, so the shopping is 5 bags of rice: 25 / 5 = $5 for rice; flour costs 2 x 5 = $10.

## B10. Ages: the gap never changes

Book type and share: 2 of 101 in Book 2 (the book keeps them simple). Suggested home: T3.7.

**B10.1** [T] Ali is 9 years old. His aunt is 34 years old. How old will his aunt be when Ali is 21?
Answer: 46 years old. Solution: The gap stays 34 - 9 = 25 years; 21 + 25 = 46.

**B10.2** [G] Siti is 9 years old. Her father is 4 times as old as she is. How old was her father when Siti was born?
Answer: 27 years old. Solution: Father is 4 x 9 = 36 now; he was 36 - 9 = 27 when Siti was born (her age was 0).

**B10.3** [G] Ravi and his sister are 23 years old altogether. Ravi is 5 years older than his sister. How old will each of them be in 4 years' time?
Answer: Ravi 18, his sister 13. Solution: Take away the extra 5: 18, share: 9 for his sister, so Ravi is 14 now. In 4 years: Ravi 14 + 4 = 18, sister 9 + 4 = 13. Check: 18 + 13 = 31.

## B11. Fractions of a whole from pieces

Book type and share: 3 of 101 in Book 2. Suggested home: T1.7, T3.2.

**B11.1** [S] A pizza was cut into equal slices. Mei ate 3 slices, Ben ate 2 slices and 5 slices were left. What fraction of the pizza did the two children eat?
Answer: 5/10 (5 out of 10 slices). Solution: There are 3 + 2 + 5 = 10 slices; the children ate 3 + 2 = 5 of them, so 5/10.

**B11.2** [S] Ana used 3/8 of a ribbon for a card and 2/8 of the ribbon for a bow. What fraction of the ribbon is left?
Answer: 3/8. Solution: 3/8 + 2/8 = 5/8 is used; 8/8 - 5/8 = 3/8.

**B11.3** [T] A cake was cut into 12 equal pieces. Siti ate 3 pieces and Ben ate 4 pieces. What fraction of the cake was left?
Answer: 5/12. Solution: 3 + 4 = 7 pieces eaten; 12 - 7 = 5 pieces left, so 5/12.

## B12. Three to five steps in one problem

Book type and share: about 60 of 101 in Book 2 need 3 or more operations. Suggested home: T3.7, T4.9.

**B12.1** [G] A stall had 350 oranges and 280 apples. It sold 95 oranges and 120 apples. The rest of the fruit was packed equally into 5 crates. How many pieces of fruit are in each crate?
Answer: 83 pieces. Solution: Oranges left 350 - 95 = 255; apples left 280 - 120 = 160; fruit left 415; 415 / 5 = 83.

**B12.2** [G] Tom saved $48. Ana saved $15 more than Tom. Raj saved $20 less than Ana. Together they spent $75 on a gift. How much money did they have left?
Answer: $79. Solution: Ana $63, Raj $43; all three saved 48+63+43 = $154; $154 - $75 = $79.

**B12.3** [T] A hall has 4 rows of 10 chairs and 3 rows of 5 chairs. 18 chairs are taken to another room. Then 27 people sit down, each on a different chair. How many chairs are empty?
Answer: 10 chairs. Solution: 4 x 10 = 40 and 3 x 5 = 15, so 55 chairs; 55 - 18 = 37; 37 - 27 = 10.

## B13. Guess and check with two kinds

Book type and share: 1 of 101 in Book 2, a named heuristic. Suggested home: T1.3 Go further, T2.9.

**B13.1** [T] A shed has bicycles and tricycles. There are 9 of them and they have 24 wheels altogether. How many bicycles and how many tricycles are there?
Answer: 3 bicycles and 6 tricycles. Solution: Guess and check with a table: 9 bicycles would have 18 wheels; every swap of a bicycle for a tricycle adds 1 wheel, so 24 - 18 = 6 swaps. 6 tricycles and 3 bicycles: 3 x 2 + 6 x 3 = 24.

**B13.2** [G] A kiosk sells pens at $2 each and notebooks at $5 each. Mei buys 8 items and pays $25. How many pens and how many notebooks does she buy?
Answer: 5 pens and 3 notebooks. Solution: 8 pens would cost $16, which is $9 too little; each swap of a pen for a notebook adds $3, so 9 / 3 = 3 notebooks. Check: 3 x 5 + 5 x 2 = 25.

**B13.3** [G] Ben has 10 coins. Each coin is either a 5-cent coin or a 10-cent coin. They add up to 70 cents. How many coins of each kind does he have?
Answer: 6 five-cent coins and 4 ten-cent coins. Solution: 10 five-cent coins would make 50 cents, 20 too little; each swap adds 5 cents, so 4 swaps: 4 ten-cent coins and 6 five-cent coins. Check: 30 + 40 = 70.

## B14. Corners, queues and lamp posts (count the ends and yourself)

Book type and share: 2 to 3 of 101 in Book 2. Suggested home: holiday L9 ribbon work; no lesson.

**B14.1** [T] Paper lanterns are hung round a square stage, one on each corner, with 6 lanterns on every side counting the two corner ones. How many lanterns are there altogether?
Answer: 20 lanterns. Solution: 4 sides x 6 = 24, but each of the 4 corner lanterns is counted twice: 24 - 4 = 20. (Or 4 corners + 4 sides x 4 lanterns between = 20.)

**B14.2** [T] Siti is standing in a queue. There are 5 children in front of her and 4 children behind her. Each child in the queue has $3. How much money do the children in the queue have altogether?
Answer: $30. Solution: Count Siti too: 5 + 1 + 4 = 10 children; 10 x 3 = $30.

**B14.3** [T] A straight path is 60 m long. There is a lamp post at each end and one every 10 m in between. How many lamp posts are there?
Answer: 7 lamp posts. Solution: 60 / 10 = 6 gaps, and one more post than gaps: 6 + 1 = 7.

## B15. Growing amounts until a total

Book type and share: 1 of 101 in Book 2. Suggested home: T1.4, T3.1.

**B15.1** [T] A runner runs 200 m on day 1, 250 m on day 2, and each day 50 m more than the day before. After how many days has she run exactly 750 m altogether?
Answer: 3 days. Solution: Day 1: 200 (total 200); day 2: 250 (total 450); day 3: 300 (total 750).

**B15.2** [T] Ben reads 12 pages on day 1, 16 pages on day 2, and each day 4 pages more than the day before. His book has exactly 100 pages. On which day does he finish the book?
Answer: day 5. Solution: Pages read: 12, 16, 20, 24, 28; running totals 12, 28, 48, 72, 100. He finishes on day 5.

**B15.3** [G] Siti saves $5 in week 1, $10 in week 2, $15 in week 3 and so on, always $5 more than the week before. After how many weeks will she have saved $75 altogether?
Answer: 5 weeks. Solution: Running totals: 5, 15, 30, 50, 75. It is 75 after week 5.

# Notes for the checker

- Part A has 36 problems in 12 groups and Part B has 45 problems in 15 groups, 3 per group. All 81 answers are computed in the generating script, which was run before this file was written.

- Reading traps are used on purpose and each is marked in its solution: "with his 2 friends" means 3 people share (A9.1, B5.1); "fewer than Raj" gives the other child's number (A2.3); a queue or a row includes the child you are told about (B14.2); corners are counted once (B14.1).

- Items that stretch a level on purpose and are tagged G: three-step stories with a total (A3.3), two numbers from a total and a difference (A10.3, B3.1), "times as many" with a total (B3.2, B3.3), transfers with a total (B4.2, B4.3), change that must be split into two kinds of note or coin (A11.1, B8.2), exchange chains (B9.3), ages (B10.2, B10.3) and the longer stories in B12.

- Nothing here needs a table above 5 times 10, a number above 1000, or a fraction with a denominator above 12.
