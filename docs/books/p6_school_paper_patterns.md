# Primary 6 school papers: wording, ranges and fit against the Level 6 term plan

Written 23 Oct 2026 for the Level 6 plan (`docs/terms/level_6_year_plan.md`). It is the Primary 6 twin of `docs/books/p5_school_paper_patterns.md`. **No school question text is reproduced.** Quoted phrases are 8 words or fewer. The verbatim questions are in the private, gitignored `docs/books/private_p6_papers/non_routine_questions.md`.

## 0. What was studied, and limits

- Source: the owner's Drive folder "P6 Math" (138 PDFs: 127 in the folder and 11 in its sub-folder "2026 Prelims Math"; no zip files, none over the 10 MB download limit). **123 were read in full** and 2778 non-routine questions recorded from them. The other 15 PDFs (June 2020 1; SA1 2020 2; SA2 2020 12) were downloaded but the OCR could not be finished and read in the time available. One more PDF (July 2020 Red Swastika) holds the same questions as the CA1 2020 Red Swastika paper and was not recorded twice.
- Years read (paper labels): 2020 5, 2021 18, 2022 15, 2023 27, 2024 20, 2025 26, 2026 11. Kinds: BA1 1, CA1 3, Prelim 85, SA1 4, SA2 2, WA1 12, WA2 14, WA3 1. Schools: 22 schools (Raffles Girls', ACS (Junior), Catholic High, Methodist Girls', Nan Hua, Nanyang, Red Swastika, Henry Park, CHIJ St Nicholas, Tao Nan, Ai Tong, Rosyth, SCGS and others).
- **Every file is a scan with no text layer.** The Drive text view returned only a watermark, so each PDF was downloaded and read as OCR text (page images at about 105 dpi, RapidOCR on a CPU). The OCR drops figures and stacked fractions and garbles handwriting. A question whose figure or fraction was lost is recorded as a short summary marked "(lost)" or "(verify)". **Pictures were not seen**; picture conventions below come from the wording, the labels and the answer keys.
- The 2026 papers (11 files, the new syllabus) are read in the same way. For a count by year, 2020 is thin (5 papers read); trends before 2021 are not drawn.
- Marks are those printed on each question. School mark schemes were not seen. Answer keys were attached to a few papers and are partly legible.
- A paper's inclusion here says nothing about any child's result or about any exam. Nothing here is a promise.

## 1. Headline findings that change the Level 6 plan

1. **Speed is a 2022 to 2025 topic and is gone from the 2026 papers.** km/h and m/min items appear every year from 2022 to 2025 (12 to 19 recorded items a year: two cars meeting or passing, average speed of a journey, 2.4 km at 80 m/min). **None was recorded in the 2026 papers.** That matches the plan (speed is not in the Oct 2025 primary syllabus, lesson 14 keeps it as a labelled preview). What stays in 2026 is **rate in tanks and taps** (litres or cm per minute, "time for the heights to be equal"), 5 items in 2026. Lesson 14 should lead with taps and tanks; speed stays the labelled preview.
2. **Reasoning and justification words are much more common in 2026.** Six items in the 11 papers of 2026 against none to two a year before (the format also appears in Pei Hwa 2021, St Nicholas 2022 and 2025): "circle the words: is isosceles because ...", "parallel because the angles add to 180", "trapezium because FE is not parallel to GH", "give one possible pair". The plan's lesson 17 says angles between parallel lines are not in primary; **the papers set angles between parallel lines in every year from 2021** (explicit "AD is parallel to BC, find x" items in ACS, Henry Park and Nanyang 2021 and in Raffles and SCGS 2022; the word parallel, counting parallelogram and trapezium, is in 66 of the 2778 recorded items), and the 2026 papers ask the child to say why. Lesson 17 needs co-interior and alternate angles at Think harder, with the reason in words.
3. **Average is set in nearly every preliminary paper (80 of 87) and in every year, and early.** About 170 recorded items (average of a table, "average after one value is added or removed", "average with an ink blot", "greatest possible difference"). The plan teaches average from scratch in lesson 31 (Term 4). The papers are sat in August and September, so a child who has met average only in Term 4 meets it late for school. Move one average Think harder item into Term 1 (lessons 4 or 8) and keep lesson 31 as the full treatment.
4. **Ratio stays after the syllabus change.** Ratio appears in 33 recorded 2026 items (2022 38, 2023 60, 2024 48, 2025 58). Typical forms: ratio before and after a change ("5 : 4, then 56 women joined, now 3 : 4"), two boxes with different ratios and a "times as many" link, three-part ratio from a pie chart, ratio of a ratio. The plan's lesson 11 covers ratio and sharing; add the two-box link and the "after a change" form at Think harder.
5. **Tank pouring and two taps are the single most repeated context.** Pour from one tank to another until the heights match, or two taps at different rates fill tanks with different bases (cm per minute, litres per minute). Tanks and taps are in every year. They sit between lesson 14 (rates) and 16 (volume). Make them a named Think harder item in both.
6. **True, false or not possible to tell is a fixed table** with three statements and a tick column; about 60 recorded items, every year, with ratio, average, bar graph and angle statements. Not in the plan; add as a 5-minute item in lessons 11, 13, 17, 31 and 36.
7. **Draw-on-a-grid tasks are about 4 of every 100 recorded items** (110 CHAL items): a rhombus from two sides, a trapezium with one side half another, a triangle with a given area ratio and an obtuse angle, a rectangle with the same area as a given figure, a parallelogram with the same perimeter. The plan does not ask a child to draw to a specification outside lesson 18. Add a draw item to lessons 7, 9 and 20.
8. **Pie charts and bar graphs are set together** (about 80 pie-chart items, every year, 17 in 2026). The child matches a pie chart to a bar graph, finds a count from a sector, or completes a missing bar from a percentage. Lesson 31 covers pie charts; add "which bar graph matches" and the sector-in-degrees form.
9. **Compass directions and turns of 45, 90, 135 and 225 degrees** appear from 2021 to 2025 (about 60 items), **none recorded in 2026**. Not in the plan. Keep it as a 6-minute warm-up in lesson 24 and treat as optional.
10. **Pi is 3.14 more often than 22/7.** In the recorded items "Take pi as 3.14" is about 2.5 times as common as 22/7 (40 against 16 recorded items), and "in terms of pi" occurs. Semicircles and quarter circles on a grid of equal squares dominate. Lesson 15 should use 3.14 first, 22/7 with diameters 7, 14, 21, 28 second.
11. **GST is 9 percent** in the 2023 to 2025 papers (2023 3, 2024 3, 2025 4 recorded items; 7 percent in 2021 and earlier; none recorded in 2026). Discount is in every year (9 in 2023, 9 in 2024, 10 in 2025, 4 in 2026).
12. **The weighted assessments show the school order: ratio, fractions and percent in Term 1; circles, GST and algebra in Term 2.** The March papers (WA1, Term 1) set fractions (fraction of the remainder, packing into as many bags as possible), ratio (ratio of ratios, a ratio with a change, ratio to a fraction of a perimeter), percentage (discount, "of a number", a percent of a total) and symmetry; the May papers (WA2, Term 2) set circles and semicircles (circumference, area, perimeter), GST, interest, percent decrease, algebra in one letter, angles in rhombus and trapezium. The Level 6 plan teaches ratio at lesson 11 and percent at lesson 13, both in Term 2 (April to June), so a child meets them at school before the lesson. That is acceptable for an "alongside school" program, but the owner may want a Term 1 ratio item (see section 6, item 12).
13. **There is no probability, no negative numbers, no coordinates, no inequalities, no rotation or tessellation, no binary and no scatter plot in any paper read.** Lessons 18 (rotation, tessellation), 23, 24 (coordinates), 28, 32 and 33 are beyond the school papers; they stay as enrichment, labelled as such.

## 2. How the papers are built

| Feature | What the papers do |
|---|---|
| Length and marks (2020 to 2025) | Paper 1 (no calculator, 1 h): Booklet A 20 marks (Q1 to Q10 at 1 mark, Q11 to Q15 at 2 marks, multiple choice) and Booklet B 25 marks (Q16 to Q20 at 1 mark, Q21 to Q30 at 2 marks). Paper 2 (calculator allowed, 1 h 30): 55 marks. Total 100. |
| Length and marks (2026, new syllabus) | Booklet A 26 marks (Q1 to Q10 at 1 mark, Q11 to Q18 at 2 marks); Booklet B 24 marks (Q19 to Q30 at 2 marks); Paper 2 50 marks. Total 100. Fewer short 1-mark short answers, more 2-mark items. |
| Paper 2 | Q1 to Q5 at 2 marks (10 marks); Q6 to Q17 with parts (a), (b), sometimes (c), 3 to 5 marks each ("[4]" at the end of the question). Q16 and Q17 are the hardest, 5 marks. |
| Section instructions | "Questions 16 to 20 carry 1 mark each"; "Show your working clearly"; "give your answers in the units stated"; "Answers in fractions or ratio must be expressed in the simplest form"; "Figures are not drawn to scale". |
| Answer frames | A blank with the unit after it ("Ans: _____ m"); "(a) ___ (b) ___" for parts; a line "Ans: ___ triangular faces and ___ rectangular faces"; MCQ options numbered (1) to (4). |
| Hints in the stem | None name a heuristic. A few name the tool ("Draw a rectangle with the same area", "Express your answer in terms of n", "Leave your answer in hours and minutes", "Give your answer to 2 decimal places"). |
| Picture-based items | About half of the recorded items need a figure: angle diagrams, composite shapes on a grid of equal squares, nets, block views, pie charts, bar graphs and line graphs. |
| Typical Paper 2 sequence | 1 to 5: number puzzle, equal change, algebra, drawing on a grid, a graph; 6 to 10: ratio or percent two-part; 11 to 14: rate and volume, graphs, angles; 15 to 17: composite area, then ratio or fraction chains with three stages. |
| Time of year | The Prelim is August to September. Weighted assessments are 20 to 36 marks, 30 to 60 minutes: WA1 in March (Term 1: fractions, ratio, percent, symmetry), WA2 in May (Term 2: circles, GST, interest, algebra, angles), WA3 in Term 3. SA1 (Nanyang 2025) is a 50-mark paper in Term 2 with ratio, percent, circles, volume and views. |
| Dates | The papers use "a.m." and "p.m." with full stops and times written 7.45 p.m.; 24-hour times written 1805. |

## 3. Per topic

Quoted phrases are 8 words or fewer.

### 3.1 Whole numbers, decimals and order of operations

- **Wording:** "Find the value of"; "Arrange in decreasing order"; "Express your answer correct to 1 decimal place"; "Give your answer as a mixed number"; "Use all the digits 3, 4, 5, 8"; "greatest multiple of 5"; "smallest odd number between"; "closest to 8000".
- **Number ranges:** decimals to 3 places in sums (3.707 + 1.373); 2 places in money; products such as 2.6 x 40 and 9.03 - 0.38; four-digit digit-card puzzles; 3-digit riddles with a sum (375) and a smallest number.
- **Pictures:** none for the arithmetic; number lines for ordering.
- **Marks:** 1 mark for bare computation (Booklet B Q16 to Q20), 2 marks for digit-card puzzles in Paper 2 Q1.
- **Level 6 plan:** lessons 1 to 4 fit. The bracket item "7 x (54 - 24 / 6) + 4" is a Start here item for lesson 4. Compensation and pairing sums are not tested in school; they stay contest-style.

### 3.2 Fractions

- **Wording:** "What fraction of the figure is shaded?"; "How many sixths are there in 2?"; "Express as a mixed number in the simplest form"; "sold 1/3 of the remaining apples"; "What fraction of her money did she spend?"; "What fraction of the markers are blue?".
- **Number ranges:** denominators 2 to 12 (mostly 3, 4, 5, 6, 8); fraction of the remainder in 2 to 3 stages; mixed-number lengths of ribbon ("at most how many pieces").
- **Pictures:** shaded figures made of identical squares or triangles; bar models are not given.
- **Marks:** 1 or 2 marks in Paper 1; Paper 2 chains are 4 or 5 marks ("fraction of money on a ruler", pen cost, money at first).
- **Level 6 plan:** lessons 2, 8 and 12 fit. Work backwards from the end with three stages is in the papers from 2022 (and in 2026). Telescoping sums and continued fractions are not tested in school (they are SASMO), so lesson 12 keeps them at Think harder only as the contest item.

### 3.3 Ratio

- **Wording:** "What was the ratio of the number of apples to oranges?"; "The ratio of A to B was 3 : 5"; "became 3 : 4"; "Express your answer in its simplest form"; "4 times as many fruits in Box B".
- **Number ranges:** 1:2, 1:3, 2:3, 3:2, 4:3, 5:3, 5:2, 7:4; three-part 4:5:6; totals 56 to 1540; "56 more women joined".
- **Pictures:** pie charts and bar graphs supply the ratio; no bar models.
- **Marks:** 2 marks for a one-stage ratio; 4 or 5 marks for a ratio with a change, two boxes, or a fraction link to a third quantity.
- **Level 6 plan:** lesson 11 fits at Start here. Add the "after a change" form, the "two boxes with different ratios" form and ratio from a pie chart at Think harder. Ratios with fractions or decimals do not occur.

### 3.4 Percentage

- **Wording:** "What was the price before the discount?"; "percentage increase in the price"; "was 30% less than the usual price"; "9% GST"; "What percentage of the beads are red?"; "20% of the book unread".
- **Number ranges:** 5 to 90 percent, most often 10, 20, 25, 30, 40, 60; GST 9 percent (2023 to 2025); discounts 8 to 40 percent; interest 1 to 3 percent in a few.
- **Pictures:** pie charts with percent labels; tables with a total.
- **Marks:** 1 or 2 marks for one step; 4 marks for two-stage change ("sold some, then 30 percent of what is left were trackers") or a pair of discounts on equal price.
- **Level 6 plan:** lesson 13 fits. Add "percent of a remainder", "two shops, equal price", and "price after discount and a further $ off" at Think harder. Compound growth is not tested.

### 3.5 Average

- **Wording:** "The average mark for 4 subjects is 78"; "average speed"; "greatest possible difference"; "ink blot"; "the average became"; "average of the 8 numbers".
- **Number ranges:** averages 25 to 3150; 3 to 7 values; a changed value (2400 keyed for 4200) giving a different average.
- **Pictures:** tables with a covered value, bar graphs, a number frame on a 1-to-64 table.
- **Marks:** 1 mark for the average of 2 numbers; 2 marks for a missing value; 4 marks for "how many adults were there".
- **Level 6 plan:** lesson 31 teaches it in Term 4, **late for school**. Move one Think harder average item into lesson 4 or 8; keep lesson 31 as the full lesson.

### 3.6 Rate, speed, taps and tanks

- **Wording:** "at an average speed of 80 m/min"; "Both taps are turned on at the same time"; "in one minute, how many litres"; "Leave your answer in hours and minutes"; "same height"; "until the heights are equal".
- **Number ranges:** 20 to 120 km/h, 40 to 375 m/min, 2 to 12 litres per minute, 1.2 to 4.35 litres per minute in 2026; tank bases from 12 cm2 to 1000 cm2.
- **Pictures:** a line graph of water against time, or two tanks with taps, or the same tank before and after pouring.
- **Marks:** 2 marks for one-stage speed; 4 or 5 marks for two taps and a graph (a), (b), (c).
- **Level 6 plan:** lesson 14 fits for taps and trains of the meeting kind; **lead with taps and tanks**, since speed is gone in 2026. Trains with length do not appear in the school papers.

### 3.7 Algebra

- **Wording:** "Find the value of 9c - 3 + 2c when c = 7"; "in terms of p"; "A pen costs $p. A notebook costs $2 more"; "If y = 5, find"; "Find the value of w".
- **Number ranges:** one letter, whole-number coefficients, no brackets, no two-letter work; an equation arises from a perimeter (DE = 2w, perimeter 140).
- **Pictures:** a figure with sides in letters, or no picture.
- **Marks:** 2 marks for simplify or substitute; 4 marks for "(a) in terms of p (b) given the total, find the cost".
- **Level 6 plan:** lessons 21 and 22 fit. Expanding a(b + c) and both-sides equations are beyond the school papers.

### 3.8 Angles

- **Wording:** "Find angle x"; "PQ and RS are straight lines"; "ABCD is a rhombus"; "AD is parallel to BC"; "not drawn to scale"; "circle the words that describe"; "is parallel because".
- **Number ranges:** whole degrees (35, 48, 55, 60, 70, 100, 104, 124); chains of 2 to 4 angles through isosceles, equilateral, parallelogram, rhombus, trapezium and a straight line; a folded corner of a rectangle.
- **Pictures:** every item needs a figure; angles are labelled by letters.
- **Marks:** 2 marks for one chain; 4 marks for parts (a) and (b); (c) in 2026 asks for a reason.
- **Level 6 plan:** lesson 17 fits. Add **parallel lines with co-interior and alternate angles**, the folded-corner item, and a one-line reason ("equal angles, so isosceles").

### 3.9 Area, perimeter and circles

- **Wording:** "Take pi as 3.14"; "Take pi as 22/7"; "in terms of pi"; "Find the area of the shaded part"; "The perimeter of the figure is 112 cm"; "Give your answer to 2 decimal places".
- **Number ranges:** sides 8 to 48 cm; radius 10 and 14 cm; semicircles and quarter circles on a grid of equal squares of area 49 cm2 (side 7 cm); overlaps with a ratio of areas; two rectangles joined with an overlapping square (side 2p).
- **Pictures:** always a figure; shaded regions and grids.
- **Marks:** 2 marks for one composite; 4 or 5 marks for two parts with the second building on the first.
- **Level 6 plan:** lessons 7, 9 and 15 fit. Use 3.14 first; 22/7 only where a diameter is a multiple of 7. Add "in terms of pi" at Go further.

### 3.10 Volume, nets, views and solids

- **Wording:** "Draw the front view and top view"; "What is the least number of cubes"; "Which of the following is the net of"; "Put a cross on one face that does not fit"; "How many 4-cm cubes can fit"; "painted red, including the base".
- **Number ranges:** stacks of 7 to 14 unit cubes; cuboids with volume 96 to 400 cm3; cubes of side 1, 2 and 4 cm; water depth in cm.
- **Pictures:** isometric solid, three views drawn on a square grid, nets of a cuboid, cube or pyramid.
- **Marks:** 2 marks to draw a view; 3 marks for a view plus "least cubes to add"; 5 marks for a net and a cuboid in several parts.
- **Level 6 plan:** lessons 16 and 38 fit. Add "least cubes to remove", "which face does not fit the net", the perimeter of a net, and "greatest number of small cubes in a box".

### 3.11 Symmetry, compass and drawing on a grid

- **Wording:** "Shade 3 more squares to form a symmetric figure"; "AB as the line of symmetry"; "By joining dots on the grid with straight lines"; "must not overlap with"; "south-east of the house and north of the school"; "turns 135 degrees anti-clockwise".
- **Number ranges:** grids of 6 to 10 squares; turns of 45, 90, 135, 225 degrees; the directions N, S, E, W and the diagonals.
- **Pictures:** always a grid; the answer is a drawing or a tick in a square.
- **Marks:** 1 or 2 marks for symmetry; 3 to 4 marks for drawing a triangle with an area ratio.
- **Level 6 plan:** lesson 18 teaches rotation and tessellation, which the papers do not set; reduce them. Add "shade to complete a figure with a line of symmetry" and "draw a figure with a given area or perimeter". Compass sits in lesson 24 as a warm-up only.

### 3.12 Data: pie charts, bar graphs, line graphs, tables and statements

- **Wording:** "Which bar graph best represents the information?"; "The bar for ... has not been drawn"; "Each statement is either true, false or not possible to tell"; "During which one-hour interval"; "scale not shown".
- **Number ranges:** 4 to 6 categories; totals 40 to 1000; percent labels summing to 100; times on a 24-hour clock in a line graph.
- **Pictures:** always a chart.
- **Marks:** 2 marks to read; 4 or 5 marks with (a) a total and (b) a percentage or ratio.
- **Level 6 plan:** lesson 31. Add the true/false/not possible table and the match-the-graph item.

### 3.13 Patterns, lists and puzzles

- **Wording:** "A repeated pattern is formed using the digits"; "What is the sum of the first 99 numbers?"; "In which column will the number appear?"; "How many grey beads did she use?"; "at most"; "the greatest number of bags".
- **Number ranges:** repeating blocks of 3 to 5; the 13th, 99th and 100th terms; a table of 4 columns; figure sequences 4, 7, 10 with perimeters 10, 16, 22.
- **Pictures:** figure sequences; a column table.
- **Marks:** 2 marks in Booklet B; 5 marks for a table with the nth figure.
- **Level 6 plan:** lessons 6, 35 and 36. Add the 4-column table and the repeating-block sum.

## 6. Recommended changes to the Level 6 plan (for the owner to approve)

1. **Lesson 31 average.** Add one average Think harder item to lesson 4 or 8 in Term 1 (average with a changed value), and keep lesson 31 as the full lesson. Average appears in nearly every preliminary paper and every year.
2. **Lesson 17 Angle Chasing.** Teach angles between parallel lines (co-interior and alternate) with the reason in words, the folded corner, and rhombus, parallelogram and trapezium word-circling. Re-level the plan's "parallel lines not in primary" statement: the papers set it from 2021 and ask for a reason in 2026.
3. **Lesson 14 Rates, Work and Trains.** Lead with taps and tank pouring (cm and litres per minute). Speed stays as the labelled secondary preview; the 2026 papers have none.
4. **Lesson 11 Ratio Recipes.** Add the "after a change" form ("5 : 4, then 56 women joined, 3 : 4"), two boxes with a times-as-many link, and a ratio read from a pie chart, all at Think harder.
5. **True, false or not possible to tell.** Add as a five-minute item to lessons 11, 13, 17, 31 and 36.
6. **Draw-on-a-grid.** Add a draw item to lessons 7, 9 and 20 (same area, same perimeter, a triangle with an area ratio, a rhombus from two sides).
7. **Lesson 18 Symmetry.** Reduce rotation and tessellation (not in the papers); add "shade squares to complete a figure with a line of symmetry".
8. **Lesson 15 Circles.** Pi 3.14 first, 22/7 with diameters 7, 14, 21, 28; semicircles and quarter circles on a grid of equal squares; "in terms of pi" at Go further.
9. **Lesson 13 Percent.** State GST at 9 percent; add two shops at equal price and "a further $ off".
10. **Lesson 24 Maps.** Add a compass warm-up (turns of 45, 90, 135, 225 degrees), labelled as optional; the 2026 papers drop it.
11. **Lessons 23, 26, 28, 32, 33.** No school evidence; keep as enrichment and say so on the page.
12. **Ratio and percent in Term 1.** The Term 1 weighted assessment (WA1, March) sets ratio and percentage, so a ratio Start here item (share in a ratio, a ratio of three) can sit in lesson 8 or 9 as a warm-up. This is an optional change; lesson 11 stays the full treatment.

## 7. Paper-based wording changes for child pages

- **Section instructions:** "Show your working clearly"; "Give your answer in the units stated"; "Answers in fractions or ratio must be in the simplest form"; "Figures are not drawn to scale".
- **Answer frames:** "Ans: ____ cm" with the unit printed after the blank; "(a) ____ (b) ____"; marks in square brackets at the end "[2]".
- **Round-off words:** "correct to 1 decimal place", "to 2 decimal places", "Take pi as 3.14" (or 22/7), "in terms of pi", "Leave your answer in hours and minutes", "Give your answer in 24-hour clock".
- **Statement table:** "Each statement below is either true, false or not possible to tell from the information given. For each statement, put a tick."
- **Drawing:** "By joining dots on the grid with straight lines"; "must not overlap with"; "Shade ... to form a symmetric figure".
- **Reasons:** "Circle the words that describe ... correctly"; "(a) ... because ...".
- **Comparison words:** "times as many", "more than", "less than", "the ratio of A to B" (A first).
- **Time:** "7.45 p.m." and "1805"; accept "7:45 pm" in children's own writing.
- **Graphs:** "The bar for ... has not been drawn"; "scale is not shown".

## 8. What was not checked

- Pictures in every file. Figures, graph scales, grid sizes and angles are inferred from wording, labels and answer keys.
- The 15 unread PDFs (see section 0); the 2020 papers are the oldest and carry the old syllabus, so the findings above rest mainly on 2021 to 2026.
- School mark schemes and the weighting of each topic; the time of year each school sets a topic (the weighted assessments WA1 to WA3 carry that information and only 27 was read).
- Whether a topic is "taught by the school before the Prelim" is not known; only that it is set.
