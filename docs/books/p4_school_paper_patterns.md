# Primary 4 school paper patterns (what the papers say about the Level 4 plan)

Status: analysis written 23 Oct 2026 from the owner's Drive folder "P4 Math": about 120 distinct PDFs read from OCR text, 111 paper labels from 21 schools, 2021 to 2025 (2023 to 2025 best covered). About 45 mostly 2021 and 2022 PDFs and one image-only 2017 paper were not read. **No school question text is reproduced here**; quoted phrases are eight words or fewer. Pictures were lost in OCR, so picture conventions are inferred. The non-routine counts and examples are in `docs/books/p4_non_routine_catalogue.md`.

## 0. Headline findings

1. **The Primary 4 paper is a bar-model paper.** 214 of the 807 recorded non-routine items (27 percent) are unit chains: a total with "twice as many", "3 times as many", "$20 more than", or "the same number after". 113 of them are 4-mark Section C items. The plan has one dedicated bar-model lesson (2.3) and one supposition lesson (4.7). It needs more unit-model work, with money, mass and decimals, spread over Terms 1 to 4.
2. **Fractions carry the multi-step load**: 31 MULTI and 31 BACK items are fraction stories (fraction of the rest, "3/5 of the sweets to 5 friends", the tank that needs 120 l more). Whole from a part is as common as part from a whole.
3. **Rounding with bounds is a standing item**: "greatest possible" and "smallest possible" for a number that rounds to a given value, with whole numbers and with 2-decimal numbers rounded to the nearest tenth. About 25 items. The plan has no lesson for it.
4. **Geometry is chains, not definitions** (83 items): identical squares or rectangles joined, folded or cut; find one side from a perimeter, then an area or a new perimeter. Turns are in 45-degree steps with eight compass points.
5. **Factors and multiples appear as riddles and pack prices** (70 LOG items, about 12 CTX pack-price items at 2 to 4 marks). Pack prices (4 pencils for $3, 6 erasers for $5) ask for the least equal amount, or the most that fits a budget.
6. **Two-scenario sharing (GC)** appears every year: one rule leaves some over, another is short. The plan does not teach it.
7. **Time is always the same two moves**: durations to the minute across noon, and the start from the end; 24-hour clock written 1850 or 18 50. Already in the plan (3.8), but as a revision lesson only.
8. **Marks**: 2 marks (407 items), 4 marks (257), 3 marks (124). Section C 4-mark items nearly always have (a) and (b), where (b) uses (a).
9. **No hints in the stem**: the model, table or backwards step is the child's choice.

## 1. Per topic: wording, numbers, pictures, marks

Phrases are recurring, eight words or fewer.

### Whole numbers (to 100 000; four operations; models)
- Wording: "how many more ... than", "times as many", "the same number of ... in the end", "after giving away".
- Numbers: totals to about 10 000, 5-digit values in place value and rounding; multipliers 2 to 5.
- Pictures: bars drawn by the child; bar graphs with no scale (the child finds the unit).
- Marks: 2 (short) and 4 (parts a and b). Difficulty above the planned lessons 1.1, 1.2, 1.8: the plan teaches the operations; papers use them inside unit chains.

### Rounding and estimation
- Wording: "rounded to the nearest hundred", "smallest possible", "greatest possible", "rounded to the nearest tenth".
- Numbers: nearest 10, 100, 1000; 2-decimal numbers rounded to a tenth.
- Marks: 2. Gap: bounds are not planned.

### Factors and multiples
- Wording: "common multiple of", "factor of", "greater than ... and less than", "least number", "greatest number".
- Riddles: factor, plus a number, makes a multiple greater than 10; two-digit numbers with a digit sum.
- Context: pack prices, buses that meet again, tiles. Marks: 2 for riddles, 4 for pack prices. Fits 1.3 but pack prices are not in the plan.

### Fractions
- Wording: "of the rest", "how many more ... must be shaded so that", "what fraction", "is 3/5 of".
- Numbers: denominators 2 to 12; unlike denominators for add and subtract; mixed numbers appear in measurement stories (ribbon, tank).
- Picture: shaded stars or squares to complete a given fraction (about a dozen items: LIST).
- Marks: 3 and 4. Fits 1.4, 2.4, 2.5, 2.6 but the papers mix fraction with the bar model and time.

### Decimals
- Wording: "to the nearest tenth", "more than", "four times as much", in kg, l, m and $.
- Numbers: 1 to 3 decimal places; a decimal bar model is a 4-mark item in WA3 and EOY.
- Marks: 2 to 4. Fits 1.5, 2.7, 2.8, 2.9; decimal unit chains are a Think harder item.

### Angles, turns, perpendicular and parallel
- Wording: "turns ... clockwise", "anticlockwise", "facing ... at first", "measure angle".
- Numbers: 45, 90, 135, 225, 270, 315 degrees; eight compass points. Picture: a compass or map; protractor drawn.
- Marks: 2, with reversal (BACK) at 2 to 3. Gap: compass turns are not planned (3.2 uses clock hands).

### Area, perimeter, squares and rectangles
- Wording: "divided into identical", "joined to form", "find the perimeter", "find the area of the shaded".
- Numbers: sides 2 to 20 cm; 2 to 8 identical pieces. Marks: 2 or 4, with (a) a length and (b) an area or perimeter.
- Picture dependent. Fits 3.3, 3.4, 3.9; folded paper and joined-piece chains are not planned.

### Symmetry and nets
- Wording: "which of the following is the net of", "line of symmetry", "must be removed so that".
- Mostly multiple choice, 2 marks; picture only. Fits 3.1 and 3.6.

### Data: tables, bar, line and pie charts
- Wording: "the bar graph shows", "the pie chart shows", "which period", "how much was used between".
- Pictures: bar graphs with no scale (find the unit); line graphs of petrol or temperature; pie charts with fractions and one count; two-way tables with letters or ink blots.
- Marks: 2 for reading, 4 for build-and-use. Fits 1.7, 4.1, 4.2.

### Time, money and measures
- Wording: "leave your answer in 24-hour clock", "how much more", "for every hour including breaks".
- 12-hour with am and pm, 24-hour as 1850; durations to the minute; prices to the cent.
- Marks: 2 to 4. Fits 3.8; money is spread across 2.7.

### Patterns
- Wording: "Figure 5", "which figure has", "complete the number pattern".
- Dot and square patterns with a table (Figure 1, 2, 3, 4); letters repeating; alternating decimal steps. Marks 2 or 4. Fits 4.3.

## 2. Difficulty against the planned lessons

| Level of difficulty | Papers | Plan |
|---|---|---|
| Routine, one or two steps | Section A and B | Start here tier |
| Two to three steps, one model | Section B 2 marks, Section C (a) | Start here to Think harder |
| Unit chain with a change, then a second question | Section C 4 marks | Think harder to Go further |
| Chain plus a decimal, a fraction and a drawing | the last question of the paper | Go further |

Of the 807 recorded items, 598 are difficulty 3 (my judgement for the age): the plan's Go further tier is the school paper's ordinary hard question; Think harder is the school paper's typical Section C part (a).

## 4. Recommended changes to the Level 4 plan

1. Treat bar-model unit chains as the spine: add a unit-chain Think harder item to 1.1, 1.8, 2.4, 2.5, 2.7, 2.8 and 4.6, not only 2.3 and 4.7.
2. Add rounding bounds (greatest and smallest that rounds to a value) to 1.1 and 2.9 as Think harder.
3. Add pack prices ("n for $x") to 1.3 and 2.7.
4. Add two-scenario sharing (left over and short) to 1.8 and 4.7.
5. 3.2: add compass points and reversing a turn; 3.4 and 3.9: add joined and folded identical pieces.
6. 4.2: include the missing-sector pie chart item.
7. Keep Contest-only lessons (1.9, 3.7, 4.4, 4.8, 4.9) as Beyond MOE; label them optional.

## 5. Paper-based wording changes for child pages

- Write time as 3:20 pm, and 24-hour as 1850; accept 18 50 in children's work.
- Write money as $4.75, and ask for the change in the unit the paper uses.
- "More than ... and less than ..." is a two-condition riddle: keep both words in the stem.
- Use "identical", "divided into", "joined to form" for geometry chains.
- Name the choice (true, false or not possible to tell) in statement items.
- Do not hint the heuristic at Go further; Think harder may.

## 6. What was not checked

The oldest papers (2021 to 2022) are mostly unread. Pictures were not seen except by description. Several answer keys were not legible, so some answers were worked by me. Whether a school's scheme sets these items before or after the Term in which our lesson sits is not verified. Counts describe this set of papers, not the country.
