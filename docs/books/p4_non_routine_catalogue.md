# Primary 4 non-routine question catalogue (from school test papers)

Status: analysis written 23 Oct 2026 from the owner's Drive folder "P4 Math". **No school question text is reproduced here.** The verbatim questions are in a private, gitignored file for the owner's own study (`docs/books/private_p4_papers/non_routine_questions.md`). Every example below is an original question written for this project and checked by a script (`docs/books/private_p4_papers/verify_catalogue_examples.py`, run with `python3 -I`; it is in the private folder because it lives beside the paper records, and contains no paper text). Nothing here is a claim about what any school will set.

## 1. What was read, and what was not

- The folder holds about 170 PDFs. **About 120 distinct PDFs were read** from OCR text (the Drive text layer), giving **111 separate paper labels** (end-of-year exams, weighted assessments 1 to 3, quizzes, and a few older "CA" and "SA" papers) from **21 schools, 2021 to 2025**. Years: 2025 (33 papers), 2024 (39), 2023 (27), 2022 (3), 2021 (7), two undated.
- Copies were not read twice (several 2021 revision sets are re-labelled as 2024 papers; one Tao Nan 2024 file repeats a 2022 practice paper).
- **Not read:** about 45 PDFs, mostly 2021 and 2022 papers and a handful of 2023 weighted assessments and one 2017 Rulang paper whose text layer is empty (needs page images). They are the oldest papers, so the recent-years picture is the more complete one. Their types are probably the same; this is not checked.
- Pictures are lost in OCR. Where a figure decided the answer, the private file carries a bracketed note; picture conventions in `p4_school_paper_patterns.md` are inferred from the wording and from marks.
- School coverage is uneven: Nanyang, Tao Nan, Henry Park, Nan Hua, Red Swastika, Catholic High, St Hilda's and Pei Chun supply most items.

## 2. What counts as non-routine here

Beyond a one-step computation or a routine exercise on a taught method: multi-step stories, a heuristic (bar model with units, supposition, work backwards, pattern, list every case, table, make equal), reasoning (true or false, explain), an unfamiliar context (pack prices, posts, hourly pay), or build-from-clues. Routine questions (computation, reading a bar value, naming a shape) were not recorded.

**807 non-routine questions were recorded.** At Primary 4 the unit-and-equal-share model (EQ) is the single biggest group, and geometry chains (GEO) a clear second, so two codes are added to the 14 used at Primary 2: **GEO** (geometry chain) and **POSE** (judge or pose a statement). EQ is wider than at Primary 2: it holds every "total with relationships" problem, which Primary 2 split between CMP, MULTI and EQ.

Notes on the table.

- Difficulty is my own 1 to 3 judgement for a nine or ten year old (1 one extra idea, 2 a trap or two steps, 3 hard for the age). **598 of 807 are difficulty 3**: unlike Primary 2, the typical recorded item is already a stretch for the age, because the easier routine items were not recorded.
- Marks: **407 at 2 marks, 257 at 4, 124 at 3**. The 2-mark items are Section A or B (multiple choice or short answer); the 4-mark items are Section C problem sums with (a) and (b). Papers: end of year 100 marks; weighted assessments 20 to 40 marks.
- Items by paper year: 2024: 283; 2025: 238; 2023: 182; 2021: 43; 2022: 29; undated: 32. 2021 and 2022 are thin because those papers were mostly not read.
- As at Primary 2, there is no named Challenge section in the papers read, apart from one open performance task (Tao Nan WA2 2025) and a few starred items; the non-routine items are the Section B and C questions.
- **Primary 4 papers state no heuristic hint** (the "Hint: Make a Table" of Primary 2 is rare). The child must choose the model.
- Many items depend on a picture (identical rectangles joined, folded paper, bar graphs without a scale, pie charts, protractor and compass diagrams, timetables). The OCR text kept a bracketed note for only 62 of them, so the true share is higher. Digital and printed pages must carry the same kinds of drawing.

## 3. The types, with original examples

Tier names: **Think harder** (the T tier) and **Go further** (the G tier). Never Start here. Each example has a scripted answer.

### EQ: Unit and equal-share models: total with "times as many", "more than", or make equal (214 questions)

The core Primary 4 problem sum. Bar-model unit chains with whole numbers, money, mass and decimals; also two-quantity "after a change they are equal" and "n times as many after" forms.

Frequent contexts in the papers: whole numbers (80), money equal (26), fractions (16), whole numbers transfer (9), decimals equal (8).

## 4. Number ranges and forms seen

- Bar-model totals: mostly 3- and 4-digit whole numbers (up to about 10 000), with money to $1 000 and a few to $10 000; multipliers 2, 3, 4, 5 ("three times as many", "twice as much"); differences of $20 to $500.
- Fractions: denominators 2 to 12 (most often 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10); fraction of a quantity, fraction of the rest, "3/5 of his sweets to 5 friends"; fraction of a tank or tin to find the whole (BACK).
- Decimals: 1 to 3 decimal places, in kg, l, m and money; rounding "to the nearest tenth" with a two-decimal number (greatest and smallest values); a decimal bar model ("four times as much flour after using 4.09 kg").
- Rounding: nearest 10, 100, 1000; bounds (smallest and greatest) in about 25 items.
- Factors and multiples: factors of numbers to 100 (36, 48), common multiples of 4 and 6 or 3 and 5, pack prices (4 pencils for $3, 6 erasers for $5) with least or greatest number.
- Time: 12-hour (3:20 pm) and 24-hour (18 50 or 1850) clocks; durations to the minute; timetable durations; find the start from the end.
- Turns: 45-degree steps (135, 225, 270, 315) and 8 compass points; angles measured to 5 degrees; fan angles.
- Geometry chains: identical squares or rectangles (2 to 8), perimeters 40 to 120 cm, sides 2 to 20 cm.

## 6. How the examples were made and checked

Each example was written for this project from the pattern of a question type, not from a paper question. `verify_catalogue_examples.py` recomputes every answer by arithmetic or brute force (all 41 pass). Authors write their own items of the same kind and their own `verify/` check; they never copy a school or contest question.
