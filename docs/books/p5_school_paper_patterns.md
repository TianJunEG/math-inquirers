# Primary 5 school paper patterns (what the papers say about the Level 5 plan)

Status: analysis written 23 Oct 2026 from the owner's Drive folder "P5 Math": 173 distinct PDFs, all read from Drive OCR text, giving 166 paper labels from 19 schools, 2020 to 2025 (2020 to 2022 and 2024 to 2025 best covered; 2023 has one label per school). **No school question text is reproduced here**; quoted phrases are eight words or fewer. Pictures were not seen (no page was rendered to an image), so picture conventions are inferred from wording and marks. The non-routine counts and examples are in `docs/books/p5_non_routine_catalogue.md`.

## 0. Headline findings

1. **The Primary 5 paper is a bar-model and fraction paper with an angle section.** Of 2279 recorded non-routine items, 492 (22 percent) are unit chains (EQ), 193 work backwards (BACK) and 80 are multi-step (MULTI): fraction of the remainder, "times as many", "the same in the end". 382 (17 percent) are geometry chains, and about three in four of those are angle chains. The plan teaches the bar model in lesson 24 (Term 3) and angles in lessons 8, 31 and 32; the papers test both from the first weighted assessment.
2. **Two syllabus eras are in the folder.** 2020 to 2024 papers set ratio (174 items), average (87) and speed (1) at Primary 5. The 2025 papers drop all three and add more true/false/not possible to tell, isometric drawing, GST at 9 percent and annual interest. Level 5 keeps ratio and average as labelled previews (lessons 17 and 24), which is right for the new syllabus and still useful against the older papers.
3. **True, false or not possible to tell** (72 items, every year from 2020) is the school form for a statement item. The plan has none.
4. **Charges in steps and offers are a standing item** (48 "or part thereof" items; about 25 buy-more-get-free and least-cost items): parking, taxi, postage, delivery, "buy 4 get 1 free", packs of two sizes. Not in the plan.
5. **Views of cubes are common and getting harder** (49 view items, 11 isometric): draw the top, front and side view, and in 2025 "the greatest number of cubes that can be added" without changing a view.
6. **Packing and sharing with HCF and LCM** ("as many bags as possible", "smallest number shared among 6 or 8") are 2025 items and fit lesson 2.
7. **Tank items go beyond litres:** the water level after a block is laid in, after pouring into a second tank, "how much more water" to a stated depth (59 CTX items).
8. **Marks:** 2 marks (1099 items), 4 marks (462), 3 marks (427), 5 marks (180). Paper 2 questions 6 to 17 have parts (a) and (b); (b) uses (a). In 2025 the end-of-year paper is 50 + 50 (was 45 + 55).
9. **No hints in the stem**: the model, table or backwards step is the child's choice. Drawing answers (complete a figure, draw the missing bar, draw a triangle with a stated area) are 2 or 3 marks and need a marking rule.

## 1. Per topic: wording, numbers, pictures, marks

Phrases are recurring, eight words or fewer.

### Numbers to 10 million; four operations; order of operations
- Wording: "What is the value of the digit", "to the nearest thousand", "Form the smallest 5-digit odd number".
- Numbers: 6- and 7-digit numbers in place value and rounding; totals to 5 digits in stories; multipliers 2 to 6; the order-of-operations item is a 1-mark Paper 1 Booklet B question ("Find the value of ...").
- Pictures: number lines for decimals; none otherwise. Marks: 1 to 2. Fits lessons 1 and 3; the paper uses these as routine items inside unit stories.

### Factors and multiples
- Wording: "as many bags as possible", "common multiples of 3 and 4", "highest common factor of", "least number", "greatest number".
- Numbers: pairs under 100 (16 and 36, 24 and 56, 54 and 36); packing two kinds of item into identical bags; a number "between 15 and 50" with two clues. 2 to 3 marks, mostly 2025. Fits lesson 2; packing stories are the gap.

### Fractions
- Wording: "of the remainder", "Express your answer in the simplest form", "What fraction of the ... was", "How many thirds are there in 6".
- Numbers: denominators 2 to 12 (3, 4, 5, 8 most often); mixed numbers in ribbon, paint and time stories; fraction of a fraction; one fraction of A equals another fraction of B.
- Picture: shaded squares, a figure of identical triangles ("how many more must be shaded so that ..."), bar models drawn by the child. Marks: 2 to 5; the 4 and 5 mark items are fraction-of-remainder chains. Fits lessons 4 and 5; the papers combine the fraction with the bar model.

### Decimals and measures
- Wording: "correct to the nearest tenth", "Give your answer in kg and g", "Express ... as a decimal".
- Numbers: 1 and 2 decimal places (2 in three of four items); money to the cent; litres to ml; a water-bill table with one total missing (an open "one possible set" item). Marks 1 to 3. Fits lessons 6 and 14.

### Percentage, discount, GST, interest
- Wording: "What percentage of the ... are", "inclusive of 7% GST", "How much discount was given", "3% interest at the end of each year".
- Numbers: 8 to 80 percent, 10, 20, 25, 40, 60, 75 most often; GST 7 percent in 2020 to 2024 and 9 percent in 2025; "one quantity as a percentage of another, to the nearest whole number". Chains: discount, then GST; percentage of a remainder.
- Picture: tables of bottles and cans, a pie chart. Marks 2 to 4. Third-term weighted assessments are mostly percentage. Fits lessons 21 to 23 and 29.

### Ratio and average (2020 to 2024 papers; absent in 2025)
- Wording: "in the ratio 3 : 1", "What is the ratio of", "What was the average", "the average became".
- Numbers: small ratios (2 : 3, 4 : 5, 1 : 3), ratio chains (A : B and B : C); average of two groups combined, the average after one more value, the score needed for a stated average. Marks 2 to 4. Labelled previews in lessons 17 and 24.

### Rate
- Wording: "At this rate, how long will it take", "at the same rate", "per minute", taps and machines.
- Numbers: simple rates (300 cards in 20 minutes); taps at 1.2 l per minute into two containers; 13 items in 2025. Marks 2 to 4. Fits lesson 16; speed is not in the 2025 papers.

### Area of triangles and composite figures
- Wording: "Identify the height of triangle ABC", "total area of the shaded parts", "twice the area of triangle ABC".
- Numbers: bases 4 to 40 cm; rectangles with triangles inside; squares and triangles overlapping with a ratio of areas; a perimeter given and an area asked (a right-angled triangle on a rectangle).
- Picture: always; many on a square grid with "draw ... such that the area is" tasks. Marks: 1 to 4 (the identify-the-height item is 1 or 2). Fits lessons 7 and 15; the ratio-of-overlap form is the gap.

### Volume and liquid
- Wording: "filled to the brim", "How much more water has to be added", "without any spilling", "water level".
- Numbers: cuboids with edges 3 to 60 cm; a tank part-filled, a second tank, a block laid in; "greatest number of 3-cm cubes that fit". Picture: cuboids with dimensions on the edges. Marks 2 to 5. Fits lessons 12 and 13; immersion is the gap.

### Angles, triangles and quadrilaterals
- Wording: "are straight lines", "not drawn to scale", "Find angle ...", "is a trapezium", "ABCD is (is/is not) a parallelogram".
- Numbers: whole degrees; isosceles and equilateral triangles inside rectangles and trapeziums; a folded corner; two joined triangles; angle a is twice angle b. Chains of two to four steps. 2 to 4 marks, and as 2-mark Booklet A multiple choice with four options.
- Picture: always; angles are named with three letters or a single letter. Fits lessons 8, 31 and 32; the papers' chains are longer than the plan's Start here level.

### Symmetry, drawing on a grid, directions
- Wording: "Shade four more squares", "line of symmetry", "Complete the symmetrical figure", "In which direction is G from F", "turns 90° anti-clockwise".
- Numbers: eight compass points, quarter turns, 45-degree turns. Pictures: squares in a grid; a map with seven marked points. Marks 1 to 3. Not a Level 5 lesson (Primary 4 topic): spiral in lessons 8 and 32.

### Data: bar, line, pie, tables
- Wording: "The bar graph below shows", "Draw the bar in the graph", "In which month did she", "average number of ... each day".
- Pictures: bar graphs with a scale, line graphs of water in a tank over time, pie charts with one count, two-way tables with a total row. Marks 1 to 3. Fits lessons 17 and 18; pie charts are not in the plan.

### Patterns and sequences
- Wording: "The figures follow a pattern", "What is the 117th number?", "the first 15 numbers", "For each week, he saves $5 more".
- Forms: a repeating block (4 to 8 terms) with the nth term and the sum of the first n; matchstick, dot and triangle figures with a table of three or four rows then "Figure 10"; savings that grow by a fixed step (linear). Marks 1 to 5. Fits lesson 26; the plan puts patterns only in Term 3 but the first weighted assessment already has them.

### Solids: views, isometric drawing, nets
- Wording: "front and side views of the solid", "Draw the top view and the side view", "Which of the following shows the solid".
- Pictures: three views on a square grid; isometric dot paper (2025); a cube figure with "the least number of unit cubes" to add. Marks 1 to 3. Fits lessons 11 and 19.

### Statements, true or false, open items
- Wording: "either true, false or not possible to tell", "put a tick in the correct column", "write down one possible set of values".
- Forms: a rectangle with triangles inside and a statement about equal areas; a number with exactly six factors; "52% of the cars were red". Marks 1 to 2 per statement. Not in the plan.

## 2. Difficulty against the planned lessons

| Level of difficulty | Papers | Plan |
|---|---|---|
| Routine, one or two steps | Paper 1 Booklet A and B | Start here tier |
| Two to three steps, one model | Paper 2 Questions 1 to 5; part (a) of 3 to 5 mark items | Start here to Think harder |
| Unit chain with a change, then a second question | Paper 2 Questions 6 to 15, 3 to 4 marks | Think harder to Go further |
| Chain plus a fraction, a decimal and a drawing | The last two questions of Paper 2 (4 or 5 marks) | Go further |

Of the 2279 recorded items, 1821 (80 percent) are difficulty 3 (my judgement for the age): the plan's Go further tier is the school paper's ordinary hard question, and Think harder is the typical Paper 2 part (a). The recorded set is the hard half of each paper; Paper 1 is mostly easier.

## 4. Recommended changes to the Level 5 plan

1. Put a unit-chain Think harder item in lessons 4, 5, 6, 9 and 10 (Term 1). The first weighted assessment already has them; lesson 24 stays the full treatment.
2. Add true, false or not possible to tell as a statement type to lessons 2, 7, 8, 15, 23, 31 and 32, with all three choices in the stem.
3. Strengthen angles: two- to four-step chains in lesson 8, quadrilateral chains in 31 and 32, an angle warm-up in each Review, a folded-corner item in lesson 15.
4. Add the ratio-of-overlapping-areas and "any point on a side" items to lessons 7 and 15; a draw-on-a-grid area task to lesson 7.
5. Add charges in steps and "greatest distance" to lesson 16; buy-more-get-free and discount then GST at 9 percent to lesson 22; least cost with two pack sizes to lesson 28.
6. Add packing and sharing (HCF and LCM) stories to lesson 2; the greatest number of cubes to lessons 11, 12 and 19.
7. Add "a block is laid in the water" and "pour into a second tank" to lesson 13.
8. Add a repeating-pattern item to the Term 1 review, since the first weighted assessment already tests it.
9. Keep average (17) and ratio notation (24) as labelled previews; the 2025 papers use "times as many" and "for every".
10. Keep the Contest-only lessons (9, 33 to 35, 38, 39) as Beyond MOE; label them optional where the papers show no evidence.

## 5. Paper-based wording changes for child pages

- Write time as 3:20 pm and as 24-hour 2150 (some papers write 2.45 p.m.); accept either in children's work.
- Write money as $4.75 and give units in the stem ("Give your answer in kg and g").
- State GST at 9 percent and annual interest "at the end of each year"; accept 7 percent for older practice.
- Use "identical", "divided into", "joined to form", "not drawn to scale" for geometry.
- Name all three choices in a statement item (true, false or not possible to tell) and ask for a tick.
- "Express your answer in the simplest form" for fractions; "correct to the nearest tenth" for decimals.
- Do not hint the heuristic at Go further; Think harder may.
