## 2. What counts as non-routine here

Beyond a one-step computation or a routine exercise on a taught method: multi-step stories, a heuristic (bar model with units, supposition, work backwards, pattern, list every case, table, make equal), reasoning (true, false or not possible to tell), an unfamiliar context (a tariff, a tap, a net) or a drawing the child must produce. Plain computation (3.707 + 1.373, order of operations, bare conversions) and one-step area or angle items are left out. The 14 type codes below are those of Primary 5 minus MULTI and POSE: at Primary 6 almost every item is a multi-step item, so MULTI would hold everything, and each item is filed under its most specific type.

Notes on the table.

- Difficulty is my own 1 to 3 judgement for a twelve-year-old (1 one extra idea, 2 a trap or two steps, 3 hard for the age). **1877 of 2108 are difficulty 3** (89 percent): a recorded item is a stretch for the age because I recorded only the non-routine questions and skipped the routine ones, so the share says nothing about the whole paper.
- Marks: 2-mark items are Paper 1 Booklet B (Q21 to Q30) and Paper 2 Q1 to Q5; 3, 4 and 5-mark items are Paper 2 Q6 to Q17 with parts (a) and (b). Paper 1 Booklet A (multiple choice) items are 1 or 2 marks.
- Items by paper year: 2020: 62; 2021: 32; 2022: 300; 2023: 448; 2024: 421; 2025: 480; 2026: 365. 2023 to 2026 carry most of the weight because those were read first.
- **Two syllabus eras sit in the folder.** The 2020 to 2025 papers set speed (km/h and m/min, 2022 to 2025: 7 to 15 recorded items a year), GST (9 percent; 7 percent in older papers; none recorded in 2026), compass turns and the old PSLE shape of Booklet A, Booklet B and Paper 2 (20 + 25 + 55 marks). The **2026 papers follow the new syllabus: 26 + 24 + 50 marks, no km/h speed item recorded, no GST item and no compass item recorded**; ratio, average, percentage, algebra, pie charts, circles, nets and views, tap and tank rates (cm per minute) all stay. See the patterns file for the evidence.
- **1054 of 2108 recorded items (50 percent) needed a picture**; the true share is higher because OCR dropped figures silently. Digital and printed pages must carry the same kinds of drawing: angle diagrams, composite figures, grids for drawing, pie charts and bar graphs, block views and nets.
- There is no named challenge section. The last question of Paper 2 (5 marks) is the hardest. Some stems name the tool ("Draw a rectangle with the same area", "Express your answer in terms of n"), but not the heuristic.

## 3. The types, with original examples

Tier names: **Think harder** (T) and **Go further** (G). Never Start here. Each example has a scripted answer.

### EQ: Unit, ratio and equal-share models (404 questions)

The core P6 problem sum: a ratio or a pair of fractions with a change that makes the quantities equal or fixes a new ratio ("after giving 10 ... three times as many"), percentage and fraction of a part with a given count ("60 percent roses, half of the roses red, 24 red"), unit price chains (4 for $17, 3 for $8), equal numbers of two items, and ratio with a change in each part. Most are 4 or 5 marks with parts (a) and (b).

Frequent contexts in the papers (keyword counts, approximate): ratio (134), fractions and remainder (81), percentage, discount, GST (45), volume, tanks, cubes (36).

## 4. Number ranges and forms seen

- **Whole numbers:** totals to 4 or 5 digits in unit-chain stories (1338 and 7982 buns, 1953 balloons, 408 children); riddles with 3-digit numbers (a sum of 375 with a smallest number of 120); multiples and factors of numbers under 100 for packing.
- **Fractions:** denominators 2 to 12, mostly 3, 4, 5, 6, 8; fraction of the remainder in 2 to 3 stages; a fraction of one quantity equals a fraction of another; "how many sixths are in 2"; mixed numbers in measurement stories.
- **Decimals:** 1 and 2 decimal places; money to the cent ($1.35 per 100 g, $2.50 per half hour); litres and kg with conversions; rounding "correct to 1 decimal place".
- **Percentages:** 5 to 90 percent, most often 10, 20, 25, 30, 40, 60; one quantity as a percentage of another; percentage increase and decrease (20 percent savings up by $180 means 30 percent more); discount 8 to 40 percent; GST 9 percent (7 percent in 2021 and earlier).
- **Ratio:** 1:2, 1:3, 2:3, 3:2, 4:3, 5:3, 5:2, 7:4 and three-part 4:5:6; "after the change the ratio is ..."; the same amount given or spent from both.
- **Speed and rate (2022 to 2025 only):** 20 to 120 km/h and 40 to 375 m/min; opposite directions, meeting and passing; average speed of a two-part journey; taps at 2 to 12 litres or cm per minute.
- **Geometry:** angles in whole degrees (35, 48, 55, 60, 70, 100, 114, 124); rectangles and squares of sides 8 to 30 cm; circle radius 10 and 14 cm with pi 22/7 (8 recorded) or 3.14 (29 recorded); cuboid volumes 96 and 200 cm3; tanks 30 cm by 20 cm; cubes 1, 2 and 4 cm.
- **Time:** 12-hour clock with a.m. and p.m., 24-hour clock written 1805; durations in hours and minutes; parking and rental fares rounded up in half-hour steps.
- **Patterns:** repeating blocks of 2 to 5; the 13th, 99th and 100th terms; tables of 4 columns; sums of the first 99 terms.

## 6. How the examples were made and checked

Each example was written for this project from the pattern of a question type, not from a paper question. `verify_catalogue_examples.py` recomputes every answer by arithmetic or brute force (all 57 pass). Authors write their own items of the same kind and their own `verify/` check; they never copy a school or contest question.
