# How Singapore school papers word questions: patterns, and how Level 1 Term 1 compares

Sources studied for wording conventions only (copyrighted by the schools; nothing is copied here, examples below are our own short paraphrases of the TYPE of phrasing):

- **P1**: Henry Park Primary, Primary 1 Topical Quiz 4, Multiplication and Division, 2025 (17 questions, plus answer key). PDF is 11 pages: PDF p1-5 = paper p1-5, PDF p6-9 = paper p8-11 (paper p6-7, questions 10-13, are missing from the upload), PDF p10-11 = answer key. References below use "P1 p." for the PDF page.
- **P2**: Rosyth School, Primary 2 Term 4 Topical Review 2, 2025 (15 questions, 45 minutes, answer key). PDF is 16 pages: p1-2 teacher's assessment grid and self-reflection, p3-13 paper, p14 and p16 blank, p15 answer key. References: "P2 p.".

Caveat: this is two papers from two schools, both from late 2025 and both topical, so rules below are "what these papers do". Where a convention is typical of Singapore papers but not seen here, it is marked **(not in these papers)**.

## Part 1. Catalogue of conventions

### 1.1 Paper and section structure

| Feature | P1 (Henry Park, quiz) | P2 (Rosyth, topical review) |
|---|---|---|
| Header | School, subject, "Primary 1 Topical Quiz 4" and topic name; Name, Class, Date; a bracket after Name for class number (P1 p1) | School, subject, level, term, "Topical Review 2"; Name, Class, Date, Duration 45 min, Parent's Signature (P2 p1) |
| Sections | A: MCQ (3 questions); B: Open-ended Questions (Q5-14); C: Problem Sums (Q15-17) (P1 p1, p4, p9) | A: 6 MCQs; B: 6 short open-ended; C: 3 multi-part problem questions (P2 p3, p7, p11) |
| Section instruction | One sentence, in bold, at the top of the section | Short block of 2-3 lines: how many questions are in the section ("There are 6 questions in this section."), "Read the questions carefully.", then how to answer (P2 p3, p7, p11) |
| Marks | No marks printed on the question pages | No marks printed; the teacher's grid lists skills with question numbers and "Getting there / Got it!" boxes instead (P2 p1-2) |
| Ending | "~ End of Paper ~" and setter's name (P1 p11) | "- End of paper -" (P2 p13) |
| Child's self-check | none | Self-reflection on page 2: "Doing my workings clearly and neatly", "Identifying key words/numbers in the questions", "Checking my work after I am done" (P2 p2) |

Take-away: three-section shape (MCQ, short answer, problem sums) is the norm, with the difficulty and the writing space rising from A to C. Skills are listed to parents in plain "Your child is able to: ..." language.

### 1.2 Section instructions (what a 6-8 year old reads before a section)

Paraphrased types:

- MCQ: "Choose the correct answer and write its number (1, 2, 3 or 4) in the brackets." The options are labelled (1) (2) (3) (4), never A B C D. A pair of brackets sits at the right-hand margin of the last option (P1 p1-3, P2 p3-6).
- Open-ended: "Fill in the correct answers in the spaces provided." (P1 p4) / "Write your answers in the boxes provided." (P2 p7)
- Problem sums: "Do these sums carefully. Show all your equations and workings clearly in the spaces provided." (P1 p9) / "Show all your workings clearly. Write your answers in the space provided." (P2 p11)
- A shared picture is introduced once: "Study the picture below carefully and answer Questions 5 to 7." (P1 p4)
- True/False table: "Each of the statements below is either true or false based on the picture. For each statement, put a tick in the correct box." with a Statement / True / False table (P1 p8).

**Not seen in these papers, so do not treat as verified:** "Write the number in numerals / in words", "What is the missing number?", "Fill in the blanks" as a stand-alone item heading, "Count and write", "Match", "Colour", "Draw". The P1 quiz uses the number sentence frames above instead of "What is the missing number?". Singapore papers commonly use those phrases, but they are not evidenced here **(not in these papers)**.

Patterns of verb use:

- The command is almost always **after** the data (picture or story first, then the question). Exception: the section instruction and "Circle ..." questions, which put the picture first and the command second.
- One command per sentence. No chains of imperatives in the question stem ("Draw, then colour, then write"). Multiple jobs are split into (a), (b).
- The same job uses the same words each time: "How many ... altogether?" / "in all" for totals; "How many more ... ?" for the gap; "each" for equal groups.
- Comparison words are **greater/greatest, larger/largest, more/less, longer** (P2 p4, p9, p11). The word "bigger" is not used.

### 1.4 Sentence length and structure

- Story sentences are short and in the simple present or simple past: "Ravi has 18 pencils. He packs 6 pencils in a bag. How many bags of pencils does Ravi have?" (type of P1 p3).
- A problem sum has 1 to 3 sentences before the question, 5 to 12 words each. Longest seen: 3 short story sentences plus a question (P1 p5 Q9; P2 p8 Q9).
- Numerals are used for all quantities in the sentence: "has 18 pencils", "4 ladybirds", "a cake is cut into 6 equal pieces". **Number words are not used for quantities** (the word "one", "two" appears only inside "1 whole", "2 flat surfaces" as numerals). "Equal", "each", "altogether", "in all", "left" are the key words.
- Names appear as the subject of the sentence, then "he/she" in the next sentence, then the name again in the question: "Li Wen has 20 lollipops. She shares her lollipops equally with Jim. How many lollipops did each of them get?" (type of P1 p11).
- Question stem repeats the noun: "How many bags of pencils does Ravi have?", not "How many does he have?" (P1 p3; P2 p8 "How many pieces of cake did Lily eat?").
- Picture-dependent items say "in the picture" / "below" / "above" / "shown below" and refer to figures as Figure A-D, Jug A-C, Box A-C (P1 p5, P2 p7, p10, p11).
- Everyday words used: "sweets", "cookies", "jars", "balloons", "doughnuts", "lollipops", "pencils", "erasers", "notebooks", "cashier", "change". The P1 answer key says "erasers" and "coloured" (British spelling), and the question pages say "sweets". Both "cookies" and "sweets" appear, so "biscuits" is not required.
- Riddle questions give 2-3 short clues in first person, then "What am I?" (P2 p6).
- Statements in tables are full sentences: "The animals have a total of 14 legs." (P1 p8).

### 1.5 Names, contexts, money

Names in the two papers: Mary, Ravi, Amelia, Jenny, Li Wen, Jim, Jason, Dewi, Ray, Lily, Jamie, Ali, Linus, Bala, Meiqi, Fatimah. Mixed Chinese, Malay, Indian and western names; usually first names only; boys and girls both appear as the main actor. A pair of children is introduced with a small drawing of the two (P1 p11).

Contexts: sharing sweets and lollipops, packing pencils into bags, candles on cakes, cookies in jars, balloons, balls in baskets, ladybird and animal legs, notebooks bought with money, juice poured into glasses, books read by children, a cake cut into pieces, walking to school. All are things a child has done. No fantasy, no named brands, no large amounts.

Money: only in P2: "buys 3 notebooks at $2 each", "gave the cashier $10", "How much change will he receive?". The frame is "The 3 notebooks cost $___." and "Ali will receive $___ as change." Dollars only, no cents (the syllabus line in P2 p1 says "money in dollars only"). The dollar sign comes before the blank.

### 1.6 Pictures, blanks and boxes

- A picture comes **after** the story text and **before** the answer line (P1 p3-5, p8-11).
- Pictures show the objects to be counted one by one (doughnuts, cakes, pencils, jars); groups are shown as separate pictures (baskets, bags, jars), so the "groups of" structure can be seen.
- An answer is a **box** in a sentence frame, with the noun after it: "There are [box] jars." "Each jar has [box] cookies." "She needs [box] more sweets." "Each of them got [box] lollipops." (P1 p4-5, p11). Blank lines (___) are used instead of boxes for words and short numbers with a unit: "The mass of Box B is ____ g." "The 3 notebooks cost $____." (P2 p7, p11-12).
- For a number sentence the layout is: [box] circle-for-sign [box] = [box] (P1 p5, p9-10). The sign is for the child to choose, drawn as a circle.
- In problem sums, the child gives **two** things: the number sentence (boxes), then the full-sentence answer with a box: "Jenny has [box] balls in all." (P1 p9-10).
- Section C of P2 splits the page into a wide left column (question, then answer line at the bottom) and a right-hand column headed "Working" (P2 p11-12). Part (a) and (b) are separated by a horizontal rule.
- Multi-part items label (a), (b) with a short lead-in sentence before them; part (b) often builds on (a) (P2 p11 mass, p12 money). Some stems give a shared statement then two questions: "Box A weighs 800 g. Box A weighs 500 g more than Box B." then (a) mass of B, (b) a new fact about Box C.
- Handwritten answer boxes are big and the frame is a complete sentence ending in a full stop.

### 1.7 Typical answer formats

- Number alone in a box when the noun is already printed after the box (jars, cookies, sweets).
- "Name has [box] things in all/altogether." (P1 p9).
- Number sentence first, then sentence answer (answer key: "5 x 5 = 25", "Jenny has 25 balls in all." P1 p11).
- Units after the blank: g, m, a.m., $ before. Time format 6:45 a.m. with the colon and a.m./p.m. (P2 p9). Analogue clock times offered as 2:00, 1:59, 2:59 in MCQ (P2 p5).
- Fractions are set vertically with a fraction bar; "1 whole" is written "1 whole".
- Names of 3D shapes are plain words: cone, cube, cylinder, sphere; "flat faces"/"flat surfaces" for faces (P2 p5-6).
- Answer key style: P1 key shows MCQ as a number, open-ended as a number or a sentence, and drawings as the circled picture (P1 p10-11).

### 1.8 Topics and number ranges tested

**P1 (quiz 4, multiplication and division):**
- Repeated addition as "[n] x 3"; "2 sevens", "7 groups of 2"; equal groups as multiplication (P1 p1-2, p4-5).
- Division as equal sharing and as grouping ("equal number on each cake", "packs 6 in a bag", "shares equally with Jim") (P1 p2-3, p11).
- Number range: products from 14 up to 36 ("9 groups of 4" answer), 24, 25, 20 shared into 2 groups of 10, 18 into 3 groups of 6. Factors 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9; no number above about 40 appears.
- Also: "How many more to make equal groups?" (4 sweets for each of 4 friends, some already packed), True/False about total legs and equal grouping.
- No money, no length, no place value, no tens and ones in this quiz.

**P2 (topical review 2):**
- Money and Measures: length in metres from a ruler strip (5 m), compare volumes (glasses to a jug), mass in grams with 3-digit numbers (800 g, 500 g, 100 g, "n more than"), money in dollars ($2 x 3, $10 minus $6 for change).
- Fractions: name the fraction of a shape (3/8 type), compare like fractions (1/9 to 7/9), two fractions that make 1 whole (2/7 and [ ]), subtract like fractions within 1 whole (4/6 left, pieces eaten), order unit fractions (1/10, 1/5, 1/7). Denominators 2 to 10.
- Time: tell time to the minute (clock), find the end time given the start and a duration in minutes (6:45 a.m. + 15 min).
- Shapes and solids: faces of a cube, name a solid from clues, 3D shapes (cone, cube, cylinder, sphere).
- Graphs: picture graph with a key ("Each picture stands for 4 books") and a total given, so the child finds the missing bar.
- Numbers: whole numbers in the problems stay below 1000; computations are 1-step to 2-step; 3-digit numbers appear only as mass in g.

## Part 2. Comparison with our Level 1 Term 1 wording

Scope: lessons l01 to l10 in `term/l1/lessons`, tiers start (S), think (T), go (G), and the exit tickets (E). Quoted wording is shortened where long. "Conventional" is how a Singapore school paper would normally put it, based on Part 1 (items marked **(not in these papers)** rest on general Singapore usage).

### 2.1 Systematic departures (found in many places)

1. **"bigger / biggest" for numbers.** Papers use *greater/greatest, larger/largest, smaller/smallest*. Ours: l03 S1c, S2, S3c, S4, S7, T1, T2, G1, E1, E2 say "bigger/biggest".
2. **"Write these numbers in order. Start with the smallest."** Conventional: "Arrange the numbers in order. Begin with the smallest." (P2 p9). Ours: l03 S5, E3.
3. **Error-spotting questions in each lesson ("Siti says X. Is she right? Say what she mixed up.").** School papers test this with a True/False tick table or "Who is correct?"; they do not ask a child to write a reason in the question stem for a 6-year-old. The reason request is not in these papers. Ours: l01 S6, l02 S8, l03 S6, l04 S6, l05 S7, l06 S6, l07 S6, l08 S3, l09 S6, l10 S6. These ten are the same template and the same name (Siti) in each.
4. **Command verbs borrowed from our tutoring picture, not from school language.** "Jump forward", "Jump back, or hop", "Take the tens first, then hop the ones", "Count up", "Make a ten first", "Open one bundle". Conventional on a paper: "Add." / "Subtract." / "Find the value of ...". The lesson metaphors are fine in lessons but the exit tickets and home practice should be able to read like a paper. Ours: l06 S3, S4, E1, E2, E3; l07 T1, T2, E2; l05 S4-S6, E1, E2.
5. **"Work out ..."** is UK; Singapore says "Find ..." / "Add/Subtract". Ours: l06 T1, T2; l07 G1.
6. **Tutor hints inside the question text** ("Hint: can she make another bundle?", "Hint: who has more ... ?"). Papers contain no hints in the stem. Ours: l01 T1; l10 T1. Move to the tutor hint field.
7. **A question and a second question in the same sentence.** "Who has more sticks? How many more?" and "Is Siti right? Write the number she should write, and say what she mixed up." Papers split into (a) and (b). Ours: l01 S6, G1; l02 S8; l03 S6; l05 S7; l06 S6; l07 S6, G1, G2, G3; l08 T1; l09 S6.
8. **Blank markers.** A school paper uses a **box** for numbers and a **short line** for words with a unit after it. Our `___` and `[]` mixed in the same question (l02 S2, S3, l08 S1 with `[]` and S5 with both `___` and `[]`). Make one rule: box (or `[]`) for a single number in a sentence or number sentence; `___` for a word or a number followed by a unit.
9. **Answer lines that start "The number is ___ " or "I am ___"**: fine, but papers more often give a complete sentence with the noun last ("Siti has ___ stickers left."). Ours have "___ pencils left" (l06 S5), "___ seats are empty" (l07 S7), "___ sticks" (l01 E1), "___ children" (l10 E1), "___ marbles" (l10 E3). Conventional: a full sentence, "There are ___ children on the bus now.", with the noun after the blank.
10. **Missing "in all" / "altogether" / "left" wording is mostly good** (l01 S5, l10 S5, l10 T2), but "How many more beads does Mei have?" (l07 T1) is right; "Now how many more stickers does Ben have than Ali?" (l07 T3) should lose "Now" and say "How many more stickers does Ben have than Ali now?".
11. **Word problems missing a picture.** A P1 paper pairs almost every story with a picture of the objects (P1 p3-5, p9-11). Our l10 and l07 stories (S7, T3, T4) are text only for ages 6-7.
12. **No money, no $ blanks anywhere in Term 1** even though P2 uses $ and change heavily. Not a wording error, a coverage gap (see 2.3).

| l08 T2 | "Write SAME if the totals match. If not, write LEFT or RIGHT for the side with the bigger total." | Three options, long. "Circle the greater total, or write =" . Conventional: "Write >, < or = in the circle." (comparison circle) **(not in these papers)** |
| l08 T4 | "Is it balanced? If not, change the 19 to a number that makes it balance." | Two conditions. Ask: "Fill in the missing number: 24 + 17 = 26 + [box]". |
| l08 G1 | "Fill in both blanks so that every sum is the same." | "Fill in the missing numbers. 19 + 28 = 20 + [box] = [box] + 30" |
| l08 G2 | "Two blanks. Write three different pairs ..." | "Fill in the boxes in three different ways." and ask the pattern as a separate part. |
| l09 S1 | "Draw a line 7 cm long in the box. Put the 0 mark of your ruler at the start of the line." | "Draw a line 7 cm long." The ruler instruction belongs in the tutor line. Answer frame "My line starts at the ___ mark" is not paper style; use "The line is [box] cm long." |
| l09 S3 | "Measure your pencil. First write your guess." | "Measure the length of your pencil." Papers measure a picture of an object next to a ruler or unit strip ("What is the length of the sofa?" P2 p3). |
| l09 S6 | "She puts the 0 mark at the start and draws to the 7 mark. She says: my line is 8 cm. Is she right?" | "Mei draws a line from 0 to the 7 mark. She says her line is 8 cm long. Is she correct? Circle Yes or No." |
| l09 S7 / T4 / E-level | "To the nearest cm" | "To the nearest cm" measuring is above P2 (papers use whole m or whole units from a scale picture). |
| l09 T2 | "A line starts at the 2 mark of a ruler and ends at the 9 mark." | Starting at a mark other than 0 is a standard way to test length, but papers draw it. Use a picture of the ruler. |
| l09 E2 | "Siti's eraser ends at the 6 mark" | **eraser**: guide rule 3 says say "rubber". Note P1 and P2 papers use both "eraser" in the P1 answer key (P1 p10), so both are used in Singapore; keep the guide's choice for consistency but note "eraser" is common. |
| l10 S1 | "Read it twice. Ben has 30 cards. He gives away 20. Then he gets 6 more." | "Read it twice" is not paper wording; "Ben has 30 cards. He gives away 20 cards. Then he gets 6 more cards. How many cards does Ben have now?" |
| l10 S2 | "Write a number sentence for each step." | "Write a number sentence for each step" appears in P1-type "Write a multiplication sentence ..." phrasing; fine, but add boxes: [box] + [box] = [box]. |
| l10 S3 | "Circle the two number sentences Ravi needs." | Papers circle one answer from options; "Circle the correct number sentences" is OK. |
| l10 S4 | "Story words tell us what to do. Write + for add or - for take away." | Not paper style (a skill drill). Use "Circle + or -." |
| l10 S5 | "Ben has 10 more marbles than Ali." | Comparison by "more than" is normal (P2 p11: "weighs 500 g more than"). Good. Give a picture. |
| l10 S6 | "What did Siti do wrong?" | Open-ended explanation. Replace with "Is Siti correct? Circle Yes or No." and "Find the number of children on the bus now." |
| l10 G1-G3 | 3 to 4 story steps | P2 problem sums have 2 steps. G1 (60 - 24 - 15 + 30) is 3 steps, above paper level. |
| l10 E3 | "Ali has 7 fewer marbles than Mei." | "fewer ... than" is used in school papers; "7 fewer marbles than" is fine; give a picture or the order "Mei has 20 marbles. Ali has 7 fewer marbles than Mei." (known first, then comparison) |

### 2.3 Topics and number ranges: where our Term 1 is above or below what the papers test

Remember: the two papers are one P1 quiz on multiplication/division and one P2 revision on measures, fractions, time, solids and graphs. They do not test place value, number lines, 100-square patterns or two-digit addition/subtraction at all, so for most of our term there is **no paper evidence either way**; the comparison below is limited to what the papers show.

**Above what the papers test**
- **3-digit numbers**: l03 G1, G3 ("357", cards 4-0-9, 2-5-8) are the only place where we go beyond 100 in numbers; the P2 paper uses 3-digit numbers only for mass in grams (800 g, 500 g) with simple +/- 100s. Fine for P2 but above P1.
- **Rounding / nearest ten and nearest cm**: l02 S7-T1 ("Which ten is nearer?"), l09 S7, T4. Neither paper tests nearest-anything; P2 measures lengths exactly from a unit strip.
- **Subtraction with renaming and counting up across a ten**: l07 T2 (83 - 77), T1 (62 - 38), G3 (83 - 47) need renaming; neither paper shows 2-digit subtraction at all (P2 subtraction is 6 - 4, $10 - $6, 800 - 500, which are within 10 or whole hundreds). P1's quiz has no subtraction.
- **Multi-step word problems**: l10 G1 (3 steps), l10 T3 (3 quantities), l09 G1. The P2 problem sums are 2 steps (a then b), and P1 problem sums are 1 step.
- **Sum chains and algebra-like items**: l04 G1, G3 (Fibonacci-style, block sums), l05 G1-G3 (6-number sums to 100, 1+...+9), l08 G1-G2 (double blanks). These are enrichment (as intended), not school level.
- **Explanation requests ("Say why", "Show how you know")** appear in nearly every lesson; neither paper asks a child to explain in words. Papers test reasoning by True/False, "Is X correct?", or "Which ... does not ...?".

**Below / not covered, compared with the papers**
- **Multiplication and division** (equal groups, "groups of", sharing equally, "each"): the whole of the P1 quiz; absent from our Term 1.
- **Money in dollars ($, change)**, **mass in g**, **length in m**, **time (clock to the minute, duration)**, **fractions of a whole**, **3D shapes (faces)**, **picture graphs with a key**: all in P2; none in our Term 1. (L9 covers cm only.)
- **Number range**: the papers' numbers are small (P1 quiz up to about 36, P2 whole numbers up to 800 for mass); our term works up to 100, with 3-digit numbers only in l03 G. This is not a mismatch for tutoring but means exit tickets with numbers up to 99 are more demanding than the P1 quiz, which stays below 40.
- **MCQ and True/False formats**: not used in our Term 1; the papers use MCQ with (1)-(4) options for a third to a half of the paper. If we want children to meet exam formats, add a few MCQ-style items to home practice or the end-of-term check.

### 2.4 What we already do well

- Frames with the noun after the blank (l01 S1, l09 S4, l07 T1) match "Each jar has [box] cookies."
- "How many more ... ?" for a gap, "left", "in all", "altogether" are right and consistent.
- Names Ali, Mei, Siti, Ben, Ravi are plausible; add Jenny, Lily, Jamie, Ray, Amelia for variety.
- Short imperative sentences ("Count the sticks.", "Fill in the tens and ones.") are the same shape as the papers' section instructions.
- Units written after the blank (cm) match P2.
