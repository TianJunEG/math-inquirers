# Primary 2 non-routine question catalogue (from school test papers)

Status: analysis written 23 Oct 2026 from the owner's Drive folder "P2 Math". **No school question text is reproduced here.** The verbatim questions are in a private, gitignored file for the owner's own study (`docs/books/private_p2_papers/non_routine_questions.md`). Every example below is an original question written for this project and checked by a script (section 5). Nothing here is a claim about what any child will meet in any exam.

## 1. What was read, and what was not

- The folder holds 123 PDF entries and 3 zip files. After removing copies, **95 distinct PDFs were read in full** (OCR text; page images checked where the picture decided the answer). They hold **about 138 separate tests, quizzes and worksheets from 18 schools, dated 2018 to 2025**. 2019 is over-represented (62 of the tests) because several schools' 2019 packs are in the folder.
- About 28 files are copies of papers already read ("PastYr_" copies, re-labelled papers, and the Red Swastika, Rosyth and ACS (Junior) reviews that repeat across 2019 to 2021); they were not read twice.
- **Four PDFs had no usable text layer** and were rendered to images at about 45 dpi and read from the pictures (Henry Park 2019 quizzes, ACS (Junior) 2019 revision, Rosyth 2019 reviews, CHIJ St Nicholas 2019 revision). Small print and answer keys in these were not all legible.
- **Not readable:** the 2019 zip (58.9 MB) is over the Drive download limit; the 2021 zip (7 MB) crashed the download. The 2020 zip was opened and holds only the six loose 2020 papers. If the 2019 and 2021 zips hold papers that are not also loose in the folder, they are not covered.
- Coverage of schools is uneven: Henry Park (85 items), ACS (69), Tao Nan (57), Red Swastika (56), Raffles Girls' (49), CHIJ St Nicholas (36), Rosyth (35) and Pei Chun (35) supply most of the items. Counts describe this set of papers, not the whole country. About a fifth of the items print no marks, and several answer keys were unreadable, so marks are partial.

## 2. What counts as non-routine here

Beyond a one-step recall or computation: multi-step stories; a heuristic (draw a model, guess and check, work backwards, find a pattern, list every case, table, compare by difference or sum, make equal, how many ways); reasoning (true or false, explain, which does not belong); an unfamiliar context (hidden facts, symbols, timetables, the sharer counted); or a build-it-from-clues item. A plain one-step sum, a routine read-off from a graph and a simple "what comes next" were left out unless they carry a trap.

## 3. The types: what they look like, where they appear, how to use them

### CTX: Unfamiliar context, hidden fact, sharer counted

- **Count:** 107 questions in 59 tests from 16 schools.
- **Where found:** Maha Bodhi 13, Henry Park 13, Red Swastika 13, Tao Nan 12, SCGS 9. Years (records): 2019 (69), 2022 (9), 2023 (8), 2025 (7), 2024 (4).
- **What it is:** Money word problems (26), time (22) and division stories where the sharer counts (12) are the biggest groups; also length with hidden facts and unit prices ("4 for $3", "5 for $2").
- **What makes it non-routine:** A hidden fact, a unit price, a calendar or the sharer-counted reading decides the answer. A one-word misreading of "with her 3 cousins" changes the answer.
- **Typical number ranges:** Money to $100 (to $1000 in two-step); 5-minute clock readings; tables 2, 3, 4, 5, 10; weeks, days, dozen.
- **Typical wording (short fragments):** "shares equally with ..."; "5 for $2"; "How many more must she buy"; "most number of ..."; "Cashier"; "change".
- **Where it belongs for us:** Think harder in lessons 3, 15, 19, 23, 24, 28, 33, 35; Go further in lessons 1, 5, 10.

Original examples (written for this project; answers checked by script):

| ID | Tier | Lessons | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EX-CTX-1 | Think harder | 3, 33 | Mei shares 24 stickers equally with her 3 cousins. How many stickers does each person get? | 6 (there are 4 people). |
| EX-CTX-2 | Think harder | 5, 28 | 4 pears cost $3. Jenny has $15. How many pears can she buy? | 20 pears (5 lots of $3). |
| EX-CTX-3 | Go further | 35 | Wei saves $3 each day, starting on Monday. On which day does he first have $18? | Saturday (6 days of $3). |

### CMP: Comparison (difference or sum, more/fewer than, reversal traps)

- **Count:** 97 questions in 62 tests from 16 schools.
- **Where found:** Tao Nan 16, Henry Park 11, ACS (Primary) 11, Rosyth 10, Raffles Girls' 8. Years (records): 2019 (52), 2020 (13), 2023 (7), 2025 (6), 2021 (4).
- **What it is:** Two or three quantities compared by how many more or fewer, or a total and a difference together. Most are addition and subtraction stories (45), then length, mass and volume. The reversal trap ("49 more than Jane", find Jane) is the commonest single form, about 30 items.
- **What makes it non-routine:** The sentence names the known quantity in a way that reverses the operation, or the question asks for both quantities from a sum and a difference. It is the most frequent Section C item at 3 marks.
- **Typical number ranges:** 3-digit numbers (346, 540, 120 more); money to $100; lengths to 233 m or 487 m; 2 or 3 steps.
- **Typical wording (short fragments):** "... more than ..."; "... fewer than ..."; "altogether"; "how many more"; "3 times as many".
- **Where it belongs for us:** Think harder in lessons 8, 9, 13, 14, 27, 38; Go further in lessons 14, 15, 22, 24, 28, 35.

Original examples (written for this project; answers checked by script):

| ID | Tier | Lessons | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EX-CMP-1 | Think harder | 9, 13 | Mei has 346 stickers. She has 58 more than Ali. How many stickers does Ali have? | 288 (Ali has fewer, so take away: 346 - 58). |
| EX-CMP-2 | Go further | 14, 28 | A book costs $12. A ruler costs $7 more than the book. A pen costs $4 less than the ruler. How much does the pen cost? | $15 (ruler $19, then 19 - 4). |
| EX-CMP-3 | Go further | 8, 14, 27 | Ali and Siti have 540 stickers altogether. Ali has 120 more than Siti. How many stickers does each child have? | Siti 210 and Ali 330. |

### BACK: Work backwards (missing start or missing part)

- **Count:** 74 questions in 54 tests from 14 schools.
- **Where found:** Henry Park 13, Pei Chun 8, Red Swastika 7, Rosyth 6, Raffles Girls' 6. Years (records): 2019 (40), 2025 (6), 2024 (5), 2021 (4), 2018 (3).
- **What it is:** The end result is known and the start or a missing part must be found: "at first ... after selling ... left", paid and change given, a time that ended with a duration, a missing digit.
- **What makes it non-routine:** The direction of the story is opposite to the direction of the arithmetic. About a third are addition and subtraction; the rest sit in multiplication, money and time.
- **Typical number ranges:** Within 1000; money to $100; times to the minute from 2022; a missing digit in a 3-digit column sum.
- **Typical wording (short fragments):** "at first"; "after ... was left"; "gave him $20 and got $5 back"; "ended at ... How long"; "Hint: Working backwards".
- **Where it belongs for us:** Think harder in lessons 2, 5, 9, 13, 15, 19, 30, 35; Go further in lessons 28, 37.

Original examples (written for this project; answers checked by script):

| ID | Tier | Lessons | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EX-BACK-1 | Think harder | 2, 9, 13, 19, 28, 30, 37 | Ravi had some marbles. He gave away 60, then won 148. Now he has 480. How many marbles did he have at first? | 392 (undo the win: 480 - 148 = 332; undo the gift: 332 + 60). |
| EX-BACK-2 | Think harder | 5 | Aisha bought 4 notebooks the same price. She paid with a $50 note and got $18 change. What does one notebook cost? | $8 (she spent $32; 32 / 4). |
| EX-BACK-3 | Think harder | 15, 35 | A film ended at 4:15 pm. It lasted 1 h 40 min. What time did it start? | 2:35 pm (draw a timeline: back 1 h to 3:15 pm, then back 40 min). |

### MULTI: Multi-step problems

- **Count:** 58 questions in 41 tests from 14 schools.
- **Where found:** Henry Park 17, Tao Nan 6, Catholic High 5, Pei Chun 5, CHIJ St Nicholas 4. Years (records): 2019 (35), 2023 (5), 2024 (5), 2025 (4), 2018 (3).
- **What it is:** Two- and three-step stories in multiplication, division, volume and money. Henry Park supplies 17 of the 58.
- **What makes it non-routine:** The child must decide the order of steps; many items have no single hidden fact.
- **Typical number ranges:** Tables 2, 3, 4, 5, 10; totals to 100; litres to 20.
- **Typical wording (short fragments):** "such baskets"; "in all"; "The rest"; "left".
- **Where it belongs for us:** Think harder in lessons 9, 20, 24, 27, 28, 40; Go further in lessons 3, 23, 38.

### LOG: Logical reasoning (who is where, ordering clues, number clues)

- **Count:** 45 questions in 39 tests from 15 schools.
- **Where found:** Henry Park 6, Red Swastika 6, CHIJ St Nicholas 5, Raffles Girls' 5, Nanyang 4. Years (records): 2019 (31), 2018 (5), 2024 (3), 2022 (2).
- **What it is:** Mostly number clues (21 of 45 are numbers to 1000: even or odd, between, twice the, 3 more than), then ordering by weight or length (10 mass items) and reading a graph. Seldom the Primary 1 style of a line of children.
- **What makes it non-routine:** The answer comes from fitting several facts, not from computing. A child who rushes to add or subtract fails; many items are only solvable from the picture.
- **Typical number ranges:** Numbers 100 to 1000; three to five objects to order; 3-digit clue numbers with two or three conditions.
- **Typical wording (short fragments):** number clues: "I am a 3-digit number", "tens digit is twice the", "between ... and ..."; "heavier than", "lighter than".
- **Where it belongs for us:** Think harder in lessons 1, 4, 10, 12, 18, 25, 29, 34, 36, 39; Go further in lessons 18, 30.

| EX-LOG-2 | Think harder | 18 | Box P is heavier than box Q. Box R is heavier than box P. Box S is lighter than box Q. Write the boxes from heaviest to lightest. | R, P, Q, S. |

### LIST: Systematic list or choose numbers to fit

- **Count:** 37 questions in 31 tests from 13 schools.
- **Where found:** Raffles Girls' 8, Henry Park 6, Methodist Girls' 4, Red Swastika 4, Nanyang 2. Years (records): 2019 (22), 2024 (5), 2023 (2), 2018 (2).
- **What it is:** Digit cards (make the greatest, smallest or every number; zero not first), two numbers from a list that add to a target, numbers that fit two conditions, and three like fractions that make 1 whole.
- **What makes it non-routine:** The child must find all the cases or the right pair, and know when to stop. Digit-card items are about 20 of the 37.
- **Typical number ranges:** Three digit cards with a zero; targets 100, 500, 1000; fractions with denominators up to 12.
- **Typical wording (short fragments):** "Each card can be used once only"; "Form the smallest"; "Circle two numbers that add up to"; "Circle three fractions that add up to 1 whole".
- **Where it belongs for us:** Think harder in lessons 1, 6, 17, 22, 34; Go further in lessons 2, 4, 7, 25, 33.

| EX-LIST-2 | Think harder | 2, 17 | Circle the two numbers that add up to 1000: 258, 365, 635, 740, 480. | 365 and 635 (no other pair works). |

### DIAG: Draw a model or diagram

- **Count:** 35 questions in 28 tests from 11 schools.
- **Where found:** Raffles Girls' 6, Red Swastika 6, Henry Park 4, CHIJ St Nicholas 3, Pei Chun 3. Years (records): 2019 (22), 2024 (3), 2021 (2), 2022 (2).
- **What it is:** Balances and scales (16 of 35 are mass), ruler and post-and-gap items, and "fill in the blanks in the model" bars.
- **What makes it non-routine:** The picture is the problem. A balance with 3 melons equal to a weight, or posts along a line, can be solved by drawing.
- **Typical number ranges:** Masses to 10 kg; lengths to 100 m; gaps 3 m or 5 m.
- **Typical wording (short fragments):** "balance"; "heavier than"; "Fill in the blanks in the models"; "You may draw pictures to help you".
- **Where it belongs for us:** Think harder in lessons 7, 8, 10, 14, 27; Go further in lessons 8, 26, 39.

### EXP: Explain, true or false, odd one out

- **Count:** 29 questions in 27 tests from 11 schools.
- **Where found:** Henry Park 8, Maha Bodhi 5, CHIJ St Nicholas 3, ACS (Junior) 2, Red Swastika 2. Years (records): 2019 (14), 2025 (4), 2022 (3), 2023 (3).
- **What it is:** True or false statements: equal groups pictures ("which does not describe the pictures"), read from a graph, statements about even and odd, 8 x 3 and 3 x 8.
- **What makes it non-routine:** The child must test each statement against the picture or against a number, not compute a single answer. Henry Park uses an equal-groups true/false table every year.
- **Typical number ranges:** Tables 2, 3, 4, 5, 10; amounts to 100; graphs with keys 2 to 10.
- **Typical wording (short fragments):** "Based on the pictures, each statement is True or False"; "Which does not describe"; "Put a tick".
- **Where it belongs for us:** Think harder in lessons 3, 6, 11, 12, 16, 22, 26, 29, 31, 32, 37; Go further in lessons 13, 18, 29.

| EX-EXP-2 | Think harder | 12, 16, 29, 32, 37 | Is 8 x 3 the same as 3 x 8? Is 8 x 3 the same as 8 + 3? Say how you know. | Yes (both are 24: 8 threes and 3 eights). No (24 and 11). |

### GC: Guess and check (coins, pairs to a total)

- **Count:** 22 questions in 20 tests from 11 schools.
- **Where found:** Rosyth 5, CHIJ St Nicholas 3, Nanyang 2, Methodist Girls' 2, Pei Chun 2. Years (records): 2019 (10), 2025 (3), 2018, 2020, 2021, 2024 (2 each).
- **What it is:** Money is the main place (11 of 22): two prices from a list for an exact total, coins or notes for an exact amount, and "how many of each".
- **What makes it non-routine:** The child must try and adjust; a systematic table helps. Often exactly one or two answers exist.
- **Typical number ranges:** Totals to $100; coins 5¢, 10¢, 20¢, 50¢, $1; notes $2, $5, $10; 6 to 12 coins.
- **Typical wording (short fragments):** "exactly"; "which two"; "how many of each"; "Hint: Make a table".
- **Where it belongs for us:** Think harder in lessons 5, 40; Go further in lessons 20, 32.

### PAT: Look for a pattern

- **Count:** 17 questions in 14 tests from 10 schools.
- **Where found:** Pei Chun 3, Henry Park 2, ACS (Primary) 2, Raffles Girls' 2, Red Swastika 2. Years (records): 2019 (11), 2018, 2020, 2022 to 2024 (1 each).
- **What it is:** Growing matchstick or shape patterns, repeating cycles (which shape is the nth) and number sequences with a two-step rule.
- **What makes it non-routine:** The child must predict beyond the picture, not copy it. Papers ask for the next figure, the 5th or 8th term, or a cycle position.
- **Typical number ranges:** Terms to the 5th or 8th; cycles of 3 or 4; steps of 2, 3, 5, 10, 100 and an alternating +100, -20.
- **Typical wording (short fragments):** "Complete the pattern"; "How many sticks are needed"; "one of the shapes is wrong".
- **Where it belongs for us:** Think harder in lessons 4, 21, 26, 30, 36, 39; Go further in lessons 16, 21.

### EQ: Make equal (transfer)

- **Count:** 13 questions in 12 tests from 7 schools.
- **Where found:** Henry Park 4, Tao Nan 3, Red Swastika 2, Rulang 1, SCGS 1. Years (records): 2019 (4), 2020 (2), 2023 (2), 2021 to 2025 (1 each).
- **What it is:** A transfer so that two amounts are equal, a missing number in an equation with a rewritten side, and fraction shading ("how many more parts must be shaded").
- **What makes it non-routine:** The child must think of the difference and halve it, or rewrite one side. Half are graph items (5 of 13).
- **Typical number ranges:** Amounts to 100; equations in 3-digit numbers; denominators 8 to 12.
- **Typical wording (short fragments):** "so that they have the same"; "How many more parts must be shaded"; "give ... to ...".
- **Where it belongs for us:** Think harder in lessons 2, 12, 19, 22, 23, 33; Go further in lessons 6, 14.

### WAYS: How many ways

## 5. How the examples were made and checked

- All 39 examples are original: new contexts, names from our cast and new numbers. Each is the same kind of question as a school item, not a rewording of one. Sentences are short. Money uses the cent sign (50¢) and the decimal form where the lesson teaches it; no $20 note question.
- A script (private, gitignored: `docs/books/private_p2_papers/verify_catalogue_examples.py`; run with `python3 -I`) re-computes every answer by brute force: it tries all orders for the weights, all digit orders for the cards, all pairs and triples for the list items, all coin counts, all sticks for the pattern, all routes for the grid, and it checks that the stated number of answers is exactly right (for example, only one pair makes 1000, two ways to pay $14, and no whole numbers for the puzzle in EX-CHAL-2). All 42 checks passed on 23 Oct 2026.
- Before an item goes into a lesson, the author must write the lesson's own `verify/` check, as for all Level 1 items; the script here does not replace it.
- Tier labels are neutral (Think harder, Go further). Nothing here implies a result, a rank or a promise for any child.
