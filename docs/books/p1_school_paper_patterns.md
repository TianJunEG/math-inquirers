## 1. Headline findings that change the pre-flight defaults

1. **Cents are written with the cent sign, not "c".** Every paper that has cents writes 5¢, 10¢, 20¢, 50¢, 25¢, 70¢, 95¢, with the sign after the number, and also puts the sign after the answer blank. Money in words is "sixty cents" or "forty cents". Not one paper writes "50c". No paper writes $1.50 or $0.50. (Pre-flight section 0.1 says "50c".)
2. **Time words: only "o'clock" and "half past" appear.** No paper in the sample says "quarter past", "quarter to", "ten past", "twenty to". Times to 5 minutes appear only as digits (6:15, 4:35, 8:35, 12:15, 11:15, 2:30), with am or pm, in Review 3 and Term 4 papers. (Pre-flight D5 plans spoken forms such as "ten past four" and "quarter to".)
3. **am and pm are written without full stops** in the recent papers ("2:00pm", "8:35 pm"); only the Rosyth P2 paper in the older notes used "a.m.". Colon is the norm (6:45); one paper uses a full stop (3.30 pm). (Pre-flight says "a.m. and p.m.")
4. **Half-hour and one-hour durations, and finding the start, are school-level P1 work.** Three schools test "lasts for ___" with clocks one hour apart, "played for half an hour, stopped at 6:00, when did it start?", and "show ended, next started immediately". Our L23 note that finding a start is "P3" is too high for the half-hour and one-hour case.
5. **"Fewest coins and notes" is an actual P1 paper item** (Maris Stella, Term 4: smallest number of coins and notes for a stated amount). L24's "Fewest Notes and Coins" is school-level in wording, though the amounts there ($17, $43) are smaller than our $73.
6. **Picture graphs: always 1 picture = 1, always read or complete, never collect or tally.** About a third of the Term 4 papers carry two to seven graph questions. No scale of 2 appears. "Complete the graph from two clues" (a difference and a total) does appear at P1 (Ai Tong, Term 4).
7. **No evidence at all in this sample for L27 (grids), L30 (calendar and weekdays), L31 to L35 (half and quarter, build figures, grid copy, cube views, spot the difference) or L36 (counting everything).** The shape items found are Term 1 and 2 only (name, count, sort, pattern). See section 6.
8. **Two Term 3 and 4 lessons have direct paper twins:** L28 (two-row ratio table and "6th day" table, from ACS process-skills items) and L26 ("which 2 items did he buy" appears in four papers).
9. **Sharing wording trap in the papers:** "shares them equally with his 3 friends" means 4 people share (the sharer included); "shares with Betty and Carol" means 3. Our Term 2 and any Term 3 or 4 sharing text must pick one convention and say who shares.

## 2. Time (clocks, o'clock, half past, durations)

### 2.1 Where it appears

| Slot | Papers (examples) | What is asked |
|---|---|---|
| Term 1 and 2 (Jan to Jun) | none of the papers read | no time item seen before August |
| Term 3 (Jul to Sep) | Tao Nan Review 2, Aug 2019 | read a clock (one item); tick the clock for "half past 2"; evening event "at half past 6" |
| Term 4 (Oct to Nov), hour and half hour | River Valley Mini Test 5 (30 Oct 2018), Ai Tong Term 4 Mini Test 1, SCGS Test 6, Catholic High Checkpoint 6, Maha Bodhi Topical Review 4 (2020) | MCQ and fill-in, o'clock and half past only; compare three clocks (earliest, latest, who first) |
| Term 4, to 5 minutes and durations | Maha Bodhi Review 3 (2022, 2024), Tao Nan Review 3 (21 Oct 2022), Methodist Girls' topical (2021) | digital time with am or pm, draw the hand, duration of 1 hour or half hour, timetable and show-time lists |

Weight: one to three items of 1 to 2 marks in a 15 to 20 item paper (about 10 percent). Time items are Term 4 content in this sample; earlier terms hold only an odd half-past item.

### 2.2 Task patterns

- **Read the clock, write the time.** The stem names the person and event first ("The clock shows the time Jane has her piano lesson"), then the answer line reads "The time is ___" or "It is ___". Short stem form: "Write the time shown on the clock."
- **Hour and half hour as words (Term 4 of the year, 2018 and 2019 papers).** Four-option MCQ with two o'clock and two half past options, and a distractor family: same hour with the other form (8 o'clock / half past 8), and the opposite number (12 o'clock / half past 12). Fill-in sentences give a choice list ("o'clock or half past") or ask the child to write the whole phrase.
- **Choose the clock.** A word time is given ("It is half past 2"), the child ticks one of three or four clock pictures. Distractors seen from the text: hands swapped (the same two numbers), hour hand on the wrong side of the number. (Picture detail inferred.)
- **Draw the hand.** "Draw the missing minute hand" or "Using a ruler, draw the hour hand" for a given time (11 o'clock, 7 o'clock, 4:35). A faint clock face with one hand given.
- **Digital time with am or pm.** "Fill in the blank with am or pm" after a daily event and a time (a run at 8:35 in the evening, dinner at 6:15, breakfast at 7:00). Answer by writing or ticking.
- **Order three clocks.** Three children, three clocks, "Who went to bed first?" or "gets up the earliest" or "reached home the latest". The words earliest, latest, first, before are the whole task.
- **Duration, one hour or half hour.** Start clock and end clock a whole hour or half hour apart, "lasts for ___" with the unit written in the answer (the key says 1 hour; one key writes 1h). Or digital: start and finish given as times, find the length.
- **Find the end or find the start.** "Played for half an hour. He stopped at 6:00 pm. What time did he start?" and "started at 1:30, watched half an hour, the next show started immediately, what time?" The second is a table or schedule with one blank time.
- **Schedule and timetable lists.** A list of times and events (zoo shows, TV programmes, a party, a daily timetable). Questions: which event is on at a given in-between time; which lasted half an hour; which lasted one hour. In the timetable item, distractor rows last 30 minutes and 75 minutes while the target lasts one hour.

### 2.3 Wording and format

- Stem verbs: "Write", "Tick", "Circle", "Fill in the blank with", "Draw". Question words: "What time ...?", "How long ...?", "Who ... first?"
- Phrases seen (all short): "The time is", "It is half past 2", "lasts for", "half an hour", "o'clock or half past".
- Times written 6:15 (colon), 4:35, 12:15, 2:30; "o'clock" for hours; "am" and "pm" in the same line as the time. No leading zero. No seconds.
- Numbers: hours 1 to 12; 5-minute marks only in Term 4; durations: 30 minutes and 1 hour in the clocks; the timetable distractors reach 75 minutes. No duration across noon appears. No 24-hour clock.
- Picture: standard analogue face with 1 to 12 printed, short fat hour hand, long minute hand. Items give person plus activity pictures ("what the students do in school").
- Marks: 1 to 2 per item.

### 2.5 Recommended change to the pre-flight defaults (time)

- **D5 amended:** child pages say "o'clock" and "half past" in words, and every time is also shown as digits (7:00, 7:30). From L22, times to 5 minutes are read and written as digits (4:10, 8:35), not as "ten past". "Quarter past" and "quarter to" move to a Think harder tutor note, not core wording; "twenty to" and "ten to" leave the child pages.

- Answer lines to copy: "The time is ___", a unit written after the blank for durations ("1 hour"), and draw-the-hand items on a faint blank face.
- Drawing items should say "Use a ruler" for hands.

## 3. Money (coins, notes, change, buying)

### 3.1 Where it appears

Almost all Term 4 and late Term 3 papers: Tao Nan Review 3 (21 Oct 2019, four items), River Valley Mini Test 5 (about 6 of 18 items), Ai Tong Term 4 Mini Test 2 (6 of 15 items, the heaviest), Maris Stella Mini Test 5 (about 6 of 20), Catholic High Checkpoint 6, SCGS Test 6, Nan Hua Term 4 Test 2, Pei Chun Mini Test 4. Money is the largest of the four topics in Term 4 papers: about 15 to 30 percent of marks. Earlier terms have none.

### 3.2 Notation (this is the finding that changes D2)

| Form | Seen in papers | Our pre-flight default |
|---|---|---|
| 50¢ (number then cent sign) | yes, in pictures, options, answers, blanks | "50c" (change) |
| "fifty-cent coin", "sixty cents", "40 cents" (words) | yes in stems and MCQ | "50 cents" ok |
| $5, $12, $50 (dollar sign before the number) | yes everywhere | same |
| Answer blank with unit before: "$ ___" | yes ("Huda has $"; "She needs $ ___ more") | same |
| Answer blank with unit after: "___ ¢" | yes for cent answers | fits if we use ¢ |
| $1.50, $0.50, $0.20 | not seen in any P1 paper | not used |
| Mixed dollars and cents in one amount (e.g. $1 20¢) | not seen | not used |
| $1 coin and cents in one story | seen twice: "gave a one-dollar coin, got back 20¢" (answer in cents); "Paul has $1, buys for 40 cents, cents left" | allowed only as a $1 = 100¢ conversion story, answer in cents |

- Denominations in pictures: coins 5¢, 10¢, 20¢, 50¢, $1; notes $2, $5, $10, $50, and $100 appears in two Term 4 stories (paid with $100, got $25 change; cost of a bag). No $20 note appears (the $17 key uses $10, $10, $2, $2, $2, $1). This confirms D3.
- Coin and note names in words: "twenty-cent", "fifty-cent coin", "one-dollar coin", "two-dollar note", "fifty-dollar note", "five-dollar notes". Written hyphenated.
- Numbers: prices $1 to $54, one item to $100 with change; cents 5¢ to 95¢; sums up to 100¢. Counting a set shows at most about 8 to 9 pieces mixed.

### 3.3 Task patterns

- **Count the money.** A picture row of coins or notes, "Count the total amount of money", answer "$ ___" or "___¢", or MCQ with four amounts. Mixed notes with $10, $5, $2, $1 (28, 33 to 38, 22 to 32).
- **Colour the correct amount.** A fixed row of notes or coins larger than needed; the item has a price; the child colours the pieces that make exactly the price ($59 uses five $10, one $5, two $2). Also "Colour 4 coins to show $1" from a row. Distractors: using a different number of pieces, or a piece that is too large.
- **Circle the coins to exchange.** A coin or note on the left, a row of coins on the right, "Circle the coins needed to exchange the amount". Equivalent forms: "A one-dollar coin can be exchanged for ... coins" MCQ (options use five-cent, ten-cent, fifty-cent, twenty-cent); "1 fifty-cent coin = ___ five-cent coins"; "A fifty-dollar note = 10 five-dollar notes". Distractor: right coin type, wrong number.
- **Fewest pieces.** "Circle the smallest number of coins and notes needed to show the amount." Answer key uses only the pictured pieces.
- **Compare sets.** Sets A, B, C pictured with coins and notes. "Which set has the least amount of money?" and "Set A has $___ more than Set C."
- **Price tags and buying.**
  - total of two items ($11 and $15 gives $26);
  - total with a choice of three ("which 2 items did he buy" for exactly $28, $18, 95¢ or $40) (appears in at least five papers);
  - "How much change?" from $5, $10, $20, $50;
  - "He paid $50 and got $12 back; how much did it cost?" (work back);
  - "Sally used $27 and had $48 left; how much at first?";
  - "How much more money does he need?" ($5 vs $3; $30 vs $40);
  - "Which is the most expensive?", "costs the least", "costs $___ more than the ball";
  - "Which toy can he buy with two five-dollar notes?";
  - one item whose price is found from the money given and change ("pays $12, gets back $1");
  - multi-step: a total, then change from a larger note.
- **Match the picture to its price** (two items, two prices) and **fill the table** of money spent, left and had.
- Recurring structure: the pictured price list comes first ("items for sale in a toy shop"), followed by one to three questions that share it.

### 3.4 Wording

- Stems are 1 to 3 short sentences: "Ravi wants to buy a tub of ice cream that costs $5. He has $3." then "How much more money does he need?"
- Verbs: "buys", "bought", "pays", "paid", "spent", "gave the cashier", "got back", "left", "costs". "Change" appears as "How much change did she get back?" and "got back $12". "Cashier" appears in about half of the papers.
- Question words: "How much ... altogether?", "How much change ...?", "How much more money ...?", "Which ... can he buy?"
- Answer frames: "She needs $ ___ more." "The skateboard cost $ ___." "Peter gets $ ___ back." Number sentence boxes with dollar signs inside: "$ □ − $ □ = $ □" (the key shows "$40 − $30 = $10").
- Marks: Section B 2 marks per item; story sums 2 to 4 marks with number sentence.
- Items are named things a child would buy: pencil, notebook, bag, eraser, ball, toy car, pizza, ice cream, cookies. The cast are ordinary first names (Janice, Ahmad, Fiona, Sam, Ravi, Peter).

## 5. Picture graphs

### 5.1 Where and how often

- Term 4 and revision papers: Tao Nan Review 3 (2019: two graphs, four items), Ai Tong Term 4 Mini Test 1 (three items including "complete the graph"), ACS Primary SA2 (a graph block of four items) and the 2021 ACS topical (two graphs, five-part frames each), Pei Chun Mini Test 4 (three graph blocks, about 7 items, a "colour the graph" item).
- About 1 in 3 Term 4 papers has a graph block of 2 to 7 items (10 to 25 percent of marks).

### 5.2 Conventions

- A title above the graph ("Pets In A Pet Shop", "Favourite Fruit", "Number of stamps", "Our favourite fruits").
- **Rows, not columns:** category labels at the left (words or small pictures), one row of repeated icons to the right. Icons are circles, stars, faces, small pictures of the item. (Inferred from the label order in the text.)
- **Key under the graph, always 1:** "Each [icon] stands for 1 child / 1 animal / 1 book / 1 cookie / 1 stamp / 1 party hat / 1 food item." The noun changes to match the data. No key of 2 in any P1 paper in this sample (the P2 paper in the older notes uses 4).
- 4 to 6 categories; 0 to about 10 icons per row; a total of 15 to 27 icons.
- Data: pets, fruit, books read, cookies eaten, stamps, party hats, favourite food, games. A class survey is used as a made-up context ("the children in Class 1A").
- No tally marks, no axis numbers, no half icons.

### 5.3 Question types and frames

- Read: "Most children like ___", "ate the most number of cookies", "has least stamps."
- Equal: "The same number of children like ___ and ___." "ate as many cookies as ___."
- Gap: "There are ___ more hamsters than guinea pigs." "___ fewer ... than ...", also "Fill in the blanks with more or fewer."
- Total: "There are ___ terrapins and rabbits altogether." "The 5 children ate ___ cookies altogether." Number of pupils in the class (all rows).
- Count the kinds: "There are ___ types of fruits."
- Complete the graph: "Complete the picture graph by drawing", with one clue as a difference ("1 more than the fruit juice packs") and one as a total for two rows ("a total of 12 doughnuts and muffins", one row already drawn). Another: "Ella makes 3 fewer than Betty; colour that many." Another: "Cathy sells 2; how many left?" (read and subtract).
- Frames: 5-part lettered block (a) to (e) on one graph is common (ACS). Others use one sentence per item.
- Marks: 1 mark per blank in a block; 2 marks for the complete-the-graph.
- Question order: most to least concrete; "Use the graph to answer Questions 9 and 10" introduces the graph once.

### 5.4 Fit against L37 and L38

| Our plan | Papers | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| L37 sort a mixed set, tally, draw a graph | only complete-from-clues; no sort, no tally | Not school content; keep as the activity, and treat tally as optional |
| L37 1 picture = 1, title and key | exact | Fit |
| L37 Beyond 1 picture = 2 | not in any P1 paper | Still beyond; confirm P2 |
| L38 read most/fewest/how many more/in all | exact | Fit |
| L38 missing bar from a total | Ai Tong Term 4 Mini Test 1 (two clues) | School-level; promote from Beyond to Think harder if the clues are as above |
| L38 "each picture means 2" | not seen | Beyond P1 |
| L38 "what a graph cannot tell us" | not seen | Not school content |

### 5.5 Recommendation

- `picture_graph` (N15): rows with labels left, key "Each [icon] stands for 1 [noun]" below, title above, 4 to 6 categories, up to 10 icons a row. Build the 1-to-1 form first, the 2-form as an option.
- Use the frames above: "There are ___ more ___ than ___." "The same number of ___ like ___ and ___." "___ ate the most number of ___".
- Make "Complete the picture graph by drawing" one of the Start here or Think harder items with a difference clue and a total-of-two-rows clue.
- Class-poll data stays out (D17); made-up class data in the style of the papers is fine.

## 6. Other topics: sharing and groups, length, ordinals, number bonds

### 6.1 Sharing and equal groups (Term 3 and Term 4 weight)

- Very heavy in Term 3 and 4: one-third to half of many Term 4 papers (Tao Nan Review 3, Henry Park, Raffles, Maha Bodhi, ACS, Methodist Girls', Pei Chun, Nan Hua, SCGS, Catholic High). Not a Term 3 or 4 lesson of ours (it is Term 2, L15 to L17), but the Term 3 and 4 spiral and the Remember items should use the same wording.
- Task stems: "Circle ... to show groups of 3", "How many groups of 3?", "Put 20 sweets equally into 4 groups", "shares them equally", "Draw the number in each", "Circle Yes or No" (can 14 be put in groups of 7), "How many more ... does she need" (13 balls, 4 in each basket), True/False table on equal sharing.
- Forms: "4 groups of 5", "3 fours", "7 + 7 + 7", "4 x 7", "5 sevens". MCQ options for one picture: groups-of form, n-times form, repeated addition, and a wrong one (47, or the multiplication reversed). Operator is "x"; no division symbol appears.
- Numbers: products to about 40; division of 12 to 24; groups of 2 to 7.
- Convention warning: the sharer is counted in "shares with 3 friends" (12 toy cars, answer 3) and in "shares with Betty and Carol" (18 ribbons, answer 6). Add one line to the wording guide.

### 6.2 Length

- Term 1 and 2 plus Ai Tong Term 4: taller, tallest, shorter, shortest, longer, longest; "Circle the shorter pencil"; "Draw a shorter tree"; ordering four strings or four trees; "Strip C and Strip D are the same length." **Units, not centimetres:** "Each [icon] stands for 1 unit", paperclips, strips, "The length of the bookmark is ___ units". No cm and no ruler marks in any P1 paper in this sample.
- Our L9 uses cm; this is a note for the Term 1 wording file, not for Terms 3 and 4. See section 7.

### 6.3 Ordinals and position

- Term 1 and 2 plus revision in Nanyang numbers-to-100 quizzes: "Circle the 3rd child from the right", "Cross out the 7th frog", "Colour the 2nd cat from the left", "5th from the left; also ___ from the right". Row with "Left" and "Right" printed at the two ends. Ordinals written 1st to 10th; words first, last, after, between ("Joe came after ...").
- Position puzzles: four children in a queue with two clues ("Tom is second; Jen is between Tom and Anna"). Count puzzle: "4th in the queue, 5 behind, how many in the queue?" (9, the child counts herself).
- Fit: L11 (position words and 1st to 10th) is fine; L30 and L36 can reuse the queue puzzle.

### 6.4 Number bonds

- Part-whole diagrams with three circles. "Complete the number bond", "Use all the numbers given", "Write a subtraction equation based on the number bond", "Which pair of numbers make 10?", "8 and 2 make ___".
- Ranges: within 10 and 20 in Term 1 and 2; within 100 in Term 3 and 4 ("37, 43, ?" and "82, 25, 57"). Equation pair from a bond is a standard item.
