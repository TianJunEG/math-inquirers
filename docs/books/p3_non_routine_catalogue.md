# Primary 3 non-routine question catalogue (from school test papers)

Status: analysis written 23 Oct 2026 from the owner's Drive folder "P3 Math". **No school question text is reproduced here.** The verbatim questions are in a private, gitignored file for the owner's own study (`docs/books/private_p3_papers/non_routine_questions.md`). Every example below is an original question written for this project and checked by a script (section 5). Nothing here is a claim about what any child will meet in any exam.

## 1. What was read, and what was not

- The folder holds 217 entries: 216 Primary 3 mathematics papers (PDFs) and one 47.7 MB Primary 3 science textbook (not a paper; not opened). Papers are dated 2019 to 2025 plus five undated "PastYr" copies, from 28 school labels (about 25 schools; "ACS", "ACS (Junior)" and "ACS (Primary)" are different schools, and the CHIJ schools and Methodist schools are counted by their own names).
- Most files are scans with a poor text layer. The 2020 to 2025 papers were read from the Drive text layer (OCR); the 2019 papers, whose Drive text held only watermarks and answer keys, were read from my own OCR of rendered pages (100 dpi). Pictures are lost in all OCR text, so picture counts below are inferred from the stems.
- About 25 files are re-labelled copies of papers already read (for example one Red Swastika paper appears as a 2020 semestral paper, a 2021 revision and a 2022 revision; one Methodist Girls' paper appears as a 2022 paper, a 2024 paper and a "PastYr" copy). A repeated question is counted once; the private file lists the other papers it was seen in.
- **1115 question records were taken from 197 tests, quizzes and assessments; after removing repeated questions, 1098 distinct non-routine questions remain** and are the basis of every count below. By year: 2019: 103; 2020: 159; 2021: 156; 2022: 177; 2023: 129; 2024: 184; 2025: 174; undated: 16.
- Not every non-routine question was recorded. When a paper has 30 or more of them (end-of-year papers) I recorded the ones that matter for the lesson plan and skipped plain one-step items and repeats of a form already recorded several times in the same paper. Counts therefore describe this set, not the whole country, and a type that is easy to spot (a "how many ways" question) is better counted than one that hides inside a Section C story.
- Schools differ in how much they set: Nanyang, Rosyth, Tao Nan, Henry Park and ACS (Junior) supply the most items. Marks were read from the papers; where a paper printed none I used the section's usual mark, so marks are indicative.

## 2. What counts as non-routine here

Beyond a one-step recall or computation: multi-step stories; a heuristic (draw a model, guess and check, work backwards, find a pattern, list every case, table, compare by difference or sum, make equal, how many ways); reasoning (true or false, explain, which does not belong); an unfamiliar context (offers, hidden facts, fast and slow watches, symbols); or a build-it-from-clues item. A plain one-step sum and a routine read-off from a graph were left out unless they carry a trap.

**1098 non-routine questions** fall into the 14 types used at Primary 2 plus two more: **GEO** (area and perimeter reasoning, which Primary 2 does not have) and **POSE** (posing a question or asking "what if", seen only from 2024). EQ is used a little more widely than at Primary 2: it covers every transfer between two holders, not only "give so that they are equal".

Notes on the table.

- Difficulty is my own judgement for an eight or nine year old: 2 one extra idea or a small trap, 3 two steps or a trap, 4 hard for the age (three steps, two unknowns), 5 contest-hard. Nothing was rated 1: a Primary 3 non-routine item is seldom as easy as a Primary 1 one.
- **The papers print no challenge section.** The items are ordinary questions in Section A (multiple choice, 1 or 2 marks), Section B (short answer, 1 or 2 marks) and Section C (problem sums, 3 or 4 marks, often with (a) and (b)). In 2023 end-of-year papers (80 marks, 1 h 45) Section C holds 16 to 22 marks; in 2024 and 2025 (50 marks, 1 h 15 to 1 h 30) it holds 12 to 20.
- Unlike Primary 2, **no stem names its heuristic**: I found no "Hint: Make a Table" and no "You may draw a model". The few that help the child are "Draw a timeline" (Raffles Girls', 2025) and "Study the model below" (Maha Bodhi, 2019). Rulang's 2024 papers and Tao Nan's 2025 Weighted Assessment 2 are the exceptions that ask for a reason, a question or a full list.
- By topic: mult 200, money 181, addsub 180, div 71, len 63, time 61, graph 58, pat 42. (Topic codes are my own: MULT includes division stories with "times as many"; WHOLE is counting, wheel and note puzzles.)
- 366 of 1098 items need the picture to answer, so a text-only child page must supply a drawing or a table for the same kind of item.

## 3. The types: what they look like, where they appear, how to use them

### CMP: Comparison (difference or sum, times as many, simultaneous prices)

- **Count:** 245 questions in 136 tests from 24 schools.
- **Where found:** Rosyth 31, ACS (Junior) 22, Henry Park 18, Tao Nan 17, Maha Bodhi 17. Years (distinct questions): 2025 (66), 2024 (49), 2023 (35), 2019 (25), 2022 (24).
- **Topics most often carrying it:** ADDSUB 82, MULT 77, MONEY 36.
- **What it is:** Two or three quantities compared by how many more or fewer, "n times as many", or a total and a difference given together. The reversal trap ("358 more than Ravi", find Ravi) and "times as many with a difference" are the commonest single forms; simultaneous bills ("2 pens and a ruler cost... 1 pen and a ruler cost...") are the other.
- **What makes it non-routine:** The sentence names the known quantity so that the operation is reversed, or the child must find two numbers from a sum and a difference, or must see that one unit is the difference. It is the most frequent Section C item and often carries 3 or 4 marks.
- **Typical number ranges:** 3- and 4-digit numbers (to 5000), money to $1000, multipliers 2 to 7 (3 and 4 most common), totals to 5000; two or three people in a chain.
- **Typical wording (short fragments):** "... times as many ... as ..."; "How many more ... than ..."; "The sum of two numbers is"; "fewer ... than ..."; "altogether".
- **Where it belongs for us:** Think harder in lessons 6, 10, 11, 12, 16, 18, 20, 24, 30; Go further in lessons 5, 9, 27, 35, 38.

|---|---|---|---|---|

| EX-CMP-2 | Think harder | 6, 24 | Mei and Ali have 640 beads altogether. Mei has 128 more beads than Ali. How many beads does Ali have? | 256 (take the extra 128 off, share 512 equally; Mei has 384). |

| EX-CMP-4 | Go further | 8, 24 | A pen costs $3.40. A book costs 4 times as much as the pen. A bag costs $9.60 more than the book. What is the cost of the bag? | $23.20 (book $13.60, then add $9.60). |

### MULTI: Multi-step problems

- **Count:** 142 questions in 100 tests from 26 schools.
- **Where found:** Rosyth 19, Nanyang 14, Maha Bodhi 12, Henry Park 11, ACS (Junior) 11. Years (distinct questions): 2024 (27), 2025 (25), 2023 (24), 2022 (23), 2021 (18).
- **Topics most often carrying it:** MULT 40, MONEY 24, TIME 23.
- **What it is:** Two- and three-step stories in multiplication, division, money, mass, volume and time. Many have parts (a) and (b), with (b) built on (a).
- **What makes it non-routine:** The child must decide the order of steps; no single hidden fact. Typical frames: boxes then a share, a bill then change, legs of a journey, a total with a pack.
- **Typical number ranges:** Tables 2 to 9, 2- and 3-digit by 1-digit, totals to 6000 g or 7000 ml, money to $200.
- **Typical wording (short fragments):** "The rest"; "packed equally into"; "How many ... in all"; "altogether"; "such boxes".
- **Where it belongs for us:** Think harder in lessons 3, 6, 12, 20, 24, 27, 30, 35, 38; Go further in lessons 11.

Original examples (written for this project; answers checked by script):

| ID | Tier | Lessons | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EX-MULTI-1 | Think harder | 6, 20 | A shop has 8 boxes with 125 pencils in each. It sells 340 pencils. It packs the rest equally into 6 bags. How many pencils go in each bag? | 110 (1000 - 340 = 660; 660 / 6). |
| EX-MULTI-2 | Think harder | 8, 10 | Mrs Lim buys 2 bags of rice at $7.50 each and 3 packets of noodles at $2.40 each. She pays with a $50 note. How much change does she get? | $27.80 (rice $15, noodles $7.20). |
| EX-MULTI-3 | Go further | 11 | Ali ran 2 km 350 m on Monday. On Tuesday he ran 450 m more. On Wednesday he ran 1 km 100 m less than on Tuesday. How far did he run in all? Give the answer in km and m. | 6 km 850 m (Tuesday 2800 m, Wednesday 1700 m). |

### BACK: Work backwards (missing start or missing part)

- **Count:** 126 questions in 89 tests from 28 schools.
- **Where found:** Nanyang 12, Rosyth 11, ACS (Junior) 9, St Hilda's 8, Henry Park 7. Years (distinct questions): 2020 (24), 2021 (22), 2024 (22), 2022 (20), 2025 (15).
- **Topics most often carrying it:** MULT 33, ADDSUB 32, TIME 22.
- **What it is:** The end result is known and the start or a missing part must be found: "at first ... after ... left", paid and change given, a time that ended with a duration, a missing digit.
- **What makes it non-routine:** The direction of the story is opposite to the direction of the arithmetic, and several changes (gave, got, packed) must be undone in reverse order.
- **Typical number ranges:** Within 5000; money to $100 (to $1000 in two-step); times to the minute; a missing digit in a 3- or 4-digit column.
- **Typical wording (short fragments):** "at first"; "had ... left"; "How many ... did he have at first"; "gave the cashier ... received"; "What time did ... start".
- **Where it belongs for us:** Think harder in lessons 2, 4, 6, 9, 10, 12, 25, 28, 35; Go further in lessons 9, 20.

Original examples (written for this project; answers checked by script):

| ID | Tier | Lessons | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EX-BACK-1 | Think harder | 6, 25 | Ravi had some stickers. He gave 135 to Mei and then bought 250 more. Now he has 1068. How many stickers did he have at first? | 953 (undo the 250: 818; undo the 135: 953). |
| EX-BACK-2 | Think harder | 8, 10 | Amy paid with a $50 note for 3 books of the same price and a $6.50 pen. She got $7.50 change. What does one book cost? | $12 (spent $42.50; books $36; 36 / 3). |
| EX-BACK-3 | Think harder | 9, 35 | A film ended at 4:15 pm. It lasted 1 h 50 min. What time did it start? | 2:25 pm (draw a timeline: back 1 h to 3:15 pm, then back 50 min). |
| EX-BACK-4 | Go further | 9, 25 | Mei's watch is 15 minutes slow. By her watch she started her homework at 3:50 pm and it took 1 h 35 min. By the real clock, what time did she finish? | 5:40 pm (watch shows 5:25 pm; add 15 minutes). |

### EQ: Make equal or transfer between two holders

- **Count:** 89 questions in 62 tests from 21 schools.
- **Where found:** ACS (Junior) 11, Nanyang 9, Rosyth 9, Henry Park 8, Pei Hwa 7. Years (distinct questions): 2022 (32), 2020 (25), 2021 (21), 2019 (9), 2023 (2).
- **Topics most often carrying it:** ADDSUB 40, MONEY 22, MULT 13.
- **What it is:** A transfer so that two amounts are equal, a before-and-after gap after a gift ("gave 14 to her, how many more..."), "times as many" before a transfer, and a missing number in an equation with a rewritten side.
- **What makes it non-routine:** The child must halve the difference, or see that a gift of g changes the gap by 2g, or set up two unknowns. Some are 3 to 4 marks with parts.
- **Typical number ranges:** Amounts to 2500, gifts 14 to 900, multipliers 3 to 5.
- **Typical wording (short fragments):** "so that they have the same"; "How many must ... give"; "in the end"; "equal number ... at first".
- **Where it belongs for us:** Think harder in lessons 5, 7, 16, 25, 36; Go further in lessons 18, 24, 25, 27.

Original examples (written for this project; answers checked by script):

| ID | Tier | Lessons | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EX-EQ-1 | Think harder | 6, 25 | Amy has 156 stickers and Ben has 78. How many stickers must Amy give Ben so that they have the same number? | 39 (the difference is 78; half of it moves). |
| EX-EQ-2 | Think harder | 25 | Ali had 240 more cards than Ben. Ali gave Ben 60 cards. How many more cards did Ali have than Ben in the end? | 120 (the gap shrinks by 2 x 60). |
| EX-EQ-3 | Go further | 25, 24 | Mei has 5 times as many stamps as Ravi. She gives Ravi 36 stamps and then they have the same number. How many stamps did Mei have at first? | 90 (Ravi had 18). |

### LOG: Logical reasoning (number clues, missing digits, ordering)

- **Count:** 95 questions in 75 tests from 24 schools.
- **Where found:** Nanyang 17, Tao Nan 10, Red Swastika 9, Raffles Girls 7, Henry Park 7. Years (distinct questions): 2024 (23), 2019 (16), 2025 (13), 2022 (12), 2021 (12).
- **Topics most often carrying it:** NUM 18, PLACEVAL 17, DIV 14.
- **What it is:** Mostly number clues ("I am a 3-digit number...", between two limits with a remainder, sum of digits), digit-value comparisons, missing digits in column sums and products, ordering by height or mass from comparisons, and reading a graph with a clue.
- **What makes it non-routine:** The answer comes from fitting several facts, not from computing one; a child who rushes to add or subtract fails.
- **Typical number ranges:** 3- and 4-digit numbers; two to four conditions (even or odd, hundreds twice the tens, digit sum, divisible by 3 or 9, between).
- **Typical wording (short fragments):** "I am a 3-digit number"; "Each digit can only be used once"; "What is the missing digit"; "between ... and ..."; "is twice the".
- **Where it belongs for us:** Think harder in lessons 1, 15, 19, 21, 22, 23, 26, 29, 31, 32, 34; Go further in lessons 2, 4, 7, 26, 29, 36.

|---|---|---|---|---|

### CTX: Unfamiliar context, hidden fact, offers, fast and slow watches

- **Count:** 78 questions in 54 tests from 25 schools.
- **Where found:** Nanyang 8, Red Swastika 7, ACS (Primary) 6, ACS (Junior) 6, Rosyth 5. Years (distinct questions): 2022 (27), 2020 (22), 2021 (17), 2019 (8), 2023 (3).
- **Topics most often carrying it:** MONEY 28, DIV 22, MULT 12.
- **What it is:** Offers and unit prices ("3 for $8", "buy 3 get 1 free", "3 stickers free for every 15 books"), watches that are slow or fast, rates ("5 stamps for every $3"), and facts hidden in the picture.
- **What makes it non-routine:** A hidden rule decides the answer: the free item is part of the count, a slow watch means add the minutes, or the offer has to be applied lot by lot.
- **Typical number ranges:** Money to $200, lots of 2 to 15, clock errors of 5 to 30 minutes.
- **Typical wording (short fragments):** "Buy 3 get 1 free"; "3 for $8"; "for every ..."; "The watch was 20 minutes fast"; "Special offer".
- **Where it belongs for us:** Think harder in lessons 8, 9, 10, 27, 37; Go further in lessons 23, 35.

### DIAG: Draw a model or diagram (posts and gaps, balances, scales, maps)

- **Count:** 72 questions in 60 tests from 19 schools.
- **Where found:** Rosyth 14, Henry Park 6, Tao Nan 6, SCGS 4, Catholic High 4. Years (distinct questions): 2025 (15), 2024 (14), 2023 (13), 2022 (9), 2020 (9).
- **Topics most often carrying it:** LEN 40, FRAC 10, MASS 9.
- **What it is:** Posts, lamp posts, balloons and trees at equal distances (gaps and posts), balances and scales with identical objects, map routes, and "fill in the model" bars.

- **Typical wording (short fragments):** "at equal distance apart"; "distance between the 1st and the 6th"; "balance"; "Study the diagram"; "How much further".
- **Where it belongs for us:** Think harder in lessons 11, 12, 18, 19, 24, 28; Go further in lessons 11, 12, 28, 33.

### GEO: Area and perimeter reasoning

- **Count:** 42 questions in 38 tests from 17 schools.
- **Where found:** Rosyth 6, ACS (Primary) 5, Tao Nan 5, ACS (Junior) 4, Raffles Girls 4. Years (distinct questions): 2025 (9), 2023 (8), 2024 (6), 2022 (5), 2021 (5).
- **Topics most often carrying it:** AREA 28, PERIM 13, LEN 1.
- **What it is:** Perimeter and area reasoning: figures made of identical squares or rectangles, a wire bent into a rectangle then a square, same perimeter with different areas, a field with a given ratio of length to breadth, a fence with a gap for a gate.
- **What makes it non-routine:** The child must find an unseen side from the given ones and use it twice, or compare perimeter and area of two shapes.
- **Typical number ranges:** Sides 2 to 50 cm (to 108 m perimeters); 2 to 5 identical parts.
- **Typical wording (short fragments):** "made up of identical rectangles"; "Find the perimeter of the figure"; "bent into a square"; "Find the area of".
- **Where it belongs for us:** Think harder in lessons 11, 13, 14, 30; Go further in lessons 13.

### PAT: Look for a pattern

- **Count:** 38 questions in 35 tests from 16 schools.
- **Where found:** Catholic High 7, Tao Nan 7, CHIJ St Nicholas 5, Red Swastika 2, Methodist Girls 2. Years (distinct questions): 2024 (9), 2020 (8), 2023 (8), 2022 (6), 2021 (2).
- **Topics most often carrying it:** PAT 36, GRAPH 2.
- **What it is:** Growing figure patterns (circles, sticks, beads, chairs per day), number sequences with two alternating steps, doubling, and two patterns that share a number.
- **What makes it non-routine:** The child must predict beyond the picture: the 10th figure, the figure number for a given total.
- **Typical number ranges:** Figures to the 10th or 20th, steps of 2 to 100, alternating +1000/-200 style steps.
- **Typical wording (short fragments):** "Complete the number pattern"; "How many ... are needed to make Figure 10"; "Study the pattern".
- **Where it belongs for us:** Think harder in lessons 17, 22, 32; Go further in lessons 1, 17, 21, 31.

### EXP: Explain, true or false, not possible to tell

- **Count:** 32 questions in 28 tests from 13 schools.
- **Where found:** Nanyang 5, Catholic High 5, Maris Stella 5, Rulang 5, Raffles Girls 3. Years (distinct questions): 2024 (10), 2020 (8), 2023 (4), 2025 (4), 2022 (2).
- **Topics most often carrying it:** GRAPH 12, MONEY 6, AREA 3.
- **What it is:** True, false or not possible to tell statements read from a bar graph, two figures on a grid, a set of comparisons, or a receipt; ticks against statements; a worked answer with a mistake to explain.
- **What makes it non-routine:** Each statement is tested separately against the picture or the numbers; "not possible to tell" needs a sentence about what is missing.
- **Typical number ranges:** Three or four statements; graphs with 4 to 6 bars; numbers to 1000.
- **Typical wording (short fragments):** "true, false or not possible to tell"; "put a tick"; "Each statement below is".
- **Where it belongs for us:** Think harder in lessons 2, 3, 4, 7, 14, 16, 21, 29, 31, 38, 39; Go further in lessons 15.

## 5. How the examples were made and checked

- All 51 examples are original: new contexts, names from our cast and new numbers. Each is the same kind of question as a school item, not a rewording of one. Sentences are short.
- A script (private, gitignored: `docs/books/private_p3_papers/verify_catalogue_examples.py`; run with `python3 -I`) re-computes every answer by brute force: it tries all digit orders for the cards, all pairs for the menus and lists, all note counts, all child counts, all pairs of unknown digits, all common numbers of two patterns and all routes, and checks that the stated number of answers is exactly right (for example only one pair of menu items makes $9, two ways to pay $18, and no whole numbers for the puzzle in EX-CHAL-2). All 51 checks passed on 23 Oct 2026.
- Before an item goes into a lesson, the author must write the lesson's own `verify/` check; this script does not replace it.
- Tier labels are neutral (Think harder, Go further). Nothing here implies a result, a rank or a promise for any child.
