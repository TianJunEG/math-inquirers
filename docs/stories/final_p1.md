## Diversity check

- Women: Julia Robinson, Marie Neurath, Ingrid Daubechies (3).
- Countries: USA, Wales/England (Recorde, Newton), Germany (Neurath, Kepler), Belgium, France, Switzerland (Euler), India, Central Asia (Al-Biruni). No country more than twice.
- Centuries: 10th-11th (Al-Biruni), 16th (Recorde), 16th-17th (Kepler, Descartes), 17th-18th (Newton, Euler), 20th (Robinson, Ramanujan, Neurath), living (Daubechies).

## Claims I could not confirm (and how the stories avoid them)

- Marie Neurath studied mathematics and physics at Gottingen (1917-1924): found only on her English Wikipedia page, so it is NOT in the story. Her link to maths is as the Isotype 'transformer', hence confidence medium. Sources describe Isotype as Otto Neurath's method; the story credits Marie only for her role as transformer.
- Julia Robinson's 'pebbles' memory comes from her autobiography as quoted in the NAS memoir and on Celebratio (two pages, same underlying source); Wikipedia and Chalkdust do not mention it. The story says 'she wrote that', not that it is observed fact.
- Recorde's birth year: sources say c.1510 (Wikipedia) or c.1512 (MacTutor/ODNB), so the years are given as 'about 1510-1558'. Died by mid-June 1558. The story says 'doctor' (MD, Cambridge 1545) and avoids the 'royal physician' title.
- Descartes: the sources say he introduced x, y, z for unknowns and a, b, c for knowns (Cajori, OED quote on MacTutor, Wikipedia). Earlier writers (for example Viete) used letters for unknowns too, so the story says only that he used them in his 1637 book and people still do, not that he was the first to use letters.
- Kepler: the tale that he first saw snowflakes landing on his coat on the Charles Bridge is told in one source with 'supposedly'; it is left out. Story keeps only the 1611 booklet, six corners, honeycomb comparison and that he gave no final answer.
- Al-Biruni: no source gives a date for the Earth measurement. One source says his very close figure was partly chance (limits of the astrolabe, refraction), so the story says 'close to today's value' and no exact number. The 'hill' method (two sightings plus horizon dip) matches all three sources. Birth region: Khwarazm (sources say parts of today's Uzbekistan and Turkmenistan). Confidence medium.
- Daubechies: the 'powers of 2' memory is her own recollection quoted in MacTutor and Infinite Women; her Wikipedia page could not be opened (fetch blocked), so it was not used.

## Rejected choices

- Shakuntala Devi, Hertha Ayrton, Grace Hopper, Euclid, Thales: already reserved by other levels before I could take them.
- Raye Montague (lesson 9, length): reserved, researched, then released. Her Wikipedia page gives no measurement or length content, so the link to measuring length was not honest.
- Pythagoras: avoided (legends).
