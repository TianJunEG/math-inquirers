## Claims I could not confirm
- Adam Ries: no opened source names percentages (Prozent) specifically; link rests on prices, interest, profit and loss. Birth date varies (23 Dec 1492, 17 Jan 1492, or 1492/1493).
- Eugenia Cheng: the three-siblings cake example comes from the publisher's blurb (and a copy of it); I could not open the book. Her fractions link is general (baking uses fractions), not a claim from a source.
- Alcuin: authorship of the puzzle book is uncertain (some manuscripts name Bede); counts of 53 or 56 puzzles differ by manuscript, so the story says about fifty. The exact wording of the stork puzzle varies by translation, so the try-it is labelled as our own wording.
- Mary Jackson: a claim that she petitioned the City of Hampton to attend night classes, and her 12 papers, rest on one source (Wikipedia), so they are left out.
- Dorothy Vaughan: sources differ on exactly how 'first Black supervisor' is worded, so the story says she became head of the group. IBM computers were not mentioned in the opened sources and are omitted.
- Al-Karaji: birthplace (Karaj vs Baghdad/Karkh) disputed; his induction-style argument survives only through al-Samaw'al; priority of the cube-sum result is debated (Aryabhata is said to have known it), so the story does not say he discovered it first.

## Rejected choices
- Shakuntala Devi, Zu Chongzhi, Luca Pacioli: already reserved by other levels when I tried.
- Caroline Herschel for lesson 1: star counts only reach thousands, so the link to ten million was weak.
- Al-Kashi for lesson 7: his decimals link is already covered by Stevin elsewhere and Iran would have reached three people.

## Level checks

- Countries: China, Greece, Iran, Hungary, United States x2, England x2, Germany, Iraq/Iran (Al-Karaji; Iran at most 2). Centuries from the 3rd century BCE to today.
