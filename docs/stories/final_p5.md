## Claims I could not confirm
- Jakob Bernoulli: MacTutor says only that he studied the exponential series from compound interest; the 1683 discovery of e from a 100% yearly rate (and the $2.00, $2.61 monthly figures) comes from Wikipedia (Jacob Bernoulli and e articles) and is told in the story only in outline. He showed e lies between 2.5 and 3; the story says the limit is below $3. His feud with his brother Johann and the priority disputes are left out.
- Zhang Qiujian: dates are uncertain (MacTutor about 430-490, writing 468-486; Wikipedia 466-485; one scholarly article says ca. 431-450; some authors place him 100 years earlier), so the story says only 'about 1,500 years ago'. Facts used (preface warning about fractions, practice with fractions, dividing mixed numbers, common-denominator help, later exam textbook) come from MacTutor and Wikipedia; the Science in Context article only confirms the book recorded fraction operations. Wikipedia and MacTutor are not fully independent of the book text itself. Nothing is known of his life, so no personal details are given. The 3/4 / 6 try-it is our own example, not from the book.
- Alcuin: authorship of the puzzle book is uncertain (some manuscripts name Bede); counts of 53 or 56 puzzles differ by manuscript, so the story says about fifty. The exact wording of the stork puzzle varies by translation, so the try-it is labelled as our own wording.
- Mary Jackson: a claim that she petitioned the City of Hampton to attend night classes, and her 12 papers, rest on one source (Wikipedia), so they are left out.
- Dorothy Vaughan: sources differ on exactly how 'first Black supervisor' is worded, so the story says she became head of the group. IBM computers were not mentioned in the opened sources and are omitted.
- Al-Karaji: birthplace (Karaj vs Baghdad/Karkh) disputed; his induction-style argument survives only through al-Samaw'al; priority of the cube-sum result is debated (Aryabhata is said to have known it), so the story does not say he discovered it first.

- Shakuntala Devi, Zu Chongzhi, Luca Pacioli: already reserved by other levels when I tried.
- Caroline Herschel for lesson 1: star counts only reach thousands, so the link to ten million was weak.
- Al-Kashi for lesson 7: his decimals link is already covered by Stevin elsewhere and Iran would have reached three people.

- Eugenia Cheng (lesson 2, replaced): her fractions link rested on a publisher's blurb for How to Bake Pi (the three-siblings cake example) and the general idea that baking uses fractions; no independently opened source confirmed a specific fractions link.
- Teen candidates checked for lesson 2: none found with a sound, twice-sourced fractions link. Erdos (reserved; his teen result was on sums of reciprocals, but he is already used at Level 2), Maria Agnesi (reserved; no fractions focus) and an NRICH 12-year-old (not a documented mathematician).
