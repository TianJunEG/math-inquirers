## Claims I could not confirm (left out of the stories)
- Euclid and Ptolemy 'no royal road to geometry' anecdote: only seen on the MacTutor page, reported by Proclus about 700 years later as hearsay. Left out.
- Euclid's birthplace and whether 'Euclid' was one author: unknown; story says only that he taught in Alexandria.
- Mahavira: exact dates and birthplace uncertain (sources say 'about', 'possibly Mysore'); story says so. Britannica gives only 'reductions of fractions'; the 2/17 = 1/12 + 1/51 + 1/68 example is from MacTutor and the unit-fraction rules from Wikipedia. The Dictionary of Scientific Biography page lists the fractions chapter (chapter 3) but does not mention unit fractions. Marked medium confidence.
- Tao's age at his 1988 gold: sources conflict (VnExpress says 12, EBSCO says 13, Wikipedia gives no age). Story gives no age for the gold. His age 10 at the first IMO (1986) is stated by Wikipedia, MacTutor and VnExpress.
- Tao's 1990 book 'Solving Mathematical Problems' (MacTutor) versus 1992 (Wikipedia), and his quotes about trial and error (MacTutor only): left out.
- Euclid Book I Prop 37 (triangles on the same base between the same parallels are equal) is from ProofWiki only; not used.

## Rejected choices
- Pythagoras, Hypatia and other ancient figures: not used; legend-heavy or likely to clash with other levels.
- Women for lessons 4, 7 and 10: P4 already has four women among the kept stories and none of the candidates had a clearly honest link to fractions, composite areas or puzzle strategies, so I did not force one.
- Thabit ibn Qurra for area: link to dissection is real but too indirect for this lesson.
- Alternative for lesson 10: Shakuntala Devi (mental calculation, not strategy) not used.
