# Gifted-style audit of Math Inquirers P5 (ages 10 to 12)

Date: 2026-10-22. Scope: all 10 lessons (p5/content/l1.js to l10.js, p5/data.js, rebuild/gen_p5l1.py to gen_p5l10.py), the printed workbook (two worksheet pages and two Think Harder pages per lesson) and the question bank. Method: docs/gifted_research.md, section 2 (12-criterion rubric, 0/1/2, total /24) and section 3 (honest marketing language), plus the owner's tier principle:

* Core: manageable for a student who has NOT yet learnt the topic (low floor: objects, drawing, counting, guess and check, patterns, skills from the level below; no unexplained notation or vocabulary; the formal method is not required).
* Think Harder: needs the idea taught in the lesson, applied in a new setting.
* Challenge: for students who have COMPLETED the topic: mastery plus extension (multi-step, generalise, justify, combine, open-ended, problem posing, "what if").

Audit rule used (so the verdicts are repeatable): an item is a **violation (V)** if (Core) it needs the lesson's formal method, formula or fact and the item does not state it or give a way in; (Think Harder) it needs no idea from the lesson, or needs next year's idea without teaching it; (Challenge) it needs only one step of the taught method, or only repeats the worked example. Workbook questions are judged as Core: a question is compliant when it has a way in (a printed fact, a started table, a picture or a first step) that a child who has not yet learnt the method can use. "Slide-only" items are the Stretch and Challenge slide items that are not in the bank; they were audited too and are listed where they exist.
Counts per lesson: 6 Core + 8 Think Harder + 9 Challenge bank items, plus 5 or 6 workbook questions: 285 items in all.

## 2. Overall verdict for the level

P5 is a well-built **enrichment programme with real depth features**, not a gifted-education-styled course in the full sense. What is genuinely there: low-floor exploration tasks at the start of every lesson (million seconds, 24 cubes, tear-the-corners, high fives, printer race) each with a named extension; "who is right?" claims and counterexample tasks in the talk time and the worksheets; heuristics named and taught (test both sides, work backwards, pretend a number, supposition, list in order); find-all and unique-answer puzzles that were brute-forced; competition-style Challenge items; same-grade content explored deeply rather than next year's procedures (the exceptions are two flagged P6 previews, in L2 and L7). What is missing or thin: problem posing (only a tutor extension in most lessons), written reflection (only L10 records the strategy used), time for depth (every lesson is a fixed 90-minute routine of 5 or 6 questions in two pages, and the Think Harder and Challenge tiers are labelled "for early finishers"), and child-led inquiry beyond pair work and talk time. As found, the Core tier did not follow the owner's low-floor rule in most lessons; that is fixed now.

Mean rubric score as found: 15.5 / 24 (range 13 to 18); after fixes: 17.1 (range 15 to 19). The fixes raise two criteria only (the ladder, criterion 6, and a posing item in the Challenge bank, criterion 9); the structural gaps remain.

**The three biggest gaps against the rubric**
1. Creativity and problem posing (criterion 9: 0 in six lessons, 1 in the other four). Posing items now exist in the Challenge bank of every lesson but are not on the printed pages, so they depend on the tutor choosing them.
2. Metacognition and reflection (criterion 10: 1 in most lessons, 2 only in L10, nowhere a written "look back"). Children are told to check, but nothing asks them to record the strategy, what was hard or what they would try next (except L10 Q6b).
3. Depth over speed and pacing flexibility (criteria 2 and 11: 1 in every lesson). The routine is fixed (page 1 12 minutes, page 2 15 minutes), there is no pre-check or compacting, and the harder tiers are early-finisher extras rather than a choice made by the child. Student-led inquiry (criterion 7: 1 in nine lessons) is also limited by tutor-led worked examples.

## 3. What marketing language is safe for P5 (from research note, section 3)

Safe (and true of the P5 lessons as audited):
* "A maths enrichment programme for curious, capable P5 children, focused on reasoning and problem solving."
* "Inspired by research-based approaches used in gifted and talented education, such as low-floor, high-ceiling tasks, Polya's problem-solving steps and mathematical habits of mind." Name the frameworks used; do not imply endorsement.
* "Open-ended investigations with room to go further, whatever your child's starting point." (Each lesson starts with an open exploration and has a three-tier worksheet ladder.)
* "We emphasise explaining why, trying more than one method, and learning from mistakes."
* "Extends thinking beyond the school syllabus" (not "ahead of the syllabus"). Note that L2 and L7 include flagged P6 previews.
* "Designed for children who enjoy thinking hard about maths."
* "Small groups with differentiated challenge" (groups up to 8; true only if the tutor uses the tiers).

Do not claim: that the programme identifies, tests or reveals giftedness; any link to, equivalence with, or entry to the MOE GEP, or MOE or NIE endorsement; "gifted programme" or "for gifted children"; guaranteed score, competition or olympiad results, or that it accelerates learning; research-backed outcomes for this programme; suitability for every child. **Not yet supportable for P5:** "Not a drill programme and not a syllabus pre-teaching course" (the research note allows it only if criteria 2 and 12 are both met; criterion 2 scores 1 in every lesson and L2 and L7 carry P6 previews) and "focused on depth" as a headline (depth over speed scores 1).
