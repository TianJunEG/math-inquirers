# International syllabus mapping (Europe): Math Inquirers vs England, Finland, Estonia, Netherlands

Prepared 2026-10-22. Read-only desk review; no lesson content was changed. This file is the only thing written to the repo.
Program: Math Inquirers, six levels x 10 lessons (60 lessons). Level age bands: L1 6-8, L2 7-9, L3 8-10, L4 9-11, L5 10-12, L6 11-13 (post-PSLE maths plus a Scratch bridge). Lessons are enrichment (Core / Think Harder / Challenge; "Notice it. Test it. Explain why."). The "P1-P6" level names come from Singapore and are not school years elsewhere.

**Read this first: reliability of this mapping.**
- Official English-language primary curricula were fully readable only for **England**. For **Estonia** I read the official Estonian-language mathematics annex (Riigi Teataja) and the English general part; the English translation of the mathematics annex could not be opened. For **Finland** the official EDUFI documents returned HTTP 503 on every attempt, so Finnish content below comes from the IEA TIMSS 2023 Finland encyclopedia **[SECONDARY]**. For the **Netherlands** I read SLO's 2025 draft kerndoelen (Dutch) and the TIMSS 2023 Netherlands encyclopedia **[SECONDARY]**.
- **[UNVERIFIED]** means I could not confirm it in a document I read. A `*` in the matrix means the grade placement is inferred (usually because Finland, Estonia and the Netherlands publish end-of-band or end-of-primary goals, not a grade-by-grade list).
- The Finnish, Estonian and Dutch national documents do **not** give one list per grade. Finland sets content for grades 1-2, 3-6 and 7-9; Estonia for Stages I (grades 1-3), II (4-6) and III (7-9); the Netherlands for the whole of primary (end goals). Schools and publishers decide the grade. So most "grade" cells for those three are bands, and band cells make "SAME" look more likely than it really is. Treat the matrix as a screening tool, not as proof of alignment.

Key findings:
1. **The program is not "ahead of" any of these four systems in most topics.** It is mostly at the pace of the systems for the age, with enrichment depth (puzzles, reasoning) on top. The clearest "ahead" cases are pattern rules/algebraic thinking in L1-L2, data/graphs in L1 (Estonia), fractions in L2 for Estonia, and some P3 angle/fraction lessons for Estonia (Stage II starts at age 10).
2. **England is the system where the program can be "behind the school year".** English children meet bonds to 20, money with change, parallel/perpendicular lines and bar charts a year or two before the matching program level, and L6 topics (primes, negatives, algebra, mean, counting combinations) are mostly Year 4-6 content. Only probability, clock arithmetic and (formally) nth-term algebra sit outside KS2.
3. **Topics that appear in none of the four primary curricula:** clock/modular arithmetic and Caesar ciphers (L6.8); logic grids; cryptarithms; the Scratch coding bridge is outside the maths curricula as such (see section 6, note on coding).
4. **Probability** (L6.6) is outside England KS2, Estonian Stage I-II and Dutch primary; Finland has a qualitative introduction (impossible / possible / certain) in grades 3-6 only.
5. **Biggest gaps in the program's coverage of what these systems teach:** coordinates, transformations and position (England Y4-6, Finland, Netherlands), ratio/scale/speed (England Y6, Finland, Netherlands), circles and circumference (England Y6, Finland, Estonia), pie charts, written long multiplication/division as procedures, and telling time/basic 3-D shapes at the youngest ages. Details in each section.

### Method and age conventions

Nominal age for each level is the middle of its band: L1 7, L2 8, L3 9, L4 10, L5 11, L6 12. For each topic I identify the grade (or band) where the national curriculum first teaches it, and its age span (start of the grade to end of the grade):

| System | School start | Grade age spans used |
|---|---|---|
| England | Reception age 4-5 (gov.uk: most children start in the September after their 4th birthday; compulsory from the term after the 5th birthday) | Y1 5-6, Y2 6-7, Y3 7-8, Y4 8-9, Y5 9-10, Y6 10-11 (KS1 = Y1-2, KS2 = Y3-6) |
| Finland | Pre-primary at 6 (compulsory since 2015); basic education usually at 7 [SECONDARY: TIMSS 2023] | G1 7-8 ... G6 12-13; bands G1-2 = 7-9, G3-6 = 9-13 |
| Estonia | Grade 1 at about 7 [UNVERIFIED: not stated in the texts I read] | Stage I (G1-3) 7-10, Stage II (G4-6) 10-13 |
| Netherlands | Compulsory from 5 [SECONDARY: TIMSS 2023]; most children start at 4 in groep 1 [UNVERIFIED] | Groep 3 6-7, Gr4 7-8, Gr5 8-9, Gr6 9-10, Gr7 10-11, Gr8 11-12 (groep 1-2 = 4-6) [grade ages UNVERIFIED] |

Status rule: **SAME** if the level's nominal age lies inside the age span of the grade/band where the topic is first taught; **EARLIER** if the curriculum teaches it before that span (the child has usually already met it at school); **LATER** if the curriculum teaches it after the span (the lesson is enrichment ahead of the curriculum); **NOT** if it is not in the primary curriculum (the label in brackets says where it is, for example KS3, S-III, VO = secondary). A multi-grade topic uses the whole span from first to last grade it is developed (for example Y4-6). The rule is deliberately simple: a lesson whose Core tier is easy but whose Challenge tier goes past the curriculum is classified by its Core topic.

### 3.2 What each year teaches (statutory programme of study, condensed)
- **Y1 (5-6):** count to 100, count in 2s/5s/10s; number bonds within 20; add/subtract within 20; one-step multiplication/division with objects and arrays; half and quarter; lengths, mass, capacity, coins, time to the hour and half hour; names of 2-D and 3-D shapes; position and turns. No statistics.
- **Y2 (6-7):** place value to 100; count in 2s, 3s, 5s, 10s; recall 2, 5, 10 tables; odd/even; 1/3, 1/4, 2/4, 3/4 of a quantity; standard units, money with change, time to five minutes; properties of 2-D and 3-D shapes, vertical line symmetry; pictograms, tally charts, block diagrams, tables.
- **Y3 (7-8):** numbers to 1,000; 3, 4, 8 tables; columnar addition/subtraction to three digits; fractions with small denominators, equivalence, add/subtract within one whole; perimeter; 12- and 24-hour clocks; right angles, perpendicular and parallel lines; bar charts, pictograms, tables with scales.
- **Y4 (8-9):** four-digit numbers, rounding to 10/100/1000, negative numbers counted through zero; tables to 12 x 12; factor pairs; short multiplication and division; hundredths and decimals (two places); area by counting squares; triangles and quadrilaterals classified, acute/obtuse, lines of symmetry; coordinates in the first quadrant; translations; line graphs and time graphs.
- **Y5 (9-10):** numbers to 1,000,000; multiples, factors, primes (to 19), square and cube numbers; long multiplication (two-digit), short division with remainders; improper fractions and mixed numbers, add/subtract with related denominators, fractions x whole number; decimals to three places; percentages as parts per 100; area of rectangles, perimeter of composite shapes, volume estimates, imperial equivalents; angles in degrees, 360 at a point, 180 on a line; reflection and translation; line graphs and timetables; describe linear number sequences.
- **Y6 (10-11):** numbers to 10,000,000; long division; order of operations; simplify fractions, add/subtract unlike fractions, multiply and divide fractions by whole numbers; ratio and proportion, percentages of amounts (15% of 360), scale factor; **algebra** (simple formulae, missing-number problems, number sequences, pairs satisfying an equation); area of triangles and parallelograms, volume of cuboids; nets; angles in triangles, quadrilaterals and regular polygons, vertically opposite angles; circles (radius, diameter, circumference); four-quadrant coordinates; pie charts, line graphs, **mean**.

### 3.3 Pedagogical features
- Aims are fluency, reasoning and problem solving (all three in the statutory text). The text says pupils who grasp content quickly should be given **richer and more sophisticated problems before any acceleration into new content**. This is close to the program's enrichment stance.
- The DfE 2020 guidance is built on "ready-to-progress" criteria (six strands: NPV place value, NF number facts, AS addition and subtraction, MD multiplication and division, F fractions, G geometry; measurement and statistics are folded in). It credits NCETM and Mastery Specialist Teachers; it uses **number lines, bar models (23 mentions), part-whole models, arrays**. The words "mastery" and "concrete, pictorial, abstract" do not appear in the statutory page; "concrete" appears only a few times as a resource note in the guidance. The widely quoted CPA sequence therefore **[UNVERIFIED]** as an official DfE requirement; it is English mastery-hub practice rather than statute.
- Calculators only near the end of KS2 once written and mental arithmetic are secure.

### 3.4 Coverage: major English topics with no Math Inquirers lesson
- Telling time to the hour/half hour and 3-D shape names (Y1-2): only P1 L7 (2-D) and P2 L9 (time to 5 minutes) exist.
- Position and direction: coordinates (Y4-6), translation/reflection (Y5-6), four-quadrant grids (Y6): none.
- Ratio and proportion, scale factor, similar shapes (Y6): only the rate lesson P5 L6.
- Circles: radius, diameter, circumference (Y6): none.
- Pie charts (Y6): none; line graphs are covered (P4 L9); mean is covered (P6 L9).
- Roman numerals (Y3-5), imperial units (Y5): none.
- Formal written methods: short and long multiplication/division (Y4-6) are not taught as procedures (lessons use puzzles and models).
- Simplifying fractions, fractions x whole, percentages to decimals/fractions conversion (Y5-6) only partly (P3 L6, P5 L2, P5 L7).
- Times Tables Check (Y4, tables to 12 x 12): P2 L3 and P3 L3 cover the 2-10 tables only; 11 and 12 not.

### 3.5 Vocabulary, notation, method differences
- **Subtraction with regrouping:** the DfE guidance says "exchanging" (for example "no exchanging"), not "borrowing". The program should use "exchange / regroup" in England and expect "borrow" from Singapore and some US families.
- **Column methods:** columnar addition and subtraction are statutory from Y3; "carry" is used informally. Short division uses the "bus stop" layout and long division is taught in Y6 [layout names UNVERIFIED in the documents read].
- **Number lines and bar models:** both are used officially, so Singapore bar-model language transfers well.
- **Fractions:** written as stacked or slashed numerator/denominator; the DfE terms are "unit fraction", "non-unit fraction", "improper fraction", "mixed number".
- **Decimal point** (not comma); money in pounds and pence (£, p), not $ and cents; units l/ml, kg/g, m/cm; imperial approximations taught in Y5.
- **Times:** 12-hour and 24-hour clocks (Y3), "a.m./p.m." as in the program.
- **Term mapping:** "Primary 1..6" in the program does not equal "Year 1..6". Year 3 (age 7-8) is closer to Singapore P2.

### 3.6 Best-fit program level (cautious)
Chiefly by age, then topics: Y2 (6-7) to L1; Y3 (7-8) to L1 end/L2; Y4 (8-9) to L2/L3; Y5 (9-10) to L3/L4; Y6 (10-11) to L4/L5; Y7-8 (11-13) to L5/L6. The English ladder runs about a year ahead of the program's age bands for L1 topics (bonds, money, 2-D shapes are Y1-2) and about level with the bands for L3-L5. L6 content is largely Year 6 or KS3; Y7 children may find L5 a revision, which is a reason to offer L6 to them.

### 4.2 What the curriculum says (via TIMSS 2023, quoting the 2014 curriculum)
- Mathematics content is set in three bands: **grades 1-2, grades 3-6, grades 7-9**. Local education providers add semester/grade detail.
- **Grades 1-2:** varied experiences that build mathematical concepts and structures; instruction founded on familiar topics and problems; pupils express thinking with concrete tools, speech, writing and drawing; strong foundation in the concept of number, the decimal system and arithmetic skills. The detailed topic list for grades 1-2 was **not** in the secondary source **[UNVERIFIED]**. (From general knowledge of Finnish materials, G1 works with numbers to 20 and G2 to 100, with multiplication introduced in G2-3; this is not from a document I read.)
- **Grades 3-6 core content (confirmed in TIMSS quote):**
  - thinking skills: similarities, differences, regularities; comparing, classifying, arranging; systematically searching for alternatives; causal relationships; "graphic programming environments are used for planning and executing programs";
  - numbers and operations: decimal system; divisibility and structure of numbers; mental arithmetic; addition/subtraction algorithms; multiplication tables 6-9 (mastery of 1-10 tables); multiplication algorithms; division as quotition and partition; rounding and estimation; introduction to **negative numbers**; fractions and operations with fractions; decimals and operations with decimals; introduction to percentages; links between fractions, decimals and percentages;
  - algebra: number sequences and their rule; the concept of the unknown; equations solved by reasoning and trial;
  - geometry: build/draw/classify solids (prisms, cylinders, cones, pyramids); polygons, triangles, quadrilaterals, circles; point, segment, angle; **drawing, measuring, classifying angles**; line symmetry; rotational and translational symmetry; first quadrant of the coordinate system, later all quadrants; scale (maps, enlargements);
  - measuring: accuracy and estimation; circumference and surface area of shapes and volume of rectangular prisms; unit conversions for common units;
  - data: tables and diagrams; greatest/smallest value, **average and mode**; probability in everyday situations (impossible, possible, certain).
- Grades 7-9 carry median, relative frequency, proportion, exponentials and fuller algebra.

### 4.3 Pedagogical features
- Mathematics is meant to develop "logical, precise and creative mathematical thinking" (TIMSS quote); a **concrete and functional approach** is "essential"; ICT supports learning. Positive attitude and self-image are an explicit goal. Problem solving "independently and in a group" and comparing different solutions is named for grades 3-6.
- Later school start: pre-primary at 6, basic education usually at 7. A Finnish 7-year-old in G1 is therefore the age of an English Y2 or Dutch groep 4 child but has had far less formal mathematics.
- Teachers have wide pedagogical autonomy; textbooks fill the gaps the national text leaves.

### 4.4 Coverage: Finnish topics with no program lesson
Coordinate system and scale/enlargement (maps); rotational and translational symmetry; circles and circumference; solids other than cuboids and cube nets; surface area; arithmetic with decimals (P4 L5 reads, orders and rounds decimals but does not teach decimal operations); unit conversions beyond P3 L9; simple probability words (P6 L6 goes past it); tables and diagrams other than picture, bar and line graphs; time, money and measurement basics at G1-2 (partly P1 L6, L9).

### 4.5 Vocabulary, notation, method differences [mostly UNVERIFIED: not read in the national text]
- **Decimal comma** (3,5 not 3.5) and a space or period for thousands (10 000); euros. The program uses a decimal point and $/cents.
- Multiplication shown with a dot or cross and division often with a colon (6 : 3) [UNVERIFIED].
- 24-hour clock is used in everyday life; the program's a.m./p.m. (P2 L9, P3 L9) is unfamiliar.
- Written addition and subtraction algorithms are listed for grades 3-6; Finnish children may be less drilled in column layout at age 7-8 than English Year 3 children.
- Programming: grades 3-6 name graphic programming environments, so the P6 Scratch bridge is on-theme for ages 11-13 (Finnish G5-7).

### 4.6 Best-fit program level (cautious)
Because grades run two years behind the English year numbers, **age should lead**. G1 (7-8): L1 (age band 6-8). G2 (8-9): L1 for confidence, L2 for stretch. G3 (9-10): L2 or L3. G4 (10-11): L3-L4. G5 (11-12): L4-L5. G6 (12-13): L5-L6; L6's coding bridge fits well. For a 6-year-old in pre-primary, L1 is likely above the curriculum but the free taster is the better first step. Topics with no clear Finnish home before G3 (patterns, working backwards, picture graphs) can serve as enrichment without clashing with school.

### 5.2 What each stage teaches (official Estonian text, my translation)
**Stage I (grades 1-3, ages about 7-10), topics: calculation, measurement, geometric shapes, problem solving.** By the end of grade 3: reads and writes natural numbers 0-10,000, ordinals, compares and orders; writes a number as a sum of ones, tens, hundreds, thousands; adds and subtracts **mentally within 100 and in writing within 10,000**; knows the multiplication table (multiplies and divides mentally within 100); names the parts of the four operations; order of operations with brackets; finds 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, 1/5 of a number and explains these fractions as part of a shape or set; explains multiplication as repeated addition and division as the inverse; finds the value of a letter in an equation by trial (2025) or trial/analogy (2014); even/odd; units of length, mass, time, current money, litre, temperature (thermometer), clock and calendar; converts mostly neighbouring units; measures and draws segments; perimeter of polygons; length of a broken line; names and sorts point, line, ray/segment, circle, polygons, cube, cuboid, sphere, pyramid, cylinder, cone; draws rectangle, square, equilateral triangle, circle; one- and two-step word problems, models word problems with a drawing or scheme. **No statistics section and no algebra beyond a letter for an unknown in Stage I.** The text also asks for use of computer programs to practise skills.

**Stage II (grades 4-6, ages about 10-13), topics: calculation, data, algebra, geometric shapes and measurement, problem solving.** Natural numbers to one billion (compare to one million), **integers** and positive rational numbers (decimals to three places; common fractions with denominators to 1000); rounding; number line; conversion between fractions and decimals; mental calculation (add/subtract within 1000, multiply/divide within 100) and written within 10,000 and 1000, including common fractions with lowest common denominators up to 100; properties of operations, order of operations; odd/even, **primes and composites**, multiples and factors, divisibility tests (2, 3, 5, 10; 9 in the 2014 text), square, cube, opposite, reciprocal, absolute value; **percent concept and finding a part of a whole; line, bar and pie charts; frequency table and arithmetic mean**; temperature and motion graphs; expressions, equations, formulas; solving simple equations; units of length, area, volume, time; angles (right, acute, obtuse, straight, adjacent, vertical) measured with protractor; parallel/perpendicular lines; bisectors and symmetric figures; scale; area and volume of cube and cuboid; pi, circumference and area of circle; triangle altitudes and area; angle sum in a triangle; congruence tests; classification of triangles; coordinate axes; the Polya problem-solving scheme and strategies (sketching, visualising, organising data); multi-step word problems.

**Stage III (grades 7-9)** includes powers and roots, percentage problems and per-mille, probability (2014: "meaning of probability"), polynomials, proportion, functions, circle geometry and similarity.

### 5.3 Pedagogical features
- The curriculum is framed through **general competences**: the mathematics annex defines "mathematical competence" as using the language, symbols and methods of mathematics in varied situations, posing problems, finding and applying strategies, checking results, reasoning, justifying and proving, and understanding the social, cultural and personal role of mathematics. It also lists digital competence and problem solving as cross-cutting.
- The text asks for **learner-centred, inquiry-based learning ("uuriv õpe") and varied methods**, differentiated tasks, hands-on activity ("käelised tegevused") to support understanding, ICT environments (calculators, spreadsheets, geometry software), and for pupils to try, err and admit mistakes. Three cognitive levels are named (facts/procedures, applying, reasoning), close to the program's Core / Think Harder / Challenge.
- A national e-test in mathematics in grade 6 and a basic-school exam in grade 9 (Eurydice) [SECONDARY].
- School start: about 7 **[UNVERIFIED]**.

### 5.4 Coverage: Estonian topics with no program lesson
Ordinal numbers (järgarvud); drawing with ruler and compass (rectangle, equilateral triangle, circle, bisectors, perpendicular bisectors); broken-line length; pentagon/hexagon; temperature/thermometer; divisibility tests; opposite, reciprocal, absolute value, squares and cubes; decimal operations; percent as a basic skill (P5 L7 is a bigger step than the Estonian grade-6 goal, which is only "concept of percent, part of a whole"); pie charts and frequency tables; scale; circle (pi, circumference, area); congruence tests for triangles; coordinate system; area of a cube/cuboid surface (volume is in P5 L5); simplifying one-variable expressions (partly P6 L4). The Estonian program is heavier on written fractions and decimal arithmetic than the Math Inquirers lessons, which are puzzle-led.

### 5.5 Vocabulary, notation, method differences
- **Decimal comma**; "kümnendmurd" (decimal fraction) vs "harilik murd" (common fraction); "liidetav, summa; vähendatav, vähendaja, vahe; tegur, korrutis; jagatav, jagaja, jagatis" for the parts of the four operations (Stage I names these explicitly, so Estonian children arrive knowing the names the program uses informally); "kordne" (multiple) and "tegur" (factor); "ristkülik" (rectangle), "murdjoon" (broken line).
- Written methods are listed from Stage I (within 10,000), so column addition/subtraction is present by age 9-10.
- Euro; 24-hour time in daily life; "kell ja kalender" is a Stage I topic.
- Division sign and fraction layout conventions not read in the documents [UNVERIFIED].

### 5.6 Best-fit program level (cautious)
Stage I (about 7-10) corresponds to L1-L3 by age and L2-L3 by topic (numbers to 10,000, tables, written +/-). Stage II (about 10-13) corresponds to L4-L6. The mismatch is that Estonia teaches **data/graphs, angles, area, fractions beyond halves-fifths and negative numbers only in Stage II**, so L1.3 (picture graphs), L2.7 (fractions) and L3.8 (angles) will be new topics for a child in Stage I, which supports their enrichment role. L5-L6 topics (primes, negative numbers, algebra letters, mean) are Stage II (grades 4-6). Only probability (Stage III) and clock arithmetic lie outside.

**There is no grade-by-grade national list in the Netherlands.** The kerndoelen (core goals) describe the end of primary (groep 8) and the *referentieniveaus* give attainment targets (1F expected of at least 85 percent of pupils, 1S of at least 65 percent, by the end of primary [SECONDARY, TIMSS 2023]). ### 6.2 What the 2025 draft goals cover (primary, groep 1-8)
Goal 10 numbers and ratio: whole and decimal numbers (memorise bonds to 20 and the tables; mental, estimated, written and calculator calculation; using properties and standard procedures); fractions as number, ratio and division (unit, proper, mixed and improper fractions; links to decimals, ratios and percentages); ratios, percentages, scale and compound quantities. Goal 11 quantities and units: length, area, volume, mass, speed, time, money, temperature and memory size; perimeter, area and volume of **rectangular** figures. Goal 12 data: tables, **mean**, graphs and infographics, judging whether a conclusion drawn from a graph is right. Goal 13 patterns and relations: patterns in number and figure sequences, descriptions, tables and graphs. Goal 14 geometry: properties of figures, viewing lines, plans and routes, 2-D representations of 3-D figures, turning, reflecting, enlarging and reducing. Goal 15 mathematical ways of working: **problem solving with heuristics (guess and check, sketching, simplifying), modelling, designing and describing algorithms**. Goal 16 mathematical language, representations and instruments. Goals 17-18 attitude and applying mathematics. Not present in the primary goals as drafted: negative numbers, algebra and equations (these start in secondary, "VO"), probability, combinatorics, prime factorisation, angle measurement [the word lists searched in the Dutch text found none of these in the primary section].

### 6.3 Pedagogical features
- "Wiskunde in de werkelijkheid" (mathematics in real situations) and **models** ("schematisch weergeven van een situatie", "abstracte modellen") are explicit in the 2025 text, consistent with **Realistic Mathematics Education (RME)**. The abbreviation RME does not appear in the SLO text I read, so the label is **[UNVERIFIED]** as a quote and well established as a description of Dutch methodes.
- Heuristics are named (guess and check, make a sketch, simplify the problem, role-play) and **algorithms** (limited steps, fixed result) are a goal; this matches the program's "Challenge Day" heuristics and the P6 coding bridge.
- Calculator "with insight" is a goal. The 2025 revision explicitly strengthens reasoning, mathematical attitude and a continuous line into secondary.
- Dutch children typically start in groep 1 at 4, with formal reading and arithmetic in groep 3 (age 6-7) **[UNVERIFIED for ages]**.

### 6.4 Coverage: Dutch topics with no program lesson
Ratio table and compound quantities (speed, price per unit appear only in P5 L6); scale and map reading; routes, floor plans and viewing lines; transformations (turn, reflect, enlarge: only line symmetry in P4 L8); memory size units; money in euros; mental-strategy emphasis (jump strategy, splitting) and written algorithms ("cijferen"); estimation as a stated goal; calculator use; instruments (ruler, protractor, scale) partly in P1 L9, P4 L6.

### 6.5 Vocabulary, notation, method differences [mostly UNVERIFIED]
- **Decimal comma** (3,5), euro, 24-hour time. Terms confirmed in the 2025 text: "stambreuk" (unit fraction), "echte breuk" (proper), "gemengd getal" (mixed number), "onechte breuk" (improper), "hoofdrekenen" (mental), "schattend rekenen", "schriftelijk rekenen", "rekenmachine".
- Dutch methodes teach mental "jump" and "split" strategies and number-line models ("rekenrek", "lege getallenlijn") before and alongside written column algorithms, and may prefer "happen" (chunked) division; these are common practice, not in the documents I read.
- The program's "bar models" are close to Dutch "strookmodel/breukenstrook" but not named in the draft goals.

### 6.6 Best-fit program level (cautious)
By age: groep 3 (6-7) to L1; groep 4 (7-8) to L1-L2; groep 5 (8-9) to L2-L3; groep 6 (9-10) to L3-L4; groep 7 (10-11) to L4-L5; groep 8 (11-12) to L5 (and L6 for those moving to secondary). Dutch children are about the same age as English peers in the corresponding year, and the primary goals are comprehensive in fractions, ratio, percentages and data, so L4-L5 topics are mostly "on-curriculum" for groep 7-8. L6 topics (negative numbers, algebra, probability, primes, combinatorics) are mostly secondary (VO) content in the Netherlands.

## 8. What is safe to claim

- Safe: "Math Inquirers is an independent enrichment program. Its topics overlap with the mathematics taught in primary schools in England, Finland, Estonia and the Netherlands, and many lessons go beyond them in reasoning and problem solving." State the **ages** (6-8 ... 11-13), not school years.
- Safe, with a source: "Roughly three quarters of the lesson topics (44 to 48 of 60 by this review, including the ten mixed-puzzle days, which are aim-level) fall in the same age range as the national curricula" only if the matrix caveats are published too; otherwise say "overlaps with".
- Safe: "The program is unlike a school course; it is not a substitute for it." Accurate and consistent with the English statement that fast learners should get richer problems before acceleration.
- Not safe: "aligned with", "meets", "mapped to", "endorsed by", "approved by" or "follows" the English National Curriculum / DfE Ready to Progress criteria, the Finnish National Agency for Education core curriculum, the Estonian national curriculum or the Dutch kerndoelen/SLO; no ministry or agency has reviewed this program, and this mapping was done by the program's own desk review. Do not use logos or names of ministries, DfE, EDUFI, SLO or NCETM in marketing.
- Not safe: any grade-by-grade claim for Finland, Estonia or the Netherlands, because their national documents do not publish one; any claim about the 2025 Estonian amendment or the Dutch 2025 kerndoelen being "in force" until verified; any statement about the newest Finnish revision.
- Not safe: claims about Finnish content for grades 1-2 or Dutch grade placement without checking the primary documents (see next steps).
- Descriptions of "mastery" or "concrete-pictorial-abstract" as English requirements: not supported by the DfE text I read; call them common practice.
