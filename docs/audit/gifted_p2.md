# Gifted-style audit: Math Inquirers P2 (ages 7-9)

Audited 2026-10-22 against the 12-criterion rubric in `docs/gifted_research.md` (section 2) and the owner's tier principle. This is the state **as found, before the fixes** described in `docs/audit/gifted_fixes_p2.md`. The rubric measures design features, not outcomes. Bands (suggested in the research note, not from a source): 19 or more = gifted-style, 12 to 18 = partly, under 12 = mainly enrichment-practice.

## Tier principle used

- **Core**: a child who has NOT yet learnt the topic can start it and usually solve it with objects, drawing, counting, guess-and-check, patterns or skills from the level below; no unexplained notation; the lesson's formal method is not required.
- **Think Harder**: needs the idea taught in this lesson, applied in a new setting.
- **Challenge**: needs mastery of the topic plus extension (multi-step, generalise, justify, combine topics, open-ended, problem posing, "what if").
- Items checked per lesson: every workbook core-page question (Q1 to Q5/6/7), the 6 Core, 8 Think Harder and 9 Challenge bank items (Think Harder 1-4 and Challenge 7-9 are also printed on the workbook Think Harder pages) and the 3 new "Stretch" items printed on the lesson slide. A question with several parts is judged as one item, by its hardest part. The two slide "Challenge" items are bank items and are not counted twice.

## Overall verdict for the level

**Partly gifted-education-styled; honestly an enrichment programme with real depth in places and clear gaps.** Average rubric score 13.3 of 24 (range 11 to 15); no lesson reaches the 19-or-more band, and Lesson 8 falls in the lowest band.

What is genuinely there: every lesson opens with a low-floor, hands-on explore (digit cards, counters, cube train, folded strips, a home-made balance) with named "finished early" extensions; the content is non-routine puzzles on grade-level material rather than next-year procedures (criteria 1, 7, 12 score 1-2 almost everywhere); every lesson has three visible tiers; the Challenge tiers contain real find-all, justify and "is it always true" tasks.

Tier principle: **86% of items were in the right tier**, which is better than expected, but the failures are systematic. (1) The Challenge tier is the weak one: 28 of 90 Challenge items just repeat a worksheet or Think Harder task with new numbers (Lessons 3 and 10 most of all). (2) 8 Think Harder items were Core-level (nothing from the lesson needed), mostly in Lessons 6 and 8. (3) 8 workbook core-page questions had no entry point for a child who had not yet learnt the topic (Lessons 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9), including three labelled "Challenge!" or "Think harder!" on the core page. (4) Core bank items were all compliant, but they are fluency checks, not low-floor investigations.

Three biggest rubric gaps as found: **criterion 10 (metacognition: 0 in every lesson, no look-back anywhere)**, **criterion 9 (creativity and problem posing: 0 in 5 lessons, tutor-guide only in the rest; nothing on student pages)**, and **criterion 11 (pacing flexibility: lockstep slides and early-finisher slides only, no pre-check or compacting)**. Close behind: criterion 8 (complexity, thin story coating) and criterion 5 (multiple solution paths are shown by the tutor, not set as tasks).

## Marketing language that is safe for P2 (from research section 3)

Safe, because the lessons really do these things:
- "A maths enrichment programme for curious, capable children, focused on depth, reasoning and problem solving."
- "Inspired by research-based approaches used in gifted and talented education, such as low-floor, high-ceiling tasks, Polya's problem-solving steps and mathematical habits of mind" (name only the frameworks used: low-floor tasks, listing/table/draw heuristics and explain-why prompts are present; there is no explicit Polya look-back in this level as found).
- "Open-ended investigations with room to go further, whatever your child's starting point" (true of the explore tasks; the Core pages are less open).
- "We emphasise explaining why, trying more than one method and learning from mistakes" (explaining and checking: yes; comparing methods: only in Lesson 10; use "trying different ways to start").
- "Extends thinking beyond the school syllabus" (not "ahead of"). Lessons 3 (remainders), 7 (equivalent fractions with bars) and 8 (nets) do borrow P3 ideas; say "extends" and keep those lessons described as hands-on introductions.
- "Small groups with differentiated challenge": safe once the tier fixes are in; as found, the Challenge tier was not reliably harder.

Use with care: "Not a drill programme and not a syllabus pre-teaching course" is only safe if criteria 2 and 12 pass; criterion 12 passes (2 in 7 lessons) but criterion 2 (depth over speed) scores 1 everywhere because each worksheet has 5 to 7 question blocks in 12 to 14 minutes. Prefer "puzzle-based, not a drill programme" or omit it.

Do not claim: that the programme identifies or "reveals" giftedness; any link to, equivalence with or endorsement by the MOE Gifted Education Programme or NIE; "gifted programme" or "for gifted children" as a descriptor of the children (use "gifted-education-inspired" and explain it in one sentence); guaranteed scores, competition or olympiad results, or "accelerates"; research-backed outcomes for this programme; suitability for all children.
