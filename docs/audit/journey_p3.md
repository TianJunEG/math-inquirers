# Math Inquirers P3 (ages 8-10): student journey review and diagnostic check

Reviewer's stance: read-only, candid. Sources read: `docs/gifted_research.md`, `docs/levels_and_placement.md`, `p3/content/l1..l10.js` (lesson, script, deck, qbank, workbook), `p3/gifted.json` (warm-up checks, investigations, look-back), `p3/workbook_html.js`, `p3/guide.js`, `rebuild/p3_gifted.py`, `diag/content/p3.json`, `diag/build_diag.py`, `p3/SPEC.md`, `p2/SPEC.md`. Arithmetic and the placement model are in `docs/audit/journey_p3.py` (run with `python3 -I`). Scores are my judgement from the real text; the diagnostic probabilities are my assumptions and are labelled as such.

The P3 workbook per lesson is: Core page 1 (with the printed "Fast track" box on top), Core page 2, Think Harder (Stretch S1-S4, then Challenge numbered 5-7, the last three Challenge items of the bank), and a last page "Investigate and Look back". Day plan: 0-2 opening, 2-5 warm-up check, 5-8 game, 8-10 quick questions, 10-25 explore, 25-35 mini-lesson, 35-40 break, 40-70 worksheet, 70-80 talk time, 80-90 exit ticket, stamp and look-back (88-90).

## PART 1. Student journey

### 1.0 Things that apply to every lesson (read first)

**The warm-up check is a test of ideas not yet taught.** It sits at minute 2-5, before explore and mini-lesson, and the guide says it asks "the key idea in a new setting". So for most lessons the low child's first real act is two untaught questions, silent, with boards held up together, then the answer clicks onto the screen. Reading load of the check questions: 10 to 30 words each (L1 30/25, L4 29/21, L8 30/14, L6 27/10). The tutor script ("thank you, let us hear another way") is kind, but a child who sees their own board is blank or different from the revealed answer will know. Of the 10 lessons I judge the low child can reasonably answer at least one check question in L4 (Q1 is a one-bar problem), L10 (both) and L6 (Q1, a misconception check); L1 is borderline (both need renaming or the zero rule). In L2, L3, L5, L7, L8 and L9 both questions need fluency or vocabulary the low child does not yet have (see per-lesson notes).

**The fast-track label is printed in every child's book.** The box at the top of every Core page 1 reads: "Both Warm-up check answers right and you can explain why? Your tutor will say 'go to Think Harder'." That publishes the rule. A child who got a check question wrong reads, at minute 40, a box that tells them exactly what they were not. The guide says "nobody is labelled", the page itself labels. The Challenge half of the Think Harder page is also titled "Challenge (for strong thinkers)", which tells everyone else they are not. The tutor instruction is to name fast-track children "in the chat or a small breakout room"; in an 8-person Zoom the chat is public unless it is a private message. In this design the low child is almost never fast-tracked (my estimate: 0 to 2 lessons of 10), so the label lands on the same child every time.

**Fast track also over-promotes the middle child.** Pass both questions by luck and the child skips the core practice and starts on S1-S4 plus three Challenge items plus the Investigation. Think Harder items "need the idea taught in this lesson", and the middle child who skipped Core has had 30 minutes of the idea but no practice. Tutor check-in on fast-track children is "about minute 52 for two minutes", so 12 unsupervised minutes.

**The high child has about 35 minutes of lesson pitched below them** (minutes 5-40 warm-up, explore and mini, then 70-90 talk time and exit). The scripts do have "Finished early" hints in every explore and "useful explainers" in the fast-track notes, which is good, but "useful explainer" quietly makes the high child the teacher's assistant. After minute 40 the Think Harder page is too long: 7 items with 3-4 answer lines each, then the Investigation (3 parts) and look-back, for 30 minutes (40-70), with 12 minutes of tutor silence.

**Look-back (minute 88-90) is mostly safe but has a contradiction.** The three prompts are tick boxes (1 and 2) and one sentence stem (3, "Next time I will try ____", which needs writing). The options list "I am still stuck" and the script says "Never collect it, mark it or grade it", yet the next sentence says "If a child ticked 'I am still stuck', plan two minutes of catch-up". The tutor cannot know who ticked if nothing is collected or shown. In practice either the tutor sees the page (then it is collected) or the tick has no consequence. Also the very last two minutes of a 90-minute lesson follow the exit ticket, the stamp and a shared volunteer, so it will be squeezed. For a low-confidence child the stem "The hardest part was" followed by "I got unstuck by" is safe if private and if "I am still stuck" is not read out; the first statement does ask a child who found everything hard to pick one thing, which is a small stress, and there is no "something I did well" item.

**Talk time (70-80)** is "Each child unmutes in turn" for up to 8 children in 10 minutes (about 75 seconds each) with no pass option. That is the biggest recurring social risk for the low child, together with the Buzz game (L3) and the relay chain (L10).

**Pairs in breakout rooms with no adult for 10 minutes** (L4, L5, L6, L8, L10). The low child may be paired with a fast partner who takes over; nothing in the script says the tutor visits each room.

**Guaranteed win for the low child in the first 10 minutes of the worksheet** (minute 40-50), item by item: L1 yes (Q1a greatest number, Q3a pattern 9850, 9750, 9650), L2 no (Q1a is a 4-digit sum with two carries; Q2a needs an insight), L3 yes (Q1 fill the 9 times table, adding 9 each time), L4 partial (Q3a "How many units altogether?" is a gift but it is third; Q1 is 1250 - 486 - 378 with borrowing over a zero), L5 partial (Q1a needs 47 / 6; a child without the 6 table can share counters), L6 yes (Q1 wall equivalences), L7 yes (Q1 count squares), L8 yes (Q1 sort six angles with the checker), L9 partial to no (Q1a 3 m 6 cm hits the "2 m 5 cm = 25 cm" trap; Q2 needs order of mixed units), L10 yes (Q1a "which drink sold the most"). Tally: 6 yes, 3 partial, 1 no. In the weak lessons (L2, L9) the first page has no on-ramp.

**Workbook reading load** (words in question stems and sub-parts, excluding tables and figures): L5 384 (the heaviest), L3 317, L9 302, L4 272, L10 269, L1 258, L7 253, L6 219, L2 216, L8 214. Longest single stem about 38 words (L3 Q5 chairs, L5 Q2, L10 Q2). For a slow reader aged 8 these are readable only with a pair or the tutor reading aloud.

### 1.1 Lesson-by-lesson walk-through

Scores are in the order P = can participate, F = difficulty fit (3 right, below 3 too hard, above 3 too easy), U = fun, C = confidence and self-esteem, M = motivation, S = social/online.

#### Lesson 1 Number Sleuths (numbers to 10 000)

Minute walk: check Q1 (30 words: boxes of 1000, packs of 100, bundles of 10; 2 boxes, 13 packs, 4 bundles) and Q2 (smallest ODD number from 4, 0, 9, 3 and "say how you know") both rest on renaming and the zero-first rule that the mini-lesson teaches at minute 25. Game "Biggest number wins" (place each digit at once, cannot move it) is chance-based and fully inclusive. Quick questions are fair. Explore: cards 2, 4, 6, 8, "every 4-digit number between 4000 and 6000", closest to 5000: objects, low floor, hint cards for stuck children. Worksheet: Q1 five parts on 3, 0, 7, 5 (a greatest is a win; b smallest has the 0 trap; e needs a written reason), Q2 list from 2, 0, 5, 5 with a repeated digit (9 numbers, hard for slow lists), Q3 patterns (c is 1000, 1001, 1003, 1006, 1010, hard; d "first number greater than 5000" is a trap because 5000 is in the pattern), Q4 "4 thousands, 15 hundreds" renaming, Q5 three-clue puzzle, Q6 digit sums. Investigation "What if one card changes?" (0, 1, 2, 3) is excellent.

### 1.3 Five weakest lesson-persona cells, with fixes (exact wording)

All five are the low child. That is the finding: the program is calibrated for the middle and high child and the low child meets the content cold in six lessons.

**1. L9 LOW (2.17).**
* Check `gifted.json` "9": replace Q1 with "Which is longer: 1 m or 90 cm? Say how you know." and Q2 with "A film starts at 3:00 p.m. and lasts 2 hours. When does it end?" (keep the current Q1 "2 kg 40 g or 2400 g" and Q2 "15:50 + 1 h 25 min" as the Think Harder warm-up for fast-track children, move them to Challenge notes).
* Explore menu prices: change to whole and half dollars: pizza $8.50, chicken wings $6, curry puffs $4, fruit punch 2 l $3, cupcakes $5.50, balloons $2.50, so the low child can add without decimals. (Exact $20 is then possible in 15 ways with up to 4 of each item, brute-force checked, which is a better low-floor task.)
* Workbook Q1: insert as the new first line "(a) 2 m = ____ cm   (b) 3 kg = ____ g" and renumber; add under 3 m 6 cm the hint "Write 3 m as 300 cm first."
* Q4 (24-hour table): mark it "(if time)" and move the 24-hour clock mini-lesson to the start of the second half so the first 10 minutes of mini-lesson stay on one unit.
* Mini-lesson: split the 10 minutes into "Heuristic 1: one unit" only, and teach "draw a time line" in the pre-page-2 micro-lesson.

**2. L3 LOW (2.33).**
* Check Q1: change to "Here is the 7 times table: 7, 14, 21, 28, 35, 42, 49, 56, 63, 70. Which of these numbers are in it: 49, 54, 63? What do you notice about 14 and 28?" (the table is on screen, then every child can answer).
* Buzz: play only the 7s up to 40 on one slide that shows the table dimmed; "Buzz" is a hand sign (open palm) instead of a spoken word, and a missed Buzz is "that is a tricky one, let us check on the strip". Remove the 6s and 8s rounds from the 3-minute slot, or move them to minute 8-10.
* Workbook Q2: pre-fill the row 7, 14, 21, 28, 35, 42, 49, 56, 63, 70 (answers) and ask only for the last digits, which is the pattern the lesson is about. Q4 grid puzzle: mark "(if time)" and add under it "Hint: 81 = 9 x 9 and 56 = 7 x 8."
* Q5 (36 chairs): shorten the stem to "Ben puts 36 chairs in equal rows (at least 2 rows, at least 2 chairs in each row). Find ALL the ways. Try 2 rows, then 3 rows, then 4 rows, and so on."

### 1.5 Level-wide patterns

1. **The level is built for a middle-to-high child and the low child is under-served** (means 2.93 vs 3.93 vs 4.28). The gap grows from L1 to L9, as the content moves from number (where school fluency is more common) to compound units and angles.

3. **The first ten minutes of the worksheet give the low child a guaranteed win in 6 lessons, a partial win in 3 and no win in 1 (L2).**
4. **Pacing.** The warm-up block is 10 minutes with a 3-minute check, a 3-minute game (5-8) and 2 minutes for 5 quick questions; L3's Buzz as written cannot fit. The mini-lesson in L8 and L9 holds four to five ideas in 10 minutes.
5. **Wait time for the high child**: about 35 minutes of lesson content aimed below them, relieved by "Finished early" hints in the explore stage and by the Investigation. The Think Harder page plus Investigation is too long for 30 minutes (7 + 3 parts + look-back); a child who writes properly will not finish, a rusher will not write the explanations.
6. **Reading load.** Workbook stems run 214-384 words per lesson (L5, L3, L9 heaviest) for 8-year-olds, plus 10-30 word check questions with no audio-first routine; the tutor is told to read aloud only in the diagnostic ("Read a question aloud if they ask").
7. **Strategy-based feedback is strong in the scripts** ("Let us test that", "Ask how do you know after right answers too", "Praise the thinking") and struggle is normalised in the look-back. The weakness is not tone but difficulty calibration and the public labelling.
8. **Online social design.** Turn-taking in talk time with no pass, Buzz, the relay chain and "teach a puzzle" are the moments where the low child is exposed; pair work in breakout rooms has no tutor visit.

### 2(c) Cut scores, luck and "level below"

* **Warm-up truly level below?** Yes. P2 SPEC knows + and - within 1000, 2, 3, 4, 5, 10 tables with their division facts, so W1-W4 are P2 (W2 across a zero is the upper edge). But they sit at the floor: a typical P2-ready child gets 3.7 of 4, so the warm-up carries almost no information except W2. The "warm-up 2 or less" safety rule fires for only about 2% of typical children, 3% of weak ones (C) and 5-7% of E/F, so in practice it almost never changes a result.
* **Binomial noise with 8 items.** With a true chance p of getting each question, P(join) is 0.14 at p = 0.5, 0.32 at p = 0.6, 0.55 at p = 0.7, 0.80 at p = 0.8, 0.96 at p = 0.9; a child with true expected score 6.0 (p = .75) misses the join line 32% of the time. For a child at the join boundary a second sitting produces a different band about 60% of the time (child ladder in the py output, `P(two sittings land in different bands)` peaks at 0.60 for theta = -0.5 to 0). The misclassification zone is about 2 marks wide, so any child within a mark of a cut (3/4 or 5/6) is effectively a coin toss. The cuts are fine as "a guide" (the page says so) but the language "We suggest" at 0-3 is too firm for how uncertain one sitting is.
* **Score structure.** The 8 scored questions are 6 Core + 2 Stretch with equal weight. S2 (list 6 numbers) is easier than M2-M4, so a child with 3/6 core + S2 gets 4 ("support") and a child with 3/6 core and no S2 gets 3 ("down"): one easy list is the difference between P3 and P2. Replace S2 (below).
* **Rule disregards the warm-up for the "down" decision.** A 0-3 child with 4/4 on the warm-up (31% of D, 26% of E) is told "down" even though they have mastered everything the level below asks. The rule needs the warm-up to modify the 0-3 band as well.
* **Rule disregards age.** Dropping from P3 (8-10) to P2 (7-9) is impossible for a 10-year-old.

## 10-line summary

1. **LOW (mean 2.93):** under-served. Cold-start checks, 6 of 10 lessons with no worksheet on-ramp or with unexplained vocabulary, weakest in L9 (2.17) and L3 (2.33). Fit is 2.4 and confidence 2.7.
2. **MIDDLE (3.93):** a good program for this child. Best in L10 and L1/L4/L6 (4.17); dips in L9 (3.33), L2/L3 (3.67) from five topics in a lesson and untaught checks.
3. **HIGH (4.28):** fun and challenging (L3 4.67, L2/L8/L9 4.5); the cost is about 35 minutes of lesson aimed below them and a Think Harder page that is too long for the time.
4. **Top fix 1:** remove the public labels (printed fast-track box, "Fast track? Start here", "Challenge (for strong thinkers)"); tutor invites by private message; use the exact wording in section 1.6.
5. **Top fix 2:** change the warm-up check to one access question plus one thinking question, and rewrite the check in L2, L3, L5, L8, L9 (exact items in 1.3).
6. **Top fix 3:** lighten L9 (menu in whole and half dollars, one unit idea in the mini-lesson, 24-hour table "if time") and L3 (table on screen, shorter Buzz) as in 1.3.
7. **Top fix 4:** talk time and look-back made safe: allow pass or partner share, private "I would like help" tick, a "something I did well" line, remove the collect/no-collect contradiction.
8. **Top fix 5 (diagnostic):** apply the revised placement table (warm-up and age enter the 0-3 band), replace the easy stretch S2, fix the area unit marking and the Q7 two-part answer.
9. **Does the diagnostic place children correctly: MOSTLY.** All four warm-up questions are genuinely P2, answers and arithmetic are verified, a pure guesser cannot pass; strong and weak children land correctly (A 90% join, C 88% down). But a typical child gets a clean "join" only about 40% of the time, the cut is within one mark noise for anyone near it (60% chance of a different band on resit), the strong younger child (D) is sent to a level they are already too old for in 38% of sittings, the older weak child (E) is sent to a younger-age class, and the topic coverage skips tables, angles, measures and 4-digit numbers.
10. **Candid bottom line:** the lessons are rich and well scripted for children who can already do the work; the new gifted-style layer (check, fast track, investigation, look-back) helps the high child but, as built, it publishes the sorting to everyone else. The diagnostic is a decent first filter, not a placement test, and should say so.
