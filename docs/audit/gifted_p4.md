# Gifted-style audit: Math Inquirers P4 (ages 9-11)

Audit of all 10 lessons against (a) the owner's tier principle (Core = low floor, Think Harder = the taught idea in a new setting, Challenge = completed topic plus extension) and (b) the 12-criterion rubric in `docs/gifted_research.md` section 2. The audit describes the content as it was BEFORE this rework (the "before" columns); the "after" columns and `docs/audit/gifted_fixes_p4.md` describe what the fix changed. Nothing here is a measurement of children or outcomes: it describes design features only.

## How items were classified

- Items audited per lesson: the 6 Core, all Stretch (Think Harder) and 9 Challenge items of the question bank; the printed workbook core-page questions (Q1 to Q5/6/7); the 3 Stretch items shown on the slides. (The Think Harder pages in the workbook and the two Challenge slides reuse bank items, so they are not counted twice.)
- **Core / workbook core page: compliant** if a child who has not been taught the topic can make a start and usually solve it with objects, drawing, counting, a list, guess-and-check, a pattern or a skill from the level below, with no unexplained vocabulary or notation. **Violation** if it needs the lesson's formal method or vocabulary, or if it is really Think Harder / Challenge content (a star, "find ALL" with a proof, a generalisation).
- **Think Harder (Stretch): compliant** if it needs the idea taught in this lesson, applied in a new setting. **Violation** if it is solvable without the lesson (core level) or is a repeat of a Core item.
- **Challenge: compliant** if it needs mastery of the completed topic plus an extension (multi-step, generalise, justify, combine, open-ended, "what if"). **Violation** if it needs only one step of the taught method, only arithmetic, or recalls a fact.
- The workbook core pages are guided practice that follows tutor teaching in the 90-minute plan. They are marked compliant when a way in exists without the taught method (a grid, a figure, a table, a drawing) even if the full solution uses it. Those "borderline" questions are listed in the summary and remain the weakest part of the core pages after the fix.

## Overall verdict for the level

Before the fix the level scored 13.9/24 on average (range 11 to 16); after the fix 14.5/24 (range 12 to 17). That is an **enrichment programme with some depth-oriented features**, not a gifted-style programme. The strongest features are the hands-on low-floor explore tasks, "find all / unique answer" puzzles, counterexample thinking in L1, L7 and L8, and the heuristics of L10. The tier rework fixes the ladder inside each lesson (the question bank and Think Harder pages now run low floor, taught idea, completed topic plus extension, with one problem-posing prompt per lesson), but it moves only criterion 9 on the rubric because the session design is unchanged: a fixed 90-minute slide clock, a 12-minute tutor-led teach block, worksheet pages of 5 to 7 questions, and Think Harder only for early finishers or take-home.

**Three biggest gaps against the rubric (level-wide):**

1. **Pacing flexibility and a visible in-session ladder (criteria 6 and 11, both 1/2 in every lesson).** Everyone follows the same slides; Think Harder is for early finishers or take-home; there is no quick check that lets a child skip mastered material and no choose-your-level routine. The Core bank items that are now low floor appear in the question bank and the Weekly Dose parent pack, not on the printed student workbook, so the "access" step is available to the tutor and parent but not printed in the child's session pages.
2. **Problem posing and reflection (criteria 9 and 10).** Each lesson now has one printed make-up-your-own prompt in Challenge, which scores "occasional" (1/2) at best. No lesson asks children to record what was hard, which strategy they chose, or what they would try next, except L10.
3. **Depth over speed and student-led inquiry (criteria 2 and 7, 1/2 in nearly every lesson).** The explore phase is student-led but only 10 minutes; the teach block is a tutor think-aloud; each worksheet page has 5 to 7 questions in a 16 to 18-minute slot; answers are revealed by click. Few tasks ask "does it always work? what if the numbers change?" (criterion 3 is 2/2 only in L1, L3 and L7).

## What marketing language is safe for this level (research note, section 3)

Safe, because the sources support it and the lessons pass the relevant criteria at least partly:

- "A maths enrichment programme for curious, capable children, focused on depth, reasoning and problem solving."
- "Inspired by research-based approaches used in gifted and talented education, such as low-floor, high-ceiling tasks, Polya's problem-solving steps and mathematical habits of mind." Name only what is actually used (low-floor tasks; guess and check, work backwards, draw a model, make a list; "find all" puzzles). Do not imply endorsement.
- "Open-ended investigations with room to go further, whatever your child's starting point." True for the explore tasks and the Think Harder pages; do not say it of every worksheet question.
- "We emphasise explaining why, trying more than one method, and learning from mistakes." Justification scores 2/2 in L1, L7, L8 and L9 and multiple methods 2/2 in L4, L7 and L10; it is weaker elsewhere, so keep the claim general.
- "Extends thinking beyond the school syllabus" (rather than "ahead of the syllabus"). The content is P4 syllabus-tagged, so use "alongside" or "beyond" with care.
- "Designed for children who enjoy thinking hard about maths."
- "Small groups with differentiated challenge" only if the tutor really uses the Core / Think Harder / Challenge tiers during the session.

Do NOT claim (the level does not meet the condition, or the research does not allow it):
- "Not a drill programme / not a syllabus pre-teaching course": criterion 2 scores 1/2 in every lesson (worksheet pages of 5 to 7 questions paced by covering the set), so the first half is not supported; the second half is true in content (P4 topics only) but should not be bundled with it.
- "Gifted programme", "for gifted children", identifying or revealing giftedness, equivalence or preparation for the MOE Gifted Education Programme, or any guaranteed competition or olympiad outcome.
- "Accelerates" learning, or research-backed outcomes for this programme (the design uses ideas from the research; it has not been shown to work).
- Suitability for all children: say who it suits and welcome a placement conversation; offer adaptations for uneven profiles without implying diagnosis.

### Gifted-style strengths

- Hands-on explore with 24 tiles is a genuine low-floor task with a visible "how do you know you have them all?" justification.
- Odd-factor / square-number thread runs from the explore to the lockers challenge (generalising).
- Counterexample thinking is taught explicitly (Siti and Ben claims).

### Gaps

- Every question is answer-seeking; there was no printed "make up your own" prompt.
- Core question bank started at the formal skill (list factors) instead of at objects.
- Think Harder is only for early finishers.

### What the fix did

Core now starts at sharing sweets, skip counting and building rectangles with 20 tiles. Q5c is now a check of 49; "find all" moved to Think Harder. Challenge 5 (Ben claims) and 7 (Bus C, find all, then make up a puzzle) added.
