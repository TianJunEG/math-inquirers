# Gifted-style audit: Math Inquirers P6 (ages 11-13, post-PSLE maths + coding bridge)

Date: 2026-10-22. Scope: all 10 lessons. Sources read: `docs/gifted_research.md` (rubric, section 2; marketing language, section 3), `p6/content/l1.js` to `l10.js`, `p6/data.js`, `rebuild/gen_p6l1.py` to `gen_p6l10.py`, the built workbook and the tutor guide. The audit describes the programme **as it was before the fixes** in `docs/audit/gifted_fixes_p6.md`; an "after" column is given where the fixes change a score.

## How to read this audit

- **Tier principle (owner):** Core must be approachable by a child who has not yet learnt the topic (objects, drawing, counting, guess-and-check, earlier skills; no unexplained notation or vocabulary). Think Harder needs the idea taught in the lesson in a new setting. Challenge needs the completed topic plus extension (multi-step, generalise, justify, combine, open-ended, posing).
- **Items audited per lesson:** 6 Core + 8 Think Harder + 9 Challenge question-bank items, plus the printed worksheet items (Q1 to Q5/Q7, "core pages"). The printed Think Harder pages are Think Harder 1 to 4 and Challenge 7 to 9 of the bank, so they are not counted twice.
- **Worksheet pages:** they follow a short teaching slide, so they are guided practice. I marked a worksheet item as a violation only if a child with no route in (no picture, table, count or guess-and-check entry) could not start it. None qualified; two caveats are noted (L1 Q2, L2 Q4). I tried to add a one-line scaffold to both and reverted, because each pushed its page onto an extra page.
- **Rubric scores** are 0/1/2 on what the lesson plan, workbook and slides show on paper. They say nothing about how well a tutor delivers the lesson or about outcomes, and the bands (20 to 24, 12 to 19, under 12) are the research note's own suggestion, not from a source.
- **Shared-scoring note:** criteria 7 (inquiry) and 11 (pacing) are scored from the shared routine, so they barely vary between lessons.

## Overall verdict

P6 is **an enrichment programme with real inquiry features, not a gifted-education-styled series**. It averages 16.8/24 as audited and 18.3/24 after the fixes, which sits in the "enrichment-flavoured with gaps" band of the research note. Lessons 4, 5, 1 and 9 are the strongest; Lesson 3 is the weakest. Lesson 10 reaches 20/24 because it is a build-and-present lesson with choice and a reflection checklist, but that is borderline and depends on delivery.

What it does well: every lesson opens with a hands-on or predict-then-test task, uses "Ali says ... is he right?" justification prompts, compares methods in several lessons (rule or code, theory or simulation, backwards or search), and ties each puzzle to a test in code. Before the fixes, the Core tier was often not a low floor (49 of 230 bank items broke the tier principle, 21%), Challenge items sometimes needed one step of the taught method, and only Lessons 4 and 10 invited children to invent anything.

**Three biggest gaps against the rubric (still open after the fixes):**

1. **Metacognition and reflection (criterion 10, score 1 in nine lessons).** Testing in code is a check step, but no lesson asks children to record the strategy they used, what was hard or what they would try next. Only Lesson 10 has a look-back checklist.
2. **Pacing flexibility and compacting (criterion 11, score 1 in every lesson).** The routine is a fixed 90-minute slide plan with the same worksheet for everyone. Stretch and Challenge are for early finishers; there is no pre-check that lets a child skip mastered material and no way to go deeper on one question instead of covering the set.
3. **Extension without acceleration (criterion 12, score 1 in five lessons).** Lessons 2, 3, 4, 6 and 7 teach lower-secondary content (primes and factorisation, negative numbers, equations, probability, polygon angle sums) early. The programme's own SPEC says so, so it cannot be described as "not pre-teaching the syllabus".
Next gaps: depth over speed (criterion 2 is 1 in every lesson: five to seven multi-part questions in 35 minutes) and generalisation (criterion 3 is 1 in six lessons).

## What marketing language is safe for P6

Safe (from section 3 of the research note, adapted to what P6 actually does):
- "A maths enrichment and coding bridge programme for curious, capable children who have just finished the PSLE, focused on reasoning, testing ideas and explaining why."
- "Inspired by research-based approaches used in gifted and talented education, such as low-floor, high-ceiling tasks, Polya-style problem solving and mathematical habits of mind." Name only what is used: low-floor tasks, justification prompts, "make up your own" tasks, tiered Core / Think Harder / Challenge questions.
- "Open-ended investigations with room to go further, whatever your child's starting point." True for the Explore tasks and, after the fixes, for the Core and Challenge tiers.
- "We emphasise explaining why, trying more than one method, and testing ideas in code."
- "Small groups (up to 8) with differentiated challenge." True now that each lesson has a low-floor Core, a Think Harder and a Challenge tier.
- "Enrichment toward secondary maths and coding." This is the SPEC's own wording and is accurate.

Do not use for P6:
- "Not a syllabus pre-teaching course" and "extends thinking beyond the syllabus rather than ahead of it". Five of ten lessons are lower-secondary topics taught early (criterion 12 = 1).
- Anything implying identification, testing or certification of giftedness; "gifted programme" or "for gifted children"; equivalence to, preparation for or endorsement by MOE, NIE or the GEP; guaranteed Secondary 1 results; "accelerates learning"; research-backed outcomes for this programme; suitability for all children. (All from section 3.)
- Describing the whole programme as Secondary 1 content (SPEC rule): say "enrichment toward secondary maths and coding".
