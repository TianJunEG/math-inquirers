Audited 2026-10-22 against the rubric in `docs/gifted_research.md` (section 2, 12 criteria, scored 0/1/2) and the owner's tier principle (Core = low floor, manageable by a child who has not yet learnt the topic; Think Harder = needs the idea taught this lesson in a new setting; Challenge = needs the completed topic plus an extension). All figures below describe the content BEFORE the tier rework unless a column says "after". Nothing here has been tested with children. Tier labels and rubric scores are the auditor's judgement, not measured outcomes.

## 2. Overall verdict

**The P1 level is a well-structured enrichment programme with several gifted-style design features. It is not a gifted programme and should not be described as one.** What is real: open exploration at the start of each lesson, a hint ladder, a "how do you know" culture, find-all and what-if problems in the bank, one-topic depth inside grade-level content, and optional slides so early finishers go deeper rather than faster. What was weak before the rework: Challenge was mostly repeats of the worksheet method with new numbers (before the rework 55-88% of items passed the tier test; L7 and L9 were the worst at 55% and 57%), Core had bare "fill the blank" lines with no concrete route for a child who had not met the idea, and no lesson printed an open "make up your own" prompt in Challenge. Scored on the rubric, no lesson reached the 20+ band; the level averaged 13.2/24 before and about 16/24 after the rework.

### Three biggest rubric gaps (level-wide)

1. **Metacognition and reflection (criterion 10): 0-1 in every lesson.** Reflection is an oral talk-time question; nothing is written or recorded, and children are never asked what they would do differently.
2. **Problem posing and creativity (criterion 9): 0-1 before the rework** (0 in L2, L4, L6, L9). Open "make up your own" prompts now exist, one per lesson in Challenge, but they are a single prompt, not a regular routine.
3. **Justification and multiple solution paths (criteria 4 and 5): mostly 1.** "Why" questions are mostly oral, and no lesson asks children to compare two different methods for the same problem. Student-led inquiry (criterion 7) is also 1 almost everywhere: pairs explore, but minutes 25-35 and 55-58 are teacher-led.

## 3. Safe marketing language (from `docs/gifted_research.md` section 3)

Safe for P1:

- "A maths enrichment programme for curious, capable P1 children, focused on depth, reasoning and problem solving."
- "Inspired by research-based approaches used in gifted and talented education, such as low-floor high-ceiling tasks, Polya's problem-solving steps and mathematical habits of mind."
- "Extends thinking beyond the school syllabus" (accurate for the Challenge tier; the Core tier is deliberately school-level).
- "Every lesson has an easy way in and a hard way up."

Do not claim: that the programme identifies or certifies giftedness, that it matches or leads to the GEP or any MOE selection, guaranteed scores or places, that it "accelerates" learning, or research-backed outcomes for this programme itself. The Readiness Tracker and Screening Set are informal tutor tools, not a test.
