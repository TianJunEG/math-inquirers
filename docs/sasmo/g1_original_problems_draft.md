# Original Go further practice problems for Level 1 (ages 6-8)

**Draft, not yet in the program.** Written for the gaps and high-frequency types found in `g1_analysis.md`. Every problem is new (new context and numbers) and every answer was checked by hand and by a short script. They have not been tried with children or reviewed by a tutor, so check reading load and difficulty first. Level 1 lessons suggested in brackets.

## 1. Place value and digit puzzles (suggested beside L2)

**1.1** Pip has four digit cards: 4, 0, 7, 9. Using three different cards, what is the smallest 3-digit odd number Pip can make?
Answer: 407. Solution: the first digit cannot be 0, so use 4; then 0 next; the end must be odd, and 7 is smaller than 9.

**1.2** Who has the most: Ana has 6 tens and 14 ones; Bo has 7 tens and 3 ones; Cy has 85 ones?
Answer: Cy. Solution: Ana 60 + 14 = 74, Bo 73, Cy 85.

**1.3** A 3-digit number has a ones digit that is not 0. Its tens digit is double its ones digit, and its hundreds digit is 3 more than its tens digit. Find every such number.
Answer: 521, 742, 963. Solution: ones 1, 2, 3 give tens 2, 4, 6 and hundreds 5, 7, 9; ones 4 would need hundreds 11.

## 2. Number sentences and clever adding (L2)

**2.1** Add by making tens: 18 + 7 + 12 + 23 + 3 + 17.
Answer: 80. Solution: 18 + 12 = 30, 7 + 23 = 30, 3 + 17 = 20.

**2.2** 27 + 16 = __ + 9. What goes in the box?
Answer: 34. Solution: 27 + 16 = 43, and 43 - 9 = 34.

**2.3** Without finding the totals, which is bigger, 38 + 25 or 36 + 27? Explain.
Answer: They are equal (both 63). Solution: 38 is 2 more than 36 and 25 is 2 less than 27, so the 2s swap places.

## 3. Sequences with changing steps (L1, L4)

**3.1** 4, 5, 7, 10, 14, 19, __. What comes next, and what do the gaps do?
Answer: 25; the gaps are 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, then 6.

**3.2** A bean plant has 2 leaves on day 1 and 3 leaves on day 2. After that, each day's leaves equal the leaves of the two days before added together. How many leaves on day 7?
Answer: 34. Solution: 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, 34.

**3.3** Two rules are mixed together: 3, 40, 6, 36, 9, 32, 12, __, __. Write the next two numbers.
Answer: 28, 15. Solution: the 1st, 3rd, 5th ... numbers go up by 3 (3, 6, 9, 12, 15); the others go down by 4 (40, 36, 32, 28).

## 4. Spatial thinking (L7 warm-up)

**4.1** A square of paper is folded in half three times, each time folding the whole stack in half again. One hole is punched through all the layers. How many holes are in the paper when it is opened?
Answer: 8. Solution: each fold doubles the layers: 2, 4, 8.

**4.2** Cubes are stacked in one row against a wall, in columns 1, 2, 3 and 4 cubes high. How many cubes? Then a second identical row is built right behind the first. How many cubes now?
Answer: 10, then 20. Solution: 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 = 10; two rows is double.

**4.3** Mina looks at a wall clock in a mirror. In the mirror the clock shows 9 o'clock (left and right are swapped). What time is it really?
Answer: 3 o'clock. Solution: a mirror swaps left and right, so the hour hand pointing to 9 is really pointing to 3.

## 5. Time and calendar (extension to L9)

**5.1** Two days after the day before yesterday is Monday. What day is tomorrow?
Answer: Tuesday. Solution: the day before yesterday plus 2 days is today, so today is Monday.

**5.2** A film starts at 4:15 and lasts 1 hour 45 minutes. Meena comes 20 minutes late and leaves 10 minutes before the end. How long does she watch, and when does she leave?
Answer: 1 hour 15 minutes; she leaves at 5:50. Solution: the film ends at 6:00, so she leaves at 5:50; she arrives at 4:35; 4:35 to 5:50 is 1 hour 15 minutes.

**5.3** A month with 30 days starts on a Saturday. What day is the last day, and how many Saturdays are there?
Answer: Sunday; 5 Saturdays. Solution: days 1, 8, 15, 22 and 29 are Saturdays, so day 30 is a Sunday.

## 6. Symbol equations (L5)

**6.1** Apple + apple + banana = 17. Banana + apple = 11. Find the apple and the banana.
Answer: apple 6, banana 5. Solution: the first line has one more apple than the second, and 17 - 11 = 6.

**6.2** Each shape is a number. Square x square = 36. Heart + square = 15. Star - heart = 4. What is the star?
Answer: 13. Solution: square 6, heart 9, star 9 + 4.

**6.3** Triangle + triangle + circle = 20. Triangle + circle + circle = 19. What is triangle + circle, and what is each shape?
Answer: 13; triangle 7, circle 6. Solution: adding the lines gives 3 triangles and 3 circles = 39, so one of each is 13; then 20 - 13 = 7 is a triangle.

## 7. Bar-model word problems (L5)

**7.1** Jo has 4 times as many beads as Kit. Together they have 35 beads. How many beads does Jo have?
Answer: 28. Solution: 5 equal parts make 35, so a part is 7; Jo has 4 parts.

**7.2** Two shelves hold 52 books. Mum moves 7 books from the top shelf to the bottom shelf and now both shelves hold the same number. How many books were on the top shelf at first?
Answer: 33. Solution: 52 shared equally is 26 each; the top shelf gave away 7, so it began with 26 + 7.

**7.3** Ravi is 5 years older than his sister. In 3 years their ages will add up to 25. How old is each now?
Answer: sister 7, Ravi 12. Solution: now their ages add to 25 - 6 = 19; 19 - 5 = 14 is two sisters, so the sister is 7.

## 8. Multiplication and multiples (L1)

**8.1** A spider has 8 legs and a beetle has 6 legs. How many legs do 5 spiders and 3 beetles have?
Answer: 58. Solution: 5 x 8 = 40, 3 x 6 = 18.

**8.2** Which numbers up to 50 are in both the 4s and the 6s?
Answer: 12, 24, 36, 48. Solution: they are the numbers in both lists (every 12).

**8.3** Mia packs 6 cookies in each bag. She fills 7 bags and has 4 left over. Dan packs the same cookies 5 to a bag. How many full bags can Dan fill, and how many are left over?
Answer: 9 bags, 1 left over. Solution: 6 x 7 + 4 = 46 cookies; 9 x 5 = 45.

## 9. Counting ways (L6, L7)

**9.1** Digit cards 1, 2, 3, 4. How many 2-digit numbers can be made (a) if a card may be used twice, (b) if each card is used once?
Answer: (a) 16, (b) 12. Solution: 4 choices for the first digit; 4 choices (or 3) for the second.

**9.2** Three paths lead from Home to the pond and two paths from the pond to the Park. There is also one direct path from Home to the Park. How many different routes from Home to the Park?
Answer: 7. Solution: 3 x 2 = 6 routes through the pond, plus 1 direct.

**9.3** Five friends each shake hands once with every other friend. How many handshakes?
Answer: 10. Solution: 4 + 3 + 2 + 1, because each new person shakes with those who came before.
