# Original practice items for the K2 readiness check (ages 5-6)

**Draft, not yet in the program.** Written for the types and gaps found in `k_analysis.md`: cubes and paper, sequences, picture equations, undoing steps, numbers to 50. Every item is new (written from the skill, not from any contest problem). Every answer was checked by hand and by a short script. They have not been tried with children or reviewed by a tutor.

How to use them: these are **optional, unscored "strong child" extras** for after item 12 of the K2 Readiness Check, or for a K2 Starter Pack. They are oral, one child at a time, with real objects (cubes or blocks, counters, paper, a pencil). Do not read long sentences: say them in short pieces and let the child touch the objects. The scoring bands of the check do not change. Use the child's reasoning ("how did you know?") as the main thing to listen for. Numbers stay within 50 and no item needs the child to read more than a numeral or a letter.

## A. Counting cubes (use real cubes)

**A1.** Tower plan. Build three towers side by side: the first 2 cubes tall, the second 1 cube tall, the third 3 cubes tall. "How many cubes in all? How many more cubes do we need so that all three towers are 3 tall?"
Answer: 6 cubes; 3 more. Check: 2 + 1 + 3 = 6; three towers of 3 is 9, and 9 − 6 = 3.

**A2.** Make a flat square of 4 cubes (2 rows of 2). "Put one more cube on top of one of them. How many cubes now? How many cubes can you see from above?" (Hold it up and look down.)
Answer: 5 cubes; you can see 4 tops. Check: 4 + 1 = 5; the top cube covers one top, so there are still 4 tops showing. The look for: the child sees that "cubes" and "tops" are not always the same.

**A3.** A staircase: step 1 is 1 cube, step 2 is 2 cubes, step 3 is 3 cubes. "How many cubes in all? Now add a 4th step. How many then?"
Answer: 6 cubes, then 10 cubes. Check: 1 + 2 + 3 = 6; 6 + 4 = 10.

## B. Cutting into pieces (paper, scissors or a drawn cookie)

**B1.** Fold a piece of paper in half. Fold it in half again the other way. Open it. "How many boxes can you see?"
Answer: 4. Check: two folds across each other make 2 × 2 boxes.

**B2.** Draw a big round cookie. "One straight line across the cookie: how many pieces? Draw a second straight line. Can you make 3 pieces? Can you make 4 pieces?"
Answer: 2 pieces; yes, 3 (two lines that do not cross) and yes, 4 (two lines that cross in the cookie). Check: draw each one.

**B3.** (Go further.) Draw 3 straight lines across a cookie. "What is the most pieces you can make? Can you make 8?"
Answer: 7 is the most; 8 is not possible. Check: one line makes 2 pieces; a second line that crosses it makes 2 more (4); a third line that crosses both of them in different places makes 3 more (7). Counts of 4, 5, 6 and 7 can all be made.

## C. Number patterns

**C1.** A caterpillar has circles numbered by counting in 3s: 3, 6, 9, then three empty circles. "What numbers go in the empty circles? What number is in the 6th circle?"
Answer: 12, 15, 18; the 6th circle has 18. Check: counting in 3s: 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18.

**C2.** "Each number is the two numbers before it added together. 1, 3, 4, 7, __, __. What comes next?"
Answer: 11 and 18. Check: 4 + 7 = 11 and 7 + 11 = 18.

**C3.** Shapes in a row: circle, triangle, triangle, circle, triangle, triangle, circle, ... "What is the 8th shape? What is the 10th?"
Answer: the 8th is a triangle; the 10th is a circle. Check: the pattern repeats every 3 shapes, circle first; the circles are 1st, 4th, 7th and 10th, and the 8th is one after the 7th.

## D. Pictures and shapes in sums (use counters)

**D1.** "Two stars together make 8. How much is one star? A star and a circle together make 11. How much is the circle?"
Answer: star 4, circle 7. Check: 4 + 4 = 8; 4 + 7 = 11.

**D2.** "An apple, an apple and a banana make 11. An apple and a banana make 8. How much is one apple? How much is a banana?"
Answer: apple 3, banana 5. Check: 11 is 3 more than 8, so the extra apple is 3; then 8 − 3 = 5. Both sums work: 3 + 3 + 5 = 11 and 3 + 5 = 8.

**D3.** "Three cats make 15. A cat and a dog make 9. How much is one cat? How much is a dog?"
Answer: cat 5, dog 4. Check: 15 shared by 3 is 5 (or 5, 10, 15 on counters); 9 − 5 = 4.

## E. Undo the steps

**E1.** "I think of a number. I add 4. I get 11. What is my number?"
Answer: 7. Check: 7 + 4 = 11 (count back 4 from 11).

**E2.** "I think of a number. I double it. Then I add 1. I get 9. What is my number?"
Answer: 4. Check: undo the last step first: 9 take away 1 is 8, and half of 8 is 4; forward: 4 and 4 make 8, plus 1 is 9.

**E3.** A bunny starts somewhere on a number track. It hops forward 3, then back 1, and lands on 8. "Where did it start?"
Answer: 6. Check: forward and back is 2 steps in all, 8 − 2 = 6; test: 6 → 9 → 8.

## F. Turning, fitting, left and right (use paper and a pencil)

**F1.** A necklace has beads in a pattern that repeats: red, blue, green, red, __, green, red, blue, green. "Which bead is missing?"
Answer: blue. Check: the pattern is red, blue, green each time, so after red comes blue.

**F2.** Stripes: red, red, blue, blue, red, red, __, blue. "What colour goes in the empty place?"
Answer: blue. Check: the pattern repeats every 4: red, red, blue, blue, so the 7th is blue.

**F3.** (Go further.) Ali faces the door. The cat is on Ali's left. Ali turns right round to face the wall behind him. "Is the cat now on Ali's left or on his right?" (Let the child act it out.)
Answer: on his right. Check: turning right round puts everything that was on the left on the right.

**F4.** (Go further, paper.) Write the letters in a square: A B on the top and C D on the bottom. Turn the paper a quarter turn to the right (clockwise). Which picture shows the letters now? (1) C A over D B, (2) B D over A C, (3) D C over B A.
Answer: (1). Check: turn real paper: the left-hand column C, D becomes the top row, read C then A and D then B. (2) is a quarter turn the other way; (3) is a half turn.

## G. Numbers to 50 (the check stops at 20)

**G1.** Show the number cards 27, 31, 44 and 38. "Put them in order from the smallest."
Answer: 27, 31, 38, 44. Check: compare the tens first, then the ones.

**G2.** "Count on from 28. Say the next 5 numbers."
Answer: 29, 30, 31, 32, 33. Check: the number after 29 is 30 (a new ten). The look for: does the child say "twenty-ten"?

**G3.** "What is one more than 39? What is one less than 30?"
Answer: 40; 29. Check: 39 and 1 more is the next ten; one less than 30 is just before it.
