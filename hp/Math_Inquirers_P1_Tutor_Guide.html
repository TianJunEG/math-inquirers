Math Inquirers Holiday Program

Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Program guide for tutors: schedule, lesson routine, ten minute-by-minute lesson scripts with answer keys, a progress tracker and a parent summary. Math Inquirers is online first: every lesson runs live on video; the face-to-face notes are kept for partner centres.

The program at a glance

Level
Primary 1 (ages 6 to 7), for children ready for more thinking than standard practice
Length
10 lessons of 90 minutes, two a week over the 5 weeks of the holidays
Group size
Up to 8 children per tutor, live online. Pilot with 6. Add a second tutor or helper above 8.
Focus
Thinking skills: spotting patterns, stating rules, working backwards, reasoning and explaining. Not speed drills.
The arc of every lesson
Start concrete with objects, record in a table, spot the pattern, state the rule, use it (often backwards), then explain why.
What children take home
A student workbook with an inquiry journal, 10 worksheets and a certificate

Every tutor signs the tutor confidentiality agreement before receiving any materials.

Keeping to 90 minutes: clock checks, flex time and the skip list

  • The plan has a standing buffer: every lesson except Lesson 10 ends with 2 minutes of flex time (88 to 90). If you are on time, use it for goodbyes. If you are behind, it absorbs the delay. Do not start anything new in it. Lesson 10 has no buffer: follow its shortened plan.
  • Clock checks: at the end of the sneak-peek puzzles (minute 12; minute 14 in Lesson 1, 10 in Lesson 10), at the start of the worksheet (minute 40; 41 in Lessons 3, 4 and 9; 42 in Lesson 1) and at minute 72. If you are more than 2 minutes behind at the start of the worksheet, use the skip list. Never shorten the worksheet below 28 minutes.
  • Skip list, in this order: quick questions 4 and 5; worksheet items marked “if you have time” or with a star (take-home); the last “your turn” slide of the Mini-lesson; the second explore debrief question; the pair-talk part of Talk time (go straight to volunteers); only then the exit-ticket reveal.

Teaching habits that matter

  • Ask “How do you know?” after every answer, right or wrong.
  • Wait 10 seconds before helping. Let children struggle a little.
  • Praise the thinking (“You checked it two ways”), not just the answer.
  • Let children disagree with each other and settle it with objects or a drawing.
  • If a child wants more, point to Think harder, Go further or the Investigation (any child may choose them), not extra drill.

Sneak-peek puzzles, choice time and look-back (every lesson)

  • Sneak-peek puzzles (4 minutes, from minute 8; 10 in Lesson 1, 6 in Lesson 10). Two questions on the lesson’s key idea. The first is an access question: every child can try it with objects, drawing or counting, so everyone starts with a win. The second makes you think. Say (one short line, about 26 words): “Two sneak-peek puzzles: ideas we have not learnt yet. A guess is a great start, and I do not know is welcome. Nobody is marked.” Children write or draw on their own paper. Everyone shows their paper ONCE, after the second question: face to face, each child holds their own paper low so that only the tutor sees it; online, everyone holds their paper to the camera at the same moment. There are no private chat messages. Never use boards held up in front of the group. Never write scores down or announce them.
  • Choice time. Every workbook has the same neutral line on page 1: “After question 3 you may choose: carry on, or try Think harder.” Any child may choose. There is no list of who is ahead, and no criterion is printed. The tutor never announces who is on which page, and never names a child in front of the group. The tutor invites the WHOLE group at once, so nobody is singled out, for example: “When you have finished Start here, you can choose Think harder, Go further or the Investigation.” Say the same to any child who asks. Pairs are not split unless both children agree. A child who finds Think harder too hard goes back to Start here, with no fuss.
  • Online with a group of 8, the tutor can see each child’s page because each child already holds it up to the camera. Above 8 children, the helper keeps an eye on the children who have moved on while the tutor works with the others.
  • Talk time. Ask for volunteers first. Never call a child by name to answer. A child may pass, show a drawing or let a partner read for them. No public tallies and no rankings: do not say who is first or who got most right.
  • Look-back (three minutes, minutes 85 to 88; 87 to 90 in Lesson 10). The box is on the Choose and look-back page of the workbook, which every child reaches: “Something I did well”, “The hardest part was ...”, “What helped me”, “Next time I will try:” (optional, drawing it, using objects, checking my answer, asking a question), with tick options that include “I asked for help” and “I am still stuck (my tutor will come and help)”. Passing is always allowed. The box is never collected, shown or read out in front of the group. The tutor may look at it afterwards. If a child ticks “I am still stuck”, check in during the open session: a quick visit to the shared breakout room or a hand signal, with a parent nearby. Never send a direct message. Praise the strategy (“Drawing it got you unstuck”), not the speed.
  • The Investigation is printed on the Choose and look-back page for every child, with an easy first step, and is optional. It has several valid answers. The key gives a model answer and what to look for. Accept any correct noticing, and ask “How do you know?”.

How to use the scripts

Every lesson below is a minute-by-minute script. The slides match it: one slide or two for each part. You do not have to say the words exactly, but keep the questions, because the questions are what makes children think.

SAY
Words to say aloud, in plain language for 6- and 7-year-olds. Said in your own voice, not read from the page.
DO
What you do: set up, hand out, show a slide, or move around the room.
ASK / EXPECT
Questions to put to the group, with the answer you are listening for. Let children answer before you explain.
IF STUCK
A ladder of help. Give hint 1, wait ten seconds, then give hint 2. Do not give the answer.

Five habits that run through every script

  • Predict first. Before children test anything, ask for a guess and write it down. A wrong guess is useful because we can test it.
  • Wait ten seconds after every question. Count silently. Most children answer in the last four.
  • Ask “How do you know?” after right answers too, so that children do not read your face for the answer.
  • Praise the thinking, not the answer: “You checked it two ways” beats “Well done”.
  • When an answer is wrong, say “Let us test that,” and use objects, not “No”.

Answering a wrong answer

Recording the lesson

Every live lesson is recorded so that a child who misses it can still catch up. A child watching alone cannot ask you a question, so the recording needs spoken cues, visible timers and quiet working time.

How to teach so the recording works

  • Say the short REC line at the start of each part, once, to the camera, in a friendly “recording voice”. Keep it under ten seconds. The children in the live room will simply hear it as a routine.
  • Say the workbook page and question number aloud every time, and read each question out loud as well as showing it.
  • Put a countdown timer or a clock slide on screen while children work, and say “the timer is running” so the recording shows how long to pause.
  • Repeat a child’s answer in your own words before you respond, so it is clear on the recording without relying on their voice.
  • Do not talk through the working time. Silence is fine; the child watching later pauses the video anyway.
  • Show the answer slides clearly and read each answer aloud, so a child can mark their own work.

Privacy and consent

  • Only record on the approved platform, and only with parent consent given at registration. Do not make your own recordings or photos.
  • Record the tutor and the shared screen only. Turn off or exclude the other children’s video in the recording (use active-speaker or screen-only recording), and keep children’s names off the screen.
  • Do not read out a child’s name together with a right or wrong answer. Do not show a child’s page to the camera in the recording.
  • Share the recording only through a private link to enrolled families, and delete it when the program ends, or after the period the parents agreed to.
  • Tell the group at the start of each lesson that it is being recorded and what is recorded. A parent or child who does not wish to be in it can keep the camera off.

These are suggested working rules. Make sure the parent consent wording, the tutor agreement and the platform settings match them before the first recorded lesson.

After the lesson

  • Add a chapter marker at each time in the chapter list at the end of the lesson, so a child can jump to the part they need.
  • Turn on captions if the platform offers them.
  • Put the workbook page numbers and the materials list in the description of the recording.
