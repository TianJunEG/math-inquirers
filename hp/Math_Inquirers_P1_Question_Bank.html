How to use this bank

Each lesson has three levels. Every child starts with Core, then moves up when they are ready. Challenge questions are the ones to talk about together.

CORE matches what Primary 1 children learn in school. Use it for warm-ups and for children who need confidence.

STRETCH goes ahead of the Primary 1 syllabus: two-step problems, patterns with a hidden rule, simple unknowns and larger numbers.

GEP-STYLE CHALLENGE questions are non-routine. They ask children to find every way, work backwards, test a case, justify an answer or notice what is missing. This is the style of thinking used in gifted-education-type problems. It is not an official GEP standard, and this program is not affiliated with MOE.

Ask for the reason every time: “How do you know?” A correct answer with no reason is only half done.

Answers are at the back. Do not hand the answer pages to children.

Lesson 1: Count with the Frog

Number Patterns • Week 1

CORE (Primary 1 syllabus, fluency)

C1. Write the next two numbers and the rule: 4, 7, 10, 13, __, __

C2. Write the next two numbers and the rule: 5, 10, 15, 20, __, __

C3. Write the next two numbers and the rule: 1, 5, 9, 13, __, __

C4. Write the next two numbers and the rule: 50, 45, 40, 35, __, __

C5. Write the next two numbers and the rule: 7, 9, 11, 13, __, __

C6. Write the next two numbers and the rule: 100, 90, 80, 70, __, __

STRETCH (ahead of grade, two steps)

S1. Fill in the missing numbers: 3, 8, __, 18, __, 28

S2. Fill in the missing numbers: 40, __, 28, __, 16, 10

S3. A pattern starts at 3 and adds 5 each time. What is the 8th number?

S4. A pattern starts at 2 and adds 4 each time. What is the 7th number?

S5. A pattern starts at 60 and takes away 5 each time. What is the 9th number?

S6. Is 30 in the pattern 4, 8, 12, 16 … ? How do you know?

S7. 5, 10, 15, 22, 25, 30. Which number does not belong? What should it be?

S8. Start at 50 and take away 7 each time. What is the first number below 20?

GEP-STYLE CHALLENGE (non-routine, justify, find every way)

G1. Frog A jumps in 3s from 0. Frog B jumps in 4s from 0. Which numbers up to 40 do both frogs land on? (Do not count 0.)

G2. I am in the counting patterns of 3s and of 5s. I am more than 10 and less than 40. Which numbers can I be?

G3. Ali saves $2 in week 1, $5 in week 2, $8 in week 3 and so on. In which week does he save $29? How much does he save in week 12?

G4. 2, 4, 7, 11, 16, __ . What is the next number? Explain the pattern.

G5. A staircase needs 4 blocks for 1 step, 7 blocks for 2 steps and 10 blocks for 3 steps. How many blocks for 6 steps? How many steps can be built with 31 blocks?

G6. A pattern adds a number bigger than 0 each time, its 5th number is 20, and it starts at a whole number bigger than 0. How many different patterns can you make? List the first number and the rule for each.

G7. Frog A starts at 3 and jumps 4 each time. Frog B starts at 5 and jumps 3 each time. Which numbers up to 40 do both frogs land on?

G8. 1, 2, 4, 7, 11, 16 … What is the 8th number? Explain how the pattern grows.

G9. Ali counts up from 2 in 3s. Mei counts up from 1 in 4s. Which numbers up to 50 are in both patterns?

Lesson 2: Number Bond Inquiry

Number Bonds • Week 1

CORE (Primary 1 syllabus, fluency)

C1. 10 = 3 + __

C2. 10 = __ + 8

C3. 10 = 6 + __

C4. 20 = 5 + __

C5. 20 = __ + 12

C6. 20 = 17 + __

STRETCH (ahead of grade, two steps)

S1. 5 + 4 = 6 + __

S2. 8 + 2 = 7 + __

S3. 9 + 6 = __ + 10

S4. 12 + 3 = 10 + __

S5. 4 + __ + 3 = 10

S6. __ + 6 + 6 = 20

S7. 6 + 4 = 10, so 16 + 4 = __ and 26 + 4 = __

S8. Write three different ways to make 12 with two numbers.

GEP-STYLE CHALLENGE (non-routine, justify, find every way)

G1. Put 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6 in the triangle so each side adds to 9. Corners 1, 2 and 3. What goes in the middle of each side?

G2. Corners 4, 5 and 6, and the numbers 1, 2, 3 in the middles. What do all the sides add to? Where does each middle number go?

G3. Two numbers add to 14. One is 2 more than the other. What are they?

G4. Two numbers add to 20. One is 6 more than the other. What are they?

G5. Ali has 12 stickers in two hands. The left hand has 4 more than the right. How many in each hand?

G6. Three different numbers from 3 to 7 add to 15. What can they be? Find both ways.

G7. How many different pairs make 8 if the order matters (0 + 8 and 8 + 0 are different)? How many if the order does not matter?

G8. Three different numbers from 1 to 7 add up to 10. Find every set of three.

G9. Put 1 to 6 in the magic triangle so each side adds to 11. What goes in the corners and middles?

G10. Ali has 20 stickers. He keeps 9 and shares the rest between Ben and Mei. Ben gets 3 more than Mei. How many does each get?

Lesson 3: Let’s Plan the Fruit Party

Picture Graphs • Week 2

CORE (Primary 1 syllabus, fluency)

C1. A picture graph shows: Apple 7, Banana 5, Orange 9, Grape 4 (1 picture = 1 child). Which fruit got the most votes?

C2. A picture graph shows: Apple 7, Banana 5, Orange 9, Grape 4 (1 picture = 1 child). Which fruit got the fewest votes?

C3. A picture graph shows: Apple 7, Banana 5, Orange 9, Grape 4 (1 picture = 1 child). How many more children chose orange than grape?

C4. A picture graph shows: Apple 7, Banana 5, Orange 9, Grape 4 (1 picture = 1 child). How many children voted altogether?

C5. Pets: Dog 6, Cat 4, Fish 6, Bird 2. Which two pets got the same number of votes?

C6. Pets: Dog 6, Cat 4, Fish 6, Bird 2. How many more children chose dog than bird?

STRETCH (ahead of grade, two steps)

S1. 1 picture = 2 children. Red has 3 pictures, blue has 5 and green has 4. How many children chose blue?

S2. 1 picture = 2 children. Red has 3 pictures, blue has 5 and green has 4. How many children voted altogether?

S3. 1 picture = 2 children. Red has 3 pictures and blue has 5. How many more children chose blue than red?

S4. 1 picture = 2 children. Six children chose pizza. How many pictures should we draw?

S5. 1 picture = 2 children. A half picture means 1 child. Apple has 3 pictures and a half. How many children chose apple?

S6. Banana has 3 pictures and apple has 8 pictures (1 picture = 1 child). How many more children chose apple?

GEP-STYLE CHALLENGE (non-routine, justify, find every way)

G1. 18 children voted. 7 chose apple and 5 chose orange. The rest chose grapes. How many chose grapes?

G2. Votes: A 8, B 5, C 6. Then 4 more children join and all choose B. Which fruit wins now, and by how many votes?

G3. 12 children voted for three fruits. No two fruits got the same number of votes. The most votes was 5. How many votes did each fruit get?

G4. Mei says 7 children chose apple. Banana got 4 fewer than apple and orange got 8. How many children voted?

G5. Our class chose grapes. Ben says, “So the whole school loves grapes.” Is he right? Give two reasons.

G6. 1 picture = 3 children. Apple has 4 pictures, banana 2 and orange 5. Then 7 more children join and all choose banana. Which fruit wins now, and by how many votes?

G7. 24 children voted. Apple got 6 votes. Orange got 6 more votes than banana. How many votes did banana and orange get?

G8. 1 picture = 2 children. 20 children voted for three fruits. A has 3 pictures. B has 2 more pictures than A. How many children chose C?

Repeating and Growing Patterns • Week 2

CORE (Primary 1 syllabus, fluency)

C2. The pattern is star, heart, star, heart, star, heart … What is the 9th one?

C3. The pattern is red, red, blue, red, red, blue, red … What is the 8th one?

C4. The pattern is A, B, C, D, A, B, C … What is the 11th one?

C5. The pattern is cat, dog, dog, cat, dog, dog, cat … What is the 12th one?

C6. The pattern is sun, moon, sun, moon, sun, moon … What is the 15th one?

STRETCH (ahead of grade, two steps)

S1. Pattern: circle, triangle, triangle, square, circle, triangle, triangle, square … What is the 14th shape?

S2. Same pattern: how many triangles are in the first 20 shapes?

S3. Same pattern: in which places do the squares come? Write the first five.

S4. Red, blue, blue, red, blue, blue … What colour is the 20th bead?

S5. Red, blue, blue, red, blue, blue … How many blue beads are in the first 12 beads?

S6. A pattern repeats every 5 shapes. Is the 25th shape the same as the 5th? Why?

GEP-STYLE CHALLENGE (non-routine, justify, find every way)

G1. Squares in a row use 4 sticks for 1 square, 7 sticks for 2 squares and 10 sticks for 3 squares. How many sticks for 8 squares?

G2. Using the same pattern, how many squares can you make with 22 sticks?

G3. Tiles: the 1st pattern has 3 tiles, the 2nd has 5, the 3rd has 7. How many tiles in the 10th pattern? Which pattern has 31 tiles?

G4. Triangles made of sticks in a row use 3, 5, 7 sticks. Ben has 25 sticks. How many triangles can he make? How many sticks are left over?

G5. A pattern repeats: star, moon, sun, star, moon, sun … What is the 100th shape? Explain how you know without drawing 100 shapes.

G6. Squares in a row use 4, 7, 10 … sticks. Ben has 50 sticks. How many squares can he make? How many sticks are left?

G7. Beads repeat in a pattern of 5: red, red, blue, blue, green. How many green beads are in the first 47 beads?

G8. Dots: 1, 3, 6, 10, 15 … What are the next two numbers? What is the 10th number?

Lesson 5: Mystery Number

Working Backwards • Week 3

CORE (Primary 1 syllabus, fluency)

C1. I think of a number. I add 3. I get 8. What is my number?

C2. I think of a number. I add 6. I get 14. What is my number?

C3. I think of a number. I add 5. I get 12. What is my number?

C4. I think of a number. I add 9. I get 17. What is my number?

C5. I think of a number. I take away 4. I get 6. What is my number?

C6. I think of a number. I take away 7. I get 3. What is my number?

STRETCH (ahead of grade, two steps)

S1. Ben had some marbles. He gave 5 to Mei, then won 3. Now he has 12. How many did he start with?

S2. Ben had some stickers. He gave away 6, got 4, then gave away 3. Now he has 10. How many did he have at first?

S3. A number machine adds the same number each time. IN 4 gives OUT 9. What is the rule? What is OUT when IN is 12? What is IN when OUT is 20?

S4. I think of a number. I add 4, then I take away 7. I get 8. What is my number?

S5. I think of a number. I add 5, then I take away 3. I get 9. What is my number?

S6. I think of a number. I add 6, then I take away 2. I get 10. What is my number?

S7. I think of a number. I add 9, then I take away 4. I get 12. What is my number?

GEP-STYLE CHALLENGE (non-routine, justify, find every way)

G1. I think of a number. I add 6, take away 4, then add 5. I get 17. What is my number?

G2. A number machine has two steps: add 3, then add 4. IN 5 gives OUT 12. What is IN when OUT is 20?

G3. Zoe is 6th from the front of a queue and 6th from the back. How many children are in the queue?

G4. Ben is 4th in a queue. There are 3 children behind him. How many children are in the queue?

G5. Ali has 8 marbles. Mei has 5 more than Ali. Ben has 6 fewer than Mei. How many marbles do the three have altogether?

G6. There are 20 children. 7 are boys. Of the girls, 4 are wearing red. How many girls are not wearing red?

G7. I think of a number. I add 7, take away 3, add 5, then take away 8. I get 14. What is my number?

G8. Ali and Ben have 20 marbles together. Ali has 4 more than Ben. How many marbles does each have?

G9. A snail climbs 3 cm each day and slides back 1 cm each night. It starts at the bottom of a 9 cm wall. On which day does it reach the top?

G6. Ali pays 55¢ with exactly 5 coins. He uses at least one 5¢, one 10¢ and one 20¢ coin. Which coins does he use?

G7. You have one 5¢, one 10¢, one 20¢ and one 50¢ coin. How many different amounts can you make using any of them?

G8. A sweet costs 15¢ and a biscuit costs 20¢. Ben spends exactly 90¢ and buys some of each. How many sweets and biscuits does he buy?

Lesson 7: Shape Safari

2D Shapes • Week 4

CORE (Primary 1 syllabus, fluency)

C1. How many sides and corners does a triangle have?

C2. How many sides and corners does a square have?

C3. Here is a pentagon (a shape with 5 straight sides). How many corners does it have?

C4. Here is a hexagon (a shape with 6 straight sides). How many corners does it have?

C5. Here is an octagon (a shape with 8 straight sides). How many corners does it have?

C6. I have no corners and no straight sides. What shape am I?

STRETCH (ahead of grade, two steps)

S1. Fold a square along a diagonal and cut. Which shapes do you get?

S2. Two same-size triangles can make a square. Can they also make a bigger triangle? Draw how.

S3. How many squares are in a grid 3 squares long and 2 squares high?

S4. Join 4 same-size squares, whole sides touching. How many different shapes can you make? (Turns and flips are the same shape.)

S5. I have 4 sides, all the same length, and 4 square corners. What shape am I?

S6. How many sides do 3 triangles have altogether?

S7. How many corners do 5 squares have altogether?

S8. A shape has 7 corners. How many sides has it?

S9. Draw a shape with 4 straight sides that is NOT a square. Is it a rectangle?

GEP-STYLE CHALLENGE (non-routine, justify, find every way)

G1. A square has both diagonals drawn. How many triangles can you find?

G2. A big triangle has 1 line from the top corner to the bottom. How many triangles? What about 2 lines? 3 lines?

G3. A square is split into a 2 by 2 grid of 4 small squares. How many squares can you find altogether?

G4. A square is split into a 3 by 3 grid of 9 small squares. How many squares are there altogether?

G5. Ali cuts a rectangle along one line and gets two triangles. Where did he cut?

G6. I have 6 sides. I can be made from 6 equal triangles. What shape am I?

G7. Three squares are joined in a row to make a long rectangle. How many rectangles can you find altogether? (A square counts as a rectangle.)

G8. A triangle has 4 lines drawn from the top corner to the bottom. How many triangles are there? Follow the pattern 3, 6, 10 …

G9. A square is cut into 4 equal squares. One of the small squares is cut into 4 even smaller squares. How many squares can you find altogether?

Lesson 8: Logic Lab

Logic and Sorting • Week 4

CORE (Primary 1 syllabus, fluency)

GEP-STYLE CHALLENGE (non-routine, justify, find every way)

G6. Five friends run a race. D is first. E is last. B finishes directly behind A. C is not second. In what order do they finish?

G7. A secret number is between 10 and 30. It is even, and you say it when counting in 3s. It is 2 more than a number you say when counting in 5s. What is it?

G8. Numbers 1 to 20. How many do we say only when counting in 2s, only in 3s, in both, and in neither?
