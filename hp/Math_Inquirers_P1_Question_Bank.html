How to use this bank

Each lesson has three levels. Every child starts with Core, then moves up when they are ready. Challenge questions are the ones to talk about together.

CORE questions are low floor: a child who has not yet learnt the topic can start them with objects, a drawing, counting or guess-and-check. No special method is needed. Use them for warm-ups and for children who need confidence.

THINK HARDER questions need the idea taught in the lesson, used in a new setting: two-step problems, patterns with a hidden rule, simple unknowns and larger numbers.

CHALLENGE questions are for children who have finished the topic. They need the lesson idea plus an extension: more than one step, finding every way, working backwards, justifying an answer, changing one thing and asking what happens, or making up a puzzle of their own. Open questions have no single right answer; check the reasoning. This uses low-floor, high-ceiling task ideas and problem-solving habits from gifted-education research. It does not identify gifted children, it is not an official GEP standard, and this program is not affiliated with MOE.

Ask for the reason every time: “How do you know?” A correct answer with no reason is only half done.

Answers are at the back. Do not hand the answer pages to children.

Lesson 1: Count with the Frog

Number Patterns • Week 1

CORE (low floor: start with objects, drawing or counting)

C1. A frog starts on 0 and hops 2 each time. Colour its landing places on a number strip up to 20 (or hop a counter). Write the numbers you coloured.

C2. Count in 5s. Fill the gaps: 5, 10, __, 20, __, 30

C3. 3, 6, 9, 12, __, __. Put a counter on 3 and hop to 6, then to 9. How many steps is each hop? Write the next two numbers.

C4. Write the next two numbers and the rule: 4, 7, 10, 13, __, __

C5. 50, 45, 40, 35, __, __. Are the numbers going up or down? Write the next two numbers.

C6. Box 1 has 2 stickers, box 2 has 4 stickers and box 3 has 6 stickers. Draw box 4 and box 5. How many stickers are in box 5?

C7. Count in 10s: 10, 20, 30, __, __. What is the rule?

C8. Mei counts 1, 3, 5, 7 … Will she say 10? Keep counting with her to find out.

THINK HARDER (use this lesson's idea in a new setting)

S1. Fill in the missing numbers: 3, 8, __, 18, __, 28

S2. Fill in the missing numbers: 40, __, 28, __, 16, 10

S3. Is 30 in the pattern 4, 8, 12, 16 … ? How do you know?

S4. Start at 50 and take away 7 each time. What is the first number below 20?

S5. Ali counts from 3 in 4s: 3, 7, 11, … Will he say 39? Will he say 40?

S6. 5, 10, 15, 22, 25, 30. One number is wrong. Which number does not belong? What should it be?

S7. An add pattern has 11 as its 3rd number and 19 as its 5th number. What is the rule? What is the 1st number?

S8. A pattern starts at 3 and adds 5 each time. What is the 8th number?

S9. A pattern starts at 60 and takes away 5 each time. What is the 9th number?

S10. Frog A jumps in 3s from 0. Frog B jumps in 4s from 0. Which numbers up to 40 do both frogs land on? (Do not count 0.)

S11. I am in the counting patterns of 3s and of 5s. I am more than 10 and less than 40. Which numbers can I be?

S12. Ali saves $2 in week 1, $5 in week 2, $8 in week 3 and so on. In which week does he save $29? How much does he save in week 12?

CHALLENGE (after the topic: extend, justify, make up your own)

G1. A pattern adds a number bigger than 0 each time, its 5th number is 20, and it starts at a whole number bigger than 0. How many different patterns can you make? List the first number and the rule for each.

G2. Frog A starts at 3 and jumps 4 each time. Frog B starts at 5 and jumps 3 each time. Which numbers up to 40 do both frogs land on?

G3. 30, 25, 21, 18, 16, __. What comes next? Describe how the gaps change.

G4. 1, 2, 4, 7, 11, 16 … What is the 8th number? Explain how the pattern grows.

G5. Ali counts up from 2 in 3s. Mei counts up from 1 in 4s. Which numbers up to 50 are in both patterns? What do you notice about the gaps between them?

G6. Make up your own two counting patterns: choose a start and a jump for each, so that they share some numbers up to 50. Write both patterns and ring the shared numbers. What do you notice about the gaps between the shared numbers?

Lesson 2: Number Bond Inquiry

Number Bonds • Week 1

CORE (low floor: start with objects, drawing or counting)

C1. You have 10 counters. 3 are red and the rest are yellow. How many are yellow? Write 10 = 3 + __

C2. A ten-frame has 6 counters in it. How many more fill it up? Write 10 = 6 + __

C3. A ten-frame has 8 counters in it. How many more fill it up? Write 10 = __ + 8

C4. A page has 20 stickers. 5 are stars and the rest are hearts. How many hearts? Write 20 = 5 + __

C5. A box holds 20 pencils. 12 are inside. How many more fit in? Write 20 = __ + 12

C6. 6 + 4 = 10. Count on from 16 on a number strip. What is 16 + 4?

C7. Two bags are the SAME. Bag A has 5 counters and 3 counters. Bag B has 4 counters and some more. Use counters to make the bags equal. How many more counters go in bag B?

C8. Ali has 12 stickers in his two hands. His left hand has 4 more than his right hand. Use counters: how many stickers are in each hand?

C9. Write three different pairs of numbers that make 10.

THINK HARDER (use this lesson's idea in a new setting)

S1. 5 + 4 = 6 + __

S2. 9 + 6 = __ + 10

S3. 6 + 4 = 10, so 16 + 4 = __ and 26 + 4 = __

S4. Find all the pairs of numbers from 1 to 11 that make 12. (3 + 9 and 9 + 3 count as the same pair.) How many pairs are there?

S5. 13 + 7 = 10 + __ = 4 + __

S6. □ + □ + 4 = 20, and both boxes are the same number. What is in the box?

S7. How many pairs make 20 if both numbers are from 5 to 15? (7 + 13 and 13 + 7 count once.)

S8. 8 + 2 = 7 + __

S9. 12 + 3 = 10 + __

S10. 4 + __ + 3 = 10

S11. __ + 6 + 6 = 20

S12. Write three different ways to make 12 with two numbers.

CHALLENGE (after the topic: extend, justify, make up your own)

G1. Three different numbers from 3 to 7 add to 15. What can they be? Find both ways.

G2. Three different numbers from 1 to 7 add up to 10. Find every set of three.

G3. How many different pairs make 8 if the order matters (0 + 8 and 8 + 0 are different)? How many if the order does not matter?

G4. Ben says: "I can put 1 to 6 in the magic triangle so that every side adds to 13." Is he right? Explain how you know.

G5. Put 1 to 6 in the magic triangle so each side adds to 11. What goes in the corners and middles?

G6. Ali has 20 stickers. He keeps 9 and shares the rest between Ben and Mei. Ben gets 3 more than Mei. How many does each get?

G7. Change the magic triangle: use the numbers 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 and 7 instead of 1 to 6. Can you make every side add to the same total? Try it. Which totals can you make?

Lesson 3: Let’s Plan the Fruit Party

Picture Graphs • Week 2

CORE (low floor: start with objects, drawing or counting)

C1. Draw a picture graph with one dot for each child: Apple 7, Banana 5, Orange 9, Grape 4. Which fruit got the most votes?

C2. A picture graph shows: Apple 7, Banana 5, Orange 9, Grape 4 (1 picture = 1 child). Which fruit got the fewest votes?

C3. A picture graph shows: Apple 7, Banana 5, Orange 9, Grape 4 (1 picture = 1 child). How many more children chose orange than grape? (Line the dots up side by side.)

C4. A picture graph shows: Apple 7, Banana 5, Orange 9, Grape 4 (1 picture = 1 child). How many children voted altogether? (Point to each dot as you count.)

C5. Draw a picture graph for the pets: Dog 6, Cat 4, Fish 6, Bird 2 (1 picture = 1 child). Which two pets got the same number of votes?

C6. Pets: Dog 6, Cat 4, Fish 6, Bird 2. How many more children chose dog than bird?

C7. Ask 8 people in your family or group: "Cat or dog?" Make a mark for each vote. Which got more? By how many?

THINK HARDER (use this lesson's idea in a new setting)

S1. 1 picture = 2 children. Red has 3 pictures, blue has 5 and green has 4. How many children chose blue?

S2. 1 picture = 2 children. Red has 3 pictures, blue has 5 and green has 4. How many children voted altogether?

S3. 1 picture = 2 children. Six children chose pizza. How many pictures should we draw?

S4. 1 picture = 2 children. A half picture means 1 child. Apple has 3 pictures and a half. How many children chose apple?

S5. Key: ● = 2 children. Apple ●●●, Banana ●●●●●, Orange ●●. How many more chose banana than orange?

S6. 25 children voted: apple 8, banana 6, grape 4, and the rest chose orange. Draw the orange row. Which fruit won?

S7. The key is missing. Apple has 4 pictures and 12 children chose apple. Banana has 3 pictures. How many chose banana?

S8. 1 picture = 2 children. Red has 3 pictures and blue has 5. How many more children chose blue than red?

CHALLENGE (after the topic: extend, justify, make up your own)

G1. Votes: A 8, B 5, C 6. Then 4 more children join and all choose B. Which fruit wins now, and by how many votes?

G2. 1 picture = 3 children. Apple has 4 pictures, banana 2 and orange 5. Then 7 more children join and all choose banana. Which fruit wins now, and by how many votes?

G3. 12 children voted for three fruits. No two fruits got the same number of votes. The most votes was 5. How many votes did each fruit get?

G4. Class A has 10 children: apple 6, orange 4. Class B has 20 children: apple 8, orange 12. Which fruit won in each class? Now put both classes together: which fruit wins, and by how many votes? Is that surprising?

G5. 24 children voted. Apple got 6 votes. Orange got 6 more votes than banana. How many votes did banana and orange get?

G6. 1 picture = 2 children. 20 children voted for three fruits. A has 3 pictures. B has 2 more pictures than A. How many children chose C?

G7. Make up your own: 15 children vote for three fruits. Draw a picture graph where banana wins by exactly 2 votes (banana has 2 more than the fruit in second place) and no two fruits are tied. Then try to find a second graph that works.

Lesson 4: Pattern Inquiry

Repeating and Growing Patterns • Week 2

CORE (low floor: start with objects, drawing or counting)

C1. The pattern is circle, square, triangle, circle, square, triangle, circle … What is the 10th one? (Keep writing the pattern until you get there.)

C2. The pattern is star, heart, star, heart, star, heart … What is the 9th one?

C3. The pattern is red, red, blue, red, red, blue, red … What is the 8th one?

C4. The pattern is A, B, C, D, A, B, C … What is the 11th one? (Write the letters in a row and number them.)

C5. The pattern is cat, dog, dog, cat, dog, dog, cat … What is the 12th one?

C6. The pattern is sun, moon, sun, moon, sun, moon … What is the 15th one?

C7. Build 1 triangle with 3 sticks. Add a second triangle next to it. How many sticks did you use? Now add a third triangle. How many sticks in all?

THINK HARDER (use this lesson's idea in a new setting)

S1. Pattern: circle, triangle, triangle, square, circle, triangle, triangle, square … What is the 14th shape?

S2. Same pattern: how many triangles are in the first 20 shapes?

S3. Red, blue, blue, red, blue, blue … What colour is the 20th bead?

S4. Squares in a row need 4, 7, 10, … sticks. With 20 sticks, how many squares can you make? How many sticks are left over?

S5. star, circle, circle, triangle, star, circle, circle, triangle … What is the 23rd shape?

S6. A, B, B, C, A, B, B, C, … How many Bs are in the first 18 letters?

S7. A pattern repeats every 5 shapes. Is the 25th shape the same as the 5th? Why?

S8. Pattern: circle, triangle, triangle, square, circle, triangle, triangle, square … In which places do the squares come? Write the first five.

S9. Red, blue, blue, red, blue, blue … How many blue beads are in the first 12 beads?

S10. Tiles: the 1st pattern has 3 tiles, the 2nd has 5, the 3rd has 7. How many tiles in the 10th pattern? Which pattern has 31 tiles?

S11. Triangles made of sticks in a row use 3, 5, 7 sticks. Ben has 25 sticks. How many triangles can he make? How many sticks are left over?

S12. Squares in a row use 4, 7, 10 … sticks. Ben has 50 sticks. How many squares can he make? How many sticks are left?

S13. A pattern repeats: star, moon, sun, star, moon, sun … What is the 100th shape? Explain how you know without drawing 100 shapes.

CHALLENGE (after the topic: extend, justify, make up your own)

G1. Squares in a row use 4, 7, 10 … sticks. Mei says she can make a row of squares using EXACTLY 30 sticks. Can she? What about exactly 31 sticks? Explain how you know.

G2. Pattern A repeats star, moon, sun. Pattern B repeats red, blue. Put them together: the 1st place is a red star, the 2nd a blue moon, the 3rd a red sun … What is the 20th place? When do we first see a red star again?

G3. Beads repeat in a pattern of 5: red, red, blue, blue, green. How many green beads are in the first 47 beads?

G4. Dots: 1, 3, 6, 10, 15 … What are the next two numbers? What is the 10th number?

G5. Make up your own repeating pattern with a core of 5 beads (use up to 3 colours). Ask a partner: what colour is the 33rd bead? Write the answer and how you know.

Lesson 5: Mystery Number

Working Backwards • Week 3

CORE (low floor: start with objects, drawing or counting)

C1. I think of a number. I add 3. I get 8. What is my number? (Hide counters under a cup and check.)

C2. I think of a number. I add 6. I get 14. What is my number?

C3. I think of a number. I add 5. I get 12. What is my number?

C4. I think of a number. I add 9. I get 17. What is my number?

C5. I think of a number. I take away 4. I get 6. What is my number?

C6. I think of a number. I take away 7. I get 3. What is my number?

C7. Ben is 4th in a queue. There are 3 children behind him. Draw the queue. How many children are in it?

C8. There are 20 children in a queue. Mei is 7th from the front. Draw it. How many children are behind her?

THINK HARDER (use this lesson's idea in a new setting)

S1. Ben had some marbles. He gave 5 to Mei, then won 3. Now he has 12. How many did he start with?

S2. Ben had some stickers. He gave away 6, got 4, then gave away 3. Now he has 10. How many did he have at first?

S3. A number machine adds the same number each time. IN 4 gives OUT 9. What is the rule? What is OUT when IN is 12? What is IN when OUT is 20?

S4. I think of a number. I add 4, then I take away 7. I get 8. What is my number?

S5. Tom is 5th from the front. There are 4 children between Tom and Sue, who is last. How many are in the queue?

S6. Mei had some sweets. Her mum gave her 6 more. Then she ate 4. Now she has 9. How many did she have at first?

S7. I think of a number. I take away 5, then I add 8. I get 14. What is my number?

S8. I think of a number. I add 5, then I take away 3. I get 9. What is my number?

S9. I think of a number. I add 6, then I take away 2. I get 10. What is my number?

S10. I think of a number. I add 9, then I take away 4. I get 12. What is my number?

CHALLENGE (after the topic: extend, justify, make up your own)

G1. Zoe is 6th from the front of a queue and 6th from the back. How many children are in the queue? What if a child is 10th from the front and 10th from the back? Can you find a quick way?

G2. Ali and Ben have 20 marbles together. Ali has 4 more than Ben. How many marbles does each have?

G3. I think of a number. I add 7, take away 3, add 5, then take away 8. I get 14. What is my number? Can you find a shortcut?

G4. A number machine has two steps: add 3, then add 4. IN 5 gives OUT 12. What goes IN to give OUT 20? Can you make a one-step machine that does the same job?

G5. A snail climbs 3 cm each day and slides back 1 cm each night. It starts at the bottom of a 9 cm wall. On which day does it reach the top?

G6. Make up a mystery number story with THREE steps whose answer is 10. Write the story. Show how a partner can work backwards to find 10, and how they can check by going forwards.

Lesson 6: Coin Combos

Money • Week 3

CORE (low floor: start with objects, drawing or counting)

C1. Pay exactly 15¢ using two coins from 5¢, 10¢, 20¢ and 50¢. (Use play coins.)

C2. Pay exactly 40¢ using two coins from 5¢, 10¢, 20¢ and 50¢.

C3. Pay exactly 55¢ using two coins from 5¢, 10¢, 20¢ and 50¢.

C4. How many different ways can you make 20¢ using only 10¢ and 5¢ coins? Use play coins and write each way.

C5. Ali has a 20¢ coin, a 10¢ coin and a 5¢ coin. How much money does he have?

C6. Mei has two 20¢ coins and one 5¢ coin. How much money does she have?

C7. Pay exactly $15 with the fewest notes. The notes are $2, $5, $10 and $50. (Use play notes.)

C8. Pay exactly $17 with the fewest notes from $2, $5 and $10. Which notes do you use?

THINK HARDER (use this lesson's idea in a new setting)

S1. Make 70¢ using exactly 4 coins from 5¢, 10¢, 20¢ and 50¢. Find every set.

S2. Sam has only 10¢ and 20¢ coins. He pays exactly 90¢ using 6 coins. How many of each coin does he use?

S3. What is the fewest number of coins to make 45¢?

S4. How many ways can you make 25¢ using 20¢, 10¢ and 5¢ coins?

S5. How many ways can you make 40¢ with 20¢, 10¢ and 5¢ coins?

S6. Pay exactly $12 using exactly 3 notes or coins from $1, $2, $5 and $10. Find every way.

S7. What is the fewest number of coins for 95¢ (5¢, 10¢, 20¢, 50¢)? How do you know fewer is not possible?

S8. What is the fewest number of coins to make 65¢ from 5¢, 10¢, 20¢ and 50¢?

S9. Ali buys a ruler for 35¢. He pays with a 50¢ coin. Which coins could he get back as change?

S10. A bag costs $34. Ali pays with a $50 note. How much change does he get?

CHALLENGE (after the topic: extend, justify, make up your own)

G1. You have one 5¢, one 10¢ and one 20¢ coin. How many different totals can you make using one, two or all three coins? List them.

G2. Which amounts in 5¢ steps (5¢, 10¢, 15¢ … 50¢) cannot be made using only 20¢ and 10¢ coins? What do you notice?

G3. Ali pays 55¢ with exactly 5 coins. He uses at least one 5¢, one 10¢ and one 20¢ coin. Which coins does he use?

G4. You have one 5¢, one 10¢, one 20¢ and one 50¢ coin. How many different amounts can you make using any of them? What if you also had a $1 coin (100¢)?

G5. A sweet costs 15¢ and a biscuit costs 20¢. Ben spends exactly 90¢ and buys some of each. How many sweets and biscuits does he buy?

G6. Make up your own coin puzzle that has exactly ONE answer (for example: "exactly 3 coins that make 35¢"). Write it, swap with a partner and check their answer by listing every possibility.

Lesson 7: Shape Safari

2D Shapes • Week 4

CORE (low floor: start with objects, drawing or counting)

C1. How many sides and corners does a triangle have? Draw one and count.

C2. How many sides and corners does a square have? Draw one and count.

C3. Draw a pentagon (a shape with 5 straight sides). How many corners does it have?

C4. Draw a hexagon (a shape with 6 straight sides). How many corners does it have?

C5. I have no corners and no straight sides. What shape am I?

C6. I have 3 straight sides and 3 corners. What shape am I?

C7. Fold a square piece of paper from corner to corner and cut along the fold. Which shapes do you get?

THINK HARDER (use this lesson's idea in a new setting)

S1. Two same-size triangles can make a square. Can they also make a bigger triangle? Draw how.

S2. How many squares are in a grid 3 squares long and 2 squares high?

S3. A square has both diagonals drawn (lines joining opposite corners). How many triangles can you find? Count the small ones first.

S4. Draw a shape with 4 straight sides that is NOT a square. Is it a rectangle? How do you know?

S5. Three squares are joined in a row to make a long rectangle. How many rectangles can you find altogether? (A square counts as a rectangle.)

S6. How many rectangles are in a grid 2 squares long and 2 squares high? (Squares count too.)

S7. Cut a square along both diagonals to get 4 same-size triangles. Can you put all 4 together to make a bigger triangle? Can you make a square? Draw them.

S8. How many squares are in a grid 3 squares long and 3 squares high?

S9. Ali cuts a rectangle along one line and gets two triangles. Where did he cut?

CHALLENGE (after the topic: extend, justify, make up your own)

G1. Join 4 same-size squares, whole sides touching. How many different shapes can you make? (Turns and flips are the same shape.) How do you know you have found them all?

G2. How many squares are in a grid 4 squares long and 2 squares high? What about 4 long and 3 high?

G3. A big triangle has 1 line from the top corner to the bottom. How many triangles? What about 2 lines? 3 lines?

G4. A triangle has 4 lines drawn from the top corner to the bottom. How many triangles are there? Follow the pattern 3, 6, 10 … What would 5 lines give?

G5. A square is cut into 4 equal squares. One of the small squares is cut into 4 even smaller squares. How many squares can you find altogether?

G6. Draw your own triangle-counting puzzle: a big triangle with some lines inside. Count the triangles. Swap with a partner. Do you both get the same number? If not, find out who missed some.

Lesson 8: Logic Lab

Logic and Sorting • Week 4

CORE (low floor: start with objects, drawing or counting)

C1. Ali is taller than Ben. Ben is taller than Mei. Who is the tallest? Who is the shortest? (Draw three people.)

C2. The cat is heavier than the hen. The hen is heavier than the mouse. Who is the lightest?

C3. Red, blue and green ribbons. Red is longer than blue. Green is shorter than blue. Which ribbon is the longest?

C4. Ben stands in front of Mei. Mei stands in front of Sam. Who is in the middle?

C5. Odd numbers end in 1, 3, 5, 7 or 9. Which number is the odd one out: 2, 4, 6, 9, 10? Why?

C6. Odd numbers end in 1, 3, 5, 7 or 9. Even numbers end in 0, 2, 4, 6 or 8. Sort these numbers into odd and even: 3, 8, 5, 12, 7, 10.

C7. Use two loops of string. Put "red things" in one loop and "round things" in the other. Where does a red ball go?

THINK HARDER (use this lesson's idea in a new setting)

S1. In a race, Sam is ahead of Tia. Tia is ahead of Raj. Mei is ahead of Sam. Who is third?

S2. Ana, Bo and Cy each have a different toy: a ball, a kite or a yo-yo. Bo does not have the kite. Cy has the ball. Who has which toy?

S3. Numbers 1 to 12. Which do we say when counting in 2s? Which when counting in 3s? Which in both?

S4. Numbers 1 to 12. Which do we NOT say when counting in 2s or when counting in 3s?

S5. Ali, Ben, Cara and Dan each have a different pet: a cat, a dog, a fish or a rabbit. Cara has the dog or the fish. Cara does not have the dog. Dan does not have the cat or the rabbit. Ali does not have the cat. Who has which pet?

S6. Five children stand in a line. Ali is in the middle. Ben is just in front of Ali. Cara is last. Dan is not first. Where is Eve?

S7. Numbers 1 to 30: which do you say when counting in 2s AND when counting in 5s?

S8. Ali, Ben and Mei have a cat, a dog and a fish. Ali has no dog. Mei has no fish. Ben has neither a cat nor a fish. Who has which pet?

S9. Five friends run a race. D is first. E is last. B finishes directly behind A. C is not second. In what order do they finish?

CHALLENGE (after the topic: extend, justify, make up your own)

G1. Three boxes are labelled "Apples", "Oranges" and "Mixed". Every label is wrong. You may take one fruit from one box. Which box should you pick from, and why?

G2. A number between 20 and 40 is odd, and we say it when counting in 5s. It is also one less than a number we say when counting in 4s. What is it?

G3. Ali, Ben and Mei stand in a line. List every different order they can stand in. How many are there? How do you know you have them all? If Ben must NOT be first, how many orders are left?

G4. A secret number is between 10 and 30. It is even, and you say it when counting in 3s. It is 2 more than a number you say when counting in 5s. What is it?

G5. Numbers 1 to 20. Sort them in a two-loop picture with the loops "I say it counting in 2s" and "I say it counting in 3s". How many numbers go in each part? Then use the loops "counting in 2s" and "counting in 4s". What is different, and why?

G6. Make up your own "Who has which pet?" puzzle with three friends and three pets. Write clues so that there is only ONE answer. Swap with a partner and check that they find your answer.

Lesson 9: Measure It!

Length • Week 5

CORE (low floor: start with objects, drawing or counting)

C1. A pencil is 7 cubes long. A pen is 10 cubes long. How many cubes longer is the pen?

C2. A ribbon is 12 cubes long. You cut off 5 cubes. How long is the piece left?

C3. Ali’s book is 9 cubes long and Mei’s book is 6 cubes long. Whose is longer? By how much?

C4. Put in order from shortest: 11 cubes, 7 cubes, 9 cubes, 12 cubes.

C5. A ribbon lies from the 2 cm mark to the 7 cm mark on a ruler. How long is it? (Put a finger on 2 and hop to 7, counting each space.)

C6. A string is 9 cubes long. A rope is 4 cubes longer. How long is the rope?

C7. Line up cubes beside your shoe with no gaps. Guess first: how many cubes long is it? Then count.

THINK HARDER (use this lesson's idea in a new setting)

S1. A ribbon lies from the 3 cm mark to the 11 cm mark on a ruler. How long is it?
