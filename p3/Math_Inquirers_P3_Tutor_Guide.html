Program guide for tutors: schedule, lesson routine, ten minute-by-minute lesson scripts with answer keys, a progress tracker and a parent summary. Math Inquirers is online first: every lesson runs live on video; the face-to-face notes are kept for partner centres.

The program at a glance

Level
Children finishing Primary 3 (ages 9 to 10), going into P4, who are ready for more thinking than standard practice
Length
10 lessons of 90 minutes, two a week over the 5 weeks of the holidays
Group size
Up to 8 children per tutor, live online. Pilot with 6. Add a second tutor or helper above 8.
Focus
Thinking skills: making a list in order, guess and check, drawing a model, working backwards, finding patterns, reasoning and explaining. Not speed drills.
The arc of every lesson
Start concrete with objects, record in a table, spot the pattern, state the rule, use it (often backwards), then explain why.
What children take home
A student workbook with an inquiry journal, 10 worksheets and a certificate

Every tutor signs the tutor confidentiality agreement before receiving any materials.

Teaching habits that matter

  • Ask “How do you know?” after every answer, right or wrong.
  • Wait 10 seconds before helping. Let children struggle a little.
  • Praise the thinking (“You checked it two ways”), not just the answer.
  • Let children disagree with each other and settle it with objects or a drawing.
  • If a child finishes early, use the Challenge or Extension question, not extra drill.

Preparing the materials

  • Send the workbook PDF to parents one week before. Ask them to print it single-sided and the cut-out pages on card if they can.
  • Cut-outs used: digit cards (Lessons 1, 2), fraction wall (Lesson 6), square grid (Lesson 7), right-angle tester (Lesson 8), play coins and notes (Lesson 9).
  • Keep the answer key with the tutor only. Check answers together on screen.
  • Use the shared screen or a digital whiteboard for the mini-lesson table.

Setting up online

  • Send parents the workbook and materials list one week before. Needed: the printed workbook and cut-outs, scissors, a ruler, paper and a pencil.
  • Use one approved platform with breakout rooms and screen share. Admit only registered children and never share the link publicly.
  • Ask the parent to sit nearby but not give answers. The camera should show the child’s page and hands.
  • Hold up the answer page to the camera at the end of each lesson, or take a photo and send it through the approved channel.
  • Do not record lessons (this is in the tutor agreement). Add a second tutor or helper above 8 children.

How to use the scripts

Every lesson below is a minute-by-minute script. The slides match it: one slide or two for each part. You do not have to say the words exactly, but keep the questions, because the questions are what makes children think.

SAY
Words to say aloud, in plain language for 8- and 9-year-olds. Said in your own voice, not read from the page.
DO
What you do: set up, hand out, show a slide, or move around the room.
ASK / EXPECT
Questions to put to the group, with the answer you are listening for. Let children answer before you explain.
IF STUCK
A ladder of help. Give hint 1, wait ten seconds, then give hint 2. Do not give the answer.

Five habits that run through every script

  • Predict first. Before children test anything, ask for a guess and write it down. A wrong guess is useful because we can test it.
  • Wait ten seconds after every question. Count silently. Most children answer in the last four.
  • Ask “How do you know?” after right answers too, so that children do not read your face for the answer.
  • Praise the thinking, not the answer: “You checked it two ways” beats “Well done”.
  • When an answer is wrong, say “Let us test that,” and use objects, not “No”.
