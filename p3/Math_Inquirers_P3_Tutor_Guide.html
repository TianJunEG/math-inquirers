Math Inquirers Holiday Program

Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Program guide for tutors: schedule, lesson routine, ten minute-by-minute lesson scripts with answer keys, a progress tracker and a parent summary. Math Inquirers is online first: every lesson runs live on video; the face-to-face notes are kept for partner centres.

The program at a glance

Level
Children aged 8 to 10 who are in or have finished Primary 3 maths, and who are ready for more thinking than standard practice
Length
10 lessons of 90 minutes, two a week over the 5 weeks of the holidays
Group size
Up to 8 children per tutor, live online. Pilot with 6. Add a second tutor or helper above 8.
Focus
Thinking skills: making a list in order, guess and check, drawing a model, working backwards, finding patterns, reasoning and explaining. Not speed drills.
The arc of every lesson
Start concrete with objects, record in a table, spot the pattern, state the rule, use it (often backwards), then explain why.
What children take home
A student workbook with an inquiry journal, 10 worksheets and a certificate
Before tutors start
Every tutor signs the tutor confidentiality agreement before receiving any materials.

Keeping to 90 minutes

  • Talk time holds a named 3-minute float. Spend it on the second discussion question and the return to sneak-peek puzzle 2 when you are on time. When you are behind, take it from talk time first.
  • Never cut the sneak-peek puzzles, the exit ticket, the look back, the stamp or the certificates.
  • If you are 3 or more minutes behind at the start of the worksheet or at the start of talk time, cut in this order: (1) quick questions from 3 to 2; (2) the second game round; (3) the check of page 2 becomes a one-question self-check; (4) the Core questions marked (if time) and the teaching slides for them; (5) talk time to one question.
  • Plan the brain break as 2 minutes of movement, 2 of water and 1 to settle. Ask families to use the toilet before the break starts.
  • Pairs sit in pre-assigned breakout rooms for the whole lesson. Use auto-move into rooms and auto-close with a timer so each move takes about 45 seconds, and say “Back in 3, 2, 1” rather than going to fetch each pair.
  • Send a “what is on your desk” checklist with the one-week-before message, so that children have their materials ready and nobody spends the first minutes hunting for scissors.

Teaching habits that matter

  • Ask “How do you know?” after every answer, right or wrong. Never mark or compare children in front of the group.
  • Wait 10 seconds before helping. Let children struggle a little.
  • Praise the thinking (“You checked it two ways”), not just the answer.
  • Let children disagree with each other and settle it with objects or a drawing.
  • If a child wants more, point to Think harder, Go further or the Investigation, not extra drill. Never label children.

Sneak-peek puzzles, choice and look-back (every lesson)

  • Sneak-peek puzzles (about 3 minutes at the start of the warm-up block): two small puzzles on ideas we have not learnt yet. Puzzle 1 is an access puzzle that any child can try with objects, a drawing or counting, so everyone has a first win. Puzzle 2 is a thinking puzzle. Say: “These are ideas we have not learnt yet. Guessing and I do not know are welcome. Nobody is marked.” Children write both answers on their own paper and, when you say “show me” once for both puzzles, show them only to you on camera (or all hold paper to the camera at the same moment, so nobody is singled out); never read out.
  • Choice, not sorting: there are no public labels. Every child gets the same Start here pages. After question 3 of page 1 any child may carry on, or try Think harder (and Go further). The Investigation is open to everyone; its easy first step is printed on page 2 of Start here and on the Investigation page. You invite the WHOLE group, never one child: “When you have finished Start here you can choose Think harder, Go further or the Investigation.” You never announce who is on which page, and pairs are not split unless both children agree.
  • Go further on the explore slide: a second what-if question is on the explore slide from the start so that nobody waits. Any pair may take it up when their first task is done.
  • Talk time: ask for volunteers first. A pass is always fine, and so is sharing with a partner. Do not cold-call by name. Do not keep public tallies or rankings.
  • Look back (about 3 minutes: 2 for the children, then a short share): Something I did well, The hardest part was, What helped me. A pass is always allowed. The page is never collected or read out; children keep it. A child who ticks “I am still stuck” gets a check-in in the open session: a quick visit to the shared breakout room or a hand signal, with a parent nearby. Never a private message.

Preparing the materials

  • Send the workbook PDF to parents one week before. Ask them to print it single-sided and the cut-out pages on card if they can. Lesson 6: ask them to cut out the fraction-wall strips before the lesson.
  • Cut-outs used: digit cards (Lessons 1, 2), fraction wall (Lesson 6), square grid (Lesson 7), right-angle tester (Lesson 8), play coins and notes (Lesson 9).
  • Keep the answer key with the tutor only. Check answers together on screen.
  • Use the shared screen or a digital whiteboard for the mini-lesson table.

  • Send parents the workbook and materials list one week before. Needed: the printed workbook and cut-outs, scissors, a ruler, paper and a pencil.
  • Use one approved platform with breakout rooms and screen share. The tutor visits every breakout room at least once in each pair activity, so no pair works unseen for the whole activity. Admit only registered children and never share the link publicly. Assign the pairs to breakout rooms before the lesson starts and use auto-move and auto-close with a timer.
  • Ask the parent to sit nearby but not give answers. The camera should show the child’s page and hands.

How to use the scripts

Every lesson below is a minute-by-minute script. The slides match it: one slide or two for each part. You do not have to say the words exactly, but keep the questions, because the questions are what makes children think.

SAY
Words to say aloud, in plain language for 8- and 9-year-olds. Said in your own voice, not read from the page.
DO
What you do: set up, hand out, show a slide, or move around the room.
ASK / EXPECT
Questions to put to the group, with the answer you are listening for. Let children answer before you explain.
IF STUCK
A ladder of help. Give hint 1, wait ten seconds, then give hint 2. Do not give the answer.

Five habits that run through every script

  • Predict first. Before children test anything, ask for a guess and write it down. A wrong guess is useful because we can test it.
  • Wait ten seconds after every question. Count silently. Most children answer in the last four.
  • Ask “How do you know?” after right answers too, so that children do not read your face for the answer.
  • Praise the thinking, not the answer: “You checked it two ways” beats “Well done”.
  • When an answer is wrong, say “Let us test that,” and use objects, not “No”.

Answering a wrong answer

Recording the lesson

Every live lesson is recorded so that a child who misses it can still catch up. A child watching alone cannot ask you a question, so the recording needs spoken cues, visible timers and quiet working time.

How to teach so the recording works

  • Say the short REC line at the start of each part, once, to the camera, in a friendly “recording voice”. Keep it under ten seconds. The children in the live room will simply hear it as a routine.
  • Say the workbook page and question number aloud every time, and read each question out loud as well as showing it.
  • Put a countdown timer or a clock slide on screen while children work, and say “the timer is running” so the recording shows how long to pause.
  • Repeat a child’s answer in your own words before you respond, so it is clear on the recording without relying on their voice.
  • Do not talk through the working time. Silence is fine; the child watching later pauses the video anyway.
  • Show the answer slides clearly and read each answer aloud, so a child can mark their own work.

Privacy and consent

  • Only record on the approved platform, and only with parent consent given at registration. Do not make your own recordings or photos.
  • Record the tutor and the shared screen only. Turn off or exclude the other children’s video in the recording (use active-speaker or screen-only recording), and keep children’s names off the screen.
  • Do not read out a child’s name together with a right or wrong answer. Do not show a child’s page to the camera in the recording.
  • Share the recording only through a private link to enrolled families, and delete it when the program ends, or after the period the parents agreed to.
  • Tell the group at the start of each lesson that it is being recorded and what is recorded. A parent or child who does not wish to be in it can keep the camera off.

These are suggested working rules. Make sure the parent consent wording, the tutor agreement and the platform settings match them before the first recorded lesson.

After the lesson

  • Add a chapter marker at each time in the chapter list at the end of the lesson, so a child can jump to the part they need.
  • Turn on captions if the platform offers them.
  • Put the workbook page numbers and the materials list in the description of the recording.
