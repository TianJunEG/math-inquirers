How to help

  1. Ask before you tell. Start with "What do you notice?" and let your child answer first.
  2. Use real things. Coins, notes, cups, string, paper strips or blocks. Try it with objects or a drawing before writing.
  3. Give hints in three steps. Look again. Try it with objects. Try a smaller example. Say the answer last.
  4. Ask "How do you know?" even when the answer is right. The reason matters as much as the answer.
  5. Praise the thinking. "I like how you checked that" works better than "You are clever".
  6. Keep it short. Ten minutes and two or three questions is enough. Stop while it is still fun.
  7. Answers are at the back. Look only after your child has tried.
Hard question? Great. Getting stuck is where the thinking happens.

One dose a week

Each week has two lessons and about 18 questions. Do two or three a day, Monday to Friday, and keep the weekend for the Challenge questions if your child enjoys them.

The three levels

Core is for everyone. Think Harder stretches the idea. Challenge is for strong thinkers, and it is fine to leave it for another day.

What your child is doing in class

Ten 90-minute live online lessons over five weeks, in small groups. Each lesson starts with a hands-on puzzle, then a short teaching part, then the workbook. These pages practise the same ideas at home.

Lesson 1 • Numbers to 10 000

Number Sleuths

Core
1
Write the number. Think of 6 big boxes of 1000 sweets, 4 packets of 10 sweets and 9 loose sweets. (There are no boxes of 100.)
2
Look at the place value chart: Th 2 | H 8 | T 3 | O 7. This is the number 2837. How much is the digit 8 worth?
3
Use the cards 9, 1, 4, 6 once each. Make the smallest 4-digit number.
4
What number is 10 less than 5003?
Think Harder
5
Use the cards 5, 0, 8, 2 once each. Make the greatest even number and the smallest odd number.
6
How many 4-digit numbers can you make with the cards 1, 1, 0, 0? List them.
7
A number has 7 thousands, 13 hundreds and 6 ones. What is the number?
Challenge
8
Ali writes a 4-digit number using only odd digits, all different. It is as close to 5000 as possible. What is it?
9
A pattern starts at 1000 and goes up by 7 each time: 1000, 1007, 1014, … Is 1100 in the pattern? Explain.

What your child learns

4-digit numbers: place value, the greatest and smallest numbers from digit cards, listing every number in order, and number patterns.

Tip for you

Write four digits on scraps of paper. Ask for the greatest odd number, then the number closest to 5000.

Ask

"How do you know you have found them all?"

Lesson 2 • Addition and Subtraction

Cryptarithm Crackers

Core
1
A library has 3999 storybooks and 2456 comic books. How many books are there altogether? Write the numbers in a Th H T O grid and add the ones first.
2
A shop has 7000 stickers and sells 2468 of them. How many are left? Hint: draw a number line from 2468 to 7000 and jump to 2500, then to 3000, then to 7000. Add up the jumps.
3
Find the missing digit. Add in columns, starting with the ones, or try the digits one at a time. 4□27 + 1365 = 5992
4
Find the missing digit. Try 5, 6 and 7 and check each one by taking away. 8□50 − 3420 = 5230
Think Harder
5
Find the missing digits: 2□7□ + □6□9 = 6000
6
In a number pyramid, each brick is the sum of the two bricks under it. The bottom row is 1200, □, □. The middle row is 2650, □. The top brick is 6000. Find the missing bricks.
7
Ali meant to add 1099 to a number, but he added 1999 by mistake and got 6543. What is the correct answer?
Challenge
8
Find the missing digits: 8□0□ − □4□7 = 2468
9

Missing digits in 4-digit sums, checking subtraction by adding, magic squares and number pyramids.

Tip for you

Cover one digit of a long sum with your thumb. Can your child find it and check by adding back?

Ask

"How can you check your answer?"

Lesson 3 • Tables of 6, 7, 8 and 9

Times Table Treasure

Core
1

Think Harder
5
The digits of every answer in the 9 times table (9 × 1 to 9 × 10) add up to 9. Which of these are in the 9 times table: 54, 65, 72, 83?

Patterns inside the 6, 7, 8 and 9 times tables, last-digit patterns, and splitting arrays into facts they know.

Tip for you

Ask for 7 × 8 split into two easier facts, such as 5 × 8 and 2 × 8.

Ask

"What pattern do you notice in the last digits?"

Lesson 4 • Word Problems: Bar Models

Model Makers

Core
1
There are 2345 people at a concert. 1180 of them are adults. How many are children? Draw one long bar for all the people and mark off the part for the adults.
2
A red ribbon is 145 cm long. A blue ribbon is 68 cm longer. Draw the red ribbon as a bar, then draw a longer bar for the blue ribbon. How long is the blue ribbon?

Think Harder

What your child learns

Drawing bar models for part-whole, comparison and before-and-after word problems.

Tip for you

When your child reads a word problem, ask them to draw the bars before doing any sums.

Ask

"What stays the same? What changes?"

Lesson 5 • Division

Remainder Riddles

Core
1
Share 35 counters equally among 4 children. How many counters does each child get? How many counters are left over? (Use counters or draw dots in 4 groups.)
2
Put 58 sweets into bags with 7 sweets in each bag. How many full bags can you fill? How many sweets are left over? (Use counters or draw dots.)
3
6 friends share 45 stickers equally. How many does each friend get? How many are left over?
4
30 children go on an outing. Each car holds 4 children. How many cars are needed?
Think Harder
5
Mei has 53 beads. She makes bracelets with 8 beads each. How many bracelets can she make? How many more beads does she need to make one more bracelet?
6
I am a number between 20 and 30. When I am divided by 4, the remainder is 3. When I am divided by 6, the remainder is 5. What number am I?
7
What is the biggest 2-digit number that leaves a remainder of 8 when it is divided by 9?
Challenge
8
I am a 2-digit number. When I am divided by 3, by 4 or by 5, the remainder is always 2. What number am I?
9
A box has more than 30 but fewer than 50 chocolates. They can be shared equally among 2, 3, 4 or 6 children with none left over. Find all the numbers of chocolates there could be.

What your child learns

Division with remainders, and deciding what the leftover means in a story (round up or round down).

Tip for you

Ask: 30 people, 4 to a car. How many cars? Why is it not 7?

Ask

"What do you do with the leftover here?"

Lesson 6 • Fractions

Fraction Walls

Core
1
A chocolate bar has 10 equal pieces. Ali eats half of the bar. How many pieces is that? Write it as 1/2 = ?/10. (Draw the bar and cut it in two halves if you like.)
2
Two pizzas are the same size. One is cut into 6 equal slices and the other into 9 equal slices. Which pizza has the bigger slices? So which is bigger, 1/6 of a pizza or 1/9 of a pizza?
3
A bar is cut into 9 equal parts. Shade 2 parts. Then shade 5 more parts. What fraction of the bar is shaded now?
4
A bar is cut into 8 equal parts and 3 parts are shaded. What fraction of the bar is NOT shaded? (Draw it and count.)
Think Harder
5
Which is bigger, 3/5 or 7/10? Show how you know.
6
Siti reads 1/4 of a book on Monday and 1/2 of the book on Tuesday. What fraction of the book has she read? What fraction is left?
7
Put these in order, smallest first: 2/3, 5/6, 7/12.
Challenge
8
Which is bigger, 3/7 or 5/9? Use 1/2 to help you.
9
Which is bigger, 2/5 or 4/9? Hint: make the top numbers the same.

What your child learns

Equivalent fractions with a fraction wall, comparing fractions, and adding parts of the same whole.

Tip for you

Fold paper strips into halves, quarters and eighths. Which pieces are the same size?

Ask

"Are the parts the same size? How do you know?"

Lesson 7 • Area and Perimeter

Area Architects

Core
1
A rectangle is 9 cm long and 4 cm wide. Walk all the way round its edge: how far is that? (This distance is called the perimeter.) Draw it on squared paper if you like.
2
Cover a rectangle 7 cm long and 6 cm wide with squares that are 1 cm by 1 cm. There are 6 rows with 7 squares in each row. How many squares cover it? (The number of squares is the area, in cm².)
3
A square has 4 equal sides. The distance all the way round it is 32 cm. How long is each side?
4
A square floor is covered with 36 small squares (1 cm by 1 cm), with the same number of squares in each row and each column. How many squares are there along one side? How far is it all the way round the edge?
Think Harder
5
Find all the rectangles with whole-cm sides and an area of 24 cm². Which one has the shortest perimeter?
6
A rectangle is 12 cm long. Its perimeter is 30 cm. Find its area.
7
A rectangle 8 cm by 2 cm and a square have the same perimeter. Which has the bigger area, and by how much?
Challenge
8
List every rectangle with whole-cm sides and a perimeter of 24 cm, with its area, in a table. What do you notice as the rectangle gets closer to a square? Use what you notice to predict the biggest area for a perimeter of 40 cm, then check.
9
A rectangle is made of 2 same-size squares side by side. Its perimeter is 36 cm. What is its area?

What your child learns

Area and perimeter: same perimeter but different area, figures made of rectangles, and listing every rectangle.

Tip for you

Use 12 square biscuits or tiles. How many different rectangles can your child make? Which has the longest edge?

Ask

"Does a bigger area always mean a bigger perimeter?"

Lesson 8 • Angles, Perpendicular and Parallel Lines

Angle and Line Detectives

Core
1
A right angle is a square corner, like the corner of a page. How many right angles does a rectangle have? Check with the corner of a piece of paper.
2
At 3 o’clock the minute hand points at 12 and the hour hand points at 3. Is the angle between the hands smaller than, bigger than, or the same as the corner of a page?
3
At 2 o’clock, is the angle between the clock hands smaller or bigger than the corner of a page?
4
Parallel lines are straight lines that stay the same distance apart and never meet, like the two rails of a train track. Name 2 capital letters that have a pair of parallel lines.
Think Harder
5
I have 4 sides and 4 right angles. My sides are not all the same length. What shape am I? How many pairs of parallel lines do I have?
6
A U shape is made of 5 squares: 3 squares in a row with 1 more square on top of each end square. How many right angles are inside it?
7
Look at a clock face. For each o’clock time from 1 o’clock to 11 o’clock, is the angle between the hands (the short way round) smaller than, the same as, or bigger than a right angle? Put each time in the right group.
Challenge
8
4 lines from one point make 6 angles. How many lines from one point are needed to make exactly 10 angles?
9
A staircase is made of columns of 1, 2 and 3 squares standing side by side (3 steps). It has 6 right angles inside it. How many right angles are inside a staircase with 4 steps? With 5 steps? With 10 steps? Find a rule and explain why it works.

What your child learns

Right angles, counting angles in an organised way, and spotting parallel and perpendicular lines.

Tip for you

Fold a corner of paper to make a right-angle checker and go on a hunt around the house.

Ask

"How did you make sure you counted every angle?"
