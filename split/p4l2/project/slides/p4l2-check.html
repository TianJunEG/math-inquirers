1–4 MIN

Sneak-peek puzzles

Ideas we have not learnt yet. Guesses and "I do not know" are welcome. Nobody is marked.

Show your answer only to your tutor: hold your own paper to the camera, or use private chat.

1

Try it

Draw a number line from 7 000 to 8 000. Put a dot at 7 500, halfway. Then put a dot at 7 200. Is the 7 200 dot nearer to 7 000 or to 8 000?

Nearer to 7 000: it is only 200 away, not 800.

2

Think

A shop says "about 8 000 people came" (rounded to the nearest thousand). Which of these could be the real number: 7 499, 7 500, 8 499, 8 500? Hint: draw a number line from 7 000 to 9 000.

7 500 and 8 499.

Math Inquirers P4 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 1 to 4 (3 minutes). SNEAK-PEEK PUZZLES. Say: "These are ideas we have not learnt yet. Guessing is welcome, drawing is welcome and so is saying I do not know. Nobody is marked, and nobody's answer is shown to the group." Puzzle 1 is a TRY IT puzzle: any child can start it with objects, a drawing or counting, so everyone has a first win. Puzzle 2 needs a little thinking. About 90 seconds each. Children show their answers PRIVATELY: on their own paper held up to your camera, or in private chat. Never ask for boards up in front of the group, never read out whose answer is whose, and never say who got it right. Then click to show each answer and talk it through for everyone ("some of you tried ..."), naming no child. Ask for volunteers first if you want a reason said aloud; nobody is called on by name, and passing is always fine. ANSWERS: 1) Nearer to 7 000. 7 200 is only 200 from 7 000 but 800 from 8 000, and it is left of the halfway dot 7 500. 2) 7 500 and 8 499. The real number is from 7 500 to 8 499 (7 499 rounds down to 7 000; 8 500 rounds up to 9 000). A GOOD REASON: Find the two round numbers and the halfway mark. Halfway or more rounds up. Use what you see only to plan your help. It does not decide who is on which page and it is not recorded as a mark. After question 3 on page 1 every child may choose: carry on, or try the Think harder page. You may quietly invite a child with a PRIVATE chat message ("try the Think harder page when you are ready"), never by name in front of the group, and you never announce who is on which page. Everyone stays in the same room and every child turns to the page they choose. If a pair would be split, ask both children first; never leave a child alone on page 1 (join them to another pair or to yourself).
