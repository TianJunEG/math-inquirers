Story break: Adam Ries

1492–1559 • Germany • also spelled Adam Riese • A calculation teacher who wrote maths books in German so ordinary people could learn.

Adam Ries was born in Staffelstein, in what is now Germany, around 1492. He ran a calculation school in Erfurt and wrote arithmetic books in German, not in Latin, so apprentices in trades and crafts could read them. His second book, printed in 1522, taught sums on a counting board and with pen and paper, and it was reprinted more than 100 times. His books used everyday examples: prices, wages, profit and loss, and interest. Later, in the town of Annaberg, he made tables to help people see what a fair loaf of bread should weigh when grain prices changed. In Germany, people still say a sum is right 'nach Adam Riese', meaning 'according to Adam Ries'.

Good to know: Sources give his birth year as 1492 or 1493. The sources I opened do not name 'percentages' specifically; they list prices, interest, and profit and loss.
