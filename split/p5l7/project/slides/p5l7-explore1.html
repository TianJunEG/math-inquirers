Explore: up 10%, down 10%

EXPLORE

  1. A bicycle costs $200. Predict: if the price goes UP 10% and then DOWN 10%, is it back to $200?
  2. Work it out. Up 10% first. Then take 10% of the NEW price off.
  3. Try the other way round: down 10% first, then up 10%.
  4. Compare both answers with your prediction. What do you notice?

Math Inquirers P5 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 9 to 14. Pairs predict first (thumbs: back to $200, less, or more?) and write the prediction. Then they work it out on whiteboards. Most children predict $200. Let them find out for themselves. Anyone who wants more (open to every pair): try up 50% then down 50% on $80. Is the gap bigger or smaller than with 10%? GO FURTHER (say it at minute 9): any pair that finishes step 4 early may try the what-if question on the next slide. It is open to every pair, not a reward. Answer: Up 50% then down 50%: $200 → $300 → $150. Up 20% then down 20%: $200 → $240 → $192. The final price is always LESS than $200, because the "down" percentage is taken from the bigger new price.
