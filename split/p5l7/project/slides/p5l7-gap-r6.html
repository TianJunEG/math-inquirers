Think harder: Ratio and percentage

Tool: Draw it

PREVIEW: a Primary 6 topic, beyond Level 5. A taste, not a test.

In a class the ratio of boys to girls is 3 : 2.

(a) What fraction of the class are boys?

(b) What percentage of the class are girls?

(c) There are 30 children in the class. How many boys are there?

(a) 3/5.

(b) 40%.

(c) 18 boys.

Math Inquirers P5 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Optional, if you finish early (no clock time): show it while some pairs are working or have finished, or use it live as an extra warm-up or tutor-bank question; never at a fixed minute, and it never takes time from the plan. ADDED ITEM r6 (a slide-only addition for the skill gap "Ratio and percentage"; it is not in the printed workbook). TOOL: Draw it. PREVIEW LABEL: say "This is a first taste of a topic from a later level. We are not testing it; we are noticing how it works." SAY: Draw a bar of 5 equal units: 3 for boys and 2 for girls. The whole bar is 100%. ASK: What percentage is one unit? How does that help with the girls? LISTEN FOR: One unit is 100% / 5 = 20%, so girls are 2 × 20% = 40%. For (c) 30 ÷ 5 = 6 children per unit and 3 × 6 = 18. Listen for "the whole is 5 units". Everyone writes the answer on their own paper, then say the answer together on three, then click the answer box. Nobody holds anything up, nobody is marked, and passing is always fine. ANSWER: (a) 3/5. (b) 40%. (c) 18 boys. WHY: 3 + 2 = 5 units. Boys 3/5, girls 2/5 = 40%. 30 ÷ 5 = 6, so boys 18 and girls 12. SAME SKILL, NEW NUMBERS (tutor bank, say it aloud or write it on the board): The ratio of cats to dogs is 1 : 4. What percentage are cats? There are 60 animals: how many dogs? Answer: 20%; 48 dogs.
