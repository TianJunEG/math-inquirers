Minutes 14 to 21. Let pairs share, then draw both routes on the board. Answers: 1) No. $200, up 10% is $220 (10% of $200 = $20). Down 10% of $220 is $22 off, so $198. 2) No: down first gives $200 − $20 = $180, then up 10% is $18 more = $198. Same answer. 3) The two 10%s are of different amounts: $20 added, but $22 taken off, because the second 10% is of the NEW price. That is why it is $2 less. Write on the board: "Percentage of WHAT?" This question is the heart of the lesson. After a change, the new price is the new whole. Do NOT teach the method for the worksheet yet: the next slides do that. GO FURTHER what-if (open to every pair from minute 9; nobody waits): Up 50% then down 50%: $200 → $300 → $150. Up 20% then down 20%: $200 → $240 → $192. The final price is always LESS than $200, because the "down" percentage is taken from the bigger new price.
