1.5–6.5 MIN

Sneak-peek puzzles

Ideas we have not learnt yet. Guesses and "I do not know" are welcome. Nobody is marked.

Write your answer on your own paper. When your tutor says, say your answer together on three. Only your tutor looks.

1

Try it

Ali has 8 ten-cent coins ($0.80). Mei has 7 ten-cent coins and one 5-cent coin ($0.75). Draw the coins. Who has more money?

Ali: $0.80 is more than $0.75.

2

Think

Write a decimal that is between 3.4 and 3.5. How do you know it fits? Hint: draw a number line from 3.4 to 3.5 with 10 steps.

For example 3.45 (3.40 < 3.45 < 3.50).

Math Inquirers P4 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 1.5 to 6.5 (5 minutes). SNEAK-PEEK PUZZLES. Say: "These are ideas we have not learnt yet. Guessing is welcome, drawing is welcome and so is saying I do not know. Nobody is marked, and nobody's answer is shown to the group." Puzzle 1 is a TRY IT puzzle: any child can start it with objects, a drawing or counting, so everyone has a first win. Puzzle 2 needs a little thinking. Give about 60 seconds for puzzle 1 and about 90 seconds for puzzle 2. Children show their answers PRIVATELY: written on their own paper and said together on three (nothing is held up to the camera). Make ONE check for both puzzles, after puzzle 2: children say the answers together on three. Never use private chat or DMs. Never ask for boards or paper held up in front of the group or to the camera, never read out whose answer is whose, and never say who got it right. Then click to show each answer and talk it through for everyone ("some of you tried ..."), naming no child. Nobody is called on by name, and passing is always fine. ANSWERS: 1) Ali has more. $0.80 (8 tenths) is more than $0.75 (7 tenths and 5 hundredths). 0.8 is the same as 0.80, and 0.80 is bigger than 0.75. 2) Any decimal from 3.401 to 3.499, for example 3.45. Write 3.4 as 3.40 and 3.5 as 3.50: 3.45 sits between them. A GOOD REASON: Line up the decimal points and compare place by place. Write extra zeros so every number has the same number of places. Use what you see only to plan your help. It does not decide who is on which page and it is not recorded as a mark. ON PAGE 1 (printed): "After question 3 you may choose: carry on, or try Think harder." Make the invitation to the WHOLE group ("when you have finished Start here you can choose Think harder, Go further or the Investigation"), so nobody is singled out. Never message a child privately, never name a child, and never announce who is on which page. Everyone stays in the same room and every child turns to the page they choose. If a pair would be split, ask both children first; never leave a child alone on page 1 (join them to another pair or to yourself).
