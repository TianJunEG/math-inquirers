Explore: the Dice Race

EXPLORE

  1. Roll two dice and add. A wins on 2, 3, 4, 10, 11 or 12. B wins on 5, 6, 7, 8 or 9.
  2. Predict: who will win more often? A has 6 totals, B has only 5.
  3. Play 20 rounds with your partner. Tally the wins.
  4. Add up the whole group’s results. What do you notice?

Math Inquirers P6 • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 10 to 25 (this slide 10 to 15). The Dice Race: roll two dice and add. Player A wins on 2, 3, 4, 10, 11 or 12; player B wins on 5, 6, 7, 8 or 9. Pairs predict, play 20 rounds, tally the wins, then add up the whole group’s results. Predict first: In the Dice Race, player A wins on six totals: 2, 3, 4, 10, 11, 12. Player B wins on only five: 5, 6, 7, 8, 9. Who do you think will win more often? Write A, B or “same”, and a reason. Hints: Keep a tally: one column for A, one for B. Write the total each time. Surprised by your results? Write down which totals came up most. Finished early: in how many ways can two dice make a total of 7? Of 2? Use a red die and a blue die so 1 + 6 and 6 + 1 are different. Online: Children roll real dice at home; anyone without dice builds the dice program live in the shared Scratch window (pick random 1 to 6, twice) and uses that. This lesson is enrichment: probability is new to most children, so keep it playful. Block colours on our slides are simplified; in Scratch, find blocks by the category name on the left, not by colour. Pairs play the Explore game in breakout rooms and type their tallies into the chat so the group total can be added up. While pairs play, add up the class tallies on a shared slide or in the chat. Expect B to win about 2 rounds in every 3, but a single pair's 20 rounds can easily come out level or even favour A: that is why we pool the results.
