Mei flipped a coin 10 times and got 7 heads. She says the coin is unfair. Do you agree?

Sentence starters:

Math Inquirers P6 • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Tool today: Act it out. Did it help?
Minutes 76.5 to 82.5. Both questions are kept in this lesson (it has the time). Pairs talk for 2 minutes, then share. Follow-up: “How many flips would you need before you believed her? How could a program help?” Answer: Not yet: 10 flips is far too few. With a fair coin, 7 or more heads in 10 flips happens 176 times in 1024, about 1 time in 6. To test it, flip many more times (or simulate 1000 flips of a fair coin to see how much the counts wobble). If 1000 real flips gave 700 heads, that would be strong evidence: a fair coin almost never does that. Close: “Today you measured chance with fractions, found the hidden unfairness in a game, and let the computer play a thousand games to test your maths. Close, not exact: that is how random things behave.”
