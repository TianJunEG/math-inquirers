1. A recipe needs 9/4 cups of flour. Ali only has a 1-cup scoop. How many full scoops does he fill, and what part of a scoop is left to add?

2 full scoops and 1/4 of a scoop (9/4 = 2 1/4).

2. Which is bigger, 3/8 or 3/5? Explain how you know.

3/5. Both have 3 pieces, but fifths are bigger pieces than eighths, so 3 fifths is more than 3 eighths.

Math Inquirers P4 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 1 to 4 (3 minutes). WARM-UP CHECK: two quick questions on today's key idea in a new setting. Not a test. Read question 1, give about 90 seconds, children write the answer AND a one-sentence reason on a whiteboard and show you (or send it in private chat). Same for question 2. Do not say who is right yet. Then click to show each answer and let one or two children say their reason aloud. ANSWERS: 1) 2 full scoops and 1/4 of a scoop (9/4 = 2 1/4). 2) 3/5. Both have 3 pieces, but fifths are bigger pieces than eighths, so 3 fifths is more than 3 eighths. A GOOD REASON: The denominator tells how big the pieces are: more pieces in the whole means smaller pieces. Compare pieces, or make the denominators the same. FAST TRACK: a child who gets BOTH right and can say why starts on the Think Harder pages and the Open investigation page at minute 34, while the group works on pages 1 and 2. Tell them by private chat or a quiet word ("start on the Think Harder page today"); say nothing to the group and label nobody. Everyone still joins the explore, the mini-lesson, the page checks, talk time, the exit ticket and the reflection. A fast-track child who is stuck for 5 minutes can go back to page 1 with no fuss. It changes lesson by lesson. Groups are up to 8 and the workbook is printed, so each child only needs a different page number.
