11.5–21 MIN

Explore: pizza party

EXPLORE

  1. Cut paper circles into 4 equal slices (quarters).
  2. Ravi's group eats 11 slices. Take 11 quarter-slices.
  3. Put the slices together to build whole pizzas.
  4. Write how many pizzas they ate in two different ways.

Go further (any time): What if each pizza had 8 slices and they ate 19? How many pizzas is that?

Math Inquirers P4 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 11.5 to 21. Pairs use paper quarter-slices (online: cut paper circles on camera). Ask them to predict first: more than 2 pizzas or less? More than 3? Let them write the amount any way they like; listen for "eleven quarters" and "two and three quarters". Go further (on the slide), for any child who wants it: what if every pizza had 6 slices and they ate 11 slices? GO FURTHER (on the slide from the start of the explore, so nobody waits): What if each pizza had 8 slices and they ate 19? How many pizzas is that? Answer: 2 pizzas and 3 slices: 19/8 = 2 3/8.
