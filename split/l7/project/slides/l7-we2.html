Worked example: rectangles in a strip

A strip of 4 squares. How many rectangles? In this puzzle only, count squares as rectangles too.

Count by length: shortest first.

Step 1. 1 square long: 4

Step 2. 2 squares long: 3

Step 3. 3 squares long: 2

Step 4. 4 squares long: 1

4 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 10 rectangles

Math Inquirers Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Click to build each length. Say first: "In this puzzle a square counts as a rectangle. Why? A rectangle has 4 square corners and its opposite sides are the same length. A square has all of that too." (On the worksheet's house picture, squares still go only in the Square column.) Think-aloud: "I count by length, shortest first. 1 square long: there are 4. 2 squares long: I slide it along: 3 places. 3 squares long: 2 places. The whole strip: 1. Each time the length goes up by 1, there is one fewer place. 4 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 10." Use a paper strip and a card window to slide along it.
