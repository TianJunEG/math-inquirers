Optional, if you finish early

Go further

1. A big triangle has lines drawn from its top corner to the bottom. With 1 line there are 3 triangles, with 2 lines 6, with 3 lines 10. How many triangles with 4 lines? With 5 lines? Draw it to check.

2. Draw a square and cut it into 4 equal squares. Then cut ONE of those small squares into 4 even smaller squares. How many squares can you find altogether? Count the small ones first, then the bigger ones.

Make up your own

3. Draw your own triangle-counting puzzle: a big triangle with some lines inside. Count the triangles. Swap with a partner. Do you both get the same number? If not, find out who missed some.

Math Inquirers Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

For any pair that chooses Go further. Use it as a pair choice, or with volunteers in talk time (never call a child by name). Ask for the reason every time. Q3 is open: there is no single right answer, so ask children to check each other's work. Answers: 1) 15 triangles with 4 lines and 21 with 5 lines. The number added grows by 1 each time: 3, 6, 10, 15, 21 (+3, +4, +5, +6). Check by drawing: count the small triangles first, then the bigger ones made of 2, 3 … small ones. 2) 9 (1 big, 4 medium, 4 tiny). 3) Open. Check by counting small triangles first, then bigger ones made of 2, 3 … small ones. Example: one line from the top corner to the bottom gives 3 triangles; two lines give 6.
