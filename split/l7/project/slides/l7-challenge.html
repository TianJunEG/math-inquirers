Challenge

1. A triangle has 4 lines drawn from the top corner to the bottom. How many triangles are there? Follow the pattern 3, 6, 10 … What would 5 lines give?

2. A square is cut into 4 equal squares. One of the small squares is cut into 4 even smaller squares. How many squares can you find altogether?

Make up your own

3. Draw your own triangle-counting puzzle: a big triangle with some lines inside. Count the triangles. Swap with a partner. Do you both get the same number? If not, find out who missed some.

Math Inquirers Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

For children who have finished the topic. Use as a pair challenge or in talk time. Ask for the reason every time. Q3 is open: there is no single right answer, so ask children to check each other's work. Answers: 1) 15 triangles with 4 lines, and 21 with 5 lines (add 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 each time). 2) 9 (1 big, 4 medium, 4 tiny). 3) Open. Check by counting small triangles first, then bigger ones made of 2, 3 … small ones. Example: one line from the top corner to the bottom gives 3 triangles; two lines give 6.
