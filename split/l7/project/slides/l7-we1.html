Worked example: how many squares?

This grid is 3 squares long and 3 squares high. Count by size.

Step 1. Small squares: 3 in each row, 3 rows. 3 + 3 + 3 = 9

Step 2. Squares made of 4 small ones: slide a 2 by 2 window along. 4 of them.

Step 3. The whole grid is 1 big square.

9 + 4 + 1 = 14 squares

Math Inquirers Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Click to reveal each step. Before each click, ask the group to predict. Step 1: count the small squares row by row. Step 2: hold up a paper 2 by 2 window (or cut one from card) and slide it: top left, top right, bottom left, bottom right. "Can it go anywhere else?" No: 4 places. Step 3: the whole grid. Think-aloud: "I count by size. Small squares: 9. Squares 2 across and 2 down: I slide my window, 4 places. Squares 3 across and 3 down: just the whole grid, 1. 9 + 4 + 1 = 14." Watch for children who count squares made of 2 small squares side by side: that shape is a rectangle, not a square.
