Challenge: talk it through

Q1: What do we know? Can you draw it, or use counters?

15 triangles with 4 lines, and 21 with 5 lines (add 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 each time).

Q2: Does anything stay the same? Does anything change?

9 (1 big, 4 medium, 4 tiny).

Q3 (open): Try it. Can your partner break your rule? Change one thing: what happens?

Open. Check by counting small triangles first, then bigger ones made of 2, 3 … small ones. Example: one line from the top corner to the bottom gives 3 triangles; two lines give 6.

Math Inquirers Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Use after pairs have tried. Reveal one answer at a time and ask a child to explain before you click. Many children stop at one answer: ask "Is there another way?" and "How do you know you found them all?" For Q3, ask two pairs to swap puzzles and solve each other's.
