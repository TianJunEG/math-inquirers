Squares hide inside bigger squares

How many squares can you see? Count the small ones first, then the bigger ones.

Step 1. Small squares: 1, 2, 3, 4.

Step 2. The whole grid is a square too. It is made of 4 small ones: 1 more.

Step 3. Add them: 4 + 1 = 5 squares.

Why count by size? Then we never miss a square, and we never count one twice.

Math Inquirers Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Teach. Ask first: "How many squares can you see?" Most children say 4. Do not correct them yet. Click 1: number the small squares. Click 2: run your finger round the outside: "Is this a square? 4 straight sides, all the same length, 4 square corners. Yes! It is made of 4 small squares." Click 3: 4 + 1 = 5. Click 4: the why. Think-aloud: "Squares can hide inside bigger squares. So I count by size: all the small ones first, then the next size up, then the next. That way I do not miss any and I do not count any twice."
