Stretch

1. Three squares are joined in a row to make a long rectangle. How many rectangles can you find altogether? (A square counts as a rectangle.)

2. How many rectangles are in a grid 2 squares long and 2 squares high? (Squares count too.)

3. Cut a square along both diagonals to get 4 same-size triangles. Can you put all 4 together to make a bigger triangle? Can you make a square? Draw them.

Math Inquirers Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

For children who finish early (these are on the slide only; Think Harder T1 to T4 are in the workbook). Answers: 1) 6 (3 single squares, 2 pairs and 1 whole row). 2) 9: 4 small squares, 2 rectangles 2 long lying down, 2 rectangles 2 long standing up, and the whole grid. 3) Yes to both. The 4 triangles make the original square again (corners meeting in the middle), and they also fit together into one big triangle whose long side is twice as long as a small triangle's long side (two along the bottom and two on top).
