Worked example: name the fraction

What fraction of the bar is shaded? What fraction is NOT shaded?

Step 1. Are the parts equal? Yes. Count them: 5 equal parts. They are fifths, so the bottom number is 5.

Step 2. Count the shaded parts: 3. The top number is 3. Shaded: 35

Step 3. Not shaded: 2 parts. Not shaded: 25

Check: 35 and 25 make 55, 1 whole ✓

Math Inquirers P2 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Click to reveal each step; ask the group to predict before each click. Think-aloud: "First I check the parts are equal. They are. I count all the parts: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5. Five equal parts are called fifths, so 5 goes at the bottom. Now I count the shaded parts: 3. That goes on top: three fifths. The parts that are not shaded: 2, so two fifths." Say: "The bottom number tells us how many equal parts make the whole. The top number tells us how many parts we are talking about." The check: 3 fifths and 2 fifths make 5 fifths, the whole bar. Watch for 3/2 (shaded over not shaded).
