14–20 MIN

Explore: talk about it

1. Which part is bigger, 14 or 18? How do you know?

2. How many eighths fit on one quarter? How many quarters make 1 whole?

3. How did you fold 3 equal parts? How do you know they are equal?

Math Inquirers Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 14 to 20. Let pairs keep working, then gather them. Answers: 1) 1/4 is bigger. The strip is the same length, but cut into 4 parts instead of 8, so each part is longer. Put the strips together to show it. 2) 2 eighths fit on 1 quarter; 4 quarters make 1 whole (and 8 eighths make 1 whole). 3) Any fair method: fold and adjust until the 3 parts are the same length, then check by folding one part on top of another. Praise checking. Ask: "Is 1/8 bigger because 8 is bigger than 4?" No! More parts means each part is smaller. We use this idea all lesson.
