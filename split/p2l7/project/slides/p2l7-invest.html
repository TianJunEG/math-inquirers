Investigation: Fraction strips

A puzzle with more than one good answer. Share one way you tried it, or pass.

(a) Cover a HALF strip exactly with pieces of ONE kind only. Which kinds work? How many pieces of each?

(b) Do the same for a THIRD strip and a QUARTER strip.

(c) Can thirds ever cover a half exactly? Why not? Needs: the fraction strips from the cut-out page (halves, thirds, quarters, sixths, eighths).

Easy first step: lay the two quarter strips on top of the half strip. Do they fit exactly?

Math Inquirers P2 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 77 to 80 (3 minutes, after talk time). Investigation debrief. The Investigation is printed on the last core page of the workbook; children may have started it any time after question 3 (it is open to everyone), or it is take-home. Say to the whole group: "Who would like to share one way they tried the Investigation? Passing is fine." Ask for volunteers only; never name a child; no public tallies, scores or rankings. Several answers are right: praise the method (a list in order, a table, a drawing), not the speed. If nobody has started it, show the first step on the slide and let pairs try for one minute, then set it as take-home. Model answer and what to look for: (a) Half: 2 quarters, 3 sixths or 4 eighths (not thirds). (b) Third: 2 sixths only. Quarter: 2 eighths only (checked with exact fractions). (c) No: one third is shorter than a half but two thirds is longer, so no number of thirds fits exactly. Look for: children lay the strips side by side and say “fits exactly”. Keep to the strips and drawings: do not teach “equivalent fractions” as a rule (that is P3).
