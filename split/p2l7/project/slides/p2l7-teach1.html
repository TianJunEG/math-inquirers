23.5–25.5 MIN

Fair shares: the parts must be equal

Each bar is cut into 3 parts. Is each bar cut into thirds?

Step 1. Count the parts. Both bars have 3 parts.

Step 2. Are the parts the same size? Bar A: yes. Bar B: no.

Step 3. Only EQUAL parts have a fraction name. Each part of bar A is 13. Bar B is not cut into thirds.

Equal parts can look different. All of these rectangles are cut into halves.

Math Inquirers Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 23.5 to 25.5. Teach. Ask first: "Ali says both bars are cut into thirds. Do you agree?" Take votes. Click 1: count the parts together: 3 and 3. Click 2: "Are they the same size?" Bar A yes, bar B no: one part is much bigger. Click 3: only equal parts get a fraction name. "One third means 1 of 3 EQUAL parts." Click 4: equal parts can look different. Each rectangle is cut into 2 equal parts: down the middle, across the middle, or corner to corner (fold one triangle onto the other: they match). Think-aloud: "Before I name a fraction, I check two things: how many parts, and are they all the same size? If they are not the same size, I cannot use a fraction name."
