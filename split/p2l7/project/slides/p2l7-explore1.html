10.5–19.5 MIN

Explore: fold fair shares

EXPLORE

  1. Take 4 paper strips (already cut, all the same length).
  2. Keep one whole. Fold one in half, one into quarters and one into eighths.
  3. Open them out. Write the fraction on ONE part of each strip.
  4. Line them up under the whole.

GO FURTHER (optional, any time you finish early): Fold a strip into 3 equal parts. How do you know the parts are equal? Then fold one into 6 equal parts: how many sixths make a half? A third?

Math Inquirers P2 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 10.5 to 19.5. The strips are cut before the lesson (kit at minute 0). Each child folds three strips (halves, quarters, eighths) and writes the fraction on one part of each, not all 15; pairs compare. Show how to fold in half by matching the ends. Quarters: fold in half, then in half again. Eighths: in half three times. Children line the strips up under each other, left ends together. Folding a strip into 3 equal parts is NOT part of the pair task any more: it is the Go further box at the foot of this slide and a take-home. Thirds are hard on purpose (roll the strip into an S and flatten it, then check the 3 parts are the same length). Say if a child tries: "Thirds are tricky for everybody, grown-ups too. A strip that does not come out equal is a good try, not a mistake." Nothing is marked. Fractions are written as 1/2 on slides and as stacked fractions in the workbook: both mean the same. Go further (a second what-if, so nobody waits) is the box at the foot of this slide; the question is also posted in the chat when the pairs go to their breakout rooms. It is for ANY child who finishes the explore task or wants more: say it to the whole group as a choice, never as a prize, and never name who tries it. Pairs stay together unless both children agree to split. Answer: Thirds: fold and adjust until the 3 parts match, then check one part on top of another. 3 sixths make a half and 2 sixths make a third. Take it at the debrief only if a pair offers it.
