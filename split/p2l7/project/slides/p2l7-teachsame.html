Different parts? Line up the same whole

Mei eats 12 of a pizza. Ben eats 38 of a pizza the same size. Who eats more?

Step 1. Line them up: 12 is the same as 4 eighths.

Draw both wholes the same length.

4 eighths is more than 3 eighths. Mei eats more.

Math Inquirers P2 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Teach before worksheet page 2 (Q6 compares a quarter with eighths). Use the folded strips from Explore: lay the halves strip on the eighths strip. Click 1: one half covers 4 eighths. Click 2: Ben ate 3 eighths, Mei ate the same as 4 eighths, so Mei ate more. Think-aloud: "Halves and eighths are different parts, so I cannot just look at the numbers. I draw the same whole twice, the same length, and line the parts up." Ask: "What if Mei's pizza were tiny?" Then we cannot compare like this: the wholes must be the same size. Equivalent fractions are a P3 topic: show that one half covers 4 eighths with the lined-up strips only, and do not write 1/2 = 4/8 as a rule.
