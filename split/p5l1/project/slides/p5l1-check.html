Sneak-peek puzzles

Ideas we have not learnt yet. Guessing is welcome. "I do not know" is welcome. Nobody is marked.

1. Use the digit cards 3, 6, 7 and 9, each once, to make a 4-digit number as close to 7000 as you can. Try one that starts with 7 and one that starts with 6. Which is closer?

6973 (27 below 7000) beats 7369 (369 above).

2. Put ONE pair of brackets in 4 + 6 × 5 − 3 to make 47. How do you know no other place makes 47?

(4 + 6) × 5 − 3 = 47

Show your answer only to your tutor: on your own paper, or all hold paper to the camera together. Then a volunteer can tell us how they thought.

Minutes 0 to 3. SNEAK-PEEK PUZZLES. Show puzzle 1, give about a minute, click to show the short answer, then puzzle 2. Say it out loud: these are ideas we have not learnt yet; guessing is welcome; "I do not know" is welcome; nobody is marked. Puzzle 1 is an access puzzle: any child can try it with objects, drawing or counting, so everyone can start well. Puzzle 2 is a thinking puzzle. Answers are shown PRIVATELY (own paper shown only to you as you walk round; online, everyone holds paper to the camera at the same moment, or shows it to you only, on camera). Never boards in front of the group, never read out answers with names, no tally, no ranking. Never record scores. Answers and what to listen for. Q1: Starting with 7, the best is 7369 (369 above 7000). Starting with 6, the best is 6973 (27 below 7000). 6973 is closer, and it is the closest of all 24 numbers. A child who only tries numbers that start with 7 misses it: testing BOTH sides of a target is today's idea. Any honest try is a good start.; Q2: (4 + 6) × 5 − 3 = 47. The other single pairs give 16 or 31 (and so does no bracket at all). Brackets first, then × and ÷, then + and −. Minutes 0 to 3. SNEAK-PEEK PUZZLES. Show the two puzzles one at a time. Say: "These are ideas we have not learnt yet. Guessing is welcome and so is 'I do not know'. Nobody is marked." Puzzle 1 is one that any child can try with objects, drawing or counting, so everybody can start well. Puzzle 2 is a thinking puzzle. Answers are shown PRIVATELY, never on boards in front of the group. Face to face: children write on their own paper and show it only to you as you walk round. Online: everyone holds their paper up to the camera at the same moment, or shows it to you only, on camera. Do not read answers out with names, do not keep a tally, do not rank. Then ask for VOLUNTEERS to say how they thought (put a hand up or use a reaction); never choose a child because you saw a wrong answer. Never record scores. Choosing a start: every child has the same neutral line on workbook page 1: "After question 2 you may choose: carry on, or try Think harder." Invite the WHOLE group, never one child: "when you have finished Start here you can choose Think harder, Go further or the Investigation." Never single out a child by name and never announce who is on which page. Pairs are not split unless BOTH children agree. Any child may swap at any time.
