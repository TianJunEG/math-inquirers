Explore: talk about it

1. How many seconds are there in 1 day? How did you work it out?

2. About how many days is a million seconds? Was your guess close?

3. About how long is 10 million seconds?

Math Inquirers P5 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 14 to 21. Let pairs keep working, then gather them. Collect the guesses first: a show of hands for hour, day, week, month, year. Answers: 1) 60 × 60 = 3600 seconds in an hour, 3600 × 24 = 86 400 seconds in a day. 2) 10 days = 864 000 s (not enough), 11 days = 950 400 s, 12 days = 1 036 800 s (too many), so a million seconds is between 11 and 12 days: about 11 and a half days. 3) About 116 days, nearly 4 months (10 times as long). Ask: "Did you multiply or add? Why is multiplying faster?" and "How did you get close without dividing exactly?" (try 10 days, then add a day at a time). Point out the strategy: estimate, then test numbers either side. That links to the digit puzzles next.
