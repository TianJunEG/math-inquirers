22.5–26.5 MIN

Explore: talk about it

1. How many seconds are there in 1 day? How did you work it out?

2. About how many days is a million seconds? Was your guess close?

GO FURTHER: A SECOND WHAT-IF (OPTIONAL, IF YOU FINISH EARLY)

A million seconds is about 11 and a half days. How long is 10 million seconds? Then guess first: how long is a BILLION seconds (1 000 000 000): days, months or years? Use your calculator.

Math Inquirers P5 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 22.5 to 26.5. Pencils down: gather the pairs. Collect the guesses first: a show of hands for hour, day, week, month, year. Answers: 1) 60 × 60 = 3600 seconds in an hour, 3600 × 24 = 86 400 seconds in a day. 2) 10 days = 864 000 s (not enough), 11 days = 950 400 s, 12 days = 1 036 800 s (too many), so a million seconds is between 11 and 12 days: about 11 and a half days. 3) (Go further what-if, not asked together) 10 million seconds is about 116 days, nearly 4 months (10 times as long). Ask: "Did you multiply or add? Why is multiplying faster?" and "How did you get close without dividing exactly?" (try 10 days, then add a day at a time). Point out the strategy: estimate, then test numbers either side. That links to the digit puzzles next. TWO questions together only (about a minute each), then move on. GO FURTHER what-if (open to every pair as soon as it has finished; nobody waits): 10 million seconds is about 116 days, nearly 4 months (10 times as long). A billion seconds is about 11 574 days, which is about 32 years: 1 000 000 000 ÷ 86 400 = 11 574.07 days; 11 574 ÷ 365 is about 31.7 years. A billion is a thousand millions, so 1000 × 11.6 days.
