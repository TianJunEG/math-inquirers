Worked example: a digit riddle

1. Rounded to the nearest hundred thousand, I am 3 600 000.

2. My ten thousands digit is 4 more than my hundred thousands digit.

3. My thousands digit is the difference of those two digits.

4. My hundreds digit is half my thousands digit. My tens digit is 0.

5. My digits are all different and add up to 31.

9

Clue 1: 3 5 with a big digit next, or 3 6 with a small digit next.

Clue 2: 5 + 4 = 9 fits. 6 + 4 = 10 is not a digit.

9 − 5 = 4, half of 4 is 2, 0, 31 − 23 = 8. I am 3 594 208.

Math Inquirers P5 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Use the clues in order: start with the clue that fixes the most digits. Click order: the digits appear one by one (3, 5, 9, 4, 2, 0, 8), then the two clue notes, then the answer. Talk through the digits as they appear. Think-aloud: "Rounded to the nearest hundred thousand it is 3 600 000, so it is between 3 550 000 and 3 649 999. Either it starts 3 5 and the next digit is 5 or more, or it starts 3 6 and the next digit is 4 or less. Clue 2: 5 + 4 = 9, and 9 is 5 or more, good. 6 + 4 = 10 is not a digit. So 3 5 9. Thousands: 9 − 5 = 4. Hundreds: half of 4 is 2. Tens: 0. So far 3 + 5 + 9 + 4 + 2 + 0 = 23, and 31 − 23 = 8." Then CHECK every clue: 3 594 208 rounds to 3 600 000; all digits different; total 31. The workbook riddle uses the same idea with different clues: do not give it away.
