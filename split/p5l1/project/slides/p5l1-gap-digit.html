Think harder: Digit sums and the greatest number

Tool: Guess and check

Wei has 6 digit cards: 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9. He chooses 5 different cards and makes a 5-digit number.

(a) What is the digit sum of all 6 cards?

(b) Find the greatest 5-digit multiple of 9 that Wei can make.

(a) 35.

(b) 97542.

Optional, if you finish early (no clock time): show it while some pairs are working or have finished, or use it live as an extra warm-up or tutor-bank question; never at a fixed minute, and it never takes time from the plan. ADDED ITEM digit (a slide-only addition for the skill gap "Digit sums and the greatest number"; it is not in the printed workbook). TOOL: Guess and check. SAY: A number is a multiple of 9 when its digit sum is in the 9 times table. Wei leaves one card out. Which card must he leave out? ASK: How does the total of 35 help you choose the card to leave out? Why is the answer not 98754? LISTEN FOR: 35 − 8 = 27, so the 8 is left out. Then the digits go in order from the largest: 9, 7, 5, 4, 2. Listen for "test both sides of the target" and a digit sum used as a check. Everyone writes the answer on their own paper, then say the answer together on three, then click the answer box. Nobody holds anything up, nobody is marked, and passing is always fine. ANSWER: (a) 35. (b) 97542. WHY: 35 minus the left-out card must be a multiple of 9: 35 − 8 = 27 (35 − 17 would need a card 17). So the cards are 2, 4, 5, 7, 9. Largest first gives 97542. Check: 9 + 7 + 5 + 4 + 2 = 27, and 97542 = 9 × 10838. SAME SKILL, NEW NUMBERS (tutor bank, say it aloud or write it on the board): Siti has cards 1, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9 and chooses 5 of them. Find the greatest 5-digit multiple of 3 she can make. Answer: 98643.
