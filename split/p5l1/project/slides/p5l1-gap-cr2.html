ADDED ITEM cr2 (a slide-only addition for the skill gap "A multiplication in letters"; it is not in the printed workbook). TOOL: Guess and check. SAY: The answer has 3 digits and its first two digits are the same. Make a table: choose M, then try different K. What does 10 x M tell you about the smallest M that can work? ASK: Which values of M can you rule out quickly? How do you know when to stop trying K for one M? LISTEN FOR: M is at least 2 (a 2-digit number times 1 gives at most 2 digits), and the last digit P comes from M x M. Children list tries such as 14 × 4 = 56, 24 × 4 = 96, ... until the first two digits match. Accept an organised table. Everyone writes the answer on their own paper, then say the answer together on three, then click the answer box. Nobody holds anything up, nobody is marked, and passing is always fine. ANSWER: K = 8, M = 4, N = 3, P = 6. Check: 84 × 4 = 336. WHY: Test M = 4: the products 14 × 4, 24 × 4, ... are 56, 96, 136, 176, 216, 256, 296, 336. 336 has first two digits equal (33), and the number is 84 × 4, so K = 8, M = 4, N = 3, P = 6. No other pair of digits gives a 3-digit answer whose first two digits match and whose letters stand for 4 different digits (this was checked by a script).
