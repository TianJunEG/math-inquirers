Worked example: two different unit fractions

Write 712 as the sum of two different unit fractions (1 on top).

Try 12: 712 − 612 = 112 → a unit fraction! 12 + 112

Try 13: 712 − 412 = 312 = 14 → a unit fraction! 13 + 14

Try 14: 712 − 312 = 412 = 13 → the same pair again. Stop!

Why stop? A first part smaller than half of 7/12 would leave a bigger part, and we already tried those.

Math Inquirers P5 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Test in order, and know when to stop: today's first strategy. Click each row while children work out the subtraction first. Think-aloud: "I list the unit fractions in order, biggest first. 1/2: is it less than 7/12? Yes. 7/12 − 6/12 = 1/12, a unit fraction, so that is one way. 1/3 leaves 3/12 = 1/4, another way. 1/4 leaves 4/12 = 1/3: that is the same pair as before, so I have found them all. A unit fraction must be less than 7/12 and at least half of it (so the two parts can be different and add up): that is 1/2 and 1/3 only." Why 1/2 can be the first: 1/2 < 7/12. What about 1/1? Too big. Answer: 1/2 + 1/12 and 1/3 + 1/4. Check: 6/12 + 1/12 = 7/12 and 4/12 + 3/12 = 7/12.
