2–6.5 MIN

Sneak-peek puzzles

Guessing is welcome. Nobody is marked.

1. Two pizzas are shared equally among 4 friends. Draw the pizzas and show how you would cut them. How much pizza does each friend get?

1/2 of a pizza each

2. Mei spends 1/3 of her money on a book. Then she spends 1/2 of the money she has LEFT on lunch. What fraction of her money has she spent altogether? Use the bar.

2/3 of her money

Show your paper ONCE, after both puzzles: only to your tutor, or all hold paper to the camera together.

Minutes 2 to 6.5. SNEAK-PEEK PUZZLES. Show puzzle 1 and give about a minute; click to show the short answer later. Then show puzzle 2 and give about a minute. Open with one line only: "Guessing is welcome. Nobody is marked." Puzzle 1 is an access puzzle: any child can try it with objects, drawing or counting, so everyone can start well. Puzzle 2 is a thinking puzzle. ONE hold-up for both puzzles: after puzzle 2, everyone shows their paper at the same moment (own paper shown only to you as you walk round; online, all hold paper to the camera together, or show it to you only, on camera). Then click through the two short answers and say each with one short reason. NO volunteer round here (volunteers come in the talk time). Never boards in front of the group, never read out answers with names, no tally, no ranking. Never record scores. Answers and what to listen for. Q1: 1/2 of a pizza each. For example cut each pizza in half: that makes 4 halves, one for each friend. Any fair cutting is fine (for example cutting each pizza into quarters and giving each friend 2 quarters).; Q2: 2/3. After the book 2/3 is left; half of 2/3 is 1/3 (lunch); 1/3 + 1/3 = 2/3. The half is a half of what is LEFT, not of everything. SNEAK-PEEK PUZZLES (about 4.5 minutes). Show the two puzzles one at a time. Say one line only: "Guessing is welcome. Nobody is marked." Puzzle 1 is one that any child can try with objects, drawing or counting, so everybody can start well. Puzzle 2 is a thinking puzzle. Answers are shown PRIVATELY, never on boards in front of the group. Face to face: children write on their own paper and show it only to you as you walk round. Online: everyone holds their paper up to the camera at the same moment, or shows it to you only, on camera. Do not read answers out with names, do not keep a tally, do not rank. Use ONE hold-up for both puzzles (after puzzle 2, everyone shows their paper at the same moment), then click through the answers and say each one with one short reason. There is NO volunteer round in the sneak-peek; volunteers come in the talk time. Never choose a child because you saw a wrong answer. Never record scores. Choosing a start: every child has the same neutral line on workbook page 1: "After question 2 you may choose: carry on, or try Think harder." (The number is the last question on page 1 of this lesson.) Invite the WHOLE group, never one child: "when you have finished Start here you can choose Think harder, Go further or the Investigation." Never single out a child by name and never announce who is on which page. Pairs are not split unless BOTH children agree. Any child may swap at any time.
