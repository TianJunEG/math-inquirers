Think harder: Ratio and fractions

Tool: Draw it

PREVIEW: a Primary 6 topic, beyond Level 5. A taste, not a test.

A bag has red and blue counters in the ratio 3 : 5. This means that for every 3 red counters there are 5 blue counters.

(a) What fraction of the counters are red?

(b) What fraction of the counters are blue?

(c) There are 40 counters in the bag. How many are red?

(a) 3/8.

(b) 5/8.

(c) 15 red counters.

Math Inquirers P5 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Optional, if you finish early (no clock time): show it while some pairs are working or have finished, or use it live as an extra warm-up or tutor-bank question; never at a fixed minute, and it never takes time from the plan. ADDED ITEM r4 (a slide-only addition for the skill gap "Ratio and fractions"; it is not in the printed workbook). TOOL: Draw it. PREVIEW LABEL: say "This is a first taste of a topic from a later level. We are not testing it; we are noticing how it works." SAY: Draw a bar with 3 units for red and 5 units for blue. The whole bar is the whole bag. How many units are in the whole bar? ASK: What fraction of the whole bar is the red part? How many counters is one unit? LISTEN FOR: 3 + 5 = 8 units, red is 3 of the 8, so 3/8. 40 ÷ 8 = 5 counters per unit, 3 × 5 = 15. Listen for the bar model and for the total as 8 units. Everyone writes the answer on their own paper, then say the answer together on three, then click the answer box. Nobody holds anything up, nobody is marked, and passing is always fine. ANSWER: (a) 3/8. (b) 5/8. (c) 15 red counters. WHY: The ratio 3 : 5 gives 8 equal units. Red is 3/8 of the bag and blue is 5/8. 40 ÷ 8 = 5, so red is 3 × 5 = 15 and blue is 5 × 5 = 25. SAME SKILL, NEW NUMBERS (tutor bank, say it aloud or write it on the board): A jug has orange juice and water in the ratio 2 : 7. What fraction of the jug is juice? There are 45 cups of liquid altogether: how many are juice? Answer: 2/9; 10 cups.
