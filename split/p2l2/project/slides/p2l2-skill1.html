Math Inquirers Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Optional slide for pairs who finish early or ask for more. Show it only on request and never announce who is working on it. Children write the answer on their own paper and say it together on three; nothing is held up to the camera. Answers and tutor lines are for you only. Item 1 (Pairing sums: first and last make the same total; tool of the day idea: Try something simpler). SAY: "Write the list forwards, then write it again backwards underneath. Add the two numbers that sit on top of each other." ASK: "What do you notice about the first and the last? And the second and the second last? How many pairs are there?" LISTEN FOR: "Every pair makes the same total." Check that they count the pairs, not the numbers. ANSWER: a) 4 + 39 = 43, 9 + 34 = 43, 14 + 29 = 43, 19 + 24 = 43: four pairs of 43 = 172. b) 11 + 20 = 31, and there are 5 pairs: 155. Item 2 (Letter-digit sums; tool of the day idea: Guess and check). SAY: "Start with the ones column, just like the missing-digit sums." ASK: "What must 8 + 9 make in the ones? What is the carry? Which letter is it?" LISTEN FOR: The ones column shows C = 7 and a carry. Then test the tens. Ask them to check the whole sum. ANSWER: A = 1, B = 9, C = 7 (18 + 79 = 97). Ones: 8 + 9 = 17, so C = 7 and carry 1. Tens: A + 7 + 1 = B, so A + 8 = B. A digit stops at 9, so only A = 1 and B = 9 fit.
