Investigation: Is it always true? The reverse trick

Take a 3-digit number whose first digit is bigger than its last digit, like 842. Reverse it to make 248. Subtract the smaller from the bigger: 842 − 248 = 594.

1. Try 4 more numbers of your own. Write each subtraction and its answer.

2. What do you notice about all your answers? Compare with a partner.

3. Is it always true? Test it on a number that ends in 0, like 720, then explain why you think it works.

Easy first step: try 842 − 248 = 594 for yourself. Then try 953 and 351 (reverse it, then subtract).

DEBRIEF in talk time, minutes 72 to 75: ask for volunteers to share one thing they noticed (a pass is fine). Then show the model answer and ask "What stayed the same? What changed? Why?" Model answer and what to look for: Every answer is in the 99 times table: 99 × (first digit − last digit). 842 − 248 = 594 = 99 × 6 (8 − 2 = 6); 720 − 27 = 693 = 99 × 7. This is true for every 3-digit number whose first digit is bigger than its last (checked by computer for all of them). Why: the middle digit is in the same place in both numbers, so it cancels. The hundreds and ones swap places, so each step of difference between the first and last digits adds 99 (100 − 1). Look for: several tests, spotting the 99s (not only 495; 495 appears only when the digits differ by 5), and any reason about the middle digit. Extension: when is the answer exactly 495?
