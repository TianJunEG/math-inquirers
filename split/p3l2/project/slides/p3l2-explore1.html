Explore: what to do

EXPLORE

  1. Take the digit cards 1 to 9.
  2. Make two 3-digit numbers that add up to a 3-digit number, using every card once.
  3. Stuck? Try 1 and 2 in the hundreds places.
  4. Check the sum by adding. Find more. What is always true?

Go further, any time: With the cards 1 to 9, what is the smallest answer you can make? The biggest?

Math Inquirers Level 3 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 10 to 15. Pairs work with the digit cards 1 to 9 (online: paper squares). Ask them to predict first: "Many ways, a few, or none?" Each card is used once, so the nine boxes use all nine digits. Hints: "Put small digits in the hundreds of the two numbers you add, like 1 and 2." "A digit repeats? Swap two cards and add again." Two examples to keep in your pocket: 173 + 286 = 459 and 218 + 349 = 567. Ready for more: "Add the digits of your answer. Compare with another pair." GO FURTHER (on the slide from minute 10, so nobody waits): With the cards 1 to 9, what is the smallest answer you can make? The biggest? Answer: Smallest 459 (for example 173 + 286); biggest 981 (for example 235 + 746). Every answer has digits adding to 18. Any pair may choose it as soon as their first task is done; do not say who is ready for it.
