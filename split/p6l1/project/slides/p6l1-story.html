Story break: Grace Hopper

Grace Hopper • 1906 to 1992 • United States

Grace Hopper was born in New York City in 1906. She earned a maths PhD at Yale and taught maths at Vassar College. Later she joined the US Navy and worked on an early giant computer at Harvard. In 1952 she finished an early compiler, a program that turns instructions into code a computer can run, and she argued that programs should use English-like words so more people could write them. She is also linked to the word debugging. In 1947 her Harvard colleagues taped a real moth, found stuck in a computer, into the logbook with the note "First actual case of bug being found". Engineers had used the word bug for faults long before that. Hopper liked to teach with a 30 cm piece of wire, which is how far light travels in one billionth of a second.

Try it (optional, no pressure)

A loop starts at 3 and adds 4 each time, giving 3, 7, 11, 15. Write the nth term, then use it to predict the 10th term without running the loop.

Legend check

The moth is often told as "Hopper found the first computer bug". In fact her colleagues found it, the log page was probably not written by her, and engineers used the word bug before 1947. Some sources say she invented the word; that is not accurate.
