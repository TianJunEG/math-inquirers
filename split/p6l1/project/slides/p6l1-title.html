Objective: Continue a sequence, find the rule for the nth term of a sequence with a constant difference, use it to find far terms such as the 100th, work backwards to decide whether a number is in a sequence, and test predictions with a loop and a variable in Scratch. Materials: Laptop or desktop with Scratch 3 open (scratch.mit.edu, no account needed), about 25 matchsticks or toothpicks per child (or squared paper), mini whiteboard, workbook. Optional: a Python editor for children who already know Python. Online: The tutor shares the slides and a Scratch window. Share your Scratch window and build the four blocks together on screen; the blocks are also on the slide. Block colours on our slides are simplified; in Scratch, find blocks by the category name on the left, not by colour. Scratch is free at scratch.mit.edu and needs no account in class. Children tick their own work against the answer slide or share their screen when asked; nothing is held up to the camera. Matchsticks can be drawn as lines. Time plan (the exact minutes of this lesson, with a standing flex buffer): opening 0 to 2, sneak-peek puzzles 2 to 6, warm-up 6 to 12.5, explore 12.5 to 25 (matchsticks first, then Scratch), mini-lesson 25 to 36 (click to reveal each step and answer), brain break 36 to 40.5, worksheet 40.5 to 74 with short teaching before each page and a check-together slide after it (page 1 is the maths, page 2 is the code), flex 74 to 76.5 (unscripted: use it for Think harder or catch-up; it is lost first), talk time 76.5 to 81.5, exit slip 81.5 to 86, looking back 86 to 88, close and stamp 88 to 90. Python: the optional Python version of each program is in the notes of the code slides and in the workbook Python corner. SPINE: Today's tool is Spot the pattern. Find the rule, test it on a new case, then use it to jump far. Look for where you use it. Point at the card on page 1 of the workbook. HABIT: Focus habit: Wonder. Invite a question or a what-if before any answer is given. Say: "What could we ask? What if we changed one thing?" Do not give the answer first: ask the prompt, wait, and give a hint only if the child is stuck.
