Code the Pattern

Sequences and the nth term • loops and a variable

TODAY WE WILL

Find the rule for the nth term of a pattern, predict the 100th term, and test it with a loop in code.

Math Inquirers P6 • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Objective: Continue a sequence, find the rule for the nth term of a sequence with a constant difference, use it to find far terms such as the 100th, work backwards to decide whether a number is in a sequence, and test predictions with a loop and a variable in Scratch. Materials: Laptop or desktop with Scratch 3 open (scratch.mit.edu, no account needed), about 25 matchsticks or toothpicks per child (or squared paper), mini whiteboard, workbook. Optional: a Python editor for children who already know Python. Online: The tutor shares the slides and a Scratch window. Share your Scratch window and build the four blocks together on screen; the blocks are also on the slide. Block colours on our slides are simplified; in Scratch, find blocks by the category name on the left, not by colour. Scratch is free at scratch.mit.edu and needs no account in class. Children hold paper up to the camera or share their screen when asked. Matchsticks can be drawn as lines. Time plan: warm-up 0 to 10, explore 10 to 25 (matchsticks first, then Scratch), teaching 25 to 35 (click to reveal each step and answer), brain break 35 to 40, worksheet 40 to 75 with short teaching before each page and a check-together slide after it (page 1 is the maths, page 2 is the code), talk time 75 to 82, exit ticket 82 to 90. Python: the optional Python version of each program is in the notes of the code slides and in the workbook Python corner.
