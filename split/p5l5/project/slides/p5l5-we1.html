Worked example: water in a tank

A tank is 40 cm long, 25 cm wide and 30 cm high. The water is 12 cm deep. Then 8 ℓ more is poured in. How deep is the water now?

Step 1. Water now: 40 × 25 × 12 = 12 000 cm³ = 12 ℓ

Step 2. 8 ℓ = 8000 cm³. One cm of height holds 40 × 25 = 1000 cm³.

Step 3. The water rises 8000 ÷ 1000 = 8 cm. Now 12 + 8 = 20 cm deep.

Check: 40 × 25 × 20 = 20 000 cm³ = 20 ℓ = 12 ℓ + 8 ℓ ✓

Click to reveal each step and let children say it first. Think-aloud: "The water is a cuboid too: 40 by 25 by 12. That is 12 000 cm3, and 1000 cm3 is 1 litre, so 12 litres. Adding 8 litres is 8000 cm3. The floor of the tank is 40 x 25 = 1000 cm2, so every centimetre of depth holds 1000 cm3. That means 8000 cm3 is 8 cm more." Stress: change litres to cm3 BEFORE dividing. The drawing is to scale: the tank is 30 cm tall and the water is 12 cm, so the water is a bit less than half way. Ask: "How many more litres fill the tank to the top?"
