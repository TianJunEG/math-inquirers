Pouring from one tank into another

Container A (20 × 10 × 24 cm) is full of water. It is poured into empty tank B (base 30 × 20 cm). How deep is the water in B?

Step 1. Water in A: 20 × 10 × 24 = 4800 cm³. The amount does not change when we pour.

Step 2. Base of B is 30 × 20 = 600 cm². 4800 ÷ 600 = 8 cm deep.

Step 3. Pour some back until the depths are equal: both bases share the water. Bases: 200 + 600 = 800 cm².

4800 ÷ 800 = 6 cm in each. Check: 200 × 6 + 600 × 6 = 4800 ✓

Math Inquirers P5 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Teach before worksheet page 2 (Q6). Ask first: "Does the amount of water change when we pour it?" (No: the volume is the same, only the shape changes.) Click through. Think-aloud: "Volume stays 4800. Base of B is 600, so depth 8. Now both containers: the water is shared over both floors. Equal depth d means 200 x d + 600 x d = 4800, so 800 x d = 4800 and d = 6 cm. I add the two base areas, then divide." Do not use letters with the children: say "the same depth" and divide 4800 by 800. Check: A holds 200 x 6 = 1200 cm3 and B holds 600 x 6 = 3600 cm3 and 1200 + 3600 = 4800.
