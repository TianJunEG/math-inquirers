Volume: count a layer, then the layers

Step 1. One layer on the bottom: 5 rows of 3 cubes. 5 × 3 = 15 cubes.

Step 2. There are 4 layers on top of each other. 15 × 4 = 60 cubes.

Step 3. Volume = length × breadth × height = 5 × 3 × 4 = 60 cm³. Each cube is 1 cm³.

The base (length × breadth) is one layer. The height says how many layers.

Math Inquirers P5 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Teach. Build the 5 by 3 by 4 cuboid with real cubes (or draw it on squared paper). Ask first: "How many cubes are in the bottom layer? How many layers?" Click through the steps. Think-aloud: "One layer is 5 rows of 3: 15. I have 4 layers, so 15 four times: 60. Length times breadth times height counts the same thing quickly." Ask: "If I use a 1-cm cube, one cube is 1 cm3." Go backwards: "A cuboid has volume 60 cm3 and a base of 15 cm2. How tall is it?" (60 / 15 = 4 layers).
