Worked example: a stone sinks

The water in a tank (base 30 cm by 20 cm) is 10 cm deep. A stone sinks to the bottom. The water rises to 13 cm. What is the volume of the stone?

Step 1. The water did not change. The stone pushes it up.

Step 2. Rise = 13 − 10 = 3 cm. The new slice of water: 30 × 20 × 3 = 1800 cm³.

Step 3. That slice is exactly the room the stone takes up.

The stone is 1800 cm³.

Math Inquirers P5 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Do the live demonstration first if you can: clear box, ruler on the side, read the water level, drop in a stone, read it again. Then click through. Think-aloud: "The water went up 3 cm. The water itself is the same amount, so the stone must be taking up the space of a slice 30 by 20 by 3: 1800 cm3." Rule to write down: rise = volume of object / base area, so volume of object = base area x rise. Backwards: "A stone of 600 cm3 in this tank: 600 / 600 = 1 cm rise." Note the stone is completely under water here. If it sticks out, only the part under water counts: that comes back in the challenge.
