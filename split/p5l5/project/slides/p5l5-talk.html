Ali says: “A 1000 cm³ stone always makes the water rise 1 cm.” Is he right? Explain.

Sentence starters:

“I noticed that …” “I tested it by …” “I know because …”

Math Inquirers P5 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 70 to 80. Pairs talk for 2 minutes, then share. Answer: Ali is right only when the base is 1000 cm2 (for example 40 cm by 25 cm). The rise is the stone's volume divided by the base area. In a tank with a 20 cm by 10 cm base (200 cm2) the same stone makes the water rise 1000 / 200 = 5 cm. A wide tank spreads the volume over a big base, so the water hardly rises. Second question if time: "If the stone sticks out of the water, is it the whole volume of the stone that counts?" (No, only the part under the water.) Praise the reasons, not only the answers.
