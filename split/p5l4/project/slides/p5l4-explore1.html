Explore: what to do

EXPLORE

  1. Draw a rectangle 8 cm by 6 cm on grid paper.
  2. Mark any point on the top side. Join it to the two bottom corners.
  3. Cut out the triangle and the two leftover pieces.
  4. Do the two pieces cover the triangle exactly? Try again with a different point.

Math Inquirers P5 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 9 to 14. Pairs draw the rectangle on grid paper (cut-out page at the back of the workbook) and cut out the three pieces (online: camera up as usual; hold the cut pieces up to the camera). Predict first: what fraction of the rectangle is the triangle? Does it matter where the point is? Anyone who wants more (open to every pair): put the point at the end of the top side. What kind of triangle is it now? Is it still half? (Yes: a right-angled triangle, half of the rectangle.) GO FURTHER (say it at minute 9): any pair that finishes step 4 early may try the what-if question on the next slide. It is open to every pair, not a reward. Answer: For example base 6 and height 4, base 8 and height 3, base 12 and height 2, base 4 and height 6. Each time base × height = 24, which is double the area (12 × 2). Any triangle on the same base and height, leaning or not, is also fine.
