Same base, same height: same area

Three triangles stand on the bottom side of a 14 cm by 9 cm rectangle. Their top corners are at different points on the top side. What is the area of each?

Step 1. Each has base 14 cm and height 9 cm: 1/2 × 14 × 9 = 63 cm² for ALL three.

Same base and same height means the same area, however far the triangle leans.

Math Inquirers P5 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Teach. Ask first: "Do the three triangles have the same area? They look different." Collect opinions, then click. Think-aloud: "Look at the dashed height in each: it is 9 cm every time, because the top corner is on the top side, which is 9 cm above the bottom side. The base is the same 14 cm. So 63 cm² each, half of the rectangle." Connect to the warm-up: Rectangle 2 was half because it was this exact situation. Ask: "What if the top corner is at the very end of the top side?" (a right-angled triangle, still 63.) And: "What if it moves beyond the rectangle, along the same top line?" (the height is still 9 cm: still 63).
