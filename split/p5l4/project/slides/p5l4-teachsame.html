Same area, different triangles

A triangle has an area of 30 cm². What could its base and height be?

Step 1. Area 30 cm², so base × height = 60 (double 30).

Step 2. List pairs that multiply to 60: base 10, height 6; base 12, height 5; base 15, height 4; and more.

Check: 1/2 × 10 × 6 = 30, 1/2 × 12 × 5 = 30, 1/2 × 15 × 4 = 30 ✓

Math Inquirers P5 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Teach before worksheet page 2 (Q5, a triangle of 24 cm² with a base of 12 cm). Think-aloud: "The area is half of base times height, so base times height is DOUBLE the area: 60. Now I need two numbers that multiply to 60, and I can list them in order: 1 and 60, 2 and 30, 3 and 20, 4 and 15, 5 and 12, 6 and 10." On the worksheet they use the same idea with a different area. All the triangles lean in different ways but share the same area: link to same base and same height.
