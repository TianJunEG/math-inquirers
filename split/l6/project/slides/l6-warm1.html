Math Inquirers Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 0 to 5. The Money Shop. Hand out play money: coins (5¢, 10¢, 20¢, 50¢, $1) and notes ($2, $5, $10, $50). Point to one item at a time: "The pencil costs 80¢. Pay exactly. Can you pay with fewer pieces of money than your partner?" Children lay out their money, then you click to show the fewest way for that item (clicks reveal Eraser, Pencil, Sticker book, Water bottle, Toy car, Scooter in order). Fewest: Eraser 65¢ = 50¢ + 10¢ + 5¢ (3). Pencil 80¢ = 50¢ + 20¢ + 10¢ (3). Sticker book $3 = $2 + $1 (2). Water bottle $12 = $10 + $2 (2). Toy car $25 = $10 + $10 + $5 (3). Scooter $65 = $50 + $10 + $5 (3). Ask: "Cents or dollars? How do you know?" (the ¢ sign or the $ sign). "Someone paid $12 with six $2 notes. Is that right?" (Yes, exactly $12, just not the fewest.) "Why do we start with the biggest note that is not too big?" Quick finisher: "Buy the water bottle AND the toy car. How much? Fewest notes?" ($37: $10 + $10 + $10 + $5 + $2, 5 notes). Watch for: children who count how many pieces instead of the value, and children who mix up 50¢ and $50.
