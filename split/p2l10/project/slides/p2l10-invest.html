Investigation: Staircase

A puzzle with more than one good answer. Share one way you tried it, or pass.

Mei climbs a staircase. She can take 1 step or 2 steps at a time. For 3 steps there are 3 ways: 1+1+1, 1+2 and 2+1.

(a) Find ALL the ways for 4 steps.

(b) Find ALL the ways for 5 steps.

(c) Fill in a table for 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 steps. What pattern do you see? Can you guess the number of ways for 6 steps? Needs: paper and a pencil.

Easy first step: draw 3 steps and show the 3 ways: 1+1+1, 1+2 and 2+1.

Math Inquirers P2 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 77 to 80 (3 minutes, after talk time). Investigation debrief. The Investigation is printed on the last core page of the workbook; children may have started it any time after question 3 (it is open to everyone), or it is take-home. Say to the whole group: "Who would like to share one way they tried the Investigation? Passing is fine." Ask for volunteers only; never name a child; no public tallies, scores or rankings. Several answers are right: praise the method (a list in order, a table, a drawing), not the speed. If nobody has started it, show the first step on the slide and let pairs try for one minute, then set it as take-home. Model answer and what to look for: Number of ways: 1 step: 1, 2 steps: 2, 3 steps: 3, 4 steps: 5, 5 steps: 8 (brute-forced; 6 steps: 13). 4 steps: 1+1+1+1, 1+1+2, 1+2+1, 2+1+1, 2+2. Pattern: each number is the sum of the two before it (3 + 5 = 8, 5 + 8 = 13). Look for: a list in order (all 1s first, then swap in a 2), a table, and the pattern spotted from the table. Do not name Fibonacci numbers unless a child asks.
