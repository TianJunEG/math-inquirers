PRIMARY 2 • LESSON 10 • WEEK 5

Puzzle Day

Mixed puzzles: how to start a puzzle

TODAY WE WILL

Learn three ways to start a puzzle, then use them on mixed challenges.

Math Inquirers P2 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Objective: Choose a way to start a puzzle (draw it, list it in order, make a table) and use it on mixed puzzles: picture graphs with a key of 5 or 10, a logic grid with exactly one answer, numbers to 1000, money, fractions, and × and ÷ with the 2, 3, 4, 5 and 10 tables. Check the answer and explain it. Materials: Four station cards (all on ONE slide in the deck, printed in the tutor guide), play coins, digit cards 0 to 9, paper strips for fractions, mini whiteboards, certificates, stamps. Online: Each breakout pair takes two station cards (the tutor pastes their text into the breakout room chat), about 2 minutes a card, then everyone reports back to the group. Each child needs digit cards (cut from paper), a pencil and a mini whiteboard. Time plan: opening 0 to 1.5, sneak-peek puzzles 1.5 to 6, warm-up 6 to 10.5, explore 10.5 to 23.5, mini-lesson 23.5 to 33.5, brain break 33.5 to 36.5, worksheet teach slide 36.5 to 39, worksheet page 1 39 to 51, check together, page 1 51 to 53.5, short teaching 53.5 to 57, worksheet page 2 57 to 68.5, check together, page 2 68.5 to 71, flex (spare time) 71 to 75, talk time 75 to 80, exit ticket 80 to 85, looking back 85 to 87, certificates and closing 87 to 90. The Think harder and Go further slides are optional, if you finish early (no clock time). Kit ready at minute 0: ask families to put every item on the desk before the lesson starts (a 30-second check at the start).
