Challenge Day

Mixed puzzles: how to start a puzzle

TODAY WE WILL

Learn three ways to start a puzzle, then use them on mixed challenges.

Math Inquirers Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Objective: Choose a way to start a puzzle (draw it, list it in order, make a table) and use it on mixed puzzles: picture graphs with a key of 5 or 10, a logic grid with exactly one answer, numbers to 1000, money, fractions, and × and ÷ with the 2, 3, 4, 5 and 10 tables. Check the answer and explain it. Materials: Four station cards (one slide each in the deck, printed in the tutor guide), play coins, digit cards 0 to 9, paper strips for fractions, mini whiteboards, certificates, stamps. Online: Each breakout pair gets one station card (the tutor pastes its text into the breakout room chat), works for 3 minutes, swaps with another pair for a second card, then everyone reports back to the group. Each child needs digit cards (cut from paper), a pencil and a mini whiteboard. Time plan: Sneak-peek puzzles 1 to 4, warm-up game 4 to 6, quick questions 6 to 8, explore 8 to 20 (explore2 talk is 6 minutes), teaching 20 to 32 (how to start a puzzle: draw it, list it, make a table; click to reveal each step and answer), brain break 32 to 36, short teaching 36 to 38, worksheet page 1 38 to 50 then check together 50 to 52, short teaching 52 to 57, worksheet page 2 57 to 69 then check together 69 to 72, talk time 72 to 80, exit ticket 80 to 85, Looking back 85 to 87, certificates 87 to 90.
