Explore: what to do

EXPLORE

  1. Take the 7 strips you cut out BEFORE the lesson (the printed strip page).
  2. Keep one whole. Mark halves, quarters and eighths on three strips. The thirds, sixths and twelfths are already printed.
  3. Label every part and stack the strips into a wall.
  4. Ask: which pieces line up exactly?

Go further, any time: Find 3 DIFFERENT pieces that make exactly 12. Is there another way?

Math Inquirers P3 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 11 to 21. Predict first: "Which is bigger, one sixth or one eighth of the same strip?" Parents cut the printed strips out BEFORE the lesson (the one-week-before message says so), so nobody spends time finding or cutting strips. Pairs only MARK halves, quarters and eighths (fold in half again and again), then compare them with the printed thirds, sixths and twelfths (about 6.5 minutes of pair work). Children label every part (1/2, 1/4 …) and stack the strips. Stress: the parts in a row must be equal, or they are not halves, thirds … Hints: "Which pieces line up with the dashed line at one half?" Ready for more: "Find three different pieces that together cover exactly one half." (with three DIFFERENT pieces the only way is 1/4 + 1/6 + 1/12; 1/4 + 1/8 + 1/8 repeats a piece). Online: children describe their wall aloud. GO FURTHER (on the slide from the start of explore, so nobody waits): Find 3 DIFFERENT pieces that make exactly 1/2. Is there another way? Answer: Only one way with three different pieces: 1/4 + 1/6 + 1/12 (3/12 + 2/12 + 1/12 = 6/12). Other ways repeat a piece, e.g. 1/4 + 1/8 + 1/8. Any pair may choose it as soon as their first task is done; do not say who is ready for it. Online: pairs are in pre-assigned breakout rooms (set up before the lesson, auto-move in and auto-close with a timer, about 45 seconds per move). Visit each breakout room at least once while the pairs work (about 1 minute per room).
