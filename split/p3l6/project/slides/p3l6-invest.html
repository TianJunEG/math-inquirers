Investigation: Find all the ways

Make exactly 34 using pieces of 12, 14 and 18. You may use as many of each as you like. Order does not matter (12 + 14 is the same way as 14 + 12).

1. List every way. Start with the most halves.

2. How do you know you have found them all?

3. What if we want exactly 1 whole instead? Do you think there will be more ways or fewer? Test your idea.

Easy first step: make 34 with one half. How much is left to fill?

Math Inquirers Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 67 to 72: the Investigation has its OWN slot. Show this slide to the whole group and invite everybody: "You can choose Think harder, Go further or the Investigation now. Nobody has to." Do not say who is on which one. Not finished at 72? Take it home and bring one thing to share at the start of next lesson (volunteers only). DEBRIEF in talk time, minutes 72 to 75: ask for volunteers to share one thing they noticed (a pass is fine). Then show the model answer and ask "What stayed the same? What changed? Why?" Model answer and what to look for: 3/4 is the same as 6/8. The 6 ways (halves, quarters, eighths): 1 half + 1 quarter; 1 half + 2 eighths; 3 quarters; 2 quarters + 2 eighths; 1 quarter + 4 eighths; 6 eighths. Systematic: fix the number of halves (1 or 0), then the quarters, then fill with eighths. For 1 whole there are 9 ways, so more. Look for: a list in order, an organised method for 'all', and a reason that more room means more ways. Using the fraction wall is expected.
