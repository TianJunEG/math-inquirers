Think harder: Spinners with unequal parts

Tool: Draw it

A spinner is split into 3 parts. Yellow takes 1/2 of the circle. Green takes 1/3 of the circle. Blue is the rest.

(a) What fraction of the circle is blue?

(b) Which colour is the spinner most likely to stop on?

(c) Mei spins 60 times. About how many times should she expect yellow?

(a) 1/6.

(b) Yellow.

(c) About 30 times (green about 20, blue about 10).

ADDED ITEM spin (a slide-only addition for the skill gap "Spinners with unequal parts"; it is not in the printed workbook). TOOL: Draw it. SAY: The picture is a fair way to see the parts of one whole circle. Think of the whole as 6 equal slices. ASK: How many slices is yellow? Green? How many are left for blue? LISTEN FOR: Common denominator 6: 3/6, 2/6, 1/6. Listen for "more of the circle means more likely". For (c) accept "about", never "exactly". Everyone writes the answer on their own paper, then say the answer together on three, then click the answer box. Nobody holds anything up, nobody is marked, and passing is always fine. ANSWER: (a) 1/6. (b) Yellow. (c) About 30 times (green about 20, blue about 10). WHY: 1/2 = 3/6 and 1/3 = 2/6, so blue is 1/6. Yellow has the biggest part. 1/2 of 60 = 30, 1/3 of 60 = 20, 1/6 of 60 = 10.
