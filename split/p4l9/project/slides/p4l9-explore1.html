Explore: what to do

EXPLORE

  1. Look at the table: people in the queue at a chicken rice stall.
  2. Choose a scale for the side: how much is each line worth?
  3. Plot each point and join the points with straight lines.
  4. Write three things your graph tells you.

Go further (any time): Draw the same graph again with a different scale. Does the story look different? Why?

Math Inquirers P4 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 8 to 13. Pairs plot the queue on a blank grid (online: printed grid or squared paper on camera). Ask them to predict first: when is the queue longest? Let pairs choose their own scale: lines worth 1, 2 or 5 all work if the lines are equal steps; a scale of 1 needs 22 lines. Go further (on the slide), for any child who wants it: between which two times did the queue grow the most? Can you see it without working out every change? GO FURTHER (on the slide from minute 8, so nobody waits): Draw the same graph again with a different scale. Does the story look different? Why? Answer: Yes. A scale with small steps, or one that starts above 0, makes the line look steeper; big steps make it look flatter. The numbers are the same: only the picture changed.
