Story break: Florence Nightingale

Britain (born in Florence, Italy) • 1820-1910

Florence Nightingale was born in Florence, Italy, in 1820. She is best known as a nurse, but she also loved numbers. During the Crimean War in the 1850s she collected figures about the hospitals for soldiers. They showed that many more soldiers became sick from dirty, crowded conditions than from the fighting. She knew that long lists of numbers are hard to read, so she drew special charts to make the facts easy to see. Her ideas helped to bring in cleaner, safer hospitals. In 1858 she became the first woman elected a Fellow of the Statistical Society of London. A clear table or graph can help people make better decisions.

TRY IT (OPTIONAL)

Temperatures: 9 am 26 degrees, noon 31 degrees, 3 pm 33 degrees, 6 pm 29 degrees. Between which two times did it rise the most, and by how much?

Math Inquirers Level 4 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

STORY BREAK, optional, about 2 minutes (minutes 32 to 34, after the movement part of the brain break). SKIP IT if you are short of time or the group is restless: say "We will keep this story for another day" and go straight to the worksheet; nothing later depends on it. To run it: read the story aloud, slowly, or ask one volunteer to read it (nobody has to; pass is fine). Then read the Try it question; children may answer in the chat or hold up a thumb, nobody is marked, and you never name or rank a child. Link to today: Today you read tables and line graphs, and Florence used tables and charts to make numbers easy to understand. TRY IT ANSWER (tutor only, do not show): 9 am to 12 noon: it rose by 5 degrees (31 - 26). Noon to 3 pm: it rose by only 2 degrees. So the biggest rise was 9 am to 12 noon, by 5 degrees. (Between 3 pm and 6 pm it fell by 4 degrees.) Everything in the story is true as far as historians know. Confidence: high. Dates for people from long ago are estimates.
