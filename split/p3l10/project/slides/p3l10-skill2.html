Optional, if you finish early

Think harder: posts and rectangles

1. A path is 24 m long. Lanterns stand on both sides of the path, one every 3 m. There is a lantern at both ends of each side.

How many lanterns are there altogether?

2. A window has 3 panes across and 2 panes up and down. Count every rectangle you can find (squares count too).

How many rectangles are there? Count them by size.

Optional slide for pairs who finish early or ask for more. Show it only on request and never announce who is working on it. Children write the answer on their own paper and say it together on three; nothing is held up to the camera. Answers and tutor lines are for you only. Item 1 (Gaps and posts, both sides of a path; tool of the day idea: Draw it). SAY: "Do one side first. Count the gaps, then the lanterns." ASK: "How many gaps on one side? One more lantern than gaps, so how many? Then what?" LISTEN FOR: "8 gaps, 9 lanterns, doubled." ANSWER: 24 / 3 = 8 gaps, so 9 lanterns on one side. Two sides: 18 lanterns. Item 2 (Counting rectangles by size; tool of the day idea: List every way). SAY: "Count by size. Make a table: size, how many." ASK: "How many of the smallest size? Of the next size? How do you know you have not missed any?" LISTEN FOR: A table by size. ANSWER: 1 by 1: 6. 2 across by 1: 4. 3 across by 1: 2. 1 across by 2 up: 3. 2 by 2: 2. 3 across by 2 up: 1. In all: 18.
