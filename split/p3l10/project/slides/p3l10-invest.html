Investigation: Handshakes: draw it, list it, make a table

Some friends meet. Everyone shakes hands with every other friend once. Two friends make 1 handshake.

1. Find the number of handshakes for 3, 4, 5 and 6 friends. Draw dots and lines, or make a list, to help. Fill in the table.

2. What pattern do you see? Use it to find the handshakes for 10 friends.

3. Could there ever be exactly 20 handshakes? Explain.

Easy first step: draw 3 dots and join every pair. Count the lines. Then try 4 dots. A table for your answers is on the Investigation page of the workbook.

Do not say who is on which one. Not finished at 72? Take it home and bring one thing to share at the start of next lesson (volunteers only). DEBRIEF in talk time, minutes 72 to 75: ask for volunteers to share one thing they noticed (a pass is fine). Then show the model answer and ask "What stayed the same? What changed? Why?" Model answer and what to look for: 2 friends: 1; 3: 3; 4: 6; 5: 10; 6: 15. The extra amount goes up by 2, 3, 4, 5, so 7 friends: 21, 8: 28, 9: 36, 10: 45. Reason: each new friend shakes hands with everyone already there. 10 friends: 9 + 8 + 7 + ... + 1 = 45. 20 handshakes is impossible: 6 friends give 15 and 7 friends give 21 (checked by computer for every group size). Look for: a drawing or an organised list, the pattern in the jumps, and a sensible check of the 20 claim using the table. Extension: how many for 20 friends? (190)
