Worked example: change, then work

A bag has 2 kg 100 g of flour. Mum uses 850 g. How much flour is left?

Step 1. Change to grams: 2 kg 100 g = 2000 g + 100 g = 2100 g

Step 2. Take away: 2100 g − 850 g = 1250 g

Step 3. Change back: 1250 g = 1000 g + 250 g = 1 kg 250 g

Check: 1250 + 850 = 2100 ✓

Math Inquirers Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Worked example. Ask first: "Can we take 850 away from 100 g?" (Not without breaking the kilograms.) Click each step. Think-aloud: "I change the bag to grams: 2 kg is 2000 g, plus 100 is 2100 g. Now it is a normal take-away: 2100 − 850 = 1250 g. I change back: 1000 g of it is 1 kg, so 1 kg 250 g. I check by adding back: 1250 + 850 = 2100." Some children prefer to work in kg and g separately (renaming 1 kg as 1000 g): accept it, but show why one unit is less likely to go wrong.
