Explore: what to do

EXPLORE

  1. You have $20 to plan a party for 8 friends.
  2. Each friend drinks 250 ml of punch. Buy at least 2 ℓ of punch (any number of bottles).
  3. Buy at least one food. You may buy more than one of an item. Spend as close to $20 as you can.
  4. Challenge: can you spend exactly $20?

Go further (your choice, any time): What if there are 12 friends and you have $30? How many bottles of punch do you need? Can you spend exactly $30?

Math Inquirers Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 10 to 15. Party planner. Pairs use the menu card and play money (or paper). You may buy more than one of an item. Punch: 8 × 250 ml = 2000 ml = 2 ℓ, so 1 bottle is the least; the rule is "at least 2 ℓ of punch (any number of bottles) and at least one food". Let pairs try first; prompt with "How did you add the prices?" Ways that cost exactly $20 (there are exactly 3): pizza + 2 punch + 2 balloons ($8.90 + $6.30 + $4.80); wings + 2 punch + 3 balloons ($6.50 + $6.30 + $7.20); cupcakes + 3 punch + 2 balloons ($5.75 + $9.45 + $4.80). Hint for exact $20: look at the cents. Only the punch (15¢) and the cupcakes (75¢) end in 5, so you need an even number of those altogether. Online: share the menu on screen; pairs type their plan in the chat. GO FURTHER (on the slide from minute 10, so nobody waits): What if there are 12 friends and you have $30? How many bottles of punch do you need? Can you spend exactly $30? Answer: 12 x 250 ml = 3000 ml = 3 l, so 2 bottles (2 l each) at least. Yes, there are many ways, e.g. 2 punch + 3 cupcakes + 3 balloons ($6 + $16.50 + $7.50), or 2 curry puffs + 2 punch + 2 cupcakes + 2 balloons. Any pair may choose it as soon as their first task is done; do not say who is ready for it.
