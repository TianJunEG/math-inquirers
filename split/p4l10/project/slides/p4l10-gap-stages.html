Think harder: Counting in two stages

Tool: List every way

Aisha has 4 tops, 3 skirts and 2 hats. She wears 1 top, 1 skirt and 1 hat.

(a) How many different outfits can she make?

(b) Her red top does not go with her green skirt. How many outfits can she make now?

(a) 24 outfits.

(b) 22 outfits.

Math Inquirers P4 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Optional, if you finish early (no clock time): show it while some pairs are working or have finished, or use it live as an extra warm-up or tutor-bank question; never at a fixed minute, and it never takes time from the plan. ADDED ITEM stages (a slide-only addition for the skill gap "Counting in two stages"; it is not in the printed workbook). TOOL: List every way. SAY: Draw 4 boxes for the tops. Under each one, show 3 skirts. How many top-and-skirt pairs is that? ASK: How many outfits use the red top with the green skirt? What do you do with that number? LISTEN FOR: 4 x 3 = 12 pairs, times 2 hats = 24. Listen for "multiply the choices". For (b) the red-green pair has 2 outfits (one for each hat); 24 - 2 = 22. Everyone writes the answer on their own paper, then say the answer together on three, then click the answer box. Nobody holds anything up, nobody is marked, and passing is always fine. ANSWER: (a) 24 outfits. (b) 22 outfits. WHY: 4 x 3 x 2 = 24. The red top with the green skirt can go with either hat, so 2 outfits are removed: 24 - 2 = 22. SAME SKILL, NEW NUMBERS (tutor bank, say it aloud or write it on the board): There are 3 paths from home to the park and 4 paths from the park to school. How many routes from home to school through the park? Answer: 12 routes.
