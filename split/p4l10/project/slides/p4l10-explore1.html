Explore: four puzzle stations

EXPLORE

  1. Four puzzle cards, A to D. Start with any card.
  2. Read the card aloud with your partner.
  3. Try something: a guess, a drawing or a table.
  4. Write the answer AND how you got it.

Go further (any time): What if 10 bugs have 65 legs? Could that happen? Why or why not?

Math Inquirers P4 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 8 to 13. Pairs move around four stations (online: one card per breakout pair, then swap). Full cards: A) Two next-door numbers multiply to make 210. What are they? B) Spiders have 8 legs and beetles have 6 legs. 10 bugs have 68 legs altogether. How many spiders? C) I double a number and add 7. I get 43. What is my number? D) Ali, Mei and Ravi each have a different pet: a cat, a dog and a fish. Ali's pet has no fur. Mei's pet does not bark. Who has which pet? Answers: A) 14 and 15. B) 4 spiders and 6 beetles. C) 18. D) Ali fish, Mei cat, Ravi dog. Do not teach the methods yet: let pairs try their own way. GO FURTHER (on the slide from minute 8, so nobody waits): What if 10 bugs have 65 legs? Could that happen? Why or why not? Answer: No. Spiders have 8 legs and beetles 6: both are even numbers, so the total number of legs is always even, and 65 is odd.
