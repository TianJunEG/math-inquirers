Think harder: Smallest and largest totals

Tool: List every way

Siti rolls 4 ordinary dice (faces 1 to 6) and adds the 4 scores.

(a) Find the least total possible.

(b) Find the greatest total possible.

(c) Which of these totals can never happen: 3, 4, 13, 24, 25?

(a) 4.

(b) 24.

(c) 3 and 25 are impossible.

Math Inquirers P4 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Optional, if you finish early (no clock time): show it while some pairs are working or have finished, or use it live as an extra warm-up or tutor-bank question; never at a fixed minute, and it never takes time from the plan. ADDED ITEM dice (a slide-only addition for the skill gap "Smallest and largest totals"; it is not in the printed workbook). TOOL: List every way. SAY: Do not roll anything. Ask what the lowest face and the highest face on one die are, then think about 4 dice. ASK: Can she get every total from 4 to 24? How can you tell? LISTEN FOR: Smallest 1 + 1 + 1 + 1, largest 6 + 6 + 6 + 6. Listen for "change one die by 1 at a time", which gives every total in between. Everyone writes the answer on their own paper, then say the answer together on three, then click the answer box. Nobody holds anything up, nobody is marked, and passing is always fine. ANSWER: (a) 4. (b) 24. (c) 3 and 25 are impossible. WHY: 4 × 1 = 4 and 4 × 6 = 24. Starting at 4 you can raise one die by 1 at a time up to 24, so every total from 4 to 24 can be made. 3 is below 4 and 25 is above 24. SAME SKILL, NEW NUMBERS (tutor bank, say it aloud or write it on the board): Siti rolls 5 dice and adds the scores. Find the least and the greatest total possible. Answer: 5 and 30.
