1. A garden has only ducks (2 legs) and rabbits (4 legs). There are 8 animals and 22 legs. How many rabbits are there?

3 rabbits (and 5 ducks): 5 × 2 + 3 × 4 = 22. Suppose all 8 are ducks: 16 legs, 6 too few; each rabbit instead of a duck adds 2 legs, so 3 rabbits.

2. I think of a number. I add 7, then I double it. I get 30. What was my number? How did you work it out?

8. Work backwards: 30 ÷ 2 = 15, then 15 − 7 = 8.

Math Inquirers P4 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 1 to 4 (3 minutes). WARM-UP CHECK: two quick questions on today's key idea in a new setting. Not a test. Read question 1, give about 90 seconds, children write the answer AND a one-sentence reason on a whiteboard and show you (or send it in private chat). Same for question 2. Do not say who is right yet. Then click to show each answer and let one or two children say their reason aloud. ANSWERS: 1) 3 rabbits (and 5 ducks): 5 × 2 + 3 × 4 = 22. Suppose all 8 are ducks: 16 legs, 6 too few; each rabbit instead of a duck adds 2 legs, so 3 rabbits. 2) 8. Work backwards: 30 ÷ 2 = 15, then 15 − 7 = 8. A GOOD REASON: Pick a way in: guess and check with a table, suppose they are all the same, or work backwards from the end. Then check every fact. FAST TRACK: a child who gets BOTH right and can say why starts on the Think Harder pages and the Open investigation page at minute 34, while the group works on pages 1 and 2. Tell them by private chat or a quiet word ("start on the Think Harder page today"); say nothing to the group and label nobody. Everyone still joins the explore, the mini-lesson, the page checks, talk time, the exit ticket and the reflection. A fast-track child who is stuck for 5 minutes can go back to page 1 with no fuss. It changes lesson by lesson. Groups are up to 8 and the workbook is printed, so each child only needs a different page number.
