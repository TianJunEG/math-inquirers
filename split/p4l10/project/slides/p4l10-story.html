Story break: Terence Tao

Australia (born in Adelaide); now also works in the USA • born 1975

Terence Tao grew up in Adelaide, Australia. He enjoyed maths so much that he took university maths classes while still a schoolboy. In 1986 he entered the International Mathematical Olympiad, a contest where school students from many countries solve hard puzzle-style problems. He was only ten, the youngest competitor ever, and he won a bronze medal. In 1987 he won silver, and in 1988 gold. He is still the youngest ever winner of each of the three medals. As an adult he became a professor, and in 2006 he won the Fields Medal, a top prize in mathematics. Hard problems are for practising, and trying again is part of the plan.

Use guess and check: a farm has hens and cows. Together they have 10 heads and 28 legs. How many hens and how many cows?

STORY BREAK, optional, about 2 minutes (minutes 32 to 34, after the movement part of the brain break). SKIP IT if you are short of time or the group is restless: say "We will keep this story for another day" and go straight to the worksheet; nothing later depends on it. To run it: read the story aloud, slowly, or ask one volunteer to read it (nobody has to; pass is fine). Then read the Try it question; children may answer in the chat or hold up a thumb, nobody is marked, and you never name or rank a child. Link to today: Today is Challenge Day, with competition-style puzzles like the ones in the Olympiad that Terence Tao entered three years in a row. TRY IT ANSWER (tutor only, do not show): 6 hens and 4 cows. Check: 6 + 4 = 10 heads; 6 x 2 + 4 x 4 = 12 + 16 = 28 legs. (Try 5 cows: 20 + 10 = 30 legs, too many; 3 cows: 12 + 14 = 26, too few.) Everything in the story is true as far as historians know. Confidence: high. Dates for people from long ago are estimates.
