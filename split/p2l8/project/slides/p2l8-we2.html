30–32 MIN

Worked example: count every square

This grid is 3 squares long and 3 squares high. Count by size, smallest first.

Step 1. 1 by 1 squares: 3 in each row, 3 rows. 3 + 3 + 3 = 9

Step 2. 2 by 2 squares: slide a 2 by 2 window. It fits in 4 places.

Step 3. 3 by 3 squares: the whole grid. 1 of them.

9 + 4 + 1 = 14 squares

Math Inquirers Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Click to reveal each step; ask the group to predict before each click. Step 2: hold up a paper 2 by 2 window and slide it: top left, top right, bottom left, bottom right. "Can it go anywhere else?" No: 4 places. Think-aloud: "Squares hide inside bigger squares, so I count by size. 1 by 1: 9. 2 by 2: I slide my window: 4. 3 by 3: just the whole grid: 1. 9 + 4 + 1 = 14." Watch for children who count 2 small squares side by side: that is a rectangle, not a square. Ask: "What do you notice about 9, 4 and 1?" (3 + 3 + 3, 2 + 2, 1: the window fits in fewer places as it grows).
