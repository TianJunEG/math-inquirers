Investigation: Squares joined edge to edge

A puzzle with more than one good answer. Share one way you tried it, or pass.

Squares must touch along a whole side. A shape that is turned round or flipped over counts as the SAME shape.

(a) Find ALL the different shapes you can make with 3 squares.

(b) Now find ALL the different shapes you can make with 4 squares. Draw each one on the grid.

(c) How do you know you have found them all? Needs: square tiles or small squares of paper, and the grid on this page.

Easy first step: join 2 squares edge to edge. How many different shapes can you make? Then add a third square.

Math Inquirers P2 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 77 to 80 (3 minutes, after talk time). Investigation debrief. The Investigation is printed on the last core page of the workbook; children may have started it any time after question 3 (it is open to everyone), or it is take-home. Say to the whole group: "Who would like to share one way they tried the Investigation? Passing is fine." Ask for volunteers only; never name a child; no public tallies, scores or rankings. Several answers are right: praise the method (a list in order, a table, a drawing), not the speed. If nobody has started it, show the first step on the slide and let pairs try for one minute, then set it as take-home. Model answer and what to look for: (a) 2 shapes: a straight line and an L. (b) 5 shapes: a straight line of 4, a 2 x 2 square, an L, a T and a zigzag (S shape). Brute-forced: 1, 1, 2, 5 shapes for 1, 2, 3, 4 squares (turns and flips count as the same). If a child counts flips as different, they find 7 (L and its mirror, S and its mirror): praise this and explain the rule. (c) Look for a system: start with the straight line, then move one square, and check each new shape against the others by turning the tile.
