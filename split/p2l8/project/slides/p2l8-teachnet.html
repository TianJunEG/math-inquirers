57.5–59.5 MIN

Nets: will it fold into a cube?

A net is a flat shape that folds up into a solid. A cube net has 6 squares.

Step 1. Pick a square to be the bottom (yellow). Fold up the squares next to it: they are the walls.

Step 2. Keep folding. The square at the top end folds over to be the lid. Each square lands on a NEW face.

6 different faces, nothing left open: it folds into a cube ✓

Not a cube net: the 2 bottom squares both fold onto the same face, and there is no lid.

Math Inquirers Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 57.5 to 59.5. Teach before worksheet page 2 (Q4 asks which nets fold into a cube; the children cut out and fold nets A to D with the tutor on camera for net A; nets E and F are Think harder 3). Have a printed copy of this net cut out, or open up a small box. Click 1 and 2: fold it in front of the children. Think-aloud: "I choose a bottom. The squares touching it fold up as walls. The row of 4 wraps round: bottom, wall, top, wall. The two loose squares close the ends. Every square lands on a different face, so it is a cube." Click 4: the shape with two squares under the ends: "When I fold, both of these land on the same face. One face gets two squares, and one face gets none. Not a cube." Tip for the worksheet: squares that land on the same place, or a row of 5 or more squares, or a 2 by 2 block of squares mean it is not a cube net.
