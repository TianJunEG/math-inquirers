Investigation: Mirror numbers

A puzzle with more than one good answer. Share one way you tried it, or pass.

A mirror number reads the same forwards and backwards, like 252 or 909.

(a) Write 5 mirror numbers of your own.

(b) Find ALL the mirror numbers from 200 to 299. How do you know you have found them all?

(c) Ali says every mirror number is odd. Is he right?

Easy first step: write 252, 909 and 131, then write three more mirror numbers.

Math Inquirers P2 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 77 to 80 (3 minutes, after talk time). Investigation debrief. The Investigation is printed on the last core page of the workbook; children may have started it any time after question 3 (it is open to everyone), or it is take-home. Say to the whole group: "Who would like to share one way they tried the Investigation? Passing is fine." Ask for volunteers only; never name a child; no public tallies, scores or rankings. Several answers are right: praise the method (a list in order, a table, a drawing), not the speed. If nobody has started it, show the first step on the slide and let pairs try for one minute, then set it as take-home. Model answer and what to look for: (a) Any number whose first and last digits match, such as 101, 454, 777. (b) 10 numbers: 202, 212, 222, 232, 242, 252, 262, 272, 282, 292. The first and last digits must both be 2, and the middle digit can be 0 to 9: 10 choices. A child who lists them by the middle digit in order has not missed any. (c) No: 202 is even. A mirror number is even when its first (and last) digit is even. Extension: there are 90 mirror numbers from 100 to 999 (9 choices for the end digits x 10 for the middle). Look for: the child sees that the end digits match, lists in order, and can say why the list is complete.
