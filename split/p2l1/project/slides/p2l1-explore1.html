10.5–19.5 MIN

Explore: what to do

EXPLORE

  1. Take the digit cards 3, 5 and 8.
  2. Make a 3-digit number using each card once. Write it down.
  3. Make as many different numbers as you can.
  4. Circle the biggest and the smallest. Have you got them all?

GO FURTHER (optional, any time you finish early): Add a fourth card, 2. Use any THREE of the cards 2, 3, 5 and 8. How many different 3-digit numbers can you make?

Math Inquirers P2 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 10.5 to 19.5. Pairs work with digit cards 3, 5 and 8 (online: paper cards on camera). Ask them to predict first: how many numbers? Most children guess 3 or 9. Let them record numbers in any order at first; the teaching part shows how to list them in order. Any child who would like more: add a 0 card and use 0, 3, 5. Why are there fewer numbers? Go further (a second what-if, so nobody waits) is the box at the foot of this slide; the question is also posted in the chat when the pairs go to their breakout rooms. It is for ANY child who finishes the explore task or wants more: say it to the whole group as a choice, never as a prize, and never name who tries it. Pairs stay together unless both children agree to split. Answer: 24: 4 choices of which card to leave out, and each set of 3 cards makes 6 numbers (4 x 6 = 24). Take it at the debrief only if a pair offers it.
