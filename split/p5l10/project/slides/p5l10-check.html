2–6.5 MIN

Sneak-peek puzzles

Guessing is welcome. Nobody is marked.

1. Ali, Mei and Ravi wear a red shirt, a blue shirt and a green shirt, one each. Ali is not wearing red or green. Mei is not wearing green. Who wears which colour? Move three cards or draw a small table.

Ali blue, Mei red, Ravi green

2. Ali spends half of his money on a book. Then he spends half of the money he has LEFT on lunch. He has $6 left. How much did he have at first? Draw a picture or work from the end.

$24

Show your paper ONCE, after both puzzles: only to your tutor, or all hold paper to the camera together.

Minutes 2 to 6.5. SNEAK-PEEK PUZZLES. Show puzzle 1 and give about a minute; click to show the short answer later. Then show puzzle 2 and give about a minute. Open with one line only: "Guessing is welcome. Nobody is marked." Puzzle 1 is an access puzzle: any child can try it with objects, drawing or counting, so everyone can start well. Puzzle 2 is a thinking puzzle. ONE hold-up for both puzzles: after puzzle 2, everyone shows their paper at the same moment (own paper shown only to you as you walk round; online, all hold paper to the camera together, or show it to you only, on camera). Then click through the two short answers and say each with one short reason. NO volunteer round here (volunteers come in the talk time). Never boards in front of the group, never read out answers with names, no tally, no ranking. Never record scores. Answers and what to listen for. Q1: Ali blue, Mei red, Ravi green. Ali can only wear blue. Mei is not green and blue is taken, so Mei wears red. Ravi has green.; Q2: $24. Work backwards: 6 × 2 = 12 before lunch, 12 × 2 = 24 before the book. A bar cut into 4 equal units, 1 unit = $6, also works. SNEAK-PEEK PUZZLES (about 4.5 minutes). Show the two puzzles one at a time. Say one line only: "Guessing is welcome. Nobody is marked." Puzzle 1 is one that any child can try with objects, drawing or counting, so everybody can start well. Puzzle 2 is a thinking puzzle. Answers are shown PRIVATELY, never on boards in front of the group. Face to face: children write on their own paper and show it only to you as you walk round. Online: everyone holds their paper up to the camera at the same moment, or shows it to you only, on camera. Do not read answers out with names, do not keep a tally, do not rank. Use ONE hold-up for both puzzles (after puzzle 2, everyone shows their paper at the same moment), then click through the answers and say each one with one short reason. There is NO volunteer round in the sneak-peek; volunteers come in the talk time. Never choose a child because you saw a wrong answer. Never record scores. Choosing a start: every child has the same neutral line on workbook page 1: "After question 2 you may choose: carry on, or try Think harder." (The number is the last question on page 1 of this lesson.) Invite the WHOLE group, never one child: "when you have finished Start here you can choose Think harder, Go further or the Investigation." Never single out a child by name and never announce who is on which page. Pairs are not split unless BOTH children agree. Any child may swap at any time.
