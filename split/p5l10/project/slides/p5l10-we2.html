Logic grid: ticks and crosses

Ravi, Siti and Ben play badminton, swimming and football (one each). Ravi does not swim. Siti plays neither football nor badminton. Ben does not play football.

Step 1. Cross out what the clues say no to. Siti has two crosses, so Siti swims: tick.

Step 2. A tick means crosses in the rest of its row and column: nobody else swims.

Step 3. Ben has two crosses, so Ben plays badminton. Ravi gets football.

Ravi football, Siti swimming, Ben badminton. Check all 3 clues ✓

Math Inquirers P5 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Worked example: a logic grid. Read the clues aloud and let children say where a cross goes before you click. Click 1 shows the crosses from the three clues and the first tick: Siti's row has two crosses, so only swimming is left. Click 2 (step 2): a tick means crosses in the rest of its column, so nobody else swims. Click 3 (step 3): Ben has crosses for swimming and football, so badminton; Ravi has the one left, football. Ask: "Could we have started with a different clue?" (Ben also gets a cross in football, but Siti is the one with two crosses at the start.) Finish with: check every clue against the answer. This is the method for worksheet Q2, which has four people.
