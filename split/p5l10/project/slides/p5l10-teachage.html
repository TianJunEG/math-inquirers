The gap never changes: 38 − 10 = 28. When Dad is 3 times as old, 28 is 2 times Sam’s age, so Sam is 14. That is 4 years later.

In 4 years: Dad 42, Sam 14, and 3 × 14 = 42 ✓

Math Inquirers P5 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Teach before worksheet page 2 (Q6). Two starts: a table (both ages go up by 1 each year, so the 3 × Sam column grows faster) and the age gap (the difference between two people's ages stays the same). With the gap: if Dad is 3 times as old as Sam, then Dad is Sam's age three times, so the gap of 28 is two Sam-ages: Sam is 14. 14 − 10 = 4 years. Ask: "Which start would be quicker for Dad 41 and Sam 3?" Common mistake: 38 ÷ 10 or adding years to only one person.
