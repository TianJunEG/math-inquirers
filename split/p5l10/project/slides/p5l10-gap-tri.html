Think harder: Triangles by size

Tool: List every way

The figure is a big triangle with a line from its top corner to each of 2 points on the base, and 1 line cutting across it, parallel to the base.

Count all the triangles of every size.

12 triangles.

Math Inquirers P5 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Optional, if you finish early (no clock time): show it while some pairs are working or have finished, or use it live as an extra warm-up or tutor-bank question; never at a fixed minute, and it never takes time from the plan. ADDED ITEM tri (a slide-only addition for the skill gap "Triangles by size"; it is not in the printed workbook). TOOL: List every way. SAY: Count by size. First count the smallest triangles, then the ones made of 2 pieces, and so on. Keep a list. ASK: Which lines do you need to make any triangle? What do all the triangles have in common? LISTEN FOR: Every triangle uses the top corner and two of the 4 lines from it (6 ways to choose), and then is cut off either by the base or by the line across (2 ways): 6 × 2 = 12. Accept a size-by-size list that totals 12, for example 6 triangles that stop at the line across and 6 that reach the base. Everyone writes the answer on their own paper, then say the answer together on three, then click the answer box. Nobody holds anything up, nobody is marked, and passing is always fine. ANSWER: 12 triangles. WHY: Choose 2 of the 4 lines through the top corner (6 pairs). Each pair makes one triangle cut off by the line across and one cut off by the base. 6 × 2 = 12. No triangle can use both the line across and the base, because they are parallel.
