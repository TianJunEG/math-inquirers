B1. A water tank is 1/3 full. After 20 litres are added it is 1/2 full. Then 1/4 of the water in the tank is let out. How many litres of water are in the tank now? What fraction of the tank is full?

B2. 25 coins of 50¢, 10¢ and 20¢ are worth $5.30 altogether. There are twice as many 10¢ coins as 50¢ coins. How many coins of each kind are there?

These are the printed Go further questions B1 to B3 (the same as the workbook). Answers: B1) 45 litres, which is 3/8 of the tank. The 20 litres are 1/2 − 1/3 = 1/6 of the tank, so the full tank holds 120 litres. Half full is 60 litres; letting out 1/4 of it (15 litres) leaves 45 litres, and 45 ÷ 120 = 3/8. B2) 3 fifty-cent coins, 6 ten-cent coins and 16 twenty-cent coins. Suppose all 25 coins were 20¢: that is $5.00. Every 50¢ coin comes with two 10¢ coins; this group of 3 coins is worth 70¢, and replacing three 20¢ coins (60¢) with it adds 10¢. The extra is 30¢, so there are 3 groups. Check: 150¢ + 60¢ + 320¢ = 530¢ and 3 + 6 + 16 = 25. B3) Siti, Mei, Ali, Ben. Mei is in chair 2 or 3. If Mei is in chair 3, the only chair not next to her is chair 1, but Ben is not on the left end, so that fails. So Mei is in chair 2, the only chair not next to her is chair 4, so Ben is in chair 4. Siti and Ali are in chairs 1 and 3 and Siti is on the left: Siti 1, Ali 3. Make up your own (printed under Go further): Make up a logic puzzle with three friends, three snacks and three clues. Swap and solve; does only one answer fit? Think for 30 seconds before anyone speaks. Open-ended: check that a partner can solve it and that exactly one answer fits.
