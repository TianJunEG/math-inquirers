Go further: Least possible, the fullest bag

Tool: Guess and check

Ravi puts 23 marbles into 4 bags. No two bags hold the same number of marbles, and no bag is empty.

What is the smallest number of marbles that the fullest bag can hold?

8 marbles.

Math Inquirers P5 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Optional, if you finish early (no clock time): show it while some pairs are working or have finished, or use it live as an extra warm-up or tutor-bank question; never at a fixed minute, and it never takes time from the plan. ADDED ITEM pile (a slide-only addition for the skill gap "Least possible, the fullest bag"; it is not in the printed workbook). TOOL: Guess and check. SAY: Test a target for the fullest bag. If it holds 7, what is the most the other 3 bags can hold? ASK: Why can the fullest bag not hold 7? Show a way that works for 8. LISTEN FOR: The test of the target: with 7 the biggest total is 7 + 6 + 5 + 4 = 22, which is less than 23. With 8, one way is 8 + 6 + 5 + 4 = 23. Listen for the argument that 7 is impossible, not only that 8 works. Everyone writes the answer on their own paper, then say the answer together on three, then click the answer box. Nobody holds anything up, nobody is marked, and passing is always fine. ANSWER: 8 marbles. WHY: If the fullest bag had 7 or fewer, the 4 different numbers would add to at most 7 + 6 + 5 + 4 = 22, which is less than 23. With 8 it works: 8 + 6 + 5 + 4 = 23. The answer is 8. SAME SKILL, NEW NUMBERS (tutor bank, say it aloud or write it on the board): Ravi puts 30 marbles into 5 different bags, all different and none empty. What is the smallest the fullest bag can hold? Answer: 8.
