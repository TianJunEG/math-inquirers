Same fence, different gardens

Ravi has 20 m of fence for a rectangular garden. Each side is a whole number of metres. Which gardens can he make?

Step 1. Half the fence goes on one length and one breadth: 20 ÷ 2 = 10 m. So length + breadth = 10.

Step 2. List in order: breadth 1, 2, 3 … Stop at 5 and 5. After that the gardens are the same ones turned round.

5 gardens. Same fence, different areas. Biggest: 5 m by 5 m = 25 m²

Math Inquirers Level 3 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Teach heuristic 1: make a list in order. Click 1: half the fence. Ask: "Why half?" (The fence goes length, breadth, length, breadth: one length and one breadth is half of it.) Clicks 2 to 6: build the table row by row; children call out each row. Click 7: the stop rule. Click 8: the answer. Think-aloud: "If I guess gardens at random I might miss one or write one twice. So I start with breadth 1 and go up by 1 each time. When breadth and length are both 5 I stop, because 6 and 4 is just 4 and 6 turned round." Point at the Area column: "Same fence, but the areas go 9, 16, 21, 24, 25. The closer to a square, the bigger the area." This is a parallel example: the worksheet uses 18 m.
