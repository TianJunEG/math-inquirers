1.5–5 MIN

Sneak-peek puzzles

Ideas we have not learnt yet. Have a go: guessing and “I do not know” are welcome. Nobody is marked.

Write both answers on your own paper. When your tutor says “say it”, say both answers together on three.

Puzzle 1. Draw a rectangle 3 cm long and 2 cm wide on squared paper (or use 6 tiles). What is its area? What is its perimeter?

Try it with objects, a drawing or counting.

Puzzle 2. Two different rectangles both have an area of 12 cm². Can they have different perimeters? Show two examples.

Think about it. Show how you thought.

Math Inquirers P3 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 1.5 to 5. Sneak-peek puzzles: two small puzzles on ideas we have not learnt yet. Give about 1 minute for each puzzle, then say "say it" ONCE for both: children say both answers together on three (about 30 seconds); nothing is held up to the camera. SAY: "These are ideas we have not learnt yet. Guessing is welcome and so is I do not know. Nobody is marked." Puzzle 1 is an ACCESS puzzle: any child can try it with objects, a drawing or counting, so everyone has a first win. Puzzle 2 is a thinking puzzle. Children write both answers on their own paper and say them together on three (once for both puzzles); nothing is held up to the camera. No private messages. Never answers read out, never "who got it?". Do not show the answers on the slide now. Puzzle 1 answer (tutor only): Area 6 cm², perimeter 10 cm. Listen for: 6 squares cover it. Walk round the edge: 3 + 2 + 3 + 2 = 10. Puzzle 2 answer (tutor only): Yes, e.g. 26 cm and 16 cm. Listen for: 1 by 12 has perimeter 26 cm; 2 by 6 has 16 cm; 3 by 4 has 14 cm. Come back to puzzle 2 at talk time: "Now we know the idea, how could it help?" (volunteers only). Invite the WHOLE group once, never one child; this is an invitation, not a ranking, and any child may choose Think harder or the Investigation after question 3 of page 1. Never announce who is on which page, and never split pairs unless both children agree.
