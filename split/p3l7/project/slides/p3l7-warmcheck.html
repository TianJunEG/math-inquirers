Warm-up check

Two questions. Answer on your own whiteboard. Hold it up on three. Be ready to say why.

1. A rectangle has a perimeter of 14 cm and one side is 3 cm long. What is its area?

12 cm²

2. Two different rectangles both have an area of 12 cm². Can they have different perimeters? Show two examples.

Yes, e.g. 26 cm and 16 cm

Math Inquirers P3 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 2 to 5. Warm-up check: two quick questions on today's key idea in a new setting. Whiteboards, on their own, boards up together, then click to show the answer and ask one child to say why. Never mark or label children in front of the group. Q1: 12 cm². Listen for: Two sides of 3 cm use 6 cm, so the other two sides share 8 cm: 4 cm each. Area 3 × 4 = 12 cm². Q2: Yes, e.g. 26 cm and 16 cm. Listen for: 1 by 12 has perimeter 26 cm; 2 by 6 has 16 cm; 3 by 4 has 14 cm. FAST TRACK: a child who gets both right AND can say why starts on the Think Harder pages and the Investigation (last workbook page of the lesson) at minute 40, while the group works the core pages. Tell them by name in the chat or a small breakout room. Different children can have different pages, nobody is labelled, and the check decides again next lesson.
