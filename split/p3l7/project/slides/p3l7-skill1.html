Think harder: the top view

1. Cubes are stacked on squares. Each number shows how many cubes are stacked on that square.

a) How many cubes are there in all?

b) Look from the front. In each column you see the tallest stack. How many squares make the front view?

Math Inquirers Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Optional slide for pairs who finish early or ask for more. Show it only on request and never announce who is working on it. Children write the answer on their own paper and say it together on three; nothing is held up to the camera. Answers and tutor lines are for you only. Item 1 (Spatial: reading a top view with heights; tool of the day idea: Act it out). SAY: "Build it with cubes, then look at it from the front, at table level." ASK: "Which cubes do you not see from the front? What do you look for in each column?" LISTEN FOR: "Only the tallest stack in each column matters from the front." ANSWER: a) 11 cubes. b) 7 squares (the tallest stacks in the columns are 2, 3 and 2).
