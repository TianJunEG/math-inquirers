Add one square: what happens to the perimeter?

Each square is 1 cm. The new square is yellow.

A new square brings 4 new edges. Every side it touches hides 2 edges: one of its own and one of the shape's.

Touch 1 side: + 2 cm. Touch 2 sides: no change. Touch 3 sides: − 2 cm.

Math Inquirers P3 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Teach before worksheet page 2 (Q6 adds one square to an L of 4 squares; this L has 3). Show panel 1 and count the edges together: 8. Click 1: the yellow square touches the L along 1 side. Count: 10. Click 2: the yellow square fills the gap and touches 2 sides: still 8, and now it is a 2 by 2 square. Click 3 and 4: the reason and the rule. Think-aloud: "The new square has 4 edges. Where it touches the shape, that edge is inside now, and so is the shape's edge it touches. So each touch hides 2 edges." Ask: "What would a square that touches 3 sides do?" (take away 2). "Where would it have to go?" (into a gap with walls on 3 sides).
