Explore: what to do

EXPLORE

  1. Take 12 squares.
  2. Make a rectangle using ALL 12 squares.
  3. Record its length, breadth and perimeter in a table.
  4. Make every different rectangle. Which has the longest perimeter?

Go further (your choice, any time): What if you have 16 squares? Make every rectangle. Which has the longest perimeter? The shortest? What is special about the shortest?

Math Inquirers P3 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 10 to 15. Pairs (or each child online) build rectangles with exactly 12 squares and fill in the table on a whiteboard or squared paper. Predict first: "Will every rectangle have the same perimeter?" Remind: a rectangle turned round is the same rectangle. Hints: "Put 2 squares in each row. How many rows?" "Run your finger round the edge and count every side you pass." Ready for more: "Can you make a 12-square rectangle with a perimeter of 20 cm?" (No.) Online: cut squares from squared paper, or just draw the rectangles on squared paper. GO FURTHER (on the slide from minute 10, so nobody waits): What if you have 16 squares? Make every rectangle. Which has the longest perimeter? The shortest? What is special about the shortest? Answer: 16 squares: 1 by 16 (perimeter 34 cm), 2 by 8 (20 cm), 4 by 4 (16 cm). Longest 1 by 16, shortest 4 by 4: the square, the most compact shape. Any pair may choose it as soon as their first task is done; do not say who is ready for it.
