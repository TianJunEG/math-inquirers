1. How many different rectangles did you make with 12 squares?

2. They all have the same area. Do they have the same perimeter?

3. Which has the longest perimeter? Why do you think so?

Math Inquirers P3 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 15 to 25. Let pairs keep working, then gather the group. Make a class table in order: 1 by 12, 2 by 6, 3 by 4. Answers: 1) 3 rectangles (12 by 1, 6 by 2, 4 by 3; turning one round does not make a new one). 2) No: 26 cm, 16 cm and 14 cm. Same area, different perimeters! 3) The long thin 12 by 1: when squares are in a long line, fewer of their sides touch, so more sides are on the outside. The fattest one (4 by 3, closest to a square) has the shortest perimeter. Ask: "How do you know you have them all?" (We tried 1 row, 2 rows, 3 rows; 4 rows is the 3 by 4 turned round; 5 rows does not work.) That is listing in order: today's first heuristic.
