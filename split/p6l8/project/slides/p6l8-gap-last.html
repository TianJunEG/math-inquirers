Think harder: Last digit of a power

Tool: Spot the pattern

(a) Find the last digit of 2⁴⁵ (45 twos multiplied together).

(b) Find the last digit of 13³⁰ (30 thirteens multiplied together).

(a) 2.

(b) 9.

Optional, if you finish early (no clock time): show it while some pairs are working or have finished, or use it live as an extra warm-up or tutor-bank question; never at a fixed minute, and it never takes time from the plan. ADDED ITEM last (a slide-only addition for the skill gap "Last digit of a power"; it is not in the printed workbook). TOOL: Spot the pattern. SAY: Only the last digit matters. Write the last digit of 2, 2 × 2, 2 × 2 × 2, ... and look for the repeat. Then use the clock arithmetic idea: what is 45 on a clock of the cycle length? ASK: How long is the cycle for 2? For 3 (the last digit of 13)? How does a remainder tell you where you land? LISTEN FOR: 2: 2, 4, 8, 6, cycle of 4. 45 = 11 × 4 + 1, so the 1st in the cycle: 2. 3: 3, 9, 7, 1, cycle of 4. 30 = 7 × 4 + 2, so 9. Listen for "remainder after dividing by the cycle length". Everyone writes the answer on their own paper, then say the answer together on three, then click the answer box. Nobody holds anything up, nobody is marked, and passing is always fine. ANSWER: (a) 2. (b) 9. WHY: Last digits of powers of 2 repeat 2, 4, 8, 6. 45 mod 4 = 1, so 2. Only the last digit of 13 matters, which is 3: 3, 9, 7, 1 repeat. 30 mod 4 = 2, so 9. SAME SKILL, NEW NUMBERS (tutor bank, say it aloud or write it on the board): Find the last digit of 7²⁰²⁶. Answer: 9 (the cycle is 7, 9, 3, 1 and 2 026 mod 4 = 2).
