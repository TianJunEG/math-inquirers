Clock arithmetic: wrap round

9 o’clock + 5 hours. Count on, then use mod.

Step 1. Count on 5 hours: 10, 11, 12, 1, 2. The hand wraps round past 12.

Step 2. With adding: 9 + 5 = 14. A clock has only 12 numbers, so take away 12: 14 − 12 = 2.

Step 3. In maths: 14 mod 12 = 2. “mod” means the remainder when you divide: 14 = 1 × 12 + 2.

On a clock with m numbers, n mod m is where you land after n steps. Landing on 0 is the same as landing on 12.

Math Inquirers P6 • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Teach. Use a real clock or the picture. Click 1: the hand sweeps from 9 to 2. Click 2: the addition and subtracting a full turn. Click 3: the word "mod". Think-aloud: "It is 9 o’clock. I count on 5 hours: 10, 11, 12, 1, 2. Adding gives 14, but a clock wraps round after 12, so 14 − 12 = 2. In maths we write 14 mod 12 = 2: the remainder when 14 is divided by 12." Say: mod is just the remainder: 14 ÷ 12 = 1 remainder 2. Note: on a real clock we write 12 where maths writes 0; both mean the top of the clock.
