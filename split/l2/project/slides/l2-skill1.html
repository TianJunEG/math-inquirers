Think harder: tens and ones

1. Ben packs 10 pencils in each box. He has 4 full boxes and 6 loose pencils.

a) Find the total number of pencils.

b) Siti has 52 pencils packed the same way. Fill in: ___ full boxes and ___ loose pencils.

2. Ali lays two digit cards side by side: 3 and 8. The two cards show a 2-digit number.

a) Write every number the cards can show.

b) Circle the greater number.

c) Which number is closer to 50?

Optional slide for pairs who finish early or ask for more. Show it only on request and never announce who is working on it. Children write the answer on their own paper and say it together on three; nothing is held up to the camera. Answers and tutor lines are for you only. Item 1 (Place value: tens and ones; tool of the day idea: Act it out). SAY: "Use bundles of 10 sticks and single sticks, or draw a stick for a ten and a dot for a one." ASK: "How many tens in 46? How many ones? Where do you see the 4 and the 6 in the number?" LISTEN FOR: "4 tens and 6 ones is 46", "the 5 in 52 means 5 boxes of ten". A child who says 52 is "5 and 2" should count the sticks. ANSWER: a) 46 pencils (4 tens and 6 ones). b) 5 full boxes and 2 loose pencils. Item 2 (Place value: digit cards; tool of the day idea: List every way). SAY: "Put the cards on the table and swap them. Write each number you make." ASK: "How many different numbers? How do you know you have found them all? Which digit is the tens digit in 38?" LISTEN FOR: "Swap the cards and I get a new number." The tens card matters most when comparing. ANSWER: a) 38 and 83. b) 83 is greater (8 tens is more than 3 tens). c) 38 (it is 12 away from 50; 83 is 33 away).
