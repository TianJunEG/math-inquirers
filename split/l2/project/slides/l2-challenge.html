3. Change the magic triangle: use the numbers 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 and 7 instead of 1 to 6. Can you make every side add to the same total? Try it. Which totals can you make?

For any pair that chooses Go further. Use it as a pair choice, or with volunteers in talk time (never call a child by name). Ask for the reason every time. Q3 is open: there is no single right answer, so ask children to check each other's work. Answers: 1) Circle 5, square 2 (2 × 5 + 2 = 12 and 5 + 2 × 2 = 9). One way to see it: the two totals differ by 3, and the only change is one circle swapped for one square, so a circle is 3 more than a square. Then 3 squares and 6 more make 12, so each square is 2. 2) Ben 7, Mei 4. (20 − 9 = 11 are shared; take away Ben's extra 3: 8, share 4 and 4; Ben has 4 + 3 = 7.) 3) Open. Yes, it still works. Totals 12, 13, 14 and 15 are possible. Example for 12: corners 2, 3, 4 with 7 between 2 and 3, 5 between 3 and 4 and 6 between 4 and 2.
