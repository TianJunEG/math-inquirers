Explore: what to do

EXPLORE

  1. Take 6 paper squares.
  2. Join them edge to edge (whole sides touching). Tape them.
  3. Try to fold your shape into a cube: no gaps, no overlaps.
  4. Draw every shape that works. Did any fail? Why?

Go further (any time): What if the longest row has only 3 squares? Can the shape still fold into a cube? Find one.

Math Inquirers P4 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 8 to 13. Pairs join 6 paper squares and try to fold them into a cube (online: fold on camera, or draw the shape on squared paper and fold in their heads). Ask them to predict first: "Will every shape work?" Let them find out. Go further (on the slide), for any child who wants it: find a shape that does NOT fold into a cube, and say why it fails. GO FURTHER (on the slide from minute 8, so nobody waits): What if the longest row has only 3 squares? Can the shape still fold into a cube? Find one. Answer: Yes. 4 of the 11 cube nets have a longest row of 3 squares. One example: two rows of 3 squares, where the second row starts under the last square of the first row. (One net, the staircase, has no row longer than 2.)
