Mei says: "Every shape made of 6 squares folds into a cube." Ben says: "If a shape has 4 squares in a row, it always folds into a cube." Who is right?

Sentence starters:

“I noticed that …” “I tested it by …” “I know because …”

Math Inquirers P4 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 80 to 85. Pairs talk for 2 minutes, then share. Answer: neither is right. A 2 by 3 rectangle has 6 squares and does not fold (it has a 2 by 2 block), so Mei is wrong. A row of 4 with one square above each end does not fold either, because both extra squares cover the back, so Ben is wrong. One counter-example is enough to show a rule is not always true. Second question if time: "How can you tell two faces are opposite without folding?" (They are the ends of a line of 3 squares.) Praise the reasons, not only the answers.
