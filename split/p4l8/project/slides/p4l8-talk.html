Minutes 80 to 85. Pair talk is 1 minute (no breakout rooms: children tell the grown-up beside them, or write one sentence), then share. Answer: neither is right. A 2 by 3 rectangle has 6 squares and does not fold (it has a 2 by 2 block), so Mei is wrong. A row of 4 with one square above each end does not fold either, because both extra squares cover the back, so Ben is wrong. One counter-example is enough to show a rule is not always true. Not asked in class (moved to the Weekly Dose of Thinking Math): "How can you tell two faces are opposite without folding?" (They are the ends of a line of 3 squares.) Praise the reasons, not only the answers. TALK TIME RULES: ask for volunteers first; a child may always pass; never call on a child by name to answer; no public tallies, scores or rankings; praise the reason, not the child. If a question uses a mistaken idea (a character who gets it wrong), present it as the character's idea and ask "What would you say to them?" so no child owns the mistake. TOOL MINUTE: Talk time is one minute longer than before (the common look-back is shorter). Spend it on the question on the slide: where did you use today's tool, and what did it help you see? Listen for the tool named, not for the answer; a child may pass.
