Write your answer on your own paper. When your tutor says, hold it to the camera. Only your tutor looks.

1

Try it

Write a big capital letter H. Draw every fold line where the two halves would match exactly. How many fold lines can you find?

2 fold lines: up-down and across.

2

Think

Could a straight row of 6 squares fold into a cube? Cut 6 paper squares and try, if you like. Explain.

No: the 5th and 6th squares land on top of the 1st and 2nd.

Minutes 1.5 to 6.5 (5 minutes). SNEAK-PEEK PUZZLES. Say: "These are ideas we have not learnt yet. Guessing is welcome, drawing is welcome and so is saying I do not know. Nobody is marked, and nobody's answer is shown to the group." Puzzle 1 is a TRY IT puzzle: any child can start it with objects, a drawing or counting, so everyone has a first win. Puzzle 2 needs a little thinking. Give about 60 seconds for puzzle 1 and about 90 seconds for puzzle 2. Children show their answers PRIVATELY: written on their own paper and held to the camera at the same moment for everyone (or shown only to you on camera). Make ONE hold-up moment for both papers, after puzzle 2. Never use private chat or DMs. Never ask for boards up in front of the group, never read out whose answer is whose, and never say who got it right. Then click to show each answer and talk it through for everyone ("some of you tried ..."), naming no child. Nobody is called on by name, and passing is always fine. ANSWERS: 1) 2 fold lines: one up-down line through the middle and one across the middle. (Plain block H.) 2) No. The first 4 squares wrap round as walls. The 5th lands on top of the 1st and the 6th on top of the 2nd, so nothing makes a lid or a base. A GOOD REASON: A net needs one base, one lid and four walls with nothing overlapping. A line of symmetry is a fold where both halves match exactly. Use what you see only to plan your help. It does not decide who is on which page and it is not recorded as a mark. ON PAGE 1 (printed): "After question 3 you may choose: carry on, or try Think harder." Make the invitation to the WHOLE group ("when you have finished Start here you can choose Think harder, Go further or the Investigation"), so nobody is singled out. Never message a child privately, never name a child, and never announce who is on which page. Everyone stays in the same room and every child turns to the page they choose. If a pair would be split, ask both children first; never leave a child alone on page 1 (join them to another pair or to yourself).
