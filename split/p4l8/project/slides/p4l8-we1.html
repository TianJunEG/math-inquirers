A shape that does not fold

A row of 4 with a square above each end. Fold it in your head.

Step 1. Base: the second square. The squares next to it become the left and right walls.

Step 2. The square on the end of the row is the lid. The square above the left wall swings to the BACK.

Step 3. The square above the lid also swings to the BACK. Two squares in the same place, and nothing at the FRONT.

Not a net: two squares overlap and the front is missing.

Test every case: if any face is covered twice, it fails.

Math Inquirers P4 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Click 1: base and the two walls beside it. Click 2: "The end square is the lid. The square above the left wall: when that wall stands up, the square swings round to the back." Click 3: "Now the square above the end square. The lid has folded over the top, so this square swings to the back too." Ask: "How many squares are at the back? How many at the front?" (2 and 0.) Think-aloud: "A net needs one of each face. Here the back is covered twice and the front is empty. It fails." Say: Having 4 squares in a row does NOT guarantee a net: where the other two squares go matters. (They must be on different sides, or both on the inside of the row.)
