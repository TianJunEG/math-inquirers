1. How many different shapes that fold into a cube did your group find?

2. What made a shape fail?

3. Do all the shapes that work have 4 squares in a row?

Math Inquirers P4 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 15 to 25. Gather the shapes on the board; ask "Is this one the same as that one, just turned or flipped?" Answers: 1) There are 11 different nets of a cube altogether (turned or flipped copies count as the same); most groups find 4 to 7. 2) Two squares landed on the same face: for example a 2 by 2 block (4 squares meeting at a corner, but only 3 faces meet at a cube corner), 5 squares in a row, or two squares closing the same end. 3) No: some nets have a row of 3 and no row of 4 (the staircase and the 3-3 net). Do NOT give the method yet if nobody finds it; the teaching part comes next.
