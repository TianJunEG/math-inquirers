Story break: Emmy Noether

Germany (born in Erlangen) • 1882-1935

Emmy Noether was born in Erlangen, Germany, in 1882. When she wanted to study mathematics at university, women were only allowed to sit in on classes if the teacher gave permission. She kept going, and in 1907 she earned her doctorate. For years she worked without pay, and for a time her lectures in Göttingen had to be advertised under another professor's name. Still she carried on. In 1918 she proved an idea now called Noether's theorem. It says that when something in nature has a kind of symmetry, a matching quantity stays the same. Symmetry is not only pretty. It tells us deep things.

How many lines of symmetry does a square have? How many does a rectangle that is not a square have?

TRY IT ANSWER (tutor only, do not show): A square has 4 lines of symmetry (2 through the middles of opposite sides and 2 along the diagonals). A rectangle that is not a square has 2 (through the middles of opposite sides). Everything in the story is true as far as historians know. Confidence: high.
