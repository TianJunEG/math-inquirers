Fold it in your head: base, walls, lid

Is this a net of a cube? Pick a base, then fold the other squares round it.

Step 1. Choose a base: a square with lots of neighbours. Colour it.

Step 2. The squares touching it fold UP to be walls: left, right and back.

Step 3. This square hangs from the right wall. When the wall stands up, it swings round to the FRONT.

Step 4. The last square is joined to the right wall too: it folds over the top. It is the lid.

1 base + 4 walls + 1 lid, no overlaps: it IS a net.

Math Inquirers P4 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Use 6 paper squares, tape and a real cube if possible. Ask first: "Do you think this folds into a cube?" Take guesses. Click 1: the base (yellow). Click 2: fold the left, right and back squares up with your finger: they stand like walls. Click 3: "This square hangs from the RIGHT wall, so when the wall stands up it swings round to the front." Click 4: the lid. Think-aloud: "A cube has 6 faces: one base, one lid and four walls. I check I have all four walls: left, right, back, front, and nothing is in the same place twice." Then fold the paper model to prove it.
