10.5–20 MIN

Explore: what to do

EXPLORE

  1. Take any scrap of paper, even a torn one.
  2. Fold it once. Then fold the fold exactly onto itself.
  3. The folded corner is your right-angle checker. KEEP it for the worksheet.
  4. Hunt: find 3 right angles, 1 smaller angle and 1 bigger angle.

Go further, any time: Fold your corner in half again. Smaller or bigger than a right angle? Find two.

Math Inquirers P3 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 10.5 to 20. Predict first: "If I fold a torn, wiggly piece of paper twice, will the corner be a right angle?" Then everyone folds a checker (the tutor shows a model on the slide): folding the checker is the Explore task, and children keep it for Q1 of the worksheet, so Q1 only has to sort. Fold twice and hunt (about 6 minutes of pair work): children hunt round the room (or at home) and record on a whiteboard: 3 right angles, 1 smaller, 1 bigger. Hints: "Put the corner of the checker into the angle. Do both arms line up?" "If one arm is hidden under the checker, the angle is smaller." Ready for more: find an angle that is exactly 2 right angles (a straight line). GO FURTHER (on the slide from the start of explore, so nobody waits): Fold your corner in half again. Smaller or bigger than a right angle? Find two. Answer: Smaller than a right angle: it is half of a right angle, so two of them make one right angle. It appears at the corner of a set square, or when a square is cut along its diagonal. Any pair may choose it as soon as their first task is done; do not say who is ready for it. Online: pairs are in pre-assigned breakout rooms (set up before the lesson, auto-move in and auto-close with a timer, about 45 seconds per move). Visit each breakout room at least once while the pairs work (about 1 minute per room).
