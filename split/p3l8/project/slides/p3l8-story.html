Story break: Ibn al-Haytham

Born probably in Basra (now Iraq); worked mostly in Cairo, Egypt • about 965 to about 1040

Ibn al-Haytham was born about 1,000 years ago, probably in Basra, in what is now Iraq. He spent much of his working life in Cairo, in Egypt. He wrote the Book of Optics, in seven volumes, about light and sight. He argued that we see when light from an object enters our eyes, and he tested his ideas with experiments. He used geometry to work out how light bounces off mirrors. A story says he pretended to be mad to stay safe from a powerful ruler. Historians are not sure that is true, so it is a legend.

TRY IT (OPTIONAL)

A ray of light hits a flat mirror and makes an angle of 30 degrees with the mirror. The perpendicular to the mirror makes 90 degrees with it. What angle does the ray make with the perpendicular? The reflected ray leaves at the same angle on the other side of the perpendicular. How big is the angle between the two rays?

Who: A scholar who studied light and mirrors by experiment and geometry. Link to today: Today you look for angles and perpendicular lines, and light bouncing off a mirror makes equal angles on either side of the perpendicular. ANSWER to the try-it (tutor only, do not show): 90 - 30 = 60 degrees with the perpendicular. The reflected ray also makes 60 degrees, so the angle between the two rays is 60 + 60 = 120 degrees. TUTOR NOTE (accuracy): The house-arrest story is a legend (MacTutor says it 'appears' true; Wikipedia calls it a legend). Birth place and death place are uncertain, and sources give 1039 or 1040. The equal-angles rule is the modern law of reflection and is not claimed as his own words.
