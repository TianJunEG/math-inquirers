Investigation: Staircase corners: find the pattern

A 1-step staircase is 1 square. A 2-step staircase has 3 squares (a column of 2 beside a column of 1). A 3-step staircase has 6 squares.

1. Count the corners of each staircase where the inside angle is a right angle. Then count the corners where the inside angle is BIGGER than a right angle. Draw the 4-step staircase and fill in the table.

2. What pattern do you see? Use it to fill in the 10-step row without drawing.

3. Is the number of corners always an even number? Why do you think so?

Easy first step: draw the 1-step and 2-step staircases. Count the corners of the 2-step staircase. A table for your answers is on the Investigation page of the workbook.

DEBRIEF in talk time, minutes 72 to 75: ask for volunteers to share one thing they noticed (a pass is fine). Then show the model answer and ask "What stayed the same? What changed? Why?" Model answer and what to look for: Steps 1 to 4: right angles 4, 5, 6, 7; bigger than a right angle 0, 1, 2, 3; all corners 4, 6, 8, 10. So right-angle corners = steps + 3, bigger corners = steps − 1, all corners = 2 × steps + 2. 10 steps: 13, 9 and 22 (checked by computer by building each staircase). The total is always even because each new step adds 2 corners (one right angle and one dent). Look for: careful organised counting, a table, a stated rule, and a prediction that can be checked. Any pair can draw the 10-step staircase to test the prediction.
