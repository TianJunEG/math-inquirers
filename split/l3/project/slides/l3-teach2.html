A key of 2: count in 2s

Key: ● = 2 children

Our class lunch vote

Step 1. Read the key first: each ● stands for 2 children.

Step 2. Noodles: count in 2s. 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12. 12 children chose noodles.

Step 3. How many more noodles than rice? 4 more pictures: 2, 4, 6, 8. 8 more children.

Why a key of 2? When many children vote, the rows stay short. But we must count in 2s.

Math Inquirers Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Teach. Ask first: "How many children chose noodles?" Many children count the dots and say 6. Point to the key: "Each dot is 2 children." Click to step 2, then click to put the counting numbers on the dots and count in 2s together. Step 3: rice has 2 dots, so noodles has 4 extra dots; click to show them and count them in 2s: 8. Check: noodles 12 children, rice 4 children, 12 − 4 = 8. Think-aloud: "I always read the key first. One dot is 2 children, so I count in 2s, not 1s." Pizza: 2, 4, 6, 8. Total: 12 dots, count in 2s to 24.
