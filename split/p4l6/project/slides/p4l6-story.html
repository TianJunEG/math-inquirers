Story break: Eratosthenes

Cyrene (now Libya) and Alexandria, Egypt; ancient Greek world • about 276 BC - about 194 BC

Eratosthenes was a Greek scholar in the great library of Alexandria, Egypt, over 2,000 years ago. At noon on one day of the year, the Sun shone straight down a well in the town of Syene, so nothing had a shadow. On that day in Alexandria, an upright stick did make a shadow. He worked out that its angle was about 7.2 degrees, which is one fiftieth of a whole turn. So the two towns were one fiftieth of the way round the Earth. He multiplied the distance between them by 50. Historians are not sure how long his unit of distance was, so we cannot say how close he got.

TRY IT (OPTIONAL)

A whole turn is 360 degrees. If a shadow made an angle of 10 degrees, how many angles that size fit all the way round?

Link to today: Today you measure angles and turns, and Eratosthenes used the angle of a shadow to measure the whole Earth. TRY IT ANSWER (tutor only, do not show): 360 divided by 10 = 36, so 36 angles. (Eratosthenes had about 7.2 degrees, and 360 divided by 7.2 = 50.) Everything in the story is true as far as historians know. Confidence: high. Dates for people from long ago are estimates.
