Is turning 270° clockwise the same as turning 90° anticlockwise? How do you know?

Sentence starters:

“I noticed that …” “I tested it by …” “I know because …”

Pass is always fine. Volunteers share first. We do not keep scores.

Math Inquirers P4 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Tool today: Draw it. Did it help?
Minutes 80 to 84.5. Pair talk is 1 minute (no breakout rooms: children tell the grown-up beside them, or write one sentence), then test it standing up: everyone faces North and does both turns. Answer: yes, you end up facing the same way (both from N end at W), because 270° + 90° = 360°, a full turn. The turns are different sizes, but the direction you end up facing is the same. Not asked in class (moved to the Weekly Dose of Thinking Math): "Ben says the angle at 3 o’clock is smaller than at 9 o’clock because 3 is smaller than 9. Who agrees?" (both are 90°: 3 spaces the shorter way). Praise the reasons, not only the answers. TALK TIME RULES: ask for volunteers first; a child may always pass; never call on a child by name to answer; no public tallies, scores or rankings; praise the reason, not the child. If a question uses a mistaken idea (a character who gets it wrong), present it as the character's idea and ask "What would you say to them?" so no child owns the mistake. TOOL MINUTE: Talk time is one minute longer than before (the common look-back is shorter). Spend it on the question on the slide: where did you use today's tool, and what did it help you see? Listen for the tool named, not for the answer; a child may pass.
