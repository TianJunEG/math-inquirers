1–4 MIN

Sneak-peek puzzles

Ideas we have not learnt yet. Guesses and "I do not know" are welcome. Nobody is marked.

Write your answer on your own paper. When your tutor says, hold it to the camera. Only your tutor looks.

1

Try it

Stand up and face the screen. Turn a quarter turn to your right. Then turn another quarter turn the same way. How many quarter turns did you make? Which way are you facing now, compared with the start?

2 quarter turns: you face the opposite way.

2

Think

A square corner is cut into two angles. One angle is 35°. What is the other angle? How do you know?

55° (90° − 35°).

Math Inquirers Level 4 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 1 to 4 (3 minutes). SNEAK-PEEK PUZZLES. Say: "These are ideas we have not learnt yet. Guessing is welcome, drawing is welcome and so is saying I do not know. Nobody is marked, and nobody's answer is shown to the group." Puzzle 1 is a TRY IT puzzle: any child can start it with objects, a drawing or counting, so everyone has a first win. Puzzle 2 needs a little thinking. About 90 seconds each. Children show their answers PRIVATELY: written on their own paper and held to the camera at the same moment for everyone (or shown only to you on camera). Keep every part of this in the shared session. Never ask for boards up in front of the group, never read out whose answer is whose, and never say who got it right. Then click to show each answer and talk it through for everyone ("some of you tried ..."), naming no child. Ask for volunteers first if you want a reason said aloud; nobody is called on by name, and passing is always fine. ANSWERS: 1) 2 quarter turns, which is a half turn. You are facing the opposite way (your back is to the screen). 2) 55°. A square corner is 90°, and 90° − 35° = 55°. A GOOD REASON: A square corner is 90° and a half turn is 180°. Angles side by side add up to the whole turn. Use what you see only to plan your help. It does not decide who is on which page and it is not recorded as a mark. After question 2 on page 1 every child may choose: carry on, or try the Think harder page. Make the invitation to the WHOLE group ("when you have finished Start here you can choose Think harder, Go further or the Investigation"), so nobody is singled out. Never name a child, and never announce who is on which page. Everyone stays in the same room and every child turns to the page they choose. If a pair would be split, ask both children first; never leave a child alone on page 1 (join them to another pair or to yourself).
