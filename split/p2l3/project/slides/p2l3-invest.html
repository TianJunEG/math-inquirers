Investigation: Where tables meet

A puzzle with more than one good answer. Share one way you tried it, or pass.

(a) List the numbers up to 50 that are in BOTH the 2 times table and the 5 times table. What do you notice about them?

(b) Now find the numbers up to 50 that are in both the 3 and 5 tables.

(c) Is every number in both the 2 and 5 tables also in the 10 table? Explain.

Easy first step: write the 5 times table up to 50, then circle the even numbers.

Math Inquirers Level 2 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 77 to 80 (3 minutes, after talk time). Investigation debrief. The Investigation is printed on the last core page of the workbook; children may have started it any time after question 3 (it is open to everyone), or it is take-home. Say to the whole group: "Who would like to share one way they tried the Investigation? Passing is fine." Ask for volunteers only; never name a child; no public tallies, scores or rankings. Several answers are right: praise the method (a list in order, a table, a drawing), not the speed. If nobody has started it, show the first step on the slide and let pairs try for one minute, then set it as take-home. Model answer and what to look for: (a) 10, 20, 30, 40, 50: they jump by 10 and are the 10 times table. (b) 3 and 5: 15, 30, 45 (jump by 15). (Extension: 4 and 5 give 20, 40, jump by 20.) (c) Yes to both, brute-forced up to 100: a number in the 10 table is 10 x something = 2 x (5 x something) = 5 x (2 x something). Child-level reason: a number in the 2 table is even and a number in the 5 table ends in 0 or 5, so a number in both ends in 0, which is the 10 table; and every number in the 10 table ends in 0, so it is even and in the 5 table. Look for: organised lists (writing both tables out and circling), the noticing that the jumps are 10, 15, 20, and a test of “always”.
