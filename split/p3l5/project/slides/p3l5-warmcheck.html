Sneak-peek puzzles

Ideas we have not learnt yet. Have a go: guessing and “I do not know” are welcome. Nobody is marked.

Show your answer privately: hold your paper up to your tutor, or send a private message.

Puzzle 1. Mei puts 13 sweets in bags of 4. Use counters or draw it. How many full bags can she make? How many sweets are left over?

Try it with objects, a drawing or counting.

Puzzle 2. Ali says, "6 sweets are left over when I put the sweets in bags of 6." Draw 6 sweets in bags of 6. What is wrong?

Think about it. Show how you thought.

Math Inquirers P3 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 2 to 5. Sneak-peek puzzles: two small puzzles on ideas we have not learnt yet. SAY: "These are ideas we have not learnt yet. Guessing is welcome and so is I do not know. Nobody is marked." Puzzle 1 is an ACCESS puzzle: any child can try it with objects, a drawing or counting, so everyone has a first win. Puzzle 2 is a thinking puzzle. Children show their answers PRIVATELY: on their own paper held up to the tutor, or by private chat message. Never boards held up together, never answers read out, never "who got it?". Do not show the answers on the slide now. Puzzle 1 answer (tutor only): 3 full bags, 1 left over. Listen for: 3 bags of 4 use 12 sweets. 1 sweet is left. Puzzle 2 answer (tutor only): 6 sweets fill a bag, so 0 are left. Listen for: A leftover must be fewer than 6, otherwise another bag of 6 can be filled. Come back to puzzle 2 at talk time: "Now we know the idea, how could it help?" (volunteers only). The tutor may use what they see to decide whom to invite quietly, by PRIVATE chat message, to try Think harder; this is an invitation, not a ranking, and any child may choose Think harder or the Investigation after question 3 of page 1. Never announce who is on which page, and never split pairs unless both children agree.
