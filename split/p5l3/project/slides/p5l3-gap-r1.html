Preview: What is a ratio?

Tool: Draw it

PREVIEW: a Primary 6 topic, beyond Level 5. A taste, not a test.

Ali mixes paint. For every 2 tins of blue he uses 3 tins of white. We write blue : white = 2 : 3 and say "2 to 3".

The bar model shows 2 units of blue and 3 units of white. Every unit is the same size.

(a) Ali uses 4 tins of blue. How many tins of white does he use?

(b) Ali mixes 20 tins altogether in the ratio 2 : 3. How many tins of each colour?

(a) 6 tins of white, because 4 : 6 is the same ratio as 2 : 3.

(b) 5 units altogether. One unit is 20 ÷ 5 = 4 tins. Blue 8 tins, white 12 tins.

ADDED ITEM r1 (a slide-only addition for the skill gap "What is a ratio?"; it is not in the printed workbook). TOOL: Draw it. PREVIEW LABEL: say "This is a first taste of a topic from a later level. We are not testing it; we are noticing how it works." SAY: A ratio compares two amounts with "for every". 2 : 3 means 2 blue for every 3 white. Draw the bars first, and call each box a unit. ASK: If Ali doubles the mix, what happens to both numbers? How many units are there in all in (b), and how big is one unit? LISTEN FOR: 2 : 3 = 4 : 6 (multiply both numbers by the same number). For (b): 2 + 3 = 5 units, 20 ÷ 5 = 4 per unit, 2 × 4 = 8 and 3 × 4 = 12. Listen for "units" and "the same size". Do not teach more than this on the first meeting. Everyone writes the answer on their own paper, then say the answer together on three, then click the answer box. Nobody holds anything up, nobody is marked, and passing is always fine. ANSWER: (a) 6 tins of white, because 4 : 6 is the same ratio as 2 : 3. (b) 5 units altogether. One unit is 20 ÷ 5 = 4 tins. Blue 8 tins, white 12 tins. WHY: Worked example for the tutor. Step 1: read the ratio as "for every": 2 blue for every 3 white. Step 2: draw 2 boxes and 3 boxes, equal size. Step 3: total units = 2 + 3 = 5. Step 4: one unit = 20 ÷ 5 = 4. Step 5: blue = 2 × 4 = 8, white = 3 × 4 = 12. Step 6: check 8 + 12 = 20 and 8 : 12 = 2 : 3.
