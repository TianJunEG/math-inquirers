Explore: what to do

EXPLORE

  1. Ali takes 18 counters. Mei takes 6 counters.
  2. Ali gives Mei 1 counter at a time. After each move write Ali, Mei, the total and the difference in a table.
  3. Start again from 18 and 6. This time give BOTH children 2 more counters each time. Fill in a new table.
  4. What never changes in each table? When does Mei have 1/2 as many counters as Ali?

Minutes 13 to 26.5 (the whole explore; this slide 13 to 22.5). Pairs use 24 counters or paper squares (online: paper squares on the desk, camera up as usual; hold the table up to the camera). Predict first: when Ali gives counters away, does the total change? Does the difference change? Let pairs record in two tables: Ali, Mei, total, difference. Second table: start again at 18 and 6 and give BOTH children 2 more each time. Anyone who wants more (open to every pair): find when Mei has 1/3 as many as Ali (right at the start: 18 and 6), and 3/5 as many (after Ali gives 3: 15 and 9). PROTECTED THINK TIME: before the pairs touch anything, say "Think for 30 seconds before anyone speaks" and show the clock; then pairs talk and start. PAIR WORK IS CAPPED AT 6 MINUTES: say "Pencils down" at minute 22.5 and bring the group back (the minutes before that include the 30 seconds of thinking, finding materials and, in lessons that use them, the move to and from breakout rooms). GO FURTHER (say it as soon as a pair has finished the task): any pair that finishes early may try the what-if question on the next slide. It is open to every pair, not a reward. Answer: No for 25: equal shares would be 12 and a half each, so an odd total can never be shared equally. With 24 (18 and 6) it works: 12 each after Ali gives 6. The total never changes when they only swap counters.
