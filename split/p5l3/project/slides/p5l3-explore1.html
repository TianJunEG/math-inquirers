EXPLORE

  1. Ali takes 18 counters. Mei takes 6 counters.
  2. Ali gives Mei 1 counter at a time. After each move write Ali, Mei, the total and the difference in a table.
  3. Start again from 18 and 6. This time give BOTH children 2 more counters each time. Fill in a new table.
  4. What never changes in each table? When does Mei have 1/2 as many counters as Ali?

Math Inquirers Level 5 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 9 to 14. Pairs use 24 counters or paper squares (online: paper squares on the desk, camera up as usual; hold the table up to the camera). Predict first: when Ali gives counters away, does the total change? Does the difference change? Let pairs record in two tables: Ali, Mei, total, difference. Second table: start again at 18 and 6 and give BOTH children 2 more each time. Anyone who wants more (open to every pair): find when Mei has 1/3 as many as Ali (right at the start: 18 and 6), and 3/5 as many (after Ali gives 3: 15 and 9). GO FURTHER (say it at minute 9): any pair that finishes step 4 early may try the what-if question on the next slide. It is open to every pair, not a reward. Answer: No for 25: equal shares would be 12 and a half each, so an odd total can never be shared equally. With 24 (18 and 6) it works: 12 each after Ali gives 6. The total never changes when they only swap counters.
