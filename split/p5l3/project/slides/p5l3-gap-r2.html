ADDED ITEM r2 (a slide-only addition for the skill gap "Equal ratios and simplest form"; it is not in the printed workbook). TOOL: Make a table. PREVIEW LABEL: say "This is a first taste of a topic from a later level. We are not testing it; we are noticing how it works." SAY: Make a table. Juice in the top row, water in the bottom row. Divide both numbers by 3 to get the smallest whole numbers, then multiply up. ASK: What do you do to both numbers to keep the ratio the same? How do you know 2 : 3 is simplest and cannot go smaller? LISTEN FOR: The same number on both sides (multiply or divide). Simplest form means no whole number other than 1 divides both. 10 cups of juice is 5 groups of 2, so 5 × 3 = 15. Everyone writes the answer on their own paper, then say the answer together on three, then click the answer box. Nobody holds anything up, nobody is marked, and passing is always fine. ANSWER: (a) 6 : 9. (b) 2 : 3 (divide both numbers by 3). (c) 15 cups of water. (d) 18. WHY: 6 : 9 divided by 3 gives 2 : 3. For 10 cups of juice: 10 = 5 × 2, so water is 5 × 3 = 15. For (d): 12 = 6 × 2, so the box is 6 × 3 = 18. SAME SKILL, NEW NUMBERS (tutor bank, say it aloud or write it on the board): A paint mix is 8 tins of red for every 12 tins of yellow. Write the ratio in simplest form. How many tins of yellow go with 14 tins of red? Answer: 2 : 3; 21 tins.
