Investigation: Weights puzzle

A puzzle with more than one good answer. Share one way you tried it, or pass.

You have three different weights. Each weight is 1 g, 2 g, 3 g, 4 g or 5 g. You put some of them on one pan to make a mass.

(a) With weights of 1 g, 2 g and 4 g, which masses from 1 g to 7 g can you make?

(b) Find ALL the sets of three different weights from 1 g to 5 g that can make every mass from 1 g to 6 g.

(c) What do all the good sets have in common? Needs: paper and a pencil (cubes or coins can stand for the weights).

Easy first step: with weights of 1 g, 2 g and 4 g, can you make 3 g? Can you make 5 g?

Math Inquirers Level 2 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 77 to 80 (3 minutes, after talk time). Investigation debrief. The Investigation is printed on the last core page of the workbook; children may have started it any time after question 3 (it is open to everyone), or it is take-home. Say to the whole group: "Who would like to share one way they tried the Investigation? Passing is fine." Ask for volunteers only; never name a child; no public tallies, scores or rankings. Several answers are right: praise the method (a list in order, a table, a drawing), not the speed. If nobody has started it, show the first step on the slide and let pairs try for one minute, then set it as take-home. Model answer and what to look for: (a) All of them: 1, 2, 1+2 = 3, 4, 1+4 = 5, 2+4 = 6, 1+2+4 = 7. (b) Exactly 2 sets work: 1, 2, 3 (makes 1 to 6) and 1, 2, 4 (makes 1 to 7); brute-forced over all 10 sets. (c) Both contain 1 g and 2 g: only a 1 g weight can make 1 g, and with different weights only a 2 g weight can make 2 g. Look for: a systematic list of the possible sets, checking each mass in order, and the noticing that 1 g is needed.
