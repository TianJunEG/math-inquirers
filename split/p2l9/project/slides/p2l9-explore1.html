10.5–19.5 MIN

Explore: balance lab

EXPLORE

  1. Your two cups are already hanging from the coat hanger (done before the lesson). Check it is level when the cups are empty (30 seconds).
  2. Put a glue stick in one cup. Guess: how many 20¢ coins will balance it?
  3. Add coins one at a time until the hanger is level.
  4. Write your number. How do you know it is level?

GO FURTHER (optional, any time you finish early): One glue stick balances 8 coins. A pencil case balances 3 glue sticks. How many coins balance the pencil case? If an eraser balances 4 coins, how many erasers balance the pencil case?

Math Inquirers P2 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 10.5 to 19.5. The coat-hanger balance is hung and tested level BEFORE the lesson (kit message, then a 30-second check at minute 0). Only the glue stick is tested in class (the spoon is a take-home). Pair work is 5.5 minutes. Pairs use their coat-hanger balance. Online: children hold their balance up to the camera; if a child has none, they balance two objects in their hands and guess which is heavier, then check on a kitchen scale with an adult. Say: "Before you add any coins, write your guess." Walk round and ask: "How do you know it is level?" "What happens if you add one more coin?" Do not teach the swapping idea yet: let children notice that the number of coins tells us how heavy the object is. Go further (a second what-if, so nobody waits) is the box at the foot of this slide; the question is also posted in the chat when the pairs go to their breakout rooms. It is for ANY child who finishes the explore task or wants more: say it to the whole group as a choice, never as a prize, and never name who tries it. Pairs stay together unless both children agree to split. Answer: 24 coins (3 x 8). 6 erasers (24 / 4). Take it at the debrief only if a pair offers it.
