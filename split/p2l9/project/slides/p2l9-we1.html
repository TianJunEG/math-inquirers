Step 1. Start with the scale that has a number. Scale B: 1 pineapple = 2 kg.

Step 2. Scale A: swap each pineapple for 2 kg. 2 + 2 + 2 = 6. The watermelon is 6 kg.

Step 3. Scale C: 6 kg = 2 kg + 4 bags. So the 4 bags are 6 − 2 = 4 kg.

Step 4. 4 bags make 4 kg, so 1 bag is 1 kg. Check: 2 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 6 ✓

Math Inquirers P2 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Worked example. Ask first: "Which scale should we start with? Why?" (the one with a number). Before each click, ask the group to predict. Think-aloud: "A watermelon balances 3 pineapples. One pineapple balances a 2 kg weight. The scale is level, so both sides are equally heavy. I can swap each pineapple for 2 kg: 2, 4, 6. The watermelon is 6 kg. Now the watermelon balances one pineapple and 4 bags of sugar. The watermelon is 6 kg and the pineapple is 2 kg, so the bags must be the rest: 6 − 2 = 4 kg. 4 bags make 4 kg, so each bag is 1 kg. I check scale C: 2 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 6 ✓." Common mistake in step 3: adding 6 + 2. Point to scale C: "The watermelon is on one side, the pineapple and the bags are on the other. The bags are what is left after the pineapple."
