Think harder: A rate as an equation

Tool: Make a table

A tank holds 360 litres. A tap fills it at 24 litres each minute. A drain at the bottom lets out 4 litres each minute. The tank starts empty and both are open.

(a) How many litres does the tank gain each minute?

(b) Find the time, in minutes, until the tank is full.

(c) The tap is then closed and the drain stays open. Find the time in minutes until the tank is empty.

(a) 20 litres each minute.

(b) 18 minutes.

(c) 90 minutes.

ADDED ITEM pipe (a slide-only addition for the skill gap "A rate as an equation"; it is not in the printed workbook). TOOL: Make a table. SAY: Make a table of minutes and litres in the tank. Then write the rule as an equation: litres = rate x time. ASK: What is the rate of the tank filling when both are open? What equation can you write, and what operation undoes it? LISTEN FOR: Net rate 24 − 4 = 20 litres per minute. 20 x t = 360, so t = 18. For (c) 4 x t = 360, so t = 90. Listen for "rate x time = amount" and for undoing it by dividing. Everyone writes the answer on their own paper, then say the answer together on three, then click the answer box. Nobody holds anything up, nobody is marked, and passing is always fine. ANSWER: (a) 20 litres each minute. (b) 18 minutes. (c) 90 minutes. WHY: Net fill rate: 24 − 4 = 20 litres each minute. 20 x t = 360 gives t = 360 ÷ 20 = 18 minutes. Emptying: 4 x t = 360 gives t = 90 minutes. SAME SKILL, NEW NUMBERS (tutor bank, say it aloud or write it on the board): A tank holds 500 litres. A tap fills 30 litres each minute and a drain lets out 5 litres each minute. Find the time in minutes until the empty tank is full, with both open. Answer: 20 minutes.
