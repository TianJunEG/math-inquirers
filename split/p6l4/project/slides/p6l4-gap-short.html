ADDED ITEM short (a slide-only addition for the skill gap "Shortcuts before calculating"; it is not in the printed workbook). TOOL: Try something simpler. SAY: Try something simpler first: work out 5 × 5 − 4 × 6 and 6 × 6 − 5 × 7. What do you notice? For (b), look for a number that appears twice. ASK: Why does the answer to (a) always come out the same? What can you take out of both parts in (b)? LISTEN FOR: (a) n x n - (n - 1) × (n + 1) = 1 for every n. (b) Take out 0.65: 0.65 × (27 + 73) = 0.65 × 100 = 65. Listen for the small cases and the shared factor. Everyone writes the answer on their own paper, then say the answer together on three, then click the answer box. Nobody holds anything up, nobody is marked, and passing is always fine. ANSWER: (a) 1. (b) 65. WHY: (a) 5 × 5 − 4 × 6 = 1 and 6 × 6 − 5 × 7 = 1, so 83 × 83 − 82 × 84 = 1 (the product of the neighbours is always 1 less than the square). (b) 0.65 × (27 + 73) = 0.65 × 100 = 65.
