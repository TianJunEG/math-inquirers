Think harder: Digit sums for 3 and 9

Tool: Spot the pattern

(a) Use digit sums. Which of 6 372, 5 481 and 7 296 are multiples of 9?

(b) Which of the three numbers are multiples of 3 but not of 9?

(a) 6 372 and 5 481.

(b) 7 296.

ADDED ITEM d9 (a slide-only addition for the skill gap "Digit sums for 3 and 9"; it is not in the printed workbook). TOOL: Spot the pattern. SAY: Add the digits of each number. If the digit sum is in the 9 times table the number is too. What about the 3 times table? ASK: Why does a multiple of 9 always have a digit sum that is a multiple of 9? Is 7 296 in the 3 table? LISTEN FOR: 6 + 3 + 7 + 2 = 18, 5 + 4 + 8 + 1 = 18, 7 + 2 + 9 + 6 = 24. 24 is in the 3 table but not the 9 table. Listen for the reason: 10, 100, 1 000 are each 1 more than a multiple of 9. Everyone writes the answer on their own paper, then say the answer together on three, then click the answer box. Nobody holds anything up, nobody is marked, and passing is always fine. ANSWER: (a) 6 372 and 5 481. (b) 7 296. WHY: 6 372 has digit sum 18 and 5 481 has digit sum 18, so both are multiples of 9. 7 296 has digit sum 24, so it is a multiple of 3 and not of 9. SAME SKILL, NEW NUMBERS (tutor bank, say it aloud or write it on the board): Which of 4 536, 8 127 and 3 705 are multiples of 9? Answer: 4 536 and 8 127 (digit sums 18 and 18; 3 705 has digit sum 15).
