Why does a square number like 36 have an odd number of factors, when most numbers have an even number?

Sentence starters:

Minutes 77 to 82. One question only. Pairs talk for 90 seconds, then volunteers share. Listen for: factors come in pairs that multiply to the number, so the count is usually even. For a square number one pair is a number times itself (6 × 6), counted once, so the total is odd. Take-home question (or the opener of the next lesson): is there a biggest prime? (No. Euclid showed this about 2300 years ago: a secondary-school teaser.)
