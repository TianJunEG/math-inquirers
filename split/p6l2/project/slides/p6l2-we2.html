Factor trees: split into primes

Step 1. 60 = 6 × 10. Split any two factors you know.

Step 2. 6 = 2 × 3 and 10 = 2 × 5. Keep splitting until every branch is a prime.

Step 3. Primes at the ends: 2, 3, 2, 5. Write them in order.

60 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 5 (another start, 4 × 15, gives the same primes)

Every number above 1 has only ONE set of prime factors. A prime that is a factor is a "prime factor".

Math Inquirers P6 • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Build the tree on the whiteboard as you click. Stop each branch only at a prime: circle primes (2, 3, 5 ...). Start again with 60 = 4 × 15 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 5: the same primes, different tree. Check by multiplying: 2 × 2 × 3 × 5 = 60. Think-aloud: "Is 6 prime? No, it has factors 2 and 3. Split it."
