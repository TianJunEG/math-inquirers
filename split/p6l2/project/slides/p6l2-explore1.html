Explore: the sieve

EXPLORE

  1. On your 1 to 100 grid, cross out 1. It is not prime.
  2. Circle 2. Cross out every other multiple of 2 (4, 6, 8, …).
  3. Circle the next number not crossed out (3). Cross out its other multiples. Do the same for 5 and 7.
  4. Circle every number left. How many primes are there?

Math Inquirers P6 • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 10 to 15. Pairs work on a printed 1 to 100 grid (online: drawn grid, or the tutor annotates a shared grid and children copy). The picture shows the start: 1 greyed, 2 and 3 circled, multiples of 2 greyed. First ask for a prediction: how many primes below 100? Insist on circling each prime as they go. This is the sieve of Eratosthenes, about 2200 years old. Do not reveal the count yet. If a pair wants more: look at which numbers are left after crossing out multiples of 2, 3, 5 and 7 and whether any multiple of 11 is still uncrossed.
