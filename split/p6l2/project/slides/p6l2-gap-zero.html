Think harder: Zeros at the end

Tool: Try something simpler

How many zeros are at the end of the product 12 × 15 × 20 × 25 × 35?

Do not multiply it all out.

4 zeros.

Math Inquirers P6 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Optional, if you finish early (no clock time): show it while some pairs are working or have finished, or use it live as an extra warm-up or tutor-bank question; never at a fixed minute, and it never takes time from the plan. ADDED ITEM zero (a slide-only addition for the skill gap "Zeros at the end"; it is not in the printed workbook). TOOL: Try something simpler. SAY: A zero at the end comes from one 2 and one 5 multiplied together, because 2 × 5 = 10. Break each number into 2s and 5s. ASK: How many 2s are there in all? How many 5s? Which one limits the number of zeros? LISTEN FOR: 2s: 12 has two, 20 has two, so 4. 5s: 15, 20, 25 (two), 35, so 5. There are 4 pairs of 2 and 5, so 4 zeros; the extra 5 does not make a zero without another 2. Check by multiplying: 3 150 000. Everyone writes the answer on their own paper, then say the answer together on three, then click the answer box. Nobody holds anything up, nobody is marked, and passing is always fine. ANSWER: 4 zeros. WHY: 12 = 2 × 2 × 3, 15 = 3 × 5, 20 = 2 × 2 × 5, 25 = 5 × 5, 35 = 5 × 7. Four 2s and five 5s: 4 pairs, so 4 zeros. Check: the product is 3 150 000. SAME SKILL, NEW NUMBERS (tutor bank, say it aloud or write it on the board): How many zeros are at the end of 16 × 25 × 35 × 40? Answer: 4 zeros (seven 2s and four 5s).
