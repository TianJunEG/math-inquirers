Worked example: is 91 prime?

A prime has exactly two factors. Test small numbers.

Step 1. Not even. 9 + 1 = 10, not a multiple of 3. Does not end in 0 or 5.

Step 2. Try 7: 7 × 13 = 91. So 7 is a factor.

Step 3. Stop testing once d × d is bigger than the number: 10 × 10 = 100 is bigger than 91.

91 is NOT prime: 91 = 7 × 13

The quick tests: even (divides by 2); digits add to a multiple of 3 (divides by 3); ends in 0 or 5 (divides by 5). Then try 7. Why stop at 10? If 91 had a factor pair with both numbers 10 or more, the product would be at least 100. So one factor of any pair is less than 10. Check 91 ÷ 7 = 13 with 7 × 13.
