Go further: The greatest total you cannot make

Tool: Make a table

Stickers are sold in sheets of 4 and sheets of 9. Mei buys some sheets of each kind, or none of one kind.

What is the greatest number of stickers that she cannot buy exactly?

23 stickers.

Math Inquirers P6 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Optional, if you finish early (no clock time): show it while some pairs are working or have finished, or use it live as an extra warm-up or tutor-bank question; never at a fixed minute, and it never takes time from the plan. ADDED ITEM pack (a slide-only addition for the skill gap "The greatest total you cannot make"; it is not in the printed workbook). TOOL: Make a table. SAY: Write the numbers 1 to 40 in rows of 4. Mark every total you can make. Look for the first row where every number is marked. ASK: Once you have 4 totals in a row, what happens next? How do you know 23 is the last one? LISTEN FOR: Four in a row is enough because adding 4 reaches everything after. 24 to 27 are all buyable (24 = 4 × 6, 25 = 16 + 9, 26 = 8 + 18, 27 = 3 × 9), so nothing above 23 is missing, and 23 cannot be made. Listen for the argument. Everyone writes the answer on their own paper, then say the answer together on three, then click the answer box. Nobody holds anything up, nobody is marked, and passing is always fine. ANSWER: 23 stickers. WHY: Totals are 4a + 9b. 24, 25, 26 and 27 can all be made, so every number from 24 up can be made by adding 4s. 23 cannot: 23 − 9 = 14, 23 − 18 = 5 are not multiples of 4, and 23 is not a multiple of 4. The greatest total that cannot be made is 23. SAME SKILL, NEW NUMBERS (tutor bank, say it aloud or write it on the board): Sheets come in 5s and 6s. What is the greatest total you cannot make? Answer: 19.
