TODAY WE WILL

Take on a challenge round with puzzles from all nine lessons, then plan, build, test and show your own puzzle program.

Math Inquirers P6 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Objective: Use ideas from all nine lessons (sequences, primes, negative numbers, algebra, counting, probability, angles, clock arithmetic, averages) in a mixed challenge round, then plan, build, test and present a small puzzle program, working out the maths by hand first and explaining why it works. Materials: Laptop or desktop with Scratch 3 (scratch.mit.edu), workbook (challenge round on page 1, planning sheet on page 2), pencil, mini whiteboard, links to the three starter projects (or build them from the slides), certificates, stamps. Online: Challenge round in teams of 2 in breakout rooms. Building: everyone in the main room with cameras on, asking for help in the chat. Showcase: each child shares their screen for 3 minutes; the group gives two stars and a wish in the chat. Time plan (this lesson changes the usual routine): warm-up 0 to 10 (lightning relay, quick questions), challenge round 10 to 30 (workbook page 1 in teams of 2, check together, talk about how we started), from puzzle to program 30 to 37 (three starter projects; click to reveal), brain break 37 to 42, plan and build 42 to 60 (teaching for the planning sheet, then workbook page 2 and Scratch; early finishers: stretch and challenge), showcase 60 to 84 (3 minutes each, rubric in the showcase notes), talk and exit ticket 84 to 90, then certificates. Before the lesson: save the three starter projects (A Mystery Machine, B Prime or Not?, C Lucky Seven) as shareable Scratch links, print or fill in certificates, and decide a random showcase order.
