Showcase Challenge

Mixed: maths and code • challenge round, then your own puzzle program

TODAY WE WILL

Take on a challenge round with puzzles from all nine lessons, then plan, build, test and show your own puzzle program.

Math Inquirers P6 • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Objective: Use ideas from all nine lessons (sequences, primes, negative numbers, algebra, counting, probability, angles, clock arithmetic, averages) in a mixed challenge round, then plan, build, test and present a small puzzle program, working out the maths by hand first and explaining why it works. Materials: Laptop or desktop with Scratch 3 (scratch.mit.edu), workbook (challenge round on page 1, planning sheet on page 2), pencil, mini whiteboard, the starter projects as .sb3 files (A Mystery Machine, B Prime or Not?, C Lucky Seven, D Clock Jumper; children use File, Load from your computer), or build them from the slides, certificates, stamps. Online: Challenge round in teams of 2 in breakout rooms. Building: everyone in the main room with cameras on, asking for help in the chat. Showcase: each child shares their screen for 3 minutes; the group gives two stars and a wish in the chat. Children may paste a screenshot into the chat instead and the tutor runs their file, which removes screen-share switching. Block colours on our slides are simplified; in Scratch, find blocks by the category name on the left, not by colour. Time plan (this lesson changes the usual routine): warm-up 0 to 5 (lightning relay; the quick questions are spare), challenge round 5 to 22 (workbook page 1 in teams of 2, check together, talk about how we started), from puzzle to program 22 to 29 (starter projects; click to reveal), brain break 29 to 32, plan and build 32 to 62 (teaching for the planning sheet, then workbook page 2 and Scratch; early finishers: stretch and challenge), showcase 62 to 87 (3 minutes each plus a 20-second switch, rubric in the showcase notes), exit ticket and certificates 87 to 90. Build target: a working copy of one starter with ONE change is full marks; an own idea is optional. Children chose their starter and their twist at the end of Lesson 9. Before the lesson: build the starter projects once (A Mystery Machine, B Prime or Not?, C Lucky Seven and D Clock Jumper) and share them as .sb3 files (children use File, Load from your computer; no account needed) with shared project links as a backup. Print or fill in certificates, and decide a random showcase order. Showcase switching: ask the next child to have their screen ready while the one before answers questions; a child may paste a screenshot of the program into the chat and the tutor runs their file, which removes the switching time.
