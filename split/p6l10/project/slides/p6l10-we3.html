Starter C: Lucky Seven

6 of the 36 equally likely results make 7: that is 1/6. 1000 ÷ 6 ≈ 167.

Our runs: 152, 182, 162. Close to 167, never exactly the same: chance varies.

Math Inquirers P6 • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Starter C (Lesson 6 idea). Think-aloud: “Lucky Seven: there are 6 × 6 = 36 equally likely results for two dice. 1 + 6, 2 + 5, 3 + 4, 4 + 3, 5 + 2, 6 + 1 make 7: that is 6 of 36, one sixth. So in 1000 rolls I expect about 167. When I run it I got 152, then 182, then 162: close to 167 but never the same, because the rolls are random.” The table: there are 6 × 6 = 36 ways two dice can land, all equally likely; the yellow cells (1 + 6, 2 + 5, … , 6 + 1) make 7. Run it 3 times on screen and write the results: they will differ. A run of 1000 usually lands between about 145 and 190. Worksheet Q4c asks about 8s, so do not count the 8s now. 5 blocks. Twist ideas: let the player pick the total; count every total in a list; compare 100 rolls with 10 000 rolls. Python version (optional): import random sevens = 0 for roll in range(1000): if random.randint(1, 6) + random.randint(1, 6) == 7: sevens = sevens + 1 print(sevens, "sevens in 1000 rolls") print("We expect about", round(1000 / 6)) Three test runs printed 152, 182 and 162 sevens; the last line always prints "We expect about 167".
