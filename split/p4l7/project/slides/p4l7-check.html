Sneak-peek puzzles

Ideas we have not learnt yet. Guesses and "I do not know" are welcome. Nobody is marked.

Write your answer on your own paper. When your tutor says, hold it to the camera. Only your tutor looks.

1

Try it

Look at the picture: a 5 by 3 rectangle of squares with one corner square cut out. Count the squares that are left. How many squares is the area?

14 squares (15 − 1).

2

Think

The perimeter is the distance all the way round the edge. Is the perimeter of this shape longer than, shorter than or the same as the perimeter of the full 5 by 3 rectangle? Say why.

The same (16). Push the corner back out.

Math Inquirers Level 4 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 1 to 4 (3 minutes). SNEAK-PEEK PUZZLES. Say: "These are ideas we have not learnt yet. Guessing is welcome, drawing is welcome and so is saying I do not know. Nobody is marked, and nobody's answer is shown to the group." Puzzle 1 is a TRY IT puzzle: any child can start it with objects, a drawing or counting, so everyone has a first win. Puzzle 2 needs a little thinking. About 90 seconds each. Children show their answers PRIVATELY: written on their own paper and held to the camera at the same moment for everyone (or shown only to you on camera). Keep every part of this in the shared session. Never ask for boards up in front of the group, never read out whose answer is whose, and never say who got it right. Then click to show each answer and talk it through for everyone ("some of you tried ..."), naming no child. Ask for volunteers first if you want a reason said aloud; nobody is called on by name, and passing is always fine. ANSWERS: 1) 14 squares (the full 5 by 3 rectangle is 15 squares, and 1 is cut out). 2) The same: 16 units for both. Push the cut-out corner back out. The two edges of the notch are just as long as the two edges of the corner they replace. A GOOD REASON: For area, count the squares or take away the piece that is missing. For perimeter, push the corner back out to a rectangle. Use what you see only to plan your help. It does not decide who is on which page and it is not recorded as a mark. After question 3 on page 1 every child may choose: carry on, or try the Think harder page. Make the invitation to the WHOLE group ("when you have finished Start here you can choose Think harder, Go further or the Investigation"), so nobody is singled out. Never name a child, and never announce who is on which page. Everyone stays in the same room and every child turns to the page they choose. If a pair would be split, ask both children first; never leave a child alone on page 1 (join them to another pair or to yourself).
