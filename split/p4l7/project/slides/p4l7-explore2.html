Explore: talk about it

1. How many different rectangles did you make with 12 tiles?

2. Which has the longest perimeter? The shortest?

3. All of them have the same area. Why are the perimeters different?

Math Inquirers P4 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 15 to 25. Gather the rectangles on the board in the order children say them, then ask "Is any missing? Is any the same as another, just turned?" Answers: 1) 3 rectangles: 1 by 12, 2 by 6, 3 by 4 (12 by 1 is the same as 1 by 12 turned). 2) 1 by 12 has 26 cm; 3 by 4 has 14 cm (2 by 6 has 16 cm). 3) The area is the number of tiles, which never changes. The perimeter counts the outside edges: in a long row most tiles show two edges; squashed together, more edges are hidden inside. Close to a square hides the most edges. Do NOT give the rule yet if nobody says it: the teaching part comes back to it.
