Ben says: "A bigger area always means a bigger perimeter." Do you agree? Use rectangles to test it.

Sentence starters:

“I noticed that …” “I tested it by …” “I know because …”

Math Inquirers P4 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 70 to 80. Pairs talk for 2 minutes, then share. Answer: Ben is wrong. A 1 cm by 12 cm rectangle has area 12 cm² and perimeter 26 cm; a 4 cm by 4 cm square has a bigger area (16 cm²) but a smaller perimeter (16 cm). One counter-example is enough to show a rule is not always true. Second question if time: "When we cut a shape and move the pieces, which stays the same?" (The area, because nothing is added or lost; the perimeter can change.) Praise the reasons, not only the answers.
