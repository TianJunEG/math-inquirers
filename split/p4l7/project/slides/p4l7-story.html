Story break: Euclid

Alexandria, Egypt (Greek-speaking world) • about 325 BC - about 265 BC (dates are estimates)

Euclid taught mathematics in Alexandria, in Egypt, about 2,300 years ago. Little is known about his life, but his book the Elements is famous. It was used to teach geometry for about 2,000 years. Euclid put many ideas in a careful order and proved each step from simple facts. In Book I he shows that two parallelograms on the same base and between the same two parallel lines have the same area, even if one looks more slanted. So cutting a shape and sliding a piece can change how it looks without changing its area. Euclid wanted a reason for every claim, just as you do when you explain your working.

TRY IT (OPTIONAL)

A rectangle is 8 cm long and 5 cm high. Slide its top side along to make a slanted shape with the same base and height. What is the area of each? Which has the longer way round?

TRY IT ANSWER (tutor only, do not show): Rectangle: 8 x 5 = 40 square cm. The slanted parallelogram has the same base and height, so its area is also 40 square cm. Its slanted sides are longer than 5 cm, so it has the longer way round (a bigger perimeter). Everything in the story is true as far as historians know. Confidence: high.
