List in order: length 1, 2, 3 … 3 does not work (20 ÷ 3 has a remainder). Stop at 5 by 4: it is 4 by 5 again.

Long and thin: the biggest perimeter. Closest to a square: the smallest perimeter.

Math Inquirers P4 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Make a list in order (the factor pairs from Lesson 1). Click to add each row while children say the breadth: "1 times what is 20?" Then the note: why 3 is missing and why we stop. Then the pattern. Think-aloud: "Every rectangle has the same 20 squares inside, but the long thin one has 42 cm of edge and the squashed-up one only 18." Ask: "Could a rectangle with area 20 cm² have a perimeter of 30 cm?" (No: with whole-cm sides only 42, 24 and 18 are possible.)
