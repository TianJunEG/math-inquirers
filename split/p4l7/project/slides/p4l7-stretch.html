Think harder

1. List every rectangle with whole-cm sides and an area of 30 cm². Which one has the smallest perimeter?

2. A rectangle has whole-cm sides and a perimeter of 20 cm. List all the possible rectangles and their areas. Which has the largest area?

3. A 3 cm by 3 cm square is cut from each of the four corners of a 12 cm by 8 cm rectangle. Find the area and the perimeter of the shape that is left.

4. Three squares with sides 2 cm, 3 cm and 4 cm stand side by side in a row on the same line, smallest on the left. Find the area and the perimeter of the figure.

Answers (same four questions as the printed Think harder page): 1) 1 by 30 (62 cm), 2 by 15 (34 cm), 3 by 10 (26 cm), 5 by 6 (22 cm). Smallest: 5 cm by 6 cm, 22 cm. 2) 1 by 9 (9 cm²), 2 by 8 (16 cm²), 3 by 7 (21 cm²), 4 by 6 (24 cm²), 5 by 5 (25 cm²). Largest: the 5 cm by 5 cm square, 25 cm². 3) Area 96 − 4 × 9 = 60 cm². Perimeter 40 cm, the same as the rectangle: each cut takes away 3 + 3 and adds 3 + 3. 4) Area 4 + 9 + 16 = 29 cm². Perimeter 26 cm: push the steps out to a 9 cm by 4 cm rectangle.
