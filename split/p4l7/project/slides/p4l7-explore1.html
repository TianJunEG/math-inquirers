Explore: what to do

EXPLORE

  1. Take 12 square tiles. Each is 1 cm by 1 cm.
  2. Use all 12 to make a rectangle. Write its length, breadth and perimeter.
  3. Make every different rectangle you can.
  4. Which has the longest perimeter? The shortest? What do you notice?

Go further (any time): What if you use 24 tiles? Make every rectangle. Which has the smallest perimeter?

Math Inquirers P4 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 11.5 to 20.5. Pairs build rectangles with 12 tiles (online: paper squares on camera, or draw on 1 cm grid paper). Ask them to predict first: "How many rectangles? Will they all have the same perimeter?" Many children think the same tiles must give the same perimeter. Let them find out. Go further (on the slide), for any child who wants it: try 16 tiles. Which rectangle has the smallest perimeter now? (4 by 4, 16 cm.) GO FURTHER (on the slide from the start of the explore, so nobody waits): What if you use 24 tiles? Make every rectangle. Which has the smallest perimeter? Answer: 4 rectangles: 1 × 24 (perimeter 50 cm), 2 × 12 (28 cm), 3 × 8 (22 cm) and 4 × 6 (20 cm). The 4 by 6 has the smallest perimeter: it is the closest to a square.
