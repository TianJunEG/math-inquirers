LESSON 7 • WEEK 4

Area Architects II

Area and perimeter, rectangles and squares

TODAY WE WILL

Find the area and perimeter of tricky shapes the smart way, and find out why the same area can have different perimeters.

Math Inquirers P4 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Objective: Find the area of a composite figure by splitting it into rectangles or by taking away a missing piece, find its perimeter by pushing the steps out to a rectangle, find every rectangle with a given area and compare their perimeters, and explain what changes when a shape is cut and rearranged. Thinking strategies taught today: SPLIT IT or TAKE AWAY, PUSH THE STEPS OUT, and MAKE A LIST IN ORDER. Materials: 1 cm square tiles or 24 paper squares per child, 1 cm grid paper, scissors, a 30 cm ruler, mini whiteboards, a paper strip 8 squares by 2 squares for cutting. Online: children cut out paper squares and 1 cm grid strips before the lesson and build on camera; the tutor draws on the grid figures on the slides. Time plan: warm-up game 0 to 5, quick questions 5 to 8, explore 8 to 18, teaching 18 to 30 (click to reveal each step and answer; the "your turn" slides are optional, skip them if short of time), brain break 30 to 34, worksheet page 1 from 34 to 50 and check together 50 to 53, short teaching before page 2 from 53 to 58, worksheet page 2 from 58 to 76 and check together 76 to 80, talk time 80 to 85, exit ticket 85 to 90. The Challenge slides and the Think Harder pages are for early finishers and for take-home: they have no fixed time slot. Online: page 1 is pair work, so allow about 2 minutes to move into and out of breakout rooms (or run page 1 as a whole-group screen-share if there are only 8 children).
