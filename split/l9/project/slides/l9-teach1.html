Measure fairly: equal units

The same pencil is measured three ways. Which way is fair?

✓ Fair: equal cubes, touching, starting at the end: 6 cubes

Not fair: gaps. Only 4 cubes, but the pencil did not get shorter.

Not fair: big and small blocks. "4" does not tell us the length.

Same-size units, no gaps, no overlaps, start at the end. Then everyone gets the same number.

Math Inquirers Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Teach: the idea and why. Hold up a real pencil and some cubes. Click 1: six equal cubes from the very end of the pencil, touching. Click 2: the same pencil with gaps. Ask: "Is the pencil really 4 cubes long? Did it shrink?" (No: the gaps are length we did not count.) Click 3: big and small blocks. Ask: "If Ali says 4 blocks and Mei says 6 cubes, who has the longer pencil?" (We cannot tell: the units are not the same.) Click 4: read the rule together. Think-aloud: "Rules for measuring: equal units, no gaps, no overlaps, start at the end. If I break a rule, my number is not fair." Link back to the explore: "This is why you and your partner got different numbers of hand spans."
