Explore: High Fives

EXPLORE

  1. Guess first: if all 8 of us high-five everyone else once, how many high fives is that?
  2. Start small: 2 children, then 3, then 4. Draw each child as a dot and each high five as a line.
  3. Make a table: number of children, number of high fives.
  4. Look for a pattern and use it to find the answer for 8.

Math Inquirers P5 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 12 to 25.5 (the whole explore; this slide 12 to 21.5). Pairs. Online: breakout rooms, each pair draws dots on a whiteboard and joins them. Predict first: most children guess 8 × 8 = 64 or 8 + 7; write guesses down. Let them draw 2, 3, 4 and 5 dots before they look for the pattern. Pair work is at most 5.5 minutes: stop the table at 6 children (the quick rule for 8 and for 30 children is the Go further what-if). Anyone who wants more (open to every pair): a class of 8 or of 30. The picture shows 1, 3 and 6 lines for 2, 3 and 4 children. PROTECTED THINK TIME: before the pairs touch anything, say "Think for 30 seconds before anyone speaks" and show the clock; then pairs talk and start. PAIR WORK IS CAPPED AT 5.5 MINUTES: say "Pencils down" at minute 21.5 and bring the group back (the minutes before that include the 30 seconds of thinking, finding materials and, in lessons that use them, the move to and from breakout rooms). GO FURTHER (say it as soon as a pair has finished the task): any pair that finishes early may try the what-if question on the next slide. It is open to every pair, not a reward. Answer: 8 children: 28. 30 children: 435. 12 children: 66. With n children each high-fives (n − 1) others, but every high five is counted twice, so the number is n × (n − 1) ÷ 2. For 12 children: 12 × 11 ÷ 2 = 66. The table 1, 3, 6, 10, 15, 21 … also works.
