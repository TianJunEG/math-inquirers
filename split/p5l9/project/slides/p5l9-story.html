Story break: Al-Karaji

about 953–1029 • Baghdad (today Iraq) • A mathematician who wrote about number patterns in a famous algebra book.

Al-Karaji lived about a thousand years ago and spent most of his life in Baghdad. His name is also written al-Karkhi, and historians are not sure whether it comes from the city of Karaj or from a district of Baghdad called Karkh. He wrote a major algebra book called Al-Fakhri, where he explained rules for working with unknown numbers. He also wrote about a number pattern: if you add the cubes of the numbers 1, 2, 3 and so on up to some number, you get the square of the sum of those numbers. He showed this for the numbers up to 10 and explained how the same idea would carry on. Later in life he also worked on engineering, such as finding water underground.

Good to know: His birthplace and the origin of his name are debated by historians. Some of his work survives only through later writers.
