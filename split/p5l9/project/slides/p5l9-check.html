0–3 MIN

Sneak-peek puzzles

Ideas we have not learnt yet. Guessing is welcome. "I do not know" is welcome. Nobody is marked.

1. Sticks make a row of triangles: 1 triangle uses 3 sticks, 2 triangles use 5 sticks, 3 triangles use 7 sticks. Build or draw 4 triangles. How many sticks does it use?

9 sticks

2. Using the same pattern, how many sticks do 10 triangles need? How many triangles can you make with exactly 41 sticks? Say how you know.

21 sticks; 20 triangles

Show your answer only to your tutor: on your own paper, or all hold paper to the camera together. Then a volunteer can tell us how they thought.

Math Inquirers Level 5 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 0 to 3. SNEAK-PEEK PUZZLES. Show puzzle 1, give about a minute, click to show the short answer, then puzzle 2. Say it out loud: these are ideas we have not learnt yet; guessing is welcome; "I do not know" is welcome; nobody is marked. Puzzle 1 is an access puzzle: any child can try it with objects, drawing or counting, so everyone can start well. Puzzle 2 is a thinking puzzle. Answers are shown PRIVATELY (own paper shown only to you as you walk round; online, everyone holds paper to the camera at the same moment, or shows it to you only, on camera). Never boards in front of the group, never read out answers with names, no tally, no ranking. Never record scores. Answers and what to listen for. Q1: 9 sticks. Each new triangle shares a side with the one before, so it adds 2 sticks.; Q2: 21 sticks for 10 triangles (3 + 9 × 2, or 2 × 10 + 1). 41 sticks make 20 triangles (take away the 1 extra stick, then 40 ÷ 2). Continuing the list also works. Minutes 0 to 3. SNEAK-PEEK PUZZLES. Show the two puzzles one at a time. Say: "These are ideas we have not learnt yet. Guessing is welcome and so is 'I do not know'. Nobody is marked." Puzzle 1 is one that any child can try with objects, drawing or counting, so everybody can start well. Puzzle 2 is a thinking puzzle. Answers are shown PRIVATELY, never on boards in front of the group. Face to face: children write on their own paper and show it only to you as you walk round. Online: everyone holds their paper up to the camera at the same moment, or shows it to you only, on camera. Do not read answers out with names, do not keep a tally, do not rank. Then ask for VOLUNTEERS to say how they thought (put a hand up or use a reaction); never choose a child because you saw a wrong answer. Never record scores. Choosing a start: every child has the same neutral line on workbook page 1: "After question 3 you may choose: carry on, or try Think harder." Invite the WHOLE group, never one child: "when you have finished Start here you can choose Think harder, Go further or the Investigation." Never single out a child by name and never announce who is on which page. Pairs are not split unless BOTH children agree. Any child may swap at any time.
