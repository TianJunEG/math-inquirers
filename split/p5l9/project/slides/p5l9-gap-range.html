ADDED ITEM range (a slide-only addition for the skill gap "Multiples in a range"; it is not in the printed workbook). TOOL: Try something simpler. SAY: Try something simpler first: how many multiples of 7 are there up to 100? Then up to 500? What do you do with the two answers? ASK: Is 100 a multiple of 7? Is 500? Why does that matter? What number do you test for "multiple of 7 and of 2"? LISTEN FOR: 500 ÷ 7 = 71 remainder 3 gives 71 multiples up to 500; 100 ÷ 7 = 14 remainder 2 gives 14 up to 100. 71 − 14 = 57. In (b) in the 2 table and the 7 table means in the 14 table: 500 ÷ 14 = 35, 100 ÷ 14 = 7, 35 − 7 = 28. Everyone writes the answer on their own paper, then say the answer together on three, then click the answer box. Nobody holds anything up, nobody is marked, and passing is always fine. ANSWER: (a) 57 multiples of 7. (b) 28 multiples of 14. WHY: Multiples of 7 up to 500: 71. Up to 100: 14. Neither 100 nor 500 is a multiple of 7, so 71 − 14 = 57. Multiples of 14 up to 500: 35, up to 100: 7, so 28. SAME SKILL, NEW NUMBERS (tutor bank, say it aloud or write it on the board): How many multiples of 9 are between 200 and 700? Answer: 55.
