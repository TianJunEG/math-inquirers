Seeing it two ways: tiles with a border

Grey tiles: 8, 10, 12. How many in figure 12?

Step 1. Way 1: a top row and a bottom row of (figure + 2) tiles, and 1 tile at each end: 2 × figure number + 6

Step 2. Way 2: the whole rectangle has 3 rows of (figure + 2) tiles. Take away the yellow ones: 3 × (figure + 2) − figure. Figure 12: 3 × 14 − 12

Both ways give 30 grey tiles in figure 12.

Different pictures, different-looking rules, the same answer. Check each rule on figures 1, 2, 3.

Math Inquirers P5 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Worked example: two ways to see one pattern. First let children count the grey tiles in figures 1, 2 and 3 on their own. Then show Way 1 (top and bottom rows plus the two end tiles): figure 12 has 14 + 14 + 2 = 30 grey tiles. Way 2: the whole 3 by 14 rectangle has 42 tiles; 12 are yellow, so 42 − 12 = 30. Both rules are the same: 3 × (figure + 2) − figure = 2 × figure + 6. Do not need to simplify with letters; just check that both give 8, 10, 12 for the first three figures. The grey count grows by 2 each time, which is why the rule has 2 × figure number in it, like the chairs in Q1.
