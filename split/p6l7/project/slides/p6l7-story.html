Story break: Cynthia Solomon

Cynthia Solomon • born 1938 • United States

Cynthia Solomon was born in 1938 and studied history at Radcliffe College. Later she studied computer science and then education, and she earned a PhD at Harvard. In 1967 she worked with Wally Feurzeig and Seymour Papert at a company called BBN to design Logo, which is described as the first programming language made for children. Logo is best known for turtle graphics. The turtle moves relative to its own position, so a command such as LEFT 90 makes it turn a quarter turn. Children in Lexington, Massachusetts were among the first to use Logo in school, and Solomon taught programming at a junior high school there in 1968 and 1969. Later she helped create Apple Logo. Ideas from Logo live on in Scratch.

A turtle draws a regular octagon by repeating FORWARD and RIGHT 8 times. How many degrees does it turn each time, and what is one inside angle of the octagon?

Read the story aloud, or ask a volunteer to read it (never pick a child). Who: A computer scientist who helped create Logo, a programming language for children. Link to today: Today's turtle draws polygons by moving forward and turning, and Logo is where that way of drawing began.
