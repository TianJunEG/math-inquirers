Worked example: a 5-point star

Change ONE number in the pentagon program.

Step 1. Add the turns: 5 × 144° = 720° = 2 full turns. It goes round twice.

Step 2. Star rule: turn = 360° × times round ÷ points = 720° ÷ 5 = 144°.

Step 3. Angle at each point = 180° − 144° = 36°.

Math Inquirers P6 • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Ask first: "The pentagon was repeat 5, turn 72. What happens if I double the turn to 144?" Take predictions, then click through. Think-aloud: "For the star the turtle goes round twice: 720° altogether. 5 equal turns: 720 ÷ 5 = 144°. The angle at each point is 180 − 144 = 36°." Last click: run it in Scratch and show the star. Point out: the turtle still turns at 5 corners, but it goes round twice, so the turns add up to 720°, not 360°. The angle at each point is still on a straight line with the turn: 180 − 144 = 36°. Ask: "How many times round would a turn of 216 go?" (5 × 216 = 1080: 3 times; it is the same star drawn the other way round).
