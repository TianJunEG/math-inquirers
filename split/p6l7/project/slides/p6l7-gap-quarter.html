Go further: A quarter circle in a square

Tool: Draw it

A square has sides of 10 cm. A quarter circle of radius 10 cm is drawn from one corner of the square, as in the picture. Use 3.14 for the number pi.

Find the area of the blue part of the square, outside the quarter circle.

21.5 cm².

Optional, if you finish early (no clock time): show it while some pairs are working or have finished, or use it live as an extra warm-up or tutor-bank question; never at a fixed minute, and it never takes time from the plan. ADDED ITEM quarter (a slide-only addition for the skill gap "A quarter circle in a square"; it is not in the printed workbook). TOOL: Draw it. SAY: Find two areas you know: the whole square and the quarter circle. How are they related to the blue part? ASK: What fraction of a full circle with radius 10 cm is the quarter circle? What is the area of the full circle? LISTEN FOR: Area of the full circle 3.14 × 10 × 10 = 314, the quarter is 314 ÷ 4 = 78.5. Blue = 100 − 78.5 = 21.5. Listen for "subtract the quarter circle from the square". Everyone writes the answer on their own paper, then say the answer together on three, then click the answer box. Nobody holds anything up, nobody is marked, and passing is always fine. ANSWER: 21.5 cm². WHY: Square: 10 × 10 = 100 cm². Quarter circle: 3.14 × 10 × 10 ÷ 4 = 78.5 cm². Blue part: 100 − 78.5 = 21.5 cm². SAME SKILL, NEW NUMBERS (tutor bank, say it aloud or write it on the board): A square has sides of 20 cm. A quarter circle of radius 20 cm is drawn from one corner. Use 3.14. Find the area of the part outside the quarter circle. Answer: 86 cm².
