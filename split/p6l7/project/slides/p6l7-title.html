LESSON 7 • WEEK 4 • MATHS + CODE

Turtle Geometry

Angles in polygons

TODAY WE WILL

Program a turtle to draw regular polygons and stars, and use the 360° turn to predict every angle before we test it in code.

Math Inquirers P6 • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Objective: Use the idea that a turtle going once round a closed shape turns 360° altogether to find the turn angle (360° ÷ sides) and inside angle (180° − turn) of regular polygons, work backwards from an angle to the number of sides, extend to star polygons (turn = 360° × times round ÷ points), and test every prediction with a pen program in Scratch (or Python turtle). Materials: A laptop or desktop with Scratch 3 open (scratch.mit.edu) and the Pen extension added, mini whiteboards, a protractor, plain or squared paper. Online: Pairs work in breakout rooms: one child drives Scratch and shares the screen, the other predicts, then they swap. Children hold drawings up to the camera. A tablet works for Scratch but is slower. Scratch set-up: before the lesson, check every child can open scratch.mit.edu and add the Pen extension (the button at the bottom left of the block menu). The turtle is any sprite with its pen down; in Scratch the turn block is "turn ↻ … degrees" (clockwise), shown on our slides as "turn right". Time plan: warm-up 0 to 10, explore at the computer 10 to 25, teaching 25 to 35 (click to reveal each step and answer), brain break 35 to 40, worksheet 40 to 70 with short teaching before each page and a check-together slide after it, talk time 70 to 80, exit ticket 80 to 90.
