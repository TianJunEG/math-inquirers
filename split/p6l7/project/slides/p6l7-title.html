LESSON 7 • WEEK 4 • MATHS + CODE

Turtle Geometry

Angles in polygons

TODAY WE WILL

Program a turtle to draw regular polygons and stars, and use the 360° turn to predict every angle before we test it in code.

Math Inquirers P6 • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Objective: Use the idea that a turtle going once round a closed shape turns 360° altogether to find the turn angle (360° ÷ sides) and inside angle (180° − turn) of regular polygons, work backwards from an angle to the number of sides, extend to star polygons (turn = 360° × times round ÷ points), and test every prediction with a pen program in Scratch (or Python turtle). Materials: A laptop or desktop with Scratch 3 open (scratch.mit.edu) and the Pen extension added, mini whiteboards, a protractor, plain or squared paper. Online: The tutor builds a “Turtle start” Scratch file beforehand (Pen extension added and the 4 set-up blocks already in place; no .sb3 file exists in this repository) and shares it through the approved channel, so children only open it. Pairs work in breakout rooms: one child drives Scratch and shares the screen, the other predicts, then they swap. Children hold drawings up to the camera. A tablet works for Scratch but is slower. Every drawing starts with the same 4 set-up blocks (erase all, go to x: -50 y: 100, point in direction 90, pen down) so shapes stay on the stage; turn blocks in Scratch show an arrow (turn ↻ is clockwise, our “turn right”). Block colours on our slides are simplified; in Scratch, find blocks by the category name on the left (Motion, Pen, Control), not by colour. Scratch set-up: before the lesson, check every child can open scratch.mit.edu and add the Pen extension (the button at the bottom left of the block menu). The turtle is any sprite with its pen down; in Scratch the turn block is "turn ↻ … degrees" (clockwise), shown on our slides as "turn right". Time plan (the exact minutes of this lesson, with a standing flex buffer): opening 0 to 2, sneak-peek puzzles 2 to 6.5, warm-up 6.5 to 13, explore at the computer 13 to 27.5, mini-lesson 27.5 to 40 (click to reveal each step and answer), brain break 40 to 45.5, worksheet 45.5 to 75.5 with short teaching before each page and a check-together slide after it, flex 75.5 to 78 (unscripted: use it for Think harder or catch-up; it is lost first), talk time 78 to 83, exit slip 83 to 86, looking back 86 to 88, close and stamp 88 to 90.
