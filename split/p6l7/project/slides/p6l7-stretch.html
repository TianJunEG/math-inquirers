Think harder

1. In a regular polygon the turn angle is the same as the inside angle. Which polygon is it?

2. Which regular polygons with 10 to 20 sides have a whole-number turn angle?

3. Regular hexagons fit together around a point with no gaps, like a honeycomb. Explain why using angles. Can regular pentagons do the same?

Math Inquirers P6 • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Optional, if you finish early (no clock time): show it only while some children are working, never as a group slide at a fixed minute. For children who finish early (not on the worksheet). Answers: 1) A square (turn = inside = 90°, because they add up to 180°) 2) 10, 12, 15, 18 and 20 sides (turns 36°, 30°, 24°, 20°, 18°); 360 cannot be shared exactly into 11, 13, 14, 16, 17 or 19 equal turns 3) Three hexagon angles make 3 × 120° = 360°. Pentagons cannot: 3 × 108° = 324° leaves a gap and 4 × 108° = 432° overlaps.
