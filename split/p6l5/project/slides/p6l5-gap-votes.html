Go further: Worst case, the runner-up

Tool: Act it out

Ali, Mei and Ravi stand for class captain. The child with the most votes wins. There are 31 votes in all. After 20 votes are counted, Ali has 9, Mei has 7 and Ravi has 4.

How many more of the remaining 11 votes must Ali get to be sure of winning, whatever happens to the other votes?

5 more votes.

Math Inquirers P6 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Optional, if you finish early (no clock time): show it while some pairs are working or have finished, or use it live as an extra warm-up or tutor-bank question; never at a fixed minute, and it never takes time from the plan. ADDED ITEM votes (a slide-only addition for the skill gap "Worst case, the runner-up"; it is not in the printed workbook). TOOL: Act it out. SAY: Act it out as the worst case: the votes Ali does not get all go to Mei. Try Ali getting 3 more, then 4 more, then 5 more. ASK: In the worst case who is Ali's rival? How do you know that 4 is not enough? LISTEN FOR: If Ali gets x more, Mei could get all the other 11 - x. Ali needs 9 + x to be more than 7 + (11 - x). With x = 4: Ali 13 and Mei 14. With x = 5: Ali 14 and Mei 13. Listen for "give the worst case to the runner-up". Everyone writes the answer on their own paper, then say the answer together on three, then click the answer box. Nobody holds anything up, nobody is marked, and passing is always fine. ANSWER: 5 more votes. WHY: Ali ends with 9 + x. In the worst case all the other 11 - x votes go to Mei, who ends with 7 + 11 - x = 18 - x. Ali wins for sure when 9 + x is greater than 18 - x, so x = 5 (14 against 13). With x = 4 it is 13 against 14. SAME SKILL, NEW NUMBERS (tutor bank, say it aloud or write it on the board): Ali 12, Mei 9 and Ravi 5 after 26 votes out of 40. How many more of the 14 remaining votes must Ali get to be sure of winning? Answer: 6 more votes (Ali 18 against Mei at most 17).
