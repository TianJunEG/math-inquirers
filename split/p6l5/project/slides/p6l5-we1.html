Worked example: a bike lock

A lock has 3 dials. Each dial shows 1, 2, 3, 4 or 5. How many codes?

Step 1. Dial 1: 5 choices. For each one, dial 2 has 5 choices: 5 × 5 = 25.

Step 2. For each of those 25, dial 3 has 5 choices: 25 × 5 = 125 codes.

Step 3. No digit used twice? 5 × 4 × 3 = 60: each dial has one fewer choice.

Check: 125 codes go from 111 to 555 using only the digits 1 to 5.

Math Inquirers P6 • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 28 to 30. Click through the steps. Ask before each: "How many choices does the next dial have?" For step 3: "Dial 1 can be any of 5. Dial 2 cannot repeat it: 4. Dial 3 cannot repeat either: 3." This prepares the digit-card question on page 1. Enumerated check: 125 codes with repeats, 60 without.
