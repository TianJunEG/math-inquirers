Optional, if you finish early

Think harder: Worst case, how many to be sure

Tool: Act it out

A drawer has 8 red socks, 6 blue socks and 5 green socks. Aisha takes socks out without looking.

(a) How many socks must she take to be sure of a pair of the same colour?

(b) How many socks must she take to be sure of 3 socks of the same colour?

(c) How many socks must she take to be sure of 2 blue socks?

(a) 4 socks.

(b) 7 socks.

(c) 15 socks.

Math Inquirers P6 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Optional, if you finish early (no clock time): show it while some pairs are working or have finished, or use it live as an extra warm-up or tutor-bank question; never at a fixed minute, and it never takes time from the plan. ADDED ITEM socks (a slide-only addition for the skill gap "Worst case, how many to be sure"; it is not in the printed workbook). TOOL: Act it out. SAY: Act it out as bad luck: how many can she take with no pair? Then one more must make a pair. Use counters of three colours. ASK: What is the worst that can happen in (c)? How many socks is that before the first blue sock arrives, and what then? LISTEN FOR: (a) 1 of each colour is 3, so 4. (b) 2 of each is 6, so 7. (c) Worst case: all 8 red and all 5 green first (13), then 1 blue is not enough, so 2 blue: she needs 13 + 2 = 15. Listen for "one fewer than the target, in every colour". Everyone writes the answer on their own paper, then say the answer together on three, then click the answer box. Nobody holds anything up, nobody is marked, and passing is always fine. ANSWER: (a) 4 socks. (b) 7 socks. (c) 15 socks. WHY: The worst case is the longest run with no success. (a) 3 socks (one of each colour), so 4. (b) 6 socks (two of each colour), so 7. (c) all 13 non-blue socks and then 1 blue is 14 socks with only 1 blue, so 15. SAME SKILL, NEW NUMBERS (tutor bank, say it aloud or write it on the board): A box has 7 black pens, 5 blue pens and 4 red pens. How many must you take out to be sure of 2 pens of the same colour? Answer: 4 pens.
