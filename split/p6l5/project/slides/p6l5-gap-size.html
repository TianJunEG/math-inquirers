Think harder: Squares and rectangles by size

Tool: List every way

The grid has 5 columns and 3 rows of small squares.

(a) How many square shapes, of all sizes, are in the grid?

(b) How many rectangles of any size are in the grid? A square counts as a rectangle.

(a) 26 squares.

(b) 90 rectangles.

ADDED ITEM size (a slide-only addition for the skill gap "Squares and rectangles by size"; it is not in the printed workbook). TOOL: List every way. SAY: Try something simpler first: a 1 by 3 strip, then a 2 by 3 grid. Count by size in a table. ASK: How many 1 by 1 squares? 2 by 2? 3 by 3? For rectangles, how many ways can you choose the left and right edges? The top and bottom edges? LISTEN FOR: Squares: 15 + 8 + 3 = 26. Rectangles: choose 2 of the 6 vertical lines (15 ways) and 2 of the 4 horizontal lines (6 ways): 15 × 6 = 90. Accept a size-by-size table for rectangles too. Everyone writes the answer on their own paper, then say the answer together on three, then click the answer box. Nobody holds anything up, nobody is marked, and passing is always fine. ANSWER: (a) 26 squares. (b) 90 rectangles. WHY: Squares: 1 by 1 is 5 × 3 = 15, 2 by 2 is 4 × 2 = 8, 3 by 3 is 3 × 1 = 3, total 26. Rectangles: pairs of vertical lines 6 × 5 ÷ 2 = 15, pairs of horizontal lines 4 × 3 ÷ 2 = 6, so 15 × 6 = 90. SAME SKILL, NEW NUMBERS (tutor bank, say it aloud or write it on the board): In a grid with 4 columns and 3 rows, how many square shapes of all sizes can be found? Answer: 20 (12 + 6 + 2).
