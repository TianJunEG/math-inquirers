Ravi says an isosceles triangle can have an angle bigger than 90°. Mei says it cannot. Who is right? Can it have a right angle?

Sentence starters:

“I noticed that …” “I tested it by …” “I know because …”

Math Inquirers P5 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 70 to 80. Pairs talk for 2 minutes, then share. Answer: Ravi is right. An isosceles triangle can have one big angle: for example 120°, 30°, 30°. It can also have one right angle: 90°, 45°, 45°. It cannot have two angles of 90° or more, because they would already add up to 180° or more. The two equal BASE angles are always less than 90°, so Mei is right about those. Second question if time: "How do you know you have found every case?" (we tried the given angle as the top angle and as a base angle). Praise the reasons, not only the answers.
