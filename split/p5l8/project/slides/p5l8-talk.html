Minutes 76 to 82. Pairs talk for 2 minutes, then share. Answer: Ravi is right. An isosceles triangle can have one big angle: for example 120°, 30°, 30°. It can also have one right angle: 90°, 45°, 45°. It cannot have two angles of 90° or more, because they would already add up to 180° or more. The two equal BASE angles are always less than 90°, so Mei is right about those. Second question if time: "How do you know you have found every case?" (we tried the given angle as the top angle and as a base angle). Praise the reasons, not only the answers. Talk-time rules: say "You may say pass." Ask for VOLUNTEERS first, then invite others only if they want to. No cold-calling by name, no public tallies or rankings, and no "who got it right?" hands.
