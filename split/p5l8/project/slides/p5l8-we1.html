Worked example: an angle chase with reasons

P is a corner. S is a point on the line QR. Find angles QPS, PSR and SPR.

Step 1. Angle QPS = 180° − 50° − 75° = 55°. Reason: angles in a triangle add up to 180°.

Step 2. Angle PSR = 180° − 75° = 105°. Reason: angles on a straight line add up to 180°.

Step 3. Angle SPR = 180° − 105° − 30° = 45°. Reason: angles in a triangle add up to 180°.

Step 4. Check: angle QPR = 55° + 45° = 100°, and 50° + 100° + 30° = 180° ✓

Math Inquirers Level 5 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Click to reveal each step and its arc on the figure. Ask children to say the step before each click. Write the reason every time: that is the whole lesson. Think-aloud: "I know two angles in triangle PQS: 50 and 75. The three angles in a triangle add up to 180, so the third one, QPS, is 55. Reason: angles in a triangle." Step 2: "S is on the line QR, so angle PSQ and angle PSR sit on a straight line: 180 − 75 = 105." Step 3: "Now triangle PSR has 105 and 30, so SPR is 180 − 135 = 45." Step 4 is a check: the whole angle at P (QPR) is 55 + 45 = 100, so triangle PQR has 50 + 100 + 30 = 180. Ask: "Is the drawing to scale?" (No: never measure. We use facts.)
