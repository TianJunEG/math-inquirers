Explore: tear it up

EXPLORE

  1. Draw any triangle and colour its three corners red, blue and green.
  2. Predict: what will happen when you tear off the three corners and put them side by side, points touching?
  3. Tear them off and fit them together. Now do it with a four-sided shape (four corners).
  4. Say what you notice. Does it work for every triangle?

Math Inquirers P5 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 12 to 24.5 (the whole explore; this slide 12 to 20.5). Children draw a triangle (any shape: one fat, one thin, one with a right angle is best), colour the corners and tear them off. They must predict first (thumbs: a straight line, a curve, a gap?). Then they fit the corners together with the points touching: the three corners make a straight line. Then the four-sided shape: the four corners fit round a point. Pair work is at most 6.5 minutes. Anyone who wants more (open to every pair): a different triangle. Is it still a straight line? Online: children hold the torn corners up to the camera. Protractor optional: measure the three corners of one triangle and add them up (they will be close to 180°, not exact: that is the nature of measuring). PROTECTED THINK TIME: before the pairs touch anything, say "Think for 30 seconds before anyone speaks" and show the clock; then pairs talk and start. PAIR WORK IS CAPPED AT 6.5 MINUTES: say "Pencils down" at minute 20.5 and bring the group back (the minutes before that include the 30 seconds of thinking, finding materials and, in lessons that use them, the move to and from breakout rooms). GO FURTHER (say it as soon as a pair has finished the task): any pair that finishes early may try the what-if question on the next slide. It is open to every pair, not a reward. Answer: 540°, which is one and a half turns (360° + 180°). A five-sided shape can be cut into 3 triangles from one corner, and 3 × 180° = 540°. A four-sided shape gives 2 triangles, 360°.
