Sneak-peek puzzles

Ideas we have not learnt yet. Guessing is welcome. "I do not know" is welcome. Nobody is marked.

1. The corner of your paper is a right angle, 90°. Draw a line from the corner so that it splits the right angle into two angles, and one of them is 30°. How big is the other angle?

60°

2. Two straight lines cross. One of the four angles is 38°. Find the angle next to it. Say which angle fact you used.

142°

Show your answer only to your tutor: on your own paper, or all hold paper to the camera together. Then a volunteer can tell us how they thought.

Minutes 0 to 3. SNEAK-PEEK PUZZLES. Show puzzle 1, give about a minute, click to show the short answer, then puzzle 2. Say it out loud: these are ideas we have not learnt yet; guessing is welcome; "I do not know" is welcome; nobody is marked. Puzzle 1 is an access puzzle: any child can try it with objects, drawing or counting, so everyone can start well. Puzzle 2 is a thinking puzzle. Answers are shown PRIVATELY (own paper shown only to you as you walk round; online, everyone holds paper to the camera at the same moment, or shows it to you only, on camera). Never boards in front of the group, never read out answers with names, no tally, no ranking. Never record scores. Answers and what to listen for. Q1: 60° (30° + 60° = 90°).; Q2: 142°. Angles on a straight line add up to 180°, and 180° − 38° = 142°. Minutes 0 to 3. SNEAK-PEEK PUZZLES. Show the two puzzles one at a time. Say: "These are ideas we have not learnt yet. Guessing is welcome and so is 'I do not know'. Nobody is marked." Puzzle 1 is one that any child can try with objects, drawing or counting, so everybody can start well. Puzzle 2 is a thinking puzzle. Then ask for VOLUNTEERS to say how they thought (put a hand up or use a reaction); never choose a child because you saw a wrong answer. Never record scores.
