Explore: talk about it

1. Where did the three corners of the triangle fit? What does that tell you?

2. Where did the four corners of the four-sided shape fit? What does that tell you?

3. Does it work for every triangle? How could you find out?

GO FURTHER: A SECOND WHAT-IF (OPTIONAL, FOR ANY PAIR)

Draw any five-sided shape and tear off its five corners. Fit them together. Do they fit on a straight line, or round a point, or neither? Predict first: how many degrees do the five corners make altogether?

Math Inquirers P5 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 14 to 21. Let pairs share, then collect. Answers: 1) The three corners make a straight line, so the angles of a triangle add up to 180°. 2) The four corners fit round a point, so the angles of a four-sided shape add up to 360°. 3) Test very different triangles: a fat one, a thin one, a right-angled one. It works every time. Say: "This is a fact we can use to FIND angles without measuring." Extension: why is it 360°? Draw a line from one corner to the opposite corner: two triangles, 2 × 180° = 360°. Do not teach the method for the worksheet yet: the next slides do that. GO FURTHER what-if (open to every pair from minute 9; nobody waits): 540°, which is one and a half turns (360° + 180°). A five-sided shape can be cut into 3 triangles from one corner, and 3 × 180° = 540°. A four-sided shape gives 2 triangles, 360°.
