Math Inquirers Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Optional slide for pairs who finish early or ask for more. Show it only on request and never announce who is working on it. Children write the answer on their own paper and say it together on three; nothing is held up to the camera. Answers and tutor lines are for you only. Item 1 (Divisibility: the digit sum test for 3; tool of the day idea: Spot the pattern). SAY: "Last lesson we looked at the digit sums of the 9 table. Do the same for the 3 table." ASK: "What do the digit sums have in common? Does the test work for 30? For 483?" LISTEN FOR: "The digit sum is in the 3 table". Check by dividing: 483 / 3. ANSWER: a) The digit sums are 3, 6, 9, 3, 6, 9, 3, 6, 9, 3: always a number from the 3 table. b) 483: 4 + 8 + 3 = 15, which is in the 3 table, so yes (3 x 161). 725: 7 + 2 + 5 = 14, which is not, so no. Item 2 (Divisibility: 2 and 3 means 6; tool of the day idea: Make a table). SAY: "List the first multiples of 2 and of 3 and circle the numbers in both." ASK: "What do 6, 12, 18 have in common? Why must a number in both tables be in the 6 table?" LISTEN FOR: "Even and in the 3 table". ANSWER: a) The 6 table. b) 534 is even and its digit sum is 12, so yes (6 x 89). 357 is odd, so no.
