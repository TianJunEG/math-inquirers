Investigation: Is it always true? Digit sums

In the 9 times table the digits of each answer add up to 9: 9, 18 (1 + 8), 27 (2 + 7), 36 (3 + 6) ...

1. Carry on the 9 times table to 9 × 12. Add the digits of each answer. Is the total ALWAYS 9? Write what you find.

2. Try the 3 times table and the 6 times table up to × 12. Add the digits of each answer. What do you notice?

3. Try the 4 times table. Is it the same? What can you say about the 3, 6 and 9 times tables?

Easy first step: write the 9 times table up to 9 × 6 and add the digits of each answer.

DEBRIEF in talk time, minutes 72 to 75: ask for volunteers to share one thing they noticed (a pass is fine). Then show the model answer and ask "What stayed the same? What changed? Why?" Model answer and what to look for: 9 times table: the digit sum is 9 for 9 × 1 to 9 × 10 and for 9 × 12 = 108, but 9 × 11 = 99 gives 9 + 9 = 18. So 'always 9' is false, but 18 is also in the 9 times table (and 1 + 8 = 9 again). 3 and 6 times tables: every digit sum is in the 3 times table (3, 6, 9, 12, ...). 4 times table: digit sums 4, 8, 3, 7, 2, 6, 10 ... include numbers that are not in the 3 times table (4, 8), so it is not the same. Conclusion: the digit sum of any answer in the 3, 6 or 9 table is in the 3 times table; for the 9 table it is in the 9 times table. Look for: testing past × 10, finding the counterexample 99, and revising the claim instead of just saying 'no'. Do not expect a proof.
