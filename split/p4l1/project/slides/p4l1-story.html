Story break: Sophie Germain

France (Paris) • 1776-1831

Sophie Germain lived in Paris more than 200 years ago. As a teenager she read the books in her father's library and taught herself Latin and Greek so she could read even more. Her parents were not happy, but she kept learning. Later she wrote to famous mathematicians using a man's name, M. Le Blanc. The great mathematician Gauss praised her ideas about numbers, and later found out who she really was. Sophie loved prime numbers, which have exactly two factors: 1 and themselves. She studied primes p where 2 times p plus 1 is also prime. Today these are called Sophie Germain primes.

Link to today: Today you hunt for factors, and Sophie studied prime numbers, the numbers with only two factors. TRY IT ANSWER (tutor only, do not show): 2 x 7 + 1 = 15. The factors of 15 are 1, 3, 5 and 15, so 15 is not prime and 7 is not a Sophie Germain prime. For 5: 2 x 5 + 1 = 11, whose only factors are 1 and 11, so 11 is prime and 5 is a Sophie Germain prime. Everything in the story is true as far as historians know. Confidence: high. Dates for people from long ago are estimates.
