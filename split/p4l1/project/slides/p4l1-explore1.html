Explore: what to do

EXPLORE

  1. Take 24 square tiles.
  2. Use ALL the tiles to make a rectangle. Write it as a multiplication, like 2 × 12.
  3. Make as many different rectangles as you can. A 2 by 12 and a 12 by 2 count as the same.
  4. How do you know you have found them all?

Go further (any time): What if we use 36 tiles? What is special about one of the rectangles?

Math Inquirers P4 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 8 to 13. Pairs build rectangles with 24 tiles (online: paper squares or squared paper on camera). Ask for a prediction first: how many rectangles? Most guess too many or too few. Let them build in any order at first; the teaching part shows how to work in order. Go further (on the slide), for any child who wants it: do it again with 36 tiles. Something special happens (a 6 by 6 square). What is it? GO FURTHER (on the slide from minute 8, so nobody waits): What if we use 36 tiles? What is special about one of the rectangles? Answer: 5 rectangles: 1 × 36, 2 × 18, 3 × 12, 4 × 9 and 6 × 6. The 6 × 6 is a square: 6 is its own partner, so 36 has an odd number (9) of factors.
