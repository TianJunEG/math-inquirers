Investigation: A magic trick

A puzzle with more than one good answer. Share one way you tried it, or pass.

Think of a number. Add 5. Double it. Take away 10. Halve it.

(a) Try 5 different starting numbers. What do you notice about your final answer?

(b) Is it ALWAYS true? Try a big number and a small number, then say why.

(c) Make your own trick that always gives you back the number you started with. Test it on 3 numbers.

Easy first step: do the trick with 3, then with 10, and write down each step.

Math Inquirers P2 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 77 to 80 (3 minutes, after talk time). Investigation debrief. The Investigation is printed on the last core page of the workbook; children may have started it any time after question 3 (it is open to everyone), or it is take-home. Say to the whole group: "Who would like to share one way they tried the Investigation? Passing is fine." Ask for volunteers only; never name a child; no public tallies, scores or rankings. Several answers are right: praise the method (a list in order, a table, a drawing), not the speed. If nobody has started it, show the first step on the slide and let pairs try for one minute, then set it as take-home. Model answer and what to look for: (a) The final answer is always the number you started with (brute-forced for 1 to 999). (b) Adding 5 then doubling adds 10 and doubles the start; taking away 10 and halving undoes both: (n + 5) x 2 − 10 = n x 2, and half of that is n. A child reason: “double and halve undo each other, and add 5 doubled is 10, which I take away”. (c) Any valid trick, for example: add 3, double, take away 6, halve; or times 4, add 8, divide by 4, take away 2. Check by testing three numbers. Look for: testing small and big numbers, a reason in words, and a trick that really works.
