Story break: Mary Somerville

Optional • about 2 minutes • skip any time

Scotland (and later Italy) • 1780-1872

Mary Somerville grew up in Scotland about 240 years ago. As a girl, she often told how she found a puzzle page in a magazine. It was full of strange letters, mostly X and Y. A friend said it was a kind of arithmetic called algebra, but could not explain it. Mary wanted to know more, so she found books and taught herself. In 1831 she published a book that explained a hard book about the stars.

From her own memories, told in later accounts. The exact age is not certain.

Try it (optional)

Mary’s X was a mystery number. I double X, then take away 3, and the answer is 9. Work backwards to find X.

Say clearly that this is a story or a memory told long after the event: The magazine puzzle is an episode from her own memories, told in later accounts; the exact age is not certain. Why it is here: The X and Y in her magazine stood for mystery numbers, just like the unknown numbers you find by working backwards today. Then click for the Try it question. It is a choice, never a test: ask "Would anyone like to try this one?", take volunteers only, never name a child, nobody is marked, passing is always fine. Children can show the answer on their own paper to the tutor only, or say it to a partner. TRY-IT ANSWER (tutor only): X = 6. Undo −3: 9 + 3 = 12. Undo doubling: 12 ÷ 2 = 6. Check: 6 × 2 = 12 and 12 − 3 = 9. Keep it to 2 minutes, then say "Thank you. Water, then back to our seats." Source and checking notes: docs/stories/final_p2.json.
