Think harder: ages and the gap

1. Read each story. Draw a bar for each person.

a) Ali is 4 years older than his sister. Their ages add up to 20. How old is each?

b) Mum is 28 years older than Mei. Mei is 7 now. How old will each be in 5 years?

Optional slide for pairs who finish early or ask for more. Show it only on request and never announce who is working on it. Children write the answer on their own paper and say it together on three; nothing is held up to the camera. Answers and tutor lines are for you only. Item 1 (Ages: the gap never changes; tool of the day idea: Draw it). SAY: "For part a, take away the extra years first, then share what is left. The same take-away-the-extra idea as the worksheet." ASK: "Which person is older? By how many years? What do you do with the extra 4?" LISTEN FOR: "Both get older at the same speed, so the gap stays." For a), a Guess and check that tests both clues is also fine. ANSWER: a) Take away the 4 extra years: 16 left, shared equally is 8. Sister 8, Ali 12 (8 + 12 = 20). b) Mei will be 12; Mum is 35 now, so 40 in 5 years. The gap is still 28.
