Story break: Adolphe Quetelet

Adolphe Quetelet • 1796 to 1874 • Belgium

Adolphe Quetelet was born in 1796 in Ghent, now in Belgium. He began teaching maths at seventeen and earned a doctorate in maths when he was twenty-three. Later he went to Paris to learn astronomy and probability, and in 1832 he became director of the new observatory in Brussels. He also collected statistics about people. In 1835 he wrote about the average man, a middle value found from the measurements of a trait, with most people close to it. The average man was an idea made from many measurements, not a real person. Quetelet also helped organise the first International Statistical Congress in 1853, where people from many countries shared how they counted and compared data.

Try it (optional, no pressure)

Five children measure 138, 142, 145, 150 and 155 cm. Find the mean height. A sixth child of 164 cm joins. What is the new mean?

Read the story aloud, or ask a volunteer to read it (never pick a child). Who: A mathematician and astronomer who used averages to study people. Link to today: Quetelet used the mean, the value you get by adding all the data and dividing by how many there are, which is the topic today.
