1. Five numbers have a mean of 8. One number is removed and the mean of the other four is 7. (a) Which number was removed? (b) Ali says: “Taking a number out always makes the mean go down.” Is he right? Explain, using an example.

2. A basketball player’s mean score for 9 games is 30 points. (a) How many points must she score in the 10th game to raise her mean to 32? (b) The most she has ever scored in one game is 60. What is the highest mean she could have after game 10?

Math Inquirers P6 • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Use as a pair challenge or in talk time. Ask for the reason every time. More are in the question bank. Answers: 1) (a) 12 (5 × 8 = 40, 4 × 7 = 28, 40 − 28 = 12). (b) No. Removing a number above the mean (like 12) lowers the mean, but removing a number below the mean raises it. Example: 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 has mean 8; remove 4 and 6, 8, 10, 12 has mean 9. 2) (a) 50 (10 × 32 = 320, 9 × 30 = 270, 320 − 270 = 50). (b) 33: 270 + 60 = 330 and 330 ÷ 10 = 33.
