Math Inquirers P6 • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 12 to 26 (this slide 12 to 16.5). Everyone types the number of letters in their full name (no spaces) in the chat; copy the list onto the slide or a shared board. Each child estimates the mean, then works it out by hand (total ÷ how many). Scratch is NOT opened in this part: the list-and-loop program is built once, live, in the mean program slide of the mini-lesson, so nothing is built twice. Then the prediction: "Our tutor joins with a 30-letter name. Will the mean go up a little or a lot?" Add 30 to the list and work out the new mean by hand. Watch for children who divide by the wrong count or who add a value twice. Go further what-if (not a slide: paste it in the chat for any pair that finishes early, and take it up only if a pair wants to share): What if you add a very small value, 1, to the list instead of 30? Predict: does the mean go up or down? Does the median change? Test it and explain. Answer: The mean goes down (1 is below the other values, which are name lengths of about 8 or more). The median moves only to the next value along, so it changes by much less than the mean. One extreme value pulls the mean more than the median.
