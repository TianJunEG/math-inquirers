Go further

1. A number machine has two steps: add 3, then add 4. IN 5 gives OUT 12. What goes IN to give OUT 20? Can you make a one-step machine that does the same job?

2. A snail climbs 3 cm each day and slides back 1 cm each night. It starts at the bottom of a 9 cm wall. On which day does it reach the top?

Make up your own

3. Make up a mystery number story with THREE steps whose answer is 10. Write the story. Show how a partner can work backwards to find 10, and how they can check by going forwards.

Math Inquirers Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

For any pair that chooses Go further. Use it as a pair choice, or with volunteers in talk time (never call a child by name). Ask for the reason every time. Q3 is open: there is no single right answer, so ask children to check each other's work. Answers: 1) 13 (20 − 4 = 16, 16 − 3 = 13; check 13 + 3 + 4 = 20). A one-step machine that adds 7 does the same job. 2) Day 4 (3, 5, 7, then 9 on the fourth day before it can slide back). 3) Open. Check that the story gives 10 when done forwards and that the partner undoes the steps in the reverse order. Example: I think of a number, add 5, take away 3, add 4, and I get 16 (10 + 5 = 15, 15 − 3 = 12, 12 + 4 = 16).
