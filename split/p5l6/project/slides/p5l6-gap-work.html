Go further: Three workers

Tool: Make a table

Ali lays 10 bricks each minute and Ben lays 14 bricks each minute. They start together. After 5 minutes Kumar joins them and lays 6 bricks each minute.

(a) How many bricks do Ali and Ben lay in the first 5 minutes?

(b) They must lay 450 bricks. How many minutes do they work altogether?

(a) 120 bricks.

(b) 16 minutes.

ADDED ITEM work (a slide-only addition for the skill gap "Three workers"; it is not in the printed workbook). TOOL: Make a table. SAY: Make a table with two phases: before Kumar joins, and after. In each phase write how many bricks are laid each minute. ASK: How many bricks are left after the first phase? How many bricks each minute do all three lay together? LISTEN FOR: Phase 1: 24 bricks each minute for 5 minutes. Phase 2: 30 bricks each minute for the rest. 450 − 120 = 330, 330 ÷ 30 = 11. Listen for the two-phase table. Everyone writes the answer on their own paper, then say the answer together on three, then click the answer box. Nobody holds anything up, nobody is marked, and passing is always fine. ANSWER: (a) 120 bricks. (b) 16 minutes. WHY: First 5 minutes: (10 + 14) × 5 = 120. All three lay 10 + 14 + 6 = 30 each minute. 450 − 120 = 330 bricks left, 330 ÷ 30 = 11 minutes. In all 5 + 11 = 16 minutes. SAME SKILL, NEW NUMBERS (tutor bank, say it aloud or write it on the board): Ali lays 8 bricks each minute and Ben lays 12. After 3 minutes Kumar joins and lays 10 bricks each minute. How many minutes do they take to lay 300 bricks? Answer: 11 minutes.
