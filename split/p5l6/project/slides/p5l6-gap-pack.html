Go further: Totals you cannot make

Tool: Make a table

Cookies are sold in packs of 5 and packs of 8. Aisha may buy several packs of each kind, or none of one kind.

(a) Which totals from 1 to 20 can she buy?

(b) Find the greatest total she cannot buy.

(a) 5, 8, 10, 13, 15, 16, 18, 20.

(b) 27.

ADDED ITEM pack (a slide-only addition for the skill gap "Totals you cannot make"; it is not in the printed workbook). TOOL: Make a table. SAY: Write the numbers 1 to 40 in rows of 5. Mark the multiples of 8 and then everything you can reach by adding 5s. ASK: When you have 5 totals in a row that you can buy, what happens after that? Why? LISTEN FOR: Five in a row can be bought (28 to 32), so adding 5 reaches every larger total. 27 is the last gap. Listen for the argument "five in a row means all the rest". Everyone writes the answer on their own paper, then say the answer together on three, then click the answer box. Nobody holds anything up, nobody is marked, and passing is always fine. ANSWER: (a) 5, 8, 10, 13, 15, 16, 18, 20. (b) 27. WHY: Totals are 5a + 8b. The first run of 5 buyable totals in a row is 28, 29, 30, 31, 32 (28 = 5 × 4 + 8, 29 = 5 + 8 × 3, 30 = 5 × 6, 31 = 5 × 3 + 8 × 2, 32 = 8 × 4). Adding 5 to any of them reaches all totals after it, and 27 cannot be made. SAME SKILL, NEW NUMBERS (tutor bank, say it aloud or write it on the board): Stickers come in sheets of 4 and sheets of 7. Find the greatest number of stickers you cannot buy. Answer: 17.
