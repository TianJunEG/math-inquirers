Think harder: Equal ratios and scaling

Tool: Make a table

PREVIEW: a Primary 6 topic, beyond Level 5. A taste, not a test.

A rice recipe uses 2 cups of rice for every 5 cups of water. The ratio of rice to water is 2 : 5.

(a) How many cups of water go with 6 cups of rice?

(b) How many cups of rice go with 25 cups of water?

(c) Wei uses 12 cups of rice. How many cups of rice and water does he use altogether?

(a) 15 cups of water.

(b) 10 cups of rice.

(c) 12 + 30 = 42 cups altogether.

ADDED ITEM r5 (a slide-only addition for the skill gap "Equal ratios and scaling"; it is not in the printed workbook). TOOL: Make a table. PREVIEW LABEL: say "This is a first taste of a topic from a later level. We are not testing it; we are noticing how it works." SAY: Make a table: each column is the same recipe made again. Multiply both numbers by the same number. ASK: How many times bigger is 6 cups of rice than 2? What do you do to the water? How could you start from the water in (b)? LISTEN FOR: Scale factor: 6 ÷ 2 = 3, so water 5 × 3 = 15. In (b) 25 ÷ 5 = 5 and rice 2 × 5 = 10. In (c) 12 ÷ 2 = 6, water 5 × 6 = 30. A rate is a ratio in disguise: one number is "for every" the other. Everyone writes the answer on their own paper, then say the answer together on three, then click the answer box. Nobody holds anything up, nobody is marked, and passing is always fine. ANSWER: (a) 15 cups of water. (b) 10 cups of rice. (c) 12 + 30 = 42 cups altogether. WHY: The columns are multiples of the recipe: (2, 5), (4, 10), (6, 15), (8, 20), (10, 25), (12, 30). Altogether for 12 cups of rice: 12 + 30 = 42 cups. SAME SKILL, NEW NUMBERS (tutor bank, say it aloud or write it on the board): A drink uses 3 cups of juice for every 4 cups of water. How many cups of water go with 18 cups of juice? Answer: 24 cups.
