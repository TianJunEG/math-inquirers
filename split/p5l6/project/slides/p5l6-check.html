Sneak-peek puzzles

Ideas we have not learnt yet. Guessing is welcome. "I do not know" is welcome. Nobody is marked.

1. A tap puts 12 litres into a tank in 4 minutes. Draw 4 buckets and share the 12 litres. How many litres go in each minute? How many litres go in during 6 minutes?

3 litres a minute; 18 litres

2. Ali reads 12 pages in 4 minutes. Mei reads 20 pages in 5 minutes. Who reads faster? How can you be sure?

Mei: 4 pages a minute, Ali 3

Show your answer only to your tutor: on your own paper, or all hold paper to the camera together. Then a volunteer can tell us how they thought.

Minutes 0 to 3. SNEAK-PEEK PUZZLES. Show puzzle 1, give about a minute, click to show the short answer, then puzzle 2. Say it out loud: these are ideas we have not learnt yet; guessing is welcome; "I do not know" is welcome; nobody is marked. Puzzle 1 is an access puzzle: any child can try it with objects, drawing or counting, so everyone can start well. Puzzle 2 is a thinking puzzle. Answers are shown PRIVATELY (own paper shown only to you as you walk round; online, everyone holds paper to the camera at the same moment, or shows it to you only, on camera). Never boards in front of the group, never read out answers with names, no tally, no ranking. Never record scores. Answers and what to listen for. Q1: 3 litres each minute, so 18 litres in 6 minutes (6 × 3).; Q2: Mei. Ali reads 3 pages a minute (12 ÷ 4) and Mei reads 4 pages a minute (20 ÷ 5). Comparing the amount for 1 minute makes it fair. Minutes 0 to 3. SNEAK-PEEK PUZZLES. Show the two puzzles one at a time. Say: "These are ideas we have not learnt yet. Guessing is welcome and so is 'I do not know'. Nobody is marked." Puzzle 1 is one that any child can try with objects, drawing or counting, so everybody can start well. Puzzle 2 is a thinking puzzle. Answers are shown PRIVATELY, never on boards in front of the group. Face to face: children write on their own paper and show it only to you as you walk round. Online: everyone holds their paper up to the camera at the same moment, or shows it to you only, on camera. Do not read answers out with names, do not keep a tally, do not rank. Then ask for VOLUNTEERS to say how they thought (put a hand up or use a reaction); never choose a child because you saw a wrong answer. Never record scores. Choosing a start: every child has the same neutral line on workbook page 1: "After question 3 you may choose: carry on, or try Think harder." (The number is the last question on page 1 of this lesson.) Invite the WHOLE group, never one child: "when you have finished Start here you can choose Think harder, Go further or the Investigation." Never single out a child by name and never announce who is on which page. Pairs are not split unless BOTH children agree. Any child may swap at any time.
