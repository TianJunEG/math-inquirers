Worked example: taps working together

A tank holds 180 ℓ. Tap A fills 9 ℓ a minute and tap B fills 6 ℓ a minute. How long do both take? Then a crack lets out 3 ℓ a minute: how long now?

Tap A
+ 9 ℓ a minute
Tap B
+ 6 ℓ a minute
Together
15 ℓ a minute
Crack
− 3 ℓ a minute
Stays in
12 ℓ a minute

Step 1. Together the rates ADD: 9 + 6 = 15 ℓ every minute. 180 ÷ 15 = 12 minutes.

Step 2. The crack takes its rate AWAY: 15 − 3 = 12 ℓ stay in each minute.

Step 3. 180 ÷ 12 = 15 minutes. Check: 15 × 12 = 180 ✓

Math Inquirers P5 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Click to build the table row by row. Think-aloud: "Every minute tap A puts in 9 and tap B puts in 6, so 15 go in every minute. How many 15s make 180? 12 minutes. The crack lets out 3 every minute, so only 12 stay. How many 12s make 180? 15 minutes." Stress: we add RATES (litres a minute), never times. A common mistake is to say "A takes 20 minutes and B takes 30, so together 50". Together must be faster than either tap alone. Ask: "Is 12 minutes sensible? Tap A alone would need 180 / 9 = 20 minutes, so 12 is faster, good."
