Think harder: Catching up

Tool: Make a table

Ali walks 60 m each minute. Ben starts from the same place 5 minutes later and walks 80 m each minute in the same direction.

(a) How far ahead is Ali when Ben starts?

(b) How many metres does Ben gain on Ali each minute?

(c) How many minutes after Ben starts does he catch Ali?

(a) 300 m.

(b) 20 m.

(c) 15 minutes.

ADDED ITEM catch (a slide-only addition for the skill gap "Catching up"; it is not in the printed workbook). TOOL: Make a table. SAY: Make a table with the minutes since Ben started. Write how far each of them has walked, and the gap between them. ASK: What happens to the gap each minute? How can you get from 300 m to no gap without listing every minute? LISTEN FOR: The gap shrinks by 20 m every minute. 300 ÷ 20 = 15. Listen for "the gap closes by the difference in speeds" (this is a preview of an idea met again in later years). Everyone writes the answer on their own paper, then say the answer together on three, then click the answer box. Nobody holds anything up, nobody is marked, and passing is always fine. ANSWER: (a) 300 m. (b) 20 m. (c) 15 minutes. WHY: Ali has walked 60 × 5 = 300 m. Each minute Ben walks 20 m more than Ali, so the gap shrinks by 20 m. 300 ÷ 20 = 15 minutes. Check: Ben walks 80 × 15 = 1 200 m and Ali walks 60 × 20 = 1 200 m. SAME SKILL, NEW NUMBERS (tutor bank, say it aloud or write it on the board): Mei walks 50 m each minute. Kumar starts 4 minutes later and walks 70 m each minute. After how many minutes does Kumar catch Mei? Answer: 10 minutes.
