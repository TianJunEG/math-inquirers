Story break: Mary Jackson

1921–2005 • United States • A mathematician who became NASA's first Black female engineer and studied air moving at great speed.

Mary Jackson was born in 1921 in Hampton, Virginia. She studied mathematics and physical science at Hampton Institute and finished in 1942. In 1951 she joined the NACA laboratory at Langley, where she worked as a human computer in West Area Computing, led by Dorothy Vaughan. Later she worked with engineer Kazimierz Czarnecki in the Supersonic Pressure Tunnel, a wind tunnel that blew air over model aircraft almost twice as fast as sound travels. He encouraged her to train as an engineer. After finishing graduate-level courses, she became an aerospace engineer in 1958. In 1979 she moved to a role helping women build careers at Langley, and she retired in 1985.
