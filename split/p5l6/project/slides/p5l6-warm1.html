50¢ each vs 40¢ each: the 10-pack

Ali needs 4 pens. Mei needs 10 pens. What is the price of ONE pen each time? Which pack is the better buy?

Math Inquirers P5 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 6 to 8.5. Better Buy Showdown, whole numbers only so that everyone can start. Children point left or right (online: hold up a left or right hand), then you click to show the proof. Pens: 4 for $2.00 is 50 cents each; 10 for $4.00 is 40 cents each, so the 10-pack. Then the two questions: Ali needs only 4 pens, so he buys the 4-pack ($2.00 against $4.00 for 10); the price of one is the fair way to compare. TUTOR EXTENSION, only if the group is ready (these need decimal division): Juice: $3.00 / 1.5 = $2.00 a litre; $3.80 / 2 = $1.90 a litre, so the 2 litre bottle. Eggs: $3.20 / 10 = 32 cents each; $3.96 / 12 = 33 cents each, so the 10-pack (the bigger pack is NOT cheaper per egg). Ask: "Why is it fair to compare the price of ONE?" Watch for children who compare the pack prices ($2.40 and $3.80). Do not say "the bigger pack is always better": the eggs show it is not.
