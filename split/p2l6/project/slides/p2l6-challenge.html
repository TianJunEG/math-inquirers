1. Ali has 10 coins. Every coin is a 10¢ or a 20¢ coin. They could be worth $1.50: how many of each is that? Write ALL the totals his 10 coins could be worth. Could they be worth $1.55?

2. Mei has $1. At the snack stall: chips 45¢, sweet 15¢, jelly 25¢, biscuit 30¢, lollipop 20¢. She wants as many DIFFERENT snacks as she can. Which should she buy?

Optional, if you finish early (no clock time). Pairs try first, then the "see it" slide, then the walk-through. Answers: 1) Five 10¢ and five 20¢ (50¢ + 100¢ = 150¢). The totals are $1.00, $1.10, … $2.00 (11 totals, going up 10¢ at a time); $1.55 is not possible because every total is a multiple of 10¢. 2) Sweet, lollipop, jelly and biscuit: 15 + 20 + 25 + 30 = 90¢, with 10¢ change. All five cost $1.35, too much, and any 4 snacks with the chips cost at least $1.05.
