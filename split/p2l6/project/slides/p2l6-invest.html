77–80 MIN

Investigation: Most coins

A puzzle with more than one good answer. Share one way you tried it, or pass.

Use only 5¢, 10¢, 20¢ and 50¢ coins.

(a) Pay 35¢ using the fewest coins. How many coins is that?

(b) Try the other amounts from 5¢ to 95¢ (in steps of 5¢) and make a table of how many coins each needs.

(c) Which amount needs the MOST coins, even when you use the fewest possible? Needs: play coins (5¢, 10¢, 20¢, 50¢), or paper and a pencil.

Easy first step: pay 35¢ with the fewest coins from the 5¢, 10¢, 20¢ and 50¢ coins.

Math Inquirers P2 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 77 to 80 (3 minutes, after talk time). Investigation debrief. The Investigation is printed on the last core page of the workbook; children may have started it any time after question 3 (it is open to everyone), or it is take-home. Say to the whole group: "Who would like to share one way they tried the Investigation? Passing is fine." Ask for volunteers only; never name a child; no public tallies, scores or rankings. Several answers are right: praise the method (a list in order, a table, a drawing), not the speed. If nobody has started it, show the first step on the slide and let pairs try for one minute, then set it as take-home. Model answer and what to look for: Fewest coins (5¢ to 95¢): 5:1, 10:1, 15:2, 20:1, 25:2, 30:2, 35:3, 40:2, 45:3, 50:1, 55:2, 60:2, 65:3, 70:2, 75:3, 80:3, 85:4, 90:3, 95:4 (brute-forced). (a) 35¢ = 20¢ + 10¢ + 5¢, 3 coins. (c) 85¢ and 95¢ need 4 coins each: 85¢ = 50¢ + 20¢ + 10¢ + 5¢ and 95¢ = 50¢ + 20¢ + 20¢ + 5¢. An amount ending in 5 needs a 5¢ coin, and the rest then needs 3 more coins. Look for: a systematic table, trying biggest coin first, and checking that no smaller number of coins works.
