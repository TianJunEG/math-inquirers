Story break: Katherine Johnson

USA (West Virginia and Virginia) • 1918-2020

Katherine Johnson grew up in West Virginia in the USA. She was so quick at school that she was moved up several grades. She became a research mathematician at NASA, where in the early days people who did calculations were called computers. She worked out the flight path for Alan Shepard's 1961 flight, the first American in space. Before John Glenn orbited the Earth in 1962, accounts say he asked for Katherine to check the electronic computer's numbers by hand. Her answers matched. Checking an answer a second way is a habit every mathematician uses. She received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2015.

Check that 6 × 48 = 288 using a different method from your first one. Hint: 48 is close to 50.

STORY BREAK, optional, about 2 minutes (minutes 32 to 34, after the movement part of the brain break). SKIP IT if you are short of time or the group is restless: say "We will keep this story for another day" and go straight to the worksheet; nothing later depends on it. To run it: read the story aloud, slowly, or ask one volunteer to read it (nobody has to; pass is fine). Then read the Try it question; children may answer in the chat or hold up a thumb, nobody is marked, and you never name or rank a child. Link to today: Today you use add, subtract, multiply and divide, and Katherine's job was to get these calculations right and to check them. TRY IT ANSWER (tutor only, do not show): 6 x 50 = 300, and 6 x 2 = 12, so 6 x 48 = 300 - 12 = 288. It checks out. You can also check with division: 288 divided by 6 = 48. LEGEND LABEL: the slide says "accounts say" on purpose. Accounts say John Glenn asked for her to check the numbers. The exact timing is uncertain and the film Hidden Figures dramatised the scene, so do not describe it as in the film. Everything in the story is true as far as historians know. Confidence: high. Dates for people from long ago are estimates.
