Go further: Adding a list by pairing

Tool: Try something simpler

Here is a list: 11, 14, 17, 20, ... , 41. The numbers go up by 3 each time.

(a) How many numbers are in the list?

(b) Find the sum of all the numbers.

(a) 11 numbers.

(b) 286.

ADDED ITEM pair (a slide-only addition for the skill gap "Adding a list by pairing"; it is not in the printed workbook). TOOL: Try something simpler. SAY: Try something simpler: write the first and last number. Add them. Then the second and the second last. What do you see? ASK: What is the same about every pair? How many pairs are there, and what is left over in the middle? LISTEN FOR: Every pair adds to 52 (11 + 41, 14 + 38, ...). 5 pairs and a middle number 26. Listen for "first plus last, second plus second last". Everyone writes the answer on their own paper, then say the answer together on three, then click the answer box. Nobody holds anything up, nobody is marked, and passing is always fine. ANSWER: (a) 11 numbers. (b) 286. WHY: (41 - 11) / 3 + 1 = 11 numbers. Five pairs of 52 make 260 and the middle number is 26, so the sum is 286. Check: 11 x 52 / 2 = 286. SAME SKILL, NEW NUMBERS (tutor bank, say it aloud or write it on the board): Find the sum of 5, 9, 13, ... , 49. Answer: 324 (12 numbers, 6 pairs of 54).
