Sneak-peek puzzles

Ideas we have not learnt yet. A guess is a great start. Your own paper: say it together on three.

Anyone can try this one: use counters, draw or count

1. Beads repeat: red, blue, blue, red, blue, blue ... Draw or thread the next 3 beads.

Red, blue, blue

Now think about this one

2. Same pattern. What colour is the 12th bead? How do you know?

Blue: 12 ends a repeat

Math Inquirers Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 8 to 12 (4 minutes). SAY (about 26 words, once): "Two sneak-peek puzzles: ideas we have not learnt yet. A guess is a great start, and I do not know is welcome. Nobody is marked." Question 1 is an access question: every child can try it with objects, a drawing or counting, so everyone starts with a win. Question 2 makes you think. Children try both on their own paper (about a minute each). Then everyone answers ONCE, after the second question: face to face, each child keeps the paper low so only the tutor sees it; online, children say the answers together on three (nothing is held up to the camera). There are no private chat messages. Never use boards or paper held up in front of the group or to the camera, and never read out or compare anyone's answer. Do not write scores down or announce them. Then click to show each answer to the whole group and say "Lots of us had a different idea, and that is fine." Look for good ideas to praise in a later lesson; do not keep a list of who found question 2 easy. When the worksheet starts (minute 41) invite the WHOLE group (“When you have finished Start here, you can choose Think harder, Go further or the Investigation”), never one child by name. Everyone joins Explore and the Mini-lesson. Answers: 1) Red, blue, blue. The pattern is red, blue, blue again and again. 2) Blue. The pattern repeats every 3 beads, so the 3rd, 6th, 9th and 12th beads end a repeat, and each repeat ends with blue.
