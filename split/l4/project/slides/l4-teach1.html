Find the core

A repeating pattern has a core: the part that repeats.

Every core ends on 3, 6, 9 … : the numbers we say when we count in 3s. The bead there is always blue, the last bead of the core.

Math Inquirers Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Teach. Lay out real beads or cubes if you have them. Ask: "Which part keeps repeating?" Click 1: brackets around each core. "The core is red, blue, blue. It has 3 beads." Click 2: ring places 3, 6 and 9. Ask: "What do you notice about these numbers?" (we say them when we count in 3s). Click 3: the rule. Think-aloud: "I look for the part that repeats. That part is the core: red, blue, blue. It has 3 beads, so every core ends on a number I say when I count in 3s: 3, 6, 9, 12 … The bead there is always the last bead of the core, blue." Do not use groups or sharing language; children just count in 3s.
