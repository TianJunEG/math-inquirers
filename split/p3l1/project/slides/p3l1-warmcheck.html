Puzzle 1. Take the digit cards 4, 0 and 9. Make the biggest 3-digit number you can. Move the cards around to try.

Try it with objects, a drawing or counting.

Puzzle 2. Now use the cards 4, 0, 9 and 3, each once. What is the smallest ODD 4-digit number? Say how you know.

Math Inquirers P3 Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 2 to 5. Sneak-peek puzzles: two small puzzles on ideas we have not learnt yet. SAY: "These are ideas we have not learnt yet. Guessing is welcome and so is I do not know. Nobody is marked." Puzzle 1 is an ACCESS puzzle: any child can try it with objects, a drawing or counting, so everyone has a first win. Puzzle 2 is a thinking puzzle. Children write answers on their own paper and show them to the tutor on camera in the open group call, or keep them to share if they want (or everybody holds paper to the camera at the same moment, so nobody is singled out). The call is not private: no private messages, no photos. Never answers read out, never "who got it?". Do not show the answers on the slide now. Puzzle 1 answer (tutor only): 940. Listen for: Biggest digit first, then the next biggest: 9, 4, 0. Puzzle 2 answer (tutor only): 3049. Listen for: Odd means the ones digit is 9 or 3. 0 cannot go first. Try 3049 and 4093: 3049 is smaller. Come back to puzzle 2 at talk time: "Now we know the idea, how could it help?" (volunteers only). Invite the WHOLE group once, never one child; this is an invitation, not a ranking, and any child may choose Think harder or the Investigation once page 1 and Q4 and Q5 of page 2 are done. Never announce who is on which page, and never split pairs unless both children agree.
