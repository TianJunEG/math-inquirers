Explore: what to do

EXPLORE

  1. Take 20 cubes. Build two towers. One tower must have 4 more cubes than the other.
  2. Stand the towers side by side. How many cubes are in each?
  3. Use 20 cubes again: now one tower must be 3 times as tall as the other.
  4. Draw each pair of towers as two bars on your whiteboard.

Go further, any time: Make 3 towers from 30 cubes, each 2 cubes taller than the last. How tall is each? (Then 3 taller.)

Math Inquirers Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 10 to 15. Pairs work with Multilink cubes (about 30 per pair). Online: Lego bricks, coins in two piles, or paper strips. Let them try and adjust (move a cube from one tower to the other). Do not show the take-away-the-extra method yet: listen for pairs who find it. Ready for more: 30 cubes where one tower is 4 times as tall (6 and 24). GO FURTHER (on the slide from minute 10, so nobody waits): Make 3 towers from 30 cubes, each 2 cubes taller than the last. How tall is each? (Then 3 taller.) Answer: 30 cubes: 8, 10, 12 (3 towers, take away 2 + 4 = 6, share 24 into 3). With 3 taller each time: 7, 10, 13 (30 - 9 = 21, 21 / 3 = 7). Any pair may choose it as soon as their first task is done; do not say who is ready for it.
