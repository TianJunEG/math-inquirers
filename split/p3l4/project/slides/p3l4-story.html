Story break: Bhaskara II

India • 1114 to 1185

Bhaskara lived in India about 900 years ago. He wrote a maths book called the Lilavati. It has many word problems, with kings, elephants and pearl necklaces in them. A story often told says the book was named after his daughter. Historians have no proof that this story is true, so it is a legend. What we do know is that Bhaskara thought maths problems could be told as little tales, and that is a good way to learn.

TRY IT (OPTIONAL)

In the style of Bhaskara: a necklace has some pearls. Half of them fall on the floor, a quarter fall on the bed and 5 stay on the string. How many pearls were there? Draw a bar model.

ANSWER to the try-it (tutor only, do not show): 20 pearls. Half and a quarter make three quarters, so the 5 left is one quarter. One quarter is 5, so the whole is 4 x 5 = 20. Check: 10 + 5 + 5 = 20.
