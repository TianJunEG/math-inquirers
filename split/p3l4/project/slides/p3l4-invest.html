Investigation: What stays the same?

Mei is 13 years old and her brother is 5.

1. Fill in the table for the next 5 years.

2. What stays the same every year? What changes? How does it change?

3. Will Mei ever be exactly DOUBLE her brother's age? When? What about TRIPLE? Explain.

Easy first step: fill in the first row. In 1 year Mei is 14 and her brother is 6. What is the difference? A table for your answers is on the Investigation page of the workbook.

Math Inquirers Holiday Program • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Minutes 67 to 72: the Investigation has its OWN slot. Show this slide to the whole group and invite everybody: "You can choose Think harder, Go further or the Investigation now. Nobody has to." Do not say who is on which one. Not finished at 72? Take it home and bring one thing to share at the start of next lesson (volunteers only). DEBRIEF in talk time, minutes 72 to 75: ask for volunteers to share one thing they noticed (a pass is fine). Then show the model answer and ask "What stayed the same? What changed? Why?" Model answer and what to look for: Table (Mei, brother, difference, total): now 13, 5, 8, 18; 1: 14, 6, 8, 20; 2: 15, 7, 8, 22; 3: 16, 8, 8, 24; 4: 17, 9, 8, 26; 5: 18, 10, 8, 28. Same: the difference is always 8. Changes: both ages go up by 1 and the total goes up by 2 each year. Double: in 3 years (16 and 8). Mei is double when her brother's age equals the difference, 8. Triple: never again. She was triple last year (12 and 4), and after that the ratio only gets smaller. Look for: using the table, noticing the difference is constant, and a bar-model reason for 'double' (her brother's bar is the same length as the difference). Extension: when did she last have 3 times his age?
