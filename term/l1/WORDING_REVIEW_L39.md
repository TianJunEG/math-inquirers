# Wording review: Lesson 39 (The Problem-Solving Toolbox)

Every child-facing sentence (slides, workbook, exit ticket, home sheet, online practice, kit cards, parent page) was read aloud against WORDING_GUIDE rules 1 to 25 and `docs/books/p1_school_paper_patterns.md` (sections 3 and 6.5 for money and the process-skill items).

- Commands: Use a strip; Do one step at a time; Make a table; Work backwards; Find one first; Use a list (Start here only, one tool name at the start of the stem); Circle the tool that fits best; Circle Yes or No; Circle True or False; Fill in the table; Use the box to show your thinking. Same command for the same job every time (rules 2, 14, 23). The tool names are the lesson's own words and are taught in Explore; Think harder and Go further do not name a tool (rule 24: the tool command is a choice of method, not a lesson sentence inside a question, and it is dropped where the lesson asks the child to choose).
- Fixed question forms: 'Mei has [] more ...'; 'They have [] marbles in all.'; 'How many more ... than ...?'; 'How many ... in all?'; 'How much do 8 buns cost?'; 'Ravi's number is [] .' (rules 12, 16, 17). Answer frames end with the noun or unit; the dollar sign goes before the box ('$[]'); cents go after ('[]¢') (rule 16; papers section 3.2).
- Money: 5¢, 10¢, 20¢, 50¢, $10 note, $15, $20 as an amount (not a note), no $1.50, no mixed dollars and cents in one amount, 'fewest' not used (P1 paper patterns; no $20 note appears: the only note is $10).
- Names: Ali, Mei, Siti, Ben, Kumar, Aisha, Ravi, Lily, Jamie, Jenny, Jason, Amelia, Wei, all from the wording guide cast. The first draft used Pip, Rae, Jo and Kit (from the pre-flight spec); they are not in the guide's cast and were replaced. Wrong working belongs to 'a child in another class' (E3 and the Talk question); no cast name is given a wrong answer (rule 18, D18).
- Numbers are digits in every story (rule 15). Number words appear only in the tutor's read-aloud lines. Ordinals are '3rd', '4th', '6th', '7th' with the digit.
- Read-aloud test (rule 7): every question was read in a calm voice. 'Ravi is 3rd in a line of 7 children. How many children are behind Ravi?' and 'Siti is 4th from the front of a line. She is also 3rd from the back.' read cleanly. 'Road 2, the table of tries. Jason 8 and Jenny 24 make 32 in all. ...' is the longest child sentence group (about 35 words); it is split into short sentences and the tutor reads it aloud.
- Stems stay short: at most three short sentences before the question; the longest Start here stem is S6 (three coins and one instruction). Hints carry the method; no hint is printed in a stem (rule 18).
- No hidden second question (rule 4): every second question is a part (a), (b), (c), (d). The two-part 'I used ___ because ___' frame in G3 says 'Many answers are right.' (rule 6).
- Singapore English and context (rules 3, 22): pens, marbles, apples, buns, sweets, beads, tiles, stickers, rulers, coins, a $10 note; 'rubber'; 'maths' only on the tutor side. No brand, no fantasy.
- Kit cards: the short labels on the 8 problem cards ('1. Ali 9, Mei 4 fewer', '7. 20 sweets, 8 eaten, 5 bought') are memory aids, not full problems; the full problems are on the slide and read aloud. The tool cards read 'Strip', 'Table', 'List', 'Draw it', 'Work backwards', 'Find one first', 'Steps', the same words the lesson uses everywhere.
- Changed during review: the first draft's cast names (above); the Think harder choice list was cut to three tools so the words print on one line; 'Use a strip.' was removed from exit question E2 so the exit ticket does not name the tool it is not asking about; 'Compare two amounts, or find a total.' was shortened to fit its table cell; the online 'times as many' items changed from cards to shells because the first wording overlapped a school paper by seven words; problem cards were shortened to labels because the kit prints one line only.
- Open: no Primary 1 paper sets a 'which tool fits' question or a 'two ways' question, so those wordings ('Circle the tool that fits best', 'Do both roads give the same answer?') are marked unverified; the story items themselves follow school patterns.
