Explore: exactly $12

1Take the rubber, pencil, pen, glue, ruler and cup tags.

2Find two different tags that cost exactly $12 together.

3There are two answers. How do you know you have them all?

Math Inquirers Term 3 • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Round 1, about 5 minutes. Each child lays out six tags: rubber $2, pencil $3, pen $4, glue $5, ruler $7 and cup $8. They find two tags that cost exactly $12 together. There are two answers: pen $4 and cup $8, and glue $5 and ruler $7. Ask: how do you know you have them all? Model the way to be sure: start with the tag that costs the least and ask what partner it needs. Rubber $2 needs $10 (no tag); pencil $3 needs $9 (no tag); pen $4 needs $8 (the cup, yes); glue $5 needs $7 (the ruler, yes); the ruler and cup are already found. The prices are on the tags, so the slide shows no price. The answer appears on the next slide. A child with no tags writes the prices 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8 on paper. Answers: $4 and $8, $5 and $7.
