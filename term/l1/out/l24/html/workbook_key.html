Answer key: Lesson 4, Dollars and Change

For the tutor and grown-ups. Keep this page away from the child until they have finished and checked their own work. Ask how do you know? before saying right or wrong.

Start here (pages 1 and 2)
S1. Count the money. Fill in the blank.
Answer: 80¢
The coins are 50¢, 20¢, 5¢ and 5¢. 50 and 20 make 70. 70 and 5 make 75. 75 and 5 make 80. The total is 80¢.
Watch for: Writing 4 (counting the coins).; Writing 75 (leaving out the last 5¢ coin).
S2. Count the notes. Fill in the blank.
Answer: $19
The notes are $10, $5, $2 and $2. 10 and 5 make 15. 15 and 2 make 17. 17 and 2 make 19. The total is $19.
Watch for: Writing 4 (counting the notes).; Writing $17 (leaving out one $2 note).
S3. Look at the fifty-cent coin.
Answer: 5; 10
Five 10¢ coins make 50¢: 10, 20, 30, 40, 50. Ten 5¢ coins make 50¢: 5, 10, 15, up to 50.
Watch for: Writing 50 or 1 (the coin itself).; Writing 5 for the five-cent coins (the same as the ten-cent answer).
S4. Circle the coins that make 55¢.
Answer: 50¢ and 5¢
The 50¢ coin and one 5¢ coin make 55¢. 50 and 5 make 55. Either 5¢ coin may be circled.
Watch for: Circling the 20¢ coin first (it leaves 35¢ and the coins that are left cannot make it).; Circling every coin.
S5. Look at the two pieces of money.
Answer: $2 note
A two-dollar note is worth $2. A one-dollar coin is worth $1. 2 is greater than 1, so the $2 note is worth more.
Watch for: Circling the $1 coin because it is bigger (the value is the number, not the size).; Thinking they are worth the same.
S6. Ravi buys a toy car for $3. He pays with a five-dollar note. How much change does he get?
Answer: $2
Count up from $3 to $5: $4, $5. That is 2 dollars. The change is $2. Check: 3 and 2 make 5.
Watch for: Writing $5 (the note) or $3 (the price).; Writing $8 (adding).
Think harder (page 3)
T1. A ruler costs $4. A book costs $5. Mei pays with a ten-dollar note.
Answer: $9; $1
4 and 5 make 9, so the two things cost $9 together. Count up from $9 to $10: the change is $1. Check: 9 and 1 make 10.
Watch for: Writing $1 for the cost (giving the change first).; Writing $6 (10 minus 4).
T2. Ali makes 50¢ with ten-cent coins and twenty-cent coins. One way is shown. Find the other 2 ways.
Answer: 3 and 1; 1 and 2
Five 10¢ coins make 50¢ (shown). With one 20¢ coin, 30¢ is left, which is three 10¢ coins. With two 20¢ coins, 10¢ is left, which is one 10¢ coin. The two ways are 3 and 1, and 1 and 2, in either order.
Watch for: Writing a way that makes more or less than 50¢.; Using three 20¢ coins (60¢ is too much).
T3. Siti buys a pencil for 60¢. She pays with a one-dollar coin.
Answer: 100¢; 40¢
A one-dollar coin is worth 100¢. Count up from 60 to 100 in 10s: 70, 80, 90, 100. That is 40. The change is 40¢. Check: 60 and 40 make 100.
Watch for: Writing $1 or 1¢ for the coin.; Writing 60¢ or 160¢ for the change.
T4. Look at the three sets of coins.
Answer: Set A; 20¢
Set A is 50, 70, 80: 80¢. Set B is 20, 40, 60, 70: 70¢. Set C is 50, 55, 60: 60¢. Set A has the most money, even though Set B has the most coins. Set A has 20¢ more than Set C: 80 minus 60.
Watch for: Circling Set B because it has the most coins.; Writing 80¢ or 3¢ for the difference.
Go further (page 4)
G1. Use these notes and coins. You may use each one as many times as you like.
Answer: 5; 3
$73: $50 leaves $23; $10 and $10 leave $3; $2 and $1 leave nothing. That is $50, $10, $10, $2 and $1, which is 5 pieces. $50 minus $37 is $13: $10, $2 and $1, which is 3 pieces.
Watch for: Writing 12 (using $5 and $1 pieces only).; Using a $20 note (there is no $20 note).
G2. Mei has a $5 note, a $2 note and a $1 coin. She uses one, two or all three pieces.
Answer: 7; $1, $2, $3, $5, $6, $7, $8
One piece: $1, $2, $5. Two pieces: $1 and $2 make $3, $1 and $5 make $6, $2 and $5 make $7. All three: $8. That is 7 different amounts: $1, $2, $3, $5, $6, $7, $8.
Watch for: Writing 3 (only one piece at a time).; Forgetting the three-piece amount, $8, or writing $4 (which cannot be made).
G3. A sticker costs 30¢. Ben has a one-dollar coin.
Answer: 3; 10¢
30, 60 and 90 are three stickers, 90¢. Four stickers would cost 120¢, which is more than 100¢. Ben buys 3 stickers. Count up from 90¢ to 100¢: the change is 10¢.
Watch for: Writing 4 (120¢ is more than he has).; Writing 70¢ (100 minus 30, for one sticker).
Investigation (page 5): Coin detective
First: any way that makes 35¢, for example 20¢, 10¢ and 5¢. Exactly 3 coins: one way only, 20¢, 10¢ and 5¢. Exactly 4 coins: two ways, 10¢, 10¢, 10¢ and 5¢; or 20¢, 5¢, 5¢ and 5¢. Two coins cannot make 35¢.
Look for: A child who tries a big coin first and then fills in the rest; a child who checks each way by counting back to 35; a child who finds the second 4-coin way by swapping a 20¢ coin for ten and ten, or three 5s. Credit any clear reason. Do not mark a child down for stopping at one way.
Exit ticket (page 5)
E1. Count the money. Fill in the blank.
Answer: 90¢
The coins are 50¢, 20¢, 10¢ and 10¢. 50 and 20 make 70. 70 and 10 make 80. 80 and 10 make 90. The total is 90¢.
Watch for: Writing 4 (counting the coins).; Writing 80 (leaving out one 10¢ coin).
E2. Mei pays $10 for a pen that costs $4. How much change does she get? Choose the correct answer. Write its number in the bracket.
Answer: (3) $6
Count up from $4 to $10: $5, $6, $7, $8, $9, $10. That is 6 dollars. The change is $6. Check: 4 and 6 make 10.
Watch for: Choosing (1) $4, the price.; Choosing (4) $14, which adds the price and the note.
E3. Ali has a $10 note, two $2 notes and one $1 coin. How much money does he have?
Answer: $15
Start with the biggest: $10. Two $2 notes make $4, so 10 and 4 make $14. Add the $1 coin: $15.
Watch for: Writing $14 (leaving out the $1 coin).; Writing 4 (counting the pieces).
