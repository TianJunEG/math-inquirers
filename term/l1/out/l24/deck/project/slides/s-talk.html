A CHILD SAYS

A fifty-cent coin is worth more than a one-dollar coin.

Is the child right? How could we check with our coins?

I think ... because ...

Math Inquirers Term 3 • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

One minute of talk to the grown-up next to you, or to your screen. Children test it with their coins: put a $1 token next to two 50¢ coins. Then volunteers first; a pass is always fine; never call a child by name to answer. Model answer: no. A one-dollar coin is 100 cents, and a fifty-cent coin is only 50 cents, half of a dollar. Two fifty-cent coins make a dollar. 50 is a bigger number than 1, but a cent is much smaller than a dollar. Repeat each child answer in your own words for the recording. The child who says it is in another class: no cast name is given the wrong answer.
