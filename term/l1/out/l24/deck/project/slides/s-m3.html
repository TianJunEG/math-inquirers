A dollar is 100¢

$1 = 100¢

Math Inquirers Term 3 • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

One dollar is the same as 100 cents. Two 50-cent coins make 100 cents. Ten 10-cent coins make 100 cents: count in tens to 100 with the children on their fingers (the kit has six 10-cent coins, so nobody needs ten); the ten coins and the sentence appear on the answer slide after the children have counted. So a one-dollar coin is worth the same as two fifty-cent coins or ten ten-cent coins. Children check on the table with their own coins: put two 50¢ coins next to a $1 token. A 50-cent coin is worth less than a one-dollar coin even though 50 is more than 1: the dollar sign and the cent sign are different units. Do not write $1.00 or 1.50 anywhere: we never use a decimal point for money in this lesson. The answer slide shows the ten coins: say 'two 50¢ coins make $1, ten 10¢ coins make $1' after the children have counted.
