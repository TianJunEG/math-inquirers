Answer key: Lesson 5, Price Tags: Compare and Choose

For the tutor and grown-ups. Keep this page away from the child until they have finished and checked their own work. Ask how do you know? before saying right or wrong.

Start here (pages 1 and 2)
S1. Look at the two price tags.
Answer: bag ($18 is more than $12)
The book costs $12 and the bag costs $18. 18 is greater than 12, so the bag costs more.
Watch for: Circling the first tag because it is on the left.; Choosing the item with the bigger picture.
S2. A cup costs $8. A bag costs $17. How much more does the bag cost than the cup?
Answer: $9
Count up from 8 to 10: 2. Then from 10 to 17: 7. 2 and 7 make 9. The bag costs $9 more than the cup.
Watch for: Adding 8 and 17 to get 25.; Writing 17 or 8, a price, instead of the gap.
S3. Siti buys a pen for $7 and a ball for $8.
Answer: $7 + $8 = $15; $15
7 and 8: 7 and 3 make 10, and 5 more makes 15. Siti pays $15 altogether.
Watch for: Finding the gap, 8 take away 7, which is 1.; Writing $78 by joining the digits.
S4. Look at the three price tags.
Answer: ball ($11 is the smallest)
The book costs $16, the ball costs $11 and the cup costs $13. In order, least first, they are 11, 13, 16. The ball costs the least.
Watch for: Choosing the one that costs the most.; Choosing the last tag in the row.
S5. A ball costs $13. Ravi pays with two ten-dollar notes. How much change does he get?
Answer: $7
Two ten-dollar notes make $20. Count up from 13 to 15: 2. Then from 15 to 20: 5. 2 and 5 make 7. Ravi gets $7 change.
Watch for: Adding 13 and 20.; Counting up only to 15 and writing 2.
S6. Mei wants a book that costs $16. She has $11. How much more money does she need?
Answer: $5
Count up from 11 to 16. That is 5 steps. Mei needs $5 more.
Watch for: Adding 11 and 16.; Writing 16, the price of the book.
Think harder (page 3)
T1. Look at the three price tags.
Answer: bag; $28
The bag costs $43, the book $26 and the ball $15. The bag costs the most and the ball costs the least. Count up from 15 to 43: 15 to 20 is 5, 20 to 40 is 20, 40 to 43 is 3. 5 and 20 and 3 make 28.
Watch for: Finding the gap between the bag and the book, which is 17.; Adding the three prices.
T2. A glue stick and a cup cost $16 together. The cup costs $6 more than the glue stick.
Answer: $5; $11
Try $5 for the glue stick: the cup is 5 and 6 more, which is 11. 5 and 11 make 16. So the glue stick costs $5 and the cup costs $11.
Watch for: Giving 16 and 6 as the two prices.; Halving 16 to get 8 and 8 and stopping.
T3. A toy car costs $27. A ball costs $18.
Answer: $45; $23; No
27 and 18 make 45. For the change, count up from 27 to 30: 3. Then from 30 to 50: 20. 3 and 20 make 23. 45 is more than 40, so $40 is not enough.
Watch for: Subtracting 50 take away 27 and getting 33.; Saying Yes because 40 is a big number.
T4. Look at the five price tags. Ben spends all his money each time. There are two ways each time.
Answer: $3 and $8; $4 and $7. $2, $4 and $8; $3, $4 and $7
For $11: $2 needs 9 (no tag), $3 needs 8 (the cup, yes), $4 needs 7 (the ruler, yes). For $14 with three tags: $2 and $4 need 8 (the cup, yes); $3 and $4 need 7 (the ruler, yes). Any order is fine.
Watch for: Stopping after the first way.; Using the same tag twice.
Go further (page 4)
G1. The Hidden Price. A cake costs $12 more than a pie. A cake and a pie cost $52 together.
Answer: $20; $32
Take the extra $12 away: 52 take away 12 is 40. Share 40 into two equal parts: 20 and 20. The pie costs $20. The cake costs $12 more: $32. Check: 20 and 32 make 52.
Watch for: Writing 52 and 12 as the prices.; Halving 52 to get 26 and 26.
G2. A top costs $7 more than a kite. A doll costs $5 less than the top. The kite, the top and the doll cost $33 together.
Answer: $8; $15; $10
Try $5 for the kite: the top is 12, the doll is 7 and the three make 24. Too small. Try $8: the top is 15, the doll is 10 and 8 and 15 and 10 make 33.
Watch for: Giving 33 divided into three equal parts, 11 each.; Making the doll cost $5 more instead of $5 less.
G3. Mei has $100. She buys a bag for $27 and a toy car for $16.
Answer: $43; $57; $11
27 and 16 make 43. Count up from 43 to 50: 7. Then from 50 to 100: 50. 7 and 50 make 57. The bag costs 27 take away 16, which is 11, more than the toy car.
Watch for: Subtracting only the bag: 100 take away 27.; Writing 43 as the change.
Investigation (page 5): Mind the gap
First: $7 (3 and 4 more is 7). There are 3 pairs: $3 and $7, $7 and $11, $8 and $12. Check each: 7 take away 3 is 4; 11 take away 7 is 4; 12 take away 8 is 4. No other pair has a gap of exactly 4.
Look for: A child who picks a tag and counts up 4 to see if the new price is on a tag; a child who finds all 3 pairs by working through the tags in order; a child who notices that $7 is in two pairs. Credit any clear method. Do not mark a child down for stopping at one or two pairs.
Exit ticket (page 5)
E1. A hat costs $14. A scarf costs $5. How much more does the hat cost than the scarf?
Answer: $9
Count up from 5 to 14: 5 up to 10 is 5, and 10 up to 14 is 4. 5 and 4 make 9.
Watch for: Adding 14 and 5 to get 19.; Writing 14, the price of the hat.
E2. Ali buys a book for $8 and a pen for $5. How much does he pay altogether? Choose the correct answer. Write its number in the bracket.
Answer: (3) $13
Altogether means add. 8 and 5 make 13.
Watch for: Choosing (1) $3, which is the gap.; Choosing (2) $12 or (4) $14 after a counting slip.
E3. A cap costs $14. Ben gives the cashier $20. How much change does he get?
Answer: $6
Count up from the price to the money paid: 14 up to 20 is 6. Ben gets $6 change.
Watch for: Adding 14 and 20.; Writing 14, the price of the cap.
