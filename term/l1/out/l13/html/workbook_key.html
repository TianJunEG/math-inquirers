Answer key: Lesson 3, Doubles and Near Doubles

For the tutor and grown-ups. Keep this page away from the child until they have finished and checked their own work. Ask how do you know? before saying right or wrong.

Start here (pages 1 and 2)
S1. Look at the dominoes. Fill in the missing numbers.
Answer: 6, 8, 10
Each domino has two halves with the same number of dots. 3 + 3 = 6. 4 + 4 = 8. 5 + 5 = 10.
Watch for: Counting only one half of the domino.; Writing 5 + 5 = 55 (joining the digits).
S2. Mei has 6 red counters and 6 yellow counters. Fill in the missing numbers.
Answer: 6 + 6 = 12; double 6 is 12
The first frame has 6 red counters and the second has 6 yellow counters. 6 + 6 = 12, so double 6 is 12.
Watch for: Counting only one frame (6).; Writing 66.
S3. Fill in the missing numbers.
Answer: double 2 is 4; double 5 is 10; double 7 is 14; double 9 is 18
Double 2 is 2 + 2 = 4. Double 5 is 5 + 5 = 10. Double 7 is 7 + 7 = 14. Double 9 is 9 + 9 = 18.
Watch for: Writing the number again (double 7 is 7).; Adding 2 to the number (double 7 is 9).
S4. Look at the bars. Fill in the missing numbers.
Answer: 3 tens + 3 tens = 6 tens; 30 + 30 = 60; double 40 is 80
3 tens and 3 tens make 6 tens. 6 tens is 60. Double 4 tens is 8 tens, which is 80.
Watch for: Writing 6 for 30 + 30.; Writing 33 or 303.
S5. Ali has 8 stickers. Ben has 8 stickers. How many stickers do they have altogether?
Answer: 8 + 8 = 16; they have 16 stickers
Ali has 8 and Ben has 8. 8 + 8 = 16. They have 16 stickers altogether.
Watch for: Writing 8 (only one child).; Writing 88.
S6. Read each statement. Decide if it is True or False.
Answer: True, False, True, True
Double 4 is 8: True. Double 6 is 12, not 14: False. 9 + 9 is two of the same number, so it is a double: True. Double 30 is 60: True.
Watch for: Marking Double 6 is 14 as true because 6 + 6 is close to 14.; Saying 9 + 9 is not a double because 9 is a big number.
Think harder (page 3)
T1. Look at the counters. The ringed counter is 1 more. Fill in the missing numbers.
Answer: double 5 is 10; 5 + 6 = 11
5 red counters and 5 yellow counters make double 5 = 10. The ringed yellow counter is 1 more, so 5 + 6 = 10 + 1 = 11.
Watch for: Writing 10 for both parts.; Adding 2 instead of 1.
T2. Add.
Answer: 7, 15, 21, 5
3 + 4: double 3 is 6, and 1 more is 7. 8 + 7: double 7 is 14, and 1 more is 15. 10 + 11: double 10 is 20, and 1 more is 21. 2 + 3: double 2 is 4, and 1 more is 5.
Watch for: Taking 1 away instead of adding 1 after the smaller double.; Writing the double itself (6 for 3 + 4).
T3. Which sum is not a near double? Write the number in the bracket.
Answer: (3) 5 + 8
6 and 7 are next to each other. 8 and 9 are next to each other. 9 and 10 are next to each other. 5 and 8 are not next to each other (they are 3 apart), so 5 + 8 is not a near double.
Watch for: Choosing 9 + 10 because 10 looks different.; Looking at the total instead of the two numbers.
T4. Kumar adds 8 + 9. Step 1: 8 + 8 = 16. Step 2: 16 - 1 = 15. Answer (a) and (b).
Answer: No; 8 + 9 = 17
9 is 1 more than 8, so the total is 1 more than 16: 16 + 1 = 17. Kumar took 1 away instead of adding 1.
Watch for: Saying Yes because 16 - 1 looks like a good step.; Writing 15 for the right answer.
Go further (page 4)
G1. Add 8 + 9 + 8 + 7 + 8. Show your working on the lines.
Answer: 40
The 9 is 1 more than 8 and the 7 is 1 less than 8, so they make up for each other. The five numbers are the same as five 8s: 8 + 8 + 8 + 8 + 8 = 40. Another way: 8 + 8 = 16, 9 + 7 = 16, and 16 + 16 + 8 = 40.
Watch for: Adding from left to right and slipping.; Leaving one number out.
G2. Ben says 9 + 10 + 11 is the same as 10 + 10 + 10. Answer (a) and (b). Show your working on the lines.
Answer: Yes; 19 + 20 + 21 = 60
9 is 1 less than 10 and 11 is 1 more than 10, so they make up for each other: 9 + 10 + 11 = 30 = 10 + 10 + 10. In the same way 19 + 20 + 21 is three 20s, which is 60.
Watch for: Saying No because the numbers look different.; Writing 50 for the three numbers.
G3. Find double 35. Fill in the missing numbers. Show your working on the lines.
Answer: 60, 10, 70
35 is 30 and 5. Double 30 is 60. Double 5 is 10. 60 + 10 = 70. So double 35 is 70.
Watch for: Writing 610 (joining 60 and 10).; Forgetting to double the 5.
Investigation (page 5): Make two groups the same
It works for 6 and 8 (both 7), and for 5 and 9 (both 7): the two numbers are 2 apart or 4 apart and the total is a double (14). It does not work for 7 and 8: the total is 15 and one dot is always left over.
Look for: A child who tries several pairs, moves dots, and notices that when the total is a double the two groups can be made the same, and that 7 and 8 always leave one dot over. Credit any clear reason. Do not use the words odd or even yet: that is the next lesson.
Exit ticket (page 5)
E1. Fill in the missing number.
Answer: 18
9 + 9 = 18. Move 1 from one 9 to the other: 10 and 8.
Watch for: Writing 9 or 11.; Writing 99.
E2. Fill in the missing number.
Answer: 60
Double 3 is 6, so double 3 tens is 6 tens: 60.
Watch for: Writing 6.; Writing 33 or 303.
E3. Add.
Answer: 17
Double 8 is 16 and 1 more is 17. Or double 9 is 18 and 1 less is 17.
Watch for: Writing 18 (the double of 9).; Writing 16.
