Draw and Measure: practice at home
NameDate

About 15 minutes, a few days after the lesson. Use your ruler and spare paper. A grown-up may read the questions aloud. Wrong answers are fine: they show what to try next.

1Start here
Draw a line 8 cm long on spare paper. Start it at the 0 mark of your ruler. Which mark does it end at?
2Start here
A pencil starts at the 0 mark and its tip is at the 17 mark. A crayon is 11 cm long. How many cm longer is the pencil?
3Start here
Measure a spoon, a toy and a crayon in whole cm. Write the 3 lengths in order, shortest first.
4Think harder
A line starts at the 4 mark and ends at the 15 mark. How long is the line? Write: I know because ...
5Go further
Draw a path with 3 straight pieces. The pieces are all different lengths. The whole path is 25 cm long. Write the 3 lengths. A grown-up checks with a ruler.
Answers for grown-ups

Please let your child try first, then check together. Ask how do you know? If your child is stuck, wait 10 seconds, then suggest drawing it.

1. It starts at 0 and ends at 8
A line 8 cm long, drawn from the 0 mark, ends at the 8 mark.
2. 6 cm
The pencil is 17 cm. 17 take away 11 is 6 (count on from 11 to 17: 6 jumps).
3. OPEN ANSWER, accept any three lengths, written from smallest to largest. A grown-up can check one by measuring again
Put one end at the 0 mark and read the mark at the other end. If the end is between two marks, write the closer one. Then put the numbers in order.
4. 11 cm (15 take away 4)
Count the jumps from 4 to 15: 11 jumps. Or take 4 from 15, which is 11. It is not 15 cm, because the line starts at the 4 mark.
5. OPEN ANSWER, accept any 3 different whole numbers that add up to 25, for example 6, 8 and 11 (6 + 8 + 11 = 25)
Choose three different numbers that add to 25, draw each piece with the 0 mark at the start, then measure and add to check.
