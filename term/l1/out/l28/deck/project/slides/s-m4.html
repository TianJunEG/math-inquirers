Why not 3 times?

3 + 3 = 6?

Two triangles need 5 sticks, not 6.

They share 1 stick.

Shared sticks are counted once.

Math Inquirers Term 3 • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

The usual wrong idea: 5 triangles need 5 times 3 sticks, which is 15. That counts the shared sticks twice. Show the picture of 2 triangles: 3 sticks and 3 sticks make 6, but the middle stick is shared, so only 5 sticks are really there. Ask: which stick is counted twice (the middle one). Say: the first triangle needs 3 sticks, and each new triangle needs only 2 more. Children who guessed 15 in Explore round 3 are welcome to say so; no name is used.
