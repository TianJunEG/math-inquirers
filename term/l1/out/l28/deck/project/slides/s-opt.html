Squares in a row: 1 square uses 4 sticks, 2 squares use 7, 3 squares use 10, 4 squares use 13, 5 squares use 16.

How many sticks for 9 squares?

Math Inquirers Term 3 • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Optional, only if you are on time. A table of squares in a row: 4, 7, 10, 13, 16, going up by 3 each time (one side is shared). Nine squares: 16 and 4 more squares of 3 sticks each is 16 + 12 = 28; or 3 times 9 is 27 and 1 more is 28. Take two answers; ask: how many more sticks does each new square need (3, one side is shared). Then ask: why 3 and not 4. This is the same move as the triangles. Take the idea, not the speed.
