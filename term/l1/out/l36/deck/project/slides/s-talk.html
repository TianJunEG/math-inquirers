A CHILD IN ANOTHER CLASS SAYS

Four friends shake hands.

Each one shakes hands with 3 others.

So there are 4 times 3 handshakes.

Do you agree? How could we check?

I think ... because ...

Math Inquirers Term 4 • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

One minute of talk to the grown-up next to you, or to your screen. Volunteers first; a pass is always fine; never call a child by name to answer. Model answer: no, we do not agree. A handshake between A and B is counted once by A and once by B, so 4 times 3 counts every handshake twice. We can check by listing the pairs (A with B, A with C, A with D, B with C, B with D, C with D: 6). Link to Explore and the mini-lesson. Repeat each child answer in your own words for the recording. The child in the story is in another class: no cast name is given the wrong answer. Do not say the answer 6 before children try the Think harder question; the model answer is for the tutor.
