TUTOR GUIDE • LEVEL 1 • TERM 2 • LESSON 5 OF 10

Equal Groups

Big idea: Same-size groups can be counted fast. Equal groups have the same number in every group. We can count in jumps, add the same number again and again, or write it with the × sign: 4 groups of 3 is 4 × 3.

90 minutes • live online or face to face • up to 8 children • ages 6-8 • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

1. At a glance

StrandMultiplication and Division (Equal groups and repeated addition (Primary 1, Multiplication and Division 3.1 to 3.4, p. 31); The multiplication sign × and multiplying within 40; Rows and columns of an array: 3 × 4 = 4 × 3)
Strand label for the tutor only. Equal groups, the × sign and multiplying within 40 are in the school Primary 1 syllabus (p. 31), so the lesson stays inside Primary 1 except for one named beyond-syllabus challenge item (owner decision, 22 Oct 2026): Go further G2 part (c), which uses doubling the number of groups (6 groups of 5 = 30, so 12 groups of 5 = 60), a product above the school range of 40. Products stay within 20 at Start here and within 40 elsewhere. The holiday course has no multiplication, so this is new for every child. Reading order (owner default, 22 Oct 2026): "3 groups of 4" is written 3 × 4, groups first, the usual school convention; either order is accepted as correct in an answer, and Think harder shows that 3 × 4 = 4 × 3. Sharing and the division sign are Lesson 17, not here. Do not describe the lesson as MOE-aligned or syllabus-aligned. Contest tag (tutor only): list all the ways (Go further G3).
Objectives
  • Say whether groups are equal (the same number in every group) and add counters to make unequal groups equal.
  • Write "4 groups of 3 = 12" and 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 12 for a picture or a story, with products to 20.
  • Write the multiplication sentence 4 × 3 = 12 and read × as "groups of" (the number of groups first, then the number in each group).
  • Think harder: read an array as rows, turn it and see that 3 × 4 = 4 × 3; find a missing group size in a table of equal groups.
  • Go further: two kinds of groups in one story (bikes and cars) and totals close to 40.
Joining coldRemember warm-up covers: Count in 2s, 5s and 10s along a number square (Term 1); Bundles of ten are equal groups: 3 tens and 4 ones (Term 1); Add by making a ten: 9 + 4, 7 + 5, 6 + 8 (Term 1).
Only counting to 20 and adding small numbers. Nothing from an earlier lesson is needed: the warm-up counts in jumps along a number square, shows 3 bundles of ten as a picture and makes a ten with two coloured ten-frames. The words group, each and equal and the × sign are taught in Explore and the Mini-lesson.
Lessons 5 to 8 of Term 2 build on one another (groups, tables, sharing, stories), so try not to miss this lesson. A child who misses it watches the recording, does workbook pages 1 and 2 and tells the tutor in the open session. A child who joins at Term 2 Lesson 6 or later can follow, because that warm-up shows 5 groups of 6 as repeated addition with a picture.

Before the lesson
  • Solve every workbook question yourself first. Check the Think harder table (T4) and the Go further totals (22 wheels, 30 marbles) so you can answer a child who finds another way to make 24.
  • Tell parents a week before: print the workbook pages and the kit pages (nothing to cut), and find 4 plates or bowls and about 20 small things. Small things stay away from mouths and younger children. Send next lesson’s workbook at the same time (it needs the 100 square page).
  • Have the slides open and check the groups mat is visible on screen share. Have 4 plates and counters to show on camera if you wish.
  • Check the recording captures the tutor and the shared screen. Children’s video is excluded; any unmuted voice is recorded. Greet children by first name and say the recording notice, then start the recording, and avoid first names while it runs.
  • Read groups of as number of groups first: 4 groups of 3 is 4 × 3. Accept 3 × 4 as a correct way to write the same total in a child’s own work. Say plainly to families that equal groups and × are Primary 1 work. Do not call the lesson MOE-aligned. Lessons 15 to 18 build on one another.
Safeguarding and recording (this lesson)
  • Counters and small things stay on the table: nothing goes in the mouth or ears. No matches, toothpicks or anything sharp. Keep small things away from younger brothers and sisters. Use paper plates or the printed groups mat rather than glass or china.
  • This lesson uses the main room only: no breakout rooms, so the tutor sees every camera. A parent sits nearby but does not give answers.
  • Nobody holds paper or a board up to the camera: a gallery view shows everyone. Children tick their own work against the answer slide, answer together on a count of three, or show fingers for a choice. There is no private chat.
  • Never read out a child’s name with a right or wrong answer. The recording has the tutor, the shared screen and any voice that is unmuted. Children may mute and keep their cameras off. A child’s page is never shown.
  • Movement break: children need a little clear space, soft shoes or socks on a safe floor, no stairs and no running. Clapping is soft; a child may stay seated and tap the table.
  • A child who is still stuck ticks ‘I am still stuck’ on the look-back page. A grown-up may photograph that page after class and send it to the business WhatsApp only; photos are kept for 90 days after the program ends. The tutor then helps in the open session next lesson. No private messages to children, and no personal phones.
  • The look-back is never collected in front of the group or read out. A pass is always allowed.

4. Questions, answers, hints and common mistakes

Sneak-peek puzzles (minutes 8 to 12)

Puzzle 1 (access): Mei puts 2 cookies on each of 3 plates. How many cookies are there altogether? You may draw to help.
