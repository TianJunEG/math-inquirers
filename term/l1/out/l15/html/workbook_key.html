Answer key: Lesson 5, Equal Groups

For the tutor and grown-ups. Keep this page away from the child until they have finished and checked their own work. Ask how do you know? before saying right or wrong.

Start here (pages 1 and 2)
S1. Are the groups equal? Circle Yes or No for each picture.
Answer: Left: Yes. Right: No.
The left picture has 3 bags with 4 sweets in each bag: the groups are equal. The right picture has 2, 3 and 2 cookies: the groups are not equal.
Watch for: Counting only the number of groups.; Saying Yes because the pictures look alike.
S2. Look at the picture. Fill in the blanks.
Answer: 3 plates; 5 cookies on each plate; 3 groups of 5 = 15
There are 3 plates and each plate has 5 cookies. 5 + 5 + 5 = 15, so 3 groups of 5 = 15.
Watch for: Writing 5 groups of 3.; Counting the cookies on every plate and losing count.
S3. Mei has 5 jars. Each jar has 2 sweets. How many sweets does Mei have in all?
Answer: 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 10; 5 groups of 2 = 10
5 jars with 2 sweets each: 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 10. So 5 groups of 2 = 10 and Mei has 10 sweets in all.
Watch for: Adding 5 + 2 = 7.; Losing count of the jars.
S4. Draw 4 dots on each plate. Then fill in the blank.
Answer: 3 groups of 4 = 12
3 plates with 4 dots on each: 4 + 4 + 4 = 12, so 3 groups of 4 = 12.
Watch for: Drawing 3 dots on 4 plates.; Drawing different numbers of dots on the plates.
S5. Fill in the table.
Answer: 15, 8, 10
3 groups of 5 = 5 + 5 + 5 = 15. 4 groups of 2 = 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 8. 2 groups of 5 = 5 + 5 = 10.
Watch for: Adding the two numbers: 3 + 5 = 8.; Writing the number of groups in the Total box.
S6. Aisha has 4 boxes. Each box has 5 pencils. How many pencils does Aisha have in all?
Answer: 4 × 5 = 20; 20 pencils
4 boxes with 5 pencils in each box. 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 = 20. The number sentence is 4 × 5 = 20 and Aisha has 20 pencils.
Watch for: Writing + in the circle.; Writing 4 × 5 = 9.
Think harder (page 3)
T1. Look at the dots in rows. Fill in the blanks.
Answer: 3 × 4 = 12; 4 × 3 = 12; Yes
The picture has 3 rows with 4 dots in each row: 3 × 4 = 12. Turned on its side it has 4 rows with 3 dots in each row: 4 × 3 = 12. The totals are the same, so 3 × 4 = 4 × 3.
Watch for: Adding 3 + 4 = 7.; Thinking the totals must be different because the pictures look different.
T2. Each plate should have 3 cookies. Look at the picture. How many more cookies are needed to make the groups equal?
Answer: 3 more cookies
The plates have 3, 1 and 2 cookies. The second plate needs 2 more and the third plate needs 1 more. 2 + 1 = 3 more cookies.
Watch for: Writing 6 (the number of cookies now).; Adding only the missing cookies of one plate.
T3. Kumar has 3 plates with 4 apples on each plate. He says he has 7 apples.
Answer: No; 3 groups of 4 = 12
3 groups of 4 apples: 4 + 4 + 4 = 12. 7 is 3 + 4, which is not the same as 3 groups of 4. So Kumar is not correct.
Watch for: Saying Yes because 3 + 4 = 7.; Writing 3 × 4 = 34.
T4. Jenny has 12 stickers. She puts them into equal groups in different ways. Fill in the table.
Answer: 3, 3
4 groups of 3 = 12, so each of the 4 groups has 3. 3 groups of 4 = 12, so the number of groups is 3.
Watch for: Writing the total in every box.; Choosing numbers that do not make 12.
Go further (page 4)
G1. A shed has 3 bikes and 4 cars. Each bike has 2 wheels. Each car has 4 wheels. How many wheels are there in all?
Answer: 6 wheels, 16 wheels, 22 wheels
3 bikes have 2 + 2 + 2 = 6 wheels. 4 cars have 4 + 4 + 4 + 4 = 16 wheels. 6 + 16 = 22 wheels in all.
Watch for: Counting a car as 2 wheels.; Forgetting to add the wheels of the bikes and the cars.
G2. Jamie has 6 jars. Each jar has 5 marbles. Jamie needs 40 marbles. How many more marbles does he need?
Answer: 6 × 5 = 30; 10 more marbles
6 jars with 5 marbles in each jar: 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 = 30 marbles. 40 - 30 = 10 more marbles.
Watch for: Writing 40 - 6.; Stopping after finding 30.
G3. Wei has 24 sweets. Show 3 different ways to put them into equal groups. Many answers are right.
Answer: For example 2 groups of 12, 3 groups of 8, 4 groups of 6 (also 6 of 4, 8 of 3, 12 of 2, 1 of 24, 24 of 1)
2 × 12 = 24, 3 × 8 = 24, 4 × 6 = 24. Turning each one gives 12 × 2, 8 × 3 and 6 × 4.
Watch for: Choosing numbers that do not make 24.; Writing the same way twice.
Investigation (page 5): Rows with 12 counters
Six ways: 1 × 12, 2 × 6, 3 × 4, 4 × 3, 6 × 2 and 12 × 1. Turning a picture gives a second one (2 × 6 and 6 × 2). 5 rows does not work: 5 rows of 2 uses 10 counters and 2 are left over.
Look for: A child who builds several arrays, writes a × sentence for each and notices that turning one gives another. Credit a child who tries 5 rows and finds counters left over. Do not mark a child down for finding only three ways.
Exit ticket (page 5)
E1. Fill in the blank.
Answer: 20
4 fives: 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 = 20, so 4 groups of 5 = 20.
Watch for: Writing 9 (5 + 4).; Losing count of the fives.
E2. Which is the same as 4 groups of 3? Write its number in the bracket.
Answer: (2) 3 + 3 + 3 + 3
4 groups of 3 means 3 four times: 3 + 3 + 3 + 3.
Watch for: Choosing 4 + 3.; Choosing 3 + 3 + 3.
E3. Ali has 3 boxes. Each box has 4 pencils. How many pencils does Ali have in all?
Answer: 3 × 4 = 12; 12 pencils
3 boxes with 4 pencils in each box: 4 + 4 + 4 = 12. The sign in the circle is ×.
Watch for: Writing + in the circle.; Writing 3 + 4 = 7.
