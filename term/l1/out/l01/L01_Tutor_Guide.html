Bundles and Singles

Big idea: A number is tens and ones. Ten single sticks make one bundle. A two-digit number tells us how many bundles and how many singles.

90 minutes • live online or face to face • up to 8 children • ages 6-8 • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

1. At a glance

StrandWhole Numbers (Numbers to 100: counting a set; Tens and ones (place value); Reading and writing numerals)
Strand label for the tutor only. It is not a claim that the program covers the school syllabus, and it must not appear on child or parent pages as 'MOE-aligned'.
Objectives
  • Group up to 99 objects into bundles of ten and single ones, and say how many bundles and how many singles.
  • Read and write a two-digit number from a picture of bundles and singles, and the other way round.
  • Know that the left digit counts the tens and the right digit counts the ones, and that a 0 in the ones place means no singles.
  • Show that ten singles can be tied into one more bundle (5 tens 14 ones is 6 tens 4 ones).
Joining coldRemember warm-up covers: Count in tens to 100; Fingers to make 10 (bonds to 10); Ten and some more (11 to 19, then 20s and 30s).
Only counting to 20 and showing a number with fingers. Nothing from the holiday course is needed; if the child has done it, skip counting in 10s and bonds to 10 will feel familiar.
Lesson 1 is one of the hardest to miss in Term 1 (plan, section 2). A child who misses it watches the recording and does workbook pages 1 and 2 before Lesson 2.
Child materials
  • About 50 small things of one kind: paper clips, dried pasta, buttons, lolly sticks or straws cut short
  • 6 hair ties, rubber bands, small cups or pieces of string (to hold a bundle), or draw rings on paper
  • The printed workbook (pages 1 to 5) and the kit page (tens bars and ones squares), already cut out by a grown-up
  • A pencil, a rubber and spare paper
Tutor materials
  • A slide deck and a tray of 30 lolly sticks or straws with three rubber bands to show on camera if you wish
  • A timer slide or a visible clock
No ruler is needed. Nothing needs to be bought: everything can be found at home. No matches, toothpicks or anything sharp. Small things never go near the mouth or ears.
TiersStart here (6 questions, pages 1 and 2), Think harder (4, page 3), Go further (3, page 4), Investigation and look-back (page 5), kit page (page 6). Labels are neutral; nobody is told which page they are on.
Contest-style moveUsed at Think harder only: split a number into tens and ones; digit-clue puzzle (find the number from two clues). Never advertised to children or parents.
Before the lesson
  • Solve every workbook question yourself first.
  • Tell parents a week before: print workbook pages 1 to 6, cut out the kit page (page 6), find about 50 small things and 6 hair ties or cups.
  • Have the slides open and check the timer slide and the kit page are visible on screen share.
  • Check the recording settings exclude children's video.
Safeguarding and recording (this lesson)
  • Small things stay on the table: nothing goes in the mouth or ears. No matches, toothpicks or anything sharp. Use hair ties, cups or paper rings, not stretched rubber bands that can flick.
  • This lesson uses the main room only: no breakout rooms, so the tutor sees every camera. A parent sits nearby but does not give answers.
  • Sneak-peek and exit answers are shown on the child's own paper to the camera at the same moment, once. There is no private chat and no board held up in front of the group.
  • Never read out a child's name with a right or wrong answer. Never show a child's page in the recording; record the tutor and the screen only, with children's video excluded.
  • A child who ticks 'I am still stuck' gets a check-in in the open session next lesson (a quick visit to the shared room or a hand signal, parent nearby). No private messages to children.
  • The look-back is never collected in front of the group or read out. A pass is always allowed.

4. Questions, answers, hints and common mistakes

Sneak-peek puzzles (minutes 10 to 14)

Puzzle 1 (access): Count the stars. Write how many. You may draw rings round groups to help.
Answer: 24
Puzzle 2 (think): Mei has 3 bundles of ten sticks and 4 single sticks. Ben has 34 sticks. Could they have the same number of sticks? Write yes or no, and why.
Answer: Yes. 3 bundles of ten is 30, and 4 more is 34.
Unmarked. Papers are shown to the camera once, together. Reveal puzzle 2 in Talk time.

Start here (workbook pages 1 and 2)

S1Count the sticks.
a) ___ bundles of ten
b) ___ single sticks
c) ___ sticks altogether
Answer: 2 bundles, 6 singles, 26 sticks
Hint (say this first): Count the bundles in tens: 10, 20. Then count the singles on: 21, 22, 23, 24, 25, 26.
Worked solution: Count the 2 bundles in tens: 10, 20. Then count on the 6 singles: 21, 22, 23, 24, 25, 26. So 2 bundles, 6 singles, 26 sticks.
Common mistakes: Counting each bundle as 1 (2 + 6 = 8).; Writing the digits the wrong way round (62).
Time for a middle child: about 1.5 min.
S2Draw 3 bundles of ten and 5 single sticks in the box. Then write the number.
The number is ___
Answer: 35 (3 bundles drawn, each with ten sticks, and 5 singles)
Hint (say this first): Draw a bundle as ten short lines with a ring round them. Draw 3 of those, then 5 more short lines on their own.
Worked solution: Draw 3 rings each holding ten short lines (that is 30), then 5 single lines. Count: 10, 20, 30, 31, 32, 33, 34, 35.
Common mistakes: Drawing 3 sticks and 5 sticks instead of 3 bundles of ten.; Writing 53.
Time for a middle child: about 3 min.
S3Write the number.
a) 4 tens and 2 ones = ___
b) 7 tens and 0 ones = ___
c) 1 ten and 9 ones = ___
d) 5 ones and 8 tens = ___
Answer: 42, 70, 19, 85
Hint (say this first): Say the tens first, then the ones. In (d) the ones come first in the words, but the tens digit still goes on the left.
Worked solution: (a) 4 tens is 40, and 2 more is 42. (b) 7 tens is 70 and there are no ones: 70. (c) 10 and 9 is 19. (d) 8 tens is 80, and 5 ones makes 85.
Common mistakes: Writing 7 for 7 tens and 0 ones.; (d) writing 58 because 5 was said first.
Time for a middle child: about 3 min.
S4Fill in the tens and ones.
a) 58 = ___ tens and ___ ones
b) 40 = ___ tens and ___ ones
c) 71 = ___ tens and ___ ones
Answer: 5 tens 8 ones; 4 tens 0 ones; 7 tens 1 one
Hint (say this first): The left digit counts the tens. The right digit counts the ones. You can build it with your kit.
Worked solution: (a) 58: the 5 is the tens digit, the 8 is the ones digit. (b) 40: 4 tens and no ones. (c) 71: 7 tens and 1 one.
Common mistakes: Saying 40 has 4 ones.; Swapping the two digits.
Time for a middle child: about 2.5 min.
S5Ali has 47 straws. He ties them into bundles of ten.
a) How many full bundles can he make? ___
b) How many straws are left over? ___
Answer: 4 bundles, 7 left over
Hint (say this first): 47 is 4 tens and 7 ones. Draw it if you like.
Worked solution: 47 is 4 tens and 7 ones, so Ali makes 4 full bundles and has 7 straws left over (4 bundles hold 40 straws).
Common mistakes: Answering 47 for the bundles.; Saying 3 bundles and 17 left over: 17 singles can still make another bundle.
Time for a middle child: about 2.5 min.
S6Siti has 3 bundles of ten and 4 single sticks. She writes 43. Is Siti right? Write the number she should write, and say what she mixed up.
She should write ___
Answer: No. 34. She wrote the tens and ones digits the wrong way round (43 is 4 tens and 3 ones).
Hint (say this first): Build 3 bundles and 4 singles with your kit. Say the tens first.
Worked solution: 3 bundles is 30 and 4 singles makes 34. Siti wrote 43, which is 4 tens and 3 ones. The tens digit goes on the left.
Common mistakes: Saying she is right because the digits 3 and 4 are there.; Not saying what she mixed up (any clear sentence about the digits is fine).
Time for a middle child: about 2 min.

Think harder (page 3): the taught idea in a new setting

T1Mei has 5 bundles of ten and 14 loose sticks. How many sticks has she? Hint: can she make another bundle?
Mei has ___ sticks
Answer: 64 (14 loose sticks make 1 more bundle and 4 left, so 6 tens and 4 ones)
Hint (say this first): Take 10 of the loose sticks and tie them into a bundle. How many bundles now? How many singles?
Worked solution: 14 loose sticks = 1 bundle and 4 singles. So Mei has 5 + 1 = 6 bundles and 4 singles: 64 sticks. Check: 50 + 14 = 64.
Common mistakes: Writing 514 by joining the digits.; Writing 5 tens and 14 ones as 5 + 14 = 19.
Time for a middle child: about 4 min.
T241 is 4 tens and 1 one. Open one bundle. Now 41 is 3 tens and ___ ones.
3 tens and ___ ones
Answer: 11 (3 tens is 30, and 30 + 11 = 41)
Hint (say this first): A bundle has ten sticks. If you open one, those ten sticks become singles, and you still have the 1 you had.
Worked solution: Opening a bundle gives 10 more singles. 1 single + 10 singles = 11 singles, and 3 bundles are left. 30 + 11 = 41, so it is the same number.
Common mistakes: Writing 10 (forgetting the 1 single already there).; Writing 1 (forgetting the opened bundle).
Time for a middle child: about 3 min.
T3Mystery number. Clue 1: I am between 30 and 50. Clue 2: my ones digit is 6. Clue 3: my number of tens is even. What number am I?
I am ___
Answer: 46
Hint (say this first): List the numbers between 30 and 50 that end in 6. Then check the tens.
Worked solution: Between 30 and 50 and ending in 6: 36 and 46. 36 has 3 tens (odd). 46 has 4 tens (even). So the number is 46.
Common mistakes: Stopping at 36 and not checking clue 3.; Listing numbers that start with 6.
Time for a middle child: about 5 min.
T4Mei has 7 tens and 2 ones. Ali has 6 tens and 12 ones. Who has more, or do they have the same? Show how you know.
Answer: The same: 72 each (6 tens 12 ones is 7 tens 2 ones)
Hint (say this first): Take 10 of Ali's 12 loose ones and tie them into a bundle. How many bundles and ones does Ali have now?
Worked solution: Ali's 12 ones make 1 more bundle and 2 ones. So Ali has 7 tens and 2 ones, the same as Mei. Both have 72.
Common mistakes: Saying Ali has more because 12 is bigger than 2.; Adding 6 + 12 = 18 and comparing 18 with 9.
Time for a middle child: about 5 min.

Go further (page 4): mastery and extension

G1Ben has 6 bundles of ten and 14 loose sticks. Siti has 85 loose sticks. Who has more sticks? How many more?
Answer: Siti. Ben has 74, Siti has 85, so Siti has 11 more.
Hint (say this first): Work out Ben's number first. Remember 14 loose sticks make a bundle and 4 more.
Worked solution: Ben: 6 tens and 14 ones is 7 tens and 4 ones, so 74. Siti: 85. 85 is 8 tens 5 ones and 74 is 7 tens 4 ones: 1 ten and 1 one more, which is 11 more.
Common mistakes: Comparing only 6 and 85 without working out Ben's whole number.; Not opening 14 ones into a bundle before comparing.
Time for a middle child: about 5 min.
G2A shop sells pencils in boxes of ten. Mei needs 60 pencils. She has 4 full boxes and 18 loose pencils. How many more pencils does she need?
Answer: 2 more pencils (4 boxes and 18 pencils is 58)
Hint (say this first): First find how many pencils she has now. Remember that 18 loose pencils make 1 more box and 8 pencils.
Worked solution: 4 boxes is 40 pencils. 40 + 18 = 58. She needs 60, so she needs 60 - 58 = 2 more.
Common mistakes: Saying 18 more (the loose pencils).; Forgetting the 4 full boxes hold 40 pencils.
Time for a middle child: about 5 min.
G3How many different ways can you show 30 with bundles of ten and loose sticks, if you may open bundles? Write each way as ___ tens and ___ ones. (3 tens and 0 ones is one way.)
Answer: 4 ways: 3 tens 0 ones; 2 tens 10 ones; 1 ten 20 ones; 0 tens 30 ones
Hint (say this first): Start with 3 tens. Open one bundle. What do you have now? Keep going until there are no bundles.
Worked solution: 3 tens 0 ones. Open one bundle: 2 tens 10 ones. Open another: 1 ten 20 ones. Open the last: 0 tens 30 ones. That is 4 ways. Each time one ten becomes 10 ones.
Common mistakes: Stopping at 3 tens 0 ones and 0 tens 30 ones (2 ways).; Counting 2 tens 10 ones twice.
Time for a middle child: about 6 min.

Investigation (page 5): Swap the digits

Easy first step: Write 14 and 41. Which number has more sticks? Then try 26 and 62, 52 and 25, and 77.
Then: Pick a two-digit number. Swap its two digits. Is the new number bigger, smaller or the same? Is it always one of these three? When does each happen?
Model answer: Three things can happen. If the tens digit is smaller than the ones digit (14, 26), swapping makes a bigger number (41, 62). If the tens digit is bigger (52, 91), swapping makes a smaller number (25, 19). If the two digits are the same (77), the number stays the same.
Look for: A child who tests several numbers, says what they notice and gives a reason using tens and ones. A number that ends in 0 (30) becomes 03, which is just 3, so it is smaller: this fits 'tens digit bigger'.
Common mistakes: Saying swapping always makes the number bigger (testing only 14 and 26).; Not testing a number with equal digits.

Talk and explain (minutes 70 to 76)

Mei says 26 and 62 are the same, because they use the same digits. Do you agree? Use bundles to explain. Sentence starter: I think ... because ...
Model answer: No. 26 is 2 bundles of ten and 6 singles. 62 is 6 bundles of ten and 2 singles. 62 has more sticks, because the tens digit counts bundles of ten.
Look for: A reason that uses tens and ones (not just 'because 6 is bigger'). Volunteers first, a pass is always fine, never call a child by name to answer, no tallies.

Exit ticket (minutes 76 to 82): 3 questions

E1How many sticks?
___ sticks
Answer: 48
Worked solution: 4 bundles is 40, and 8 singles makes 48.
Common mistakes: Writing 84.; Writing 12 (4 + 8).
E2Fill in the tens and ones.
37 = ___ tens and ___ ones
Answer: 3 tens and 7 ones
Worked solution: The left digit 3 counts the tens, the right digit 7 counts the ones.
Common mistakes: Swapping: 7 tens and 3 ones.
E3Write the number: 7 tens and 0 ones. Why do we need the 0?
The number is ___
Answer: 70. The 0 shows there are no ones, and it keeps the 7 in the tens place (without it, it would look like 7 ones).
Worked solution: 7 bundles of ten and no singles is 70. The zero holds the ones place.
Common mistakes: Writing 7.; Writing 700 or 7 0 as 7 + 0.

Look-back (minutes 82 to 85)

Children tick or write: Something I did well; The hardest part was (knowing which digit is the tens; making bundles of ten; the zero in 40 or 70; opening a bundle (Think harder); something else); What helped me (bundles or objects; drawing it; trying again; I asked for help; I was not stuck); Next time I will try (drawing it; using objects; checking my answer; asking a question); I am still stuck (my tutor will come and help) and I would like to pass. Never collected or read out in front of the group. A child who ticks still stuck gets a check-in in the open session next lesson.
