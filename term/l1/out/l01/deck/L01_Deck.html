Bundles and Singles

Whole numbers: tens and ones

TODAY WE WILL

Make bundles of ten, and use them to read and write numbers.

Math Inquirers Term 1 • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Lesson 1 of Term 1, 90 minutes. Objective: read and write two-digit numbers as bundles of ten and single ones. Time plan: opening 0 to 4, warm-up 4 to 10, sneak-peek 10 to 14, explore 14 to 28, mini-lesson 28 to 38, brain break 38 to 42, workbook 42 to 70, talk 70 to 76, exit ticket 76 to 82, look-back 82 to 85, flex time and goodbye 85 to 90 (the buffer: do not start anything new). Think harder, Go further and the Investigation are for every child to choose. Nobody is told which page they should be on. Tell the group the lesson is recorded (you and the screen only) and that a child may keep the camera off.

Explore: grab and bundle

1Grab a handful of small things. Not the whole pile.

2Make bundles of ten: a band, a cup, or a ring on paper.

3Count the bundles. Count the singles left over.

WHAT IF?

What if you open a bundle? Can you show your number a different way?

Math Inquirers Term 1 • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Round 1, about 4 minutes. Objects first, no writing yet. Children bundle in tens and count the singles. Ask a few children to show their bundles to the camera. The what-if card is available from the start so nobody waits: it previews the Think harder idea (opening a bundle), and no child is named for choosing it. A child with too few things can use the kit bars and squares.

What did we notice?

How many singles can be left over?

Could you have ten singles left over? Why not?

Is it quicker to count 40 singles, or 4 tens?

Math Inquirers Term 1 • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Take one or two answers from volunteers. Key idea: the singles left over are always 0 to 9, because ten singles tie into another bundle. Counting in tens is quicker and easier to keep track of. Repeat a child's answer in your own words so the recording picks it up.

The zero holds the place

40

4 tens and 0 ones

Math Inquirers Term 1 • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Zero ones means no singles. The zero is needed so the 4 stays in the tens place. A common slip is to write 4 for 4 tens.

28–38 MIN

62

Which has more sticks? How do you know?

Math Inquirers Term 1 • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

26 is 2 bundles and 6 singles. 62 is 6 bundles and 2 singles. 62 has more because the tens digit is worth more. The order of the digits matters. This sets up the Talk time question.
