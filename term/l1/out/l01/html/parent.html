Bundles and Singles

What we did

We learned that a two-digit number is made of bundles of ten and single ones. Your child made bundles from real things, built numbers up to 58, and practised reading and writing numbers like 34 (3 tens and 4 ones) and 70 (7 tens and 0 ones).

A number is tens and ones. Ten singles tie into one bundle.
Try at home (5 minutes)
  1. Bundle it: grab a handful of pasta, clips or buttons. Tie bundles of ten with bands or cups. Ask: how many tens and how many ones? Write the number together.
  2. Show me: say a number up to 60 and let your child build it with bundles and singles. Swap roles and make a mistake on purpose for them to find.
  3. Spot it: look at page numbers, lift buttons or door numbers. Ask: how many tens is that? Which digit tells you?

When your child is stuck

Please let your child use real things or draw bundles, and wait 10 seconds before helping. Asking 'what could you try?' works better than giving the answer.

Words we used

  • bundle or ten: ten single things tied together
  • single or one: a thing on its own
  • tens digit: the left digit, it counts the bundles
  • ones digit: the right digit, it counts the singles

Good questions

  • How do you know?
  • Which digit tells how many bundles?
  • What if you opened a bundle?

Things to have ready

  • About 50 small things of one kind: paper clips, dried pasta, buttons, lolly sticks or straws cut short
  • 6 hair ties, rubber bands, small cups or pieces of string (to hold a bundle), or draw rings on paper
  • The printed workbook (pages 1 to 5) and the kit page (tens bars and ones squares), already cut out by a grown-up
