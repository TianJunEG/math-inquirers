Answer key: Lesson 1, Bundles and Singles

For the tutor and grown-ups. Keep this page away from the child until they have finished and checked their own work. Ask how do you know? before saying right or wrong.

Start here (pages 1 and 2)
S1. Count the sticks.
Answer: 2 bundles, 6 singles, 26 sticks
Count the 2 bundles in tens: 10, 20. Then count on the 6 singles: 21, 22, 23, 24, 25, 26. So 2 bundles, 6 singles, 26 sticks.
Watch for: Counting each bundle as 1 (2 + 6 = 8).; Writing the digits the wrong way round (62).
S2. Draw 5 single sticks and 3 bundles of ten in the box.
Answer: 35 (3 bundles drawn, each with ten sticks, and 5 singles)
Draw 3 rings each holding ten short lines (that is 30), then 5 single lines. Count: 10, 20, 30, 31, 32, 33, 34, 35.
Watch for: Drawing 3 sticks and 5 sticks instead of 3 bundles of ten.; Writing 53.
S3. Fill in the missing numbers.
Answer: 42, 70, 19, 85
(a) 4 tens is 40, and 2 more is 42. (b) 7 tens is 70 and there are no ones: 70. (c) 10 and 9 is 19. (d) 8 tens is 80, and 5 ones makes 85.
Watch for: Writing 7 for 7 tens and 0 ones.; (d) writing 58 because 5 was said first.
S4. Fill in the blanks.
Answer: 5 tens 8 ones; 4 tens 0 ones; 7 tens 1 one
(a) 58: the 5 is the tens digit, the 8 is the ones digit. (b) 40: 4 tens and no ones. (c) 71: 7 tens and 1 one.
Watch for: Saying 40 has 4 ones.; Swapping the two digits.
S5. Ali has 47 straws. He ties the straws into bundles of 10. Fill in the blanks.
Answer: 4 bundles, 7 left over
47 is 4 tens and 7 ones, so Ali makes 4 full bundles and has 7 straws left over (4 bundles hold 40 straws).
Watch for: Answering 47 for the bundles.; Saying 3 bundles and 17 left over: 17 singles can still make another bundle.
S6. Siti has 3 bundles of ten and 4 single sticks. She writes 43.
Answer: No. 34. She wrote the tens and ones digits the wrong way round (43 is 4 tens and 3 ones).
3 bundles is 30 and 4 singles makes 34. Siti wrote 43, which is 4 tens and 3 ones. The tens digit goes on the left.
Watch for: Saying she is right because the digits 3 and 4 are there.; Not saying what she mixed up (any clear sentence about the digits is fine).
S7. Ali has 6 bundles of ten and 0 single sticks. Mei has 0 bundles and 6 single sticks. Draw Ali's sticks and Mei's sticks in the box. Then write each number.
Answer: Ali 60, Mei 6. Ali has more (6 tens is 60; 6 singles is 6)
Ali: 6 bundles of ten is 60, with no singles, so 60. Mei: 6 single sticks is 6. The same digit 6 means very different amounts because of its place: 6 tens or 6 ones.
Watch for: Writing 6 for both children.; Drawing 6 sticks for Ali instead of 6 bundles of ten.
Think harder (page 3)
T1. Mei has 5 bundles of ten and 14 loose sticks. How many sticks does she have?
Answer: 64 (14 loose sticks make 1 more bundle and 4 left, so 6 tens and 4 ones)
14 loose sticks = 1 bundle and 4 singles. So Mei has 5 + 1 = 6 bundles and 4 singles: 64 sticks. Check: 50 + 14 = 64.
Watch for: Writing 514 by joining the digits.; Writing 5 tens and 14 ones as 5 + 14 = 19.
T2. Fill in the blank.
Answer: 11 (3 tens is 30, and 30 + 11 = 41)
Opening a bundle gives 10 more singles. 1 single + 10 singles = 11 singles, and 3 bundles are left. 30 + 11 = 41, so it is the same number.
Watch for: Writing 10 (forgetting the 1 single already there).; Writing 1 (forgetting the opened bundle).
T3. My tens digit is even. My ones digit is 6. I am between 30 and 50. What number am I?
Answer: 46
Between 30 and 50 and ending in 6: 36 and 46. 36 has 3 tens (odd). 46 has 4 tens (even). So the number is 46.
Watch for: Stopping at 36 and not checking clue 3.; Listing numbers that start with 6.
T4. Mei has 7 tens and 2 ones. Ali has 6 tens and 12 ones. Circle one: Mei has more. Ali has more. They have the same. Show how you know.
Answer: The same: 72 each (6 tens 12 ones is 7 tens 2 ones)
Ali's 12 ones make 1 more bundle and 2 ones. So Ali has 7 tens and 2 ones, the same as Mei. Both have 72.
Watch for: Saying Ali has more because 12 is greater than 2.; Adding 6 + 12 = 18 and comparing 18 with 9.
Go further (page 4)
G1. Ben has 6 bundles of ten and 14 loose sticks. Siti has 85 loose sticks.
Answer: Siti. Ben has 74, Siti has 85, so Siti has 11 more.
Ben: 6 tens and 14 ones is 7 tens and 4 ones, so 74. Siti: 85. 85 is 8 tens 5 ones and 74 is 7 tens 4 ones: 1 ten and 1 one more, which is 11 more.
Watch for: Comparing only 6 and 85 without working out Ben's whole number.; Not opening 14 ones into a bundle before comparing.
G2. Pencils come in boxes of 10. Mei needs 60 pencils. She has 4 full boxes and 18 loose pencils.
Answer: 2 more pencils (4 boxes and 18 pencils is 58)
4 boxes is 40 pencils. 40 + 18 = 58. She needs 60, so she needs 60 - 58 = 2 more.
Watch for: Saying 18 more (the loose pencils).; Forgetting the 4 full boxes hold 40 pencils.
G3. Write 30 in different ways, like 3 tens and 0 ones. Find all the ways. How many ways are there?
Answer: 4 ways: 3 tens 0 ones; 2 tens 10 ones; 1 ten 20 ones; 0 tens 30 ones
3 tens 0 ones. Open one bundle: 2 tens 10 ones. Open another: 1 ten 20 ones. Open the last: 0 tens 30 ones. That is 4 ways. Each time one ten becomes 10 ones.
Watch for: Stopping at 3 tens 0 ones and 0 tens 30 ones (2 ways).; Counting 2 tens 10 ones twice.
Investigation (page 5): Swap the digits
Three things can happen. If the tens digit is smaller than the ones digit (14, 26), swapping makes a bigger number (41, 62). If the tens digit is bigger (52, 91), swapping makes a smaller number (25, 19). If the two digits are the same (77), the number stays the same.
Look for: A child who tests several numbers, says what they notice and gives a reason using tens and ones. A number that ends in 0 (30) becomes 03, which is just 3, so it is smaller: this fits 'tens digit bigger'.
Exit ticket (page 5)
E1. How many sticks?
Answer: 48
4 bundles is 40, and 8 singles makes 48.
Watch for: Writing 84.; Writing 12 (4 + 8).
E2. Fill in the blanks.
Answer: 3 tens and 7 ones
The left digit 3 counts the tens, the right digit 7 counts the ones.
Watch for: Swapping: 7 tens and 3 ones.
E3. Write the number: 7 tens and 0 ones.
Answer: 70. The 0 shows there are no ones, and it keeps the 7 in the tens place (without it, it would look like 7 ones).
7 bundles of ten and no singles is 70. The zero holds the ones place.
Watch for: Writing 7.; Writing 700 or 7 0 as 7 + 0.
