Three routes, no more

R U U

U R U

U U R

The R can go first, second or third. That makes 3 routes.

Math Inquirers Term 4 • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

The answer for the example on the slide before. R U U, U R U and U U R. Say: where does the one R go: first, second or third. That gives 3 routes and no more, and listing them in order means none is missed. Read it aloud so that children can tick their own work. Do not give the pattern for bigger grids: that is Think harder. If a class is quick, one volunteer may say how many routes there would be if the grid were 1 across and 3 up (4: the R can be in 4 places).
