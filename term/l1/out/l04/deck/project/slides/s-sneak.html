Sneak-peek puzzles

Ideas we have not learnt yet. A guess is a great start. Nobody is marked.

PUZZLE 1: anyone can try

Count in 5s. Write the next two numbers.

5101520____

PUZZLE 2: think

This is a piece of the 100 square. A counter is on 36.

Ravi moves it down one square.

Which number is it on now? How do you know?

Math Inquirers Term 1 • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Puzzle 1 (5, 10, 15, 20, then 25 and 30) is an access question: any child who can count on can try it, with fingers if they like. Puzzle 2 is a thinking question on the lesson key idea: down one row is 10 more. The row under 31 to 40 is hidden on purpose. Say: two sneak-peek puzzles, ideas we have not learnt yet; a guess is a great start and I do not know is welcome; nobody is marked. Children write on their own paper. Everyone holds the paper to the camera once, at the same moment, after puzzle 2; there is no private chat and no board in front of the group. Answers: 25 and 30; 46 (down one row is 10 more). Do not reveal puzzle 2 until Talk time. Do not write scores or keep a list.
