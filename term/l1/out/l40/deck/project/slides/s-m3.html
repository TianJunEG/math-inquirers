A CHILD IN ANOTHER CLASS WROTE

Two numbers add up to 8.

One number is 2 more than the other.

The numbers are 6 and 2.

Is the puzzle right? How do you know?

Math Inquirers Term 4 • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Worked example, checking. Children check both clues on their own paper: 6 and 2 add up to 8 (the first clue fits), but 6 is 4 more than 2, not 2 more (the second clue does not). Ask: is the puzzle right (no); how do you know. The answer is on the next slide. Say: check every clue, not just one. The child in the story is in another class: no cast name is given the wrong answer.
