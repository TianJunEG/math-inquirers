Two roads, one answer

Pip has twice as many marbles as Rae. They have 18 marbles in all. How many marbles does Rae have?

Math Inquirers Term 4 • Notice it. Test it. Explain why.

Worked example, two roads. Pip has twice as many marbles as Rae and they have 18 marbles in all. Road 1: the strip. Rae has 1 part and Pip has 2 parts, so there are 3 equal parts, and 18 shared into 3 parts is 6 marbles for Rae. Children work out how many marbles Rae has on their own paper. Ask: how many marbles does Rae have (6). The answer is on the next slide, with a second road: a table of tries. Say: twice as many is a Primary 2 and later idea at school; we try it because the strip shows it. A child who writes 9 has shared 18 into 2 equal parts: ask, how many parts does Pip have?
