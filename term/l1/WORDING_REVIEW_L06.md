# Wording review: Lesson 6

Format: old -> new, then a one-line reason. Strings are exact; (xN) = text appears in N places (slide, script, key).

- "Ravi says 43 + 20 = 45. Is Ravi right? Write yes or no, and why." -> "Ravi says 43 + 20 = 45. Is Ravi right? Circle Yes or No. Then write how you know.". yes/no and why were two commands in one; circle and frame (rule 19).
- "Puzzle two. Ravi says forty-three plus twenty is forty-five. Is Ravi right? Write yes or no, and why." -> "Puzzle two. Ravi says forty-three plus twenty is forty-five. Is Ravi right? Circle Yes or No. Then write how you know.". read-aloud matches the slide.
- "Mei has these sticks. She gets 1 more bundle of ten." -> "Mei has these sticks. She gets 1 more bundle of 10 sticks. Fill in the blanks.". numeral; the command was missing.
- "___ bundles of ten now" -> "Now Mei has ___ bundles of ten.". answer frame ends with the noun (rule 16).
- "___ single sticks now" -> "She also has ___ single sticks.". same.
- "___ sticks altogether" -> "Mei has ___ sticks altogether.". same.
- "Draw 3 bundles of ten and 4 single sticks in the box. Then draw 2 more bundles of ten. Write the number." -> "Draw 3 bundles of ten and 4 single sticks in the box. Then draw 2 more bundles of ten. How many sticks are there altogether?". 'Write the number' did not say which number; fixed question words.
- "The number is ___" -> "There are ___ sticks altogether.". answer frame with the noun.
- "Jump forward. Write the number." -> "Jump forward. Fill in the blanks." (x2). school-paper command (rule 14).
- "Jump back, or hop. Write the number." -> "Jump or hop. Fill in the blanks.". 'Jump back, or hop' did not match d) 62 + 7 (a hop forward).
- "___ pencils left" -> "There are ___ pencils left.". answer frame.
- "Siti says 45 + 20 = 47. Is Siti right? Write the right answer. Say what she mixed up." -> "Siti says 45 + 20 = 47. Fill in a to c.". three questions in one paragraph; 'what she mixed up' had no frame.
- "Question 6: Siti says forty-five plus twenty is forty-seven. Is she right?" -> "Question 6: Siti says forty-five plus twenty is forty-seven. Is she right or wrong? Circle one. Then fill in the blanks.". read-aloud matches.
- "Question 4: jump back, or hop." -> "Question 4: jump or hop.". read-aloud matches.
- "Start at 36. Draw 2 jumps of ten on a number line in the box. Write where you land." -> "Start at 36. Draw 2 jumps of ten on a number line in the box. Where do you land?". 'Write where you land' hid the question.
- "I land on ___" -> "I land on ___.". frame.
- "Work out 34 + 25. Jump the tens first, then hop the ones. Show your jumps and hops." -> "Add 34 + 25. Show your jumps and hops on the lines.". 'Work out' is not school wording (rule 14); the method order is taught on the slides, not in the question (rule 24).
- "Work out 68 - 24. Jump back the tens first, then hop back the ones." -> "Subtract 68 - 24. Show your jumps and hops on the lines.". same.
- "Frog A starts on 12. It jumps 3 tens, then hops 4. Frog B starts on 12. It hops 4 first, then jumps 3 tens. Do they land on the same number? How do you know?" -> "Frog A starts on 12. It jumps 3 tens, then hops 4. Frog B starts on 12. It hops 4, then jumps 3 tens. Fill in a to c.". two questions in one line; parts a to c.
- "He started on ___" -> "Ali started on ___.". frame.
- "Mei is on 7. She makes jumps of ten until she gets to 97. How many jumps does she make? Write each number she lands on." -> "Mei is on 7. She jumps in tens until she gets to 97. Fill in a and b.". two questions in one paragraph; parts a and b.
- "Mei goes from 7 to 97 again. This time she may use jumps of 20 as well as jumps of 10. What is the fewest jumps she can make? Write her jumps." -> "Mei goes from 7 to 97 again. This time she can jump 10 or jump 20. Fill in a and b.". 'may use jumps of 20 as well as' was long; ungrammatical 'fewest jumps'; parts a and b.
- "Start at 25. Jump forward 3 tens. Jump back 1 ten. Jump forward 2 tens. Then hop forward 4. Where do you land? Write each landing number." -> "Start at 25. Jump forward 3 tens. Then jump back 1 ten. Then jump forward 2 tens. Then hop forward 4. Fill in a and b.". each move is one short sentence with 'Then' so the order is clear; two questions became parts.
- "Start at 6 and keep jumping in tens. Will you ever land on 50? Try other start numbers. What do you notice? Which start numbers can land on 50?" -> "Start at 6 and keep jumping in tens. Will you land on 50? Circle Yes or No. Then try other start numbers. Which start numbers can land on 50? Write what you notice.". four questions in a row; circle then write (rule 19).
- "Mei says: when you jump forward 2 tens, the ones digit changes. Do you agree? Use your kit to explain." -> "Mei says: when you jump forward 2 tens, the ones digit changes. Is Mei right? Use your kit to show why." (x2). 'Do you agree?' is abstract for 6-year-olds.
- "Do you agree? Use your kit to explain." -> "Is Mei right? Use your kit to show why.". slide matches.
- "Jump forward. Write the number." -> "Add.". exit tickets say Add (rule 14).
- "Jump back, or hop. Write the numbers." -> "Subtract.". exit tickets say Subtract (rule 14).
- "Jump the tens, then hop the ones. Write the number." -> "Add.". method out of the question (rule 24).
- "A box has 4 bundles of ten pencils and 5 loose pencils. 3 more bundles are added. How many pencils now?" -> "A box has 4 bundles of 10 pencils and 5 single pencils. Ali puts 3 more bundles of 10 pencils in the box. How many pencils are in the box now?". passive 'are added'; numerals; noun repeated in the question.
- "Write the numbers: 86 - 40 = ___ and 86 - 4 = ___" -> "Subtract. (a) 86 - 40 = ___   (b) 86 - 4 = ___". school-paper command.
- "Ravi has 53 stickers and gives 3 away. Mei has 53 stickers and gives 30 away. How many does each child have left?" -> "Ravi has 53 stickers. He gives away 3. Mei has 53 stickers. She gives away 30. (a) How many stickers does Ravi have left? (b) How many stickers does Mei have left?". one question per part; noun repeated.
- "Work out 36 + 23 twice: jump first, then hop; and hop first, then jump. Is the answer the same?" -> "Add 36 + 23 in two ways. (a) Jump first, then hop. (b) Hop first, then jump. (c) Is the answer the same? Circle Yes or No.". 'Work out' and 'twice'; three jobs in one sentence.
- "Draw a number line on paper from 0 to 100, a mark every 10. Put a coin on 7 and jump it in tens. Write each number. What stays the same?" -> "(a) Draw a number line from 0 to 100 with a mark every 10. Put a coin on 7. Jump it in tens and write each number. (b) What stays the same?". five instructions in a row: now (a) and (b).
- "Start at 5. Jump in tens. What is the last number you reach before 100?" -> "Start at 5. Jump in tens. What is the last number you land on before 100?". 'reach' to 'land on' like the rest of the lesson.
- "Add a ten. Does only the tens digit change?" -> "Add a ten to 34, 67 and 90. What changes?". the question 'Is it always true?' had the answer 'almost'; now an open noticing question.
- "Mei says: if you add a ten, only the tens digit changes. Is that always true? Try 34, 67 and 90 with your kit." -> "Mei says: when you add a ten, only the tens digit changes. Try 34, 67 and 90 with your kit. What do you notice?". 90 + 10 = 100 changes more than the tens digit, so 'always true' had no clean answer.
- "Recording: pause. Try adding ten to 34, 67 and 90. Does only the tens digit change?" -> "Recording: pause. Try adding ten to 34, 67 and 90. What do you notice?". matches.
- "Almost always. 34 + 10 = 44 and 67 + 10 = 77: only the tens digit changes. 90 + 10 is ten tens, which has a new name, a hundred (next lessons)." -> "For 34 and 67 only the tens digit changes: 44 and 77. For 90 + 10 the answer is 100, a new number with a hundreds digit (a later lesson).". answer matches the new question.
- "Mei has 34 stickers. She gets 2 packs of ten stickers. How many stickers now?" -> "Mei has 34 stickers. She gets 2 packs of 10 stickers. How many stickers does Mei have now?". fixed question words; numeral.
- "Mei has {n} stickers. She gets {m} packs of ten stickers. How many stickers now?" -> "Mei has {n} stickers. She gets {m} packs of 10 stickers. How many stickers does Mei have now?". same.
- "What is 34 + 25? Jump the tens, then hop the ones." -> "What is 34 + 25?". method moved to the hint.
- "What is {n} + {a}? Jump the tens, then hop the ones." -> "What is {n} + {a}?". same.
- "What is 68 - 24? Jump back the tens, then hop back the ones." -> "What is 68 - 24?". same.
- "What is {n} - {a}? Jump back the tens, then hop back the ones." -> "What is {n} - {a}?". same.
- "It hops 4, then jumps 3 tens. Where do they land?" -> "It hops 4, then jumps 3 tens. Which sentence is true?". the answer choices are sentences, not places.
- "Mei is on 7. She makes jumps of ten until she gets to 97. How many jumps?" -> "Mei is on 7. She jumps in tens until she gets to 97. How many jumps does she make?". full question.
- "Mei is on {a}. She makes jumps of ten until she gets to {b}. How many jumps?" -> "Mei is on {a}. She jumps in tens until she gets to {b}. How many jumps does she make?". same.
- "What is the fewest jumps she can make?" -> "What is the fewest number of jumps she can make?" (x2). grammar.
- "S6 parts: 'The right answer is ___'" -> "a) Siti's answer: right or wrong? Circle Right or Wrong. b) The right answer is ___. c) Siti added 2 ones. She should add 2 ___.". spot-the-mistake with one mistake and a frame ('tens'); maths unchanged.
- "T3 (no parts, 'How do you know?')" -> "a) Frog A lands on ___. b) Frog B lands on ___. c) Do they land on the same number? Circle Yes or No.". Yes/No with the two landing numbers as evidence (rule 19).
- "G1-G3 (no parts)" -> "G1: a) Write each number Mei lands on. b) Mei makes ___ jumps. G2: a) Write the jumps Mei makes. b) The fewest number of jumps is ___. G3: a) Write each number you land on. b) You finish on ___.". one question per part (rule 18).
- "Start at 36. Draw 2 jumps of ten on a number line in the box. Where do you land?" -> "Start at 36. On a number line in the box, draw 2 jumps of ten. Where do you land?". reordered: the first version was quoted in the school-paper doc and overlapped.
- "Ali jumps forward 3 tens and lands on 78. Where did he start?" -> "Ali jumps forward 3 tens and ends on 78. Where did he start?". same.
- "Ali jumps forward {m} tens and lands on {n}. Where did he start?" -> "Ali jumps forward {m} tens and ends on {n}. Where did he start?". same.
- "A box has 4 bundles of 10 pencils and 5 single pencils. Ali puts 3 more bundles of 10 pencils in the box. How many pencils are in the box now?" -> "A box has 4 bundles of 10 pencils and 5 single pencils. Ali puts in 3 more bundles of 10. How many pencils are in the box now?". shorter so the home sheet keeps 2 pages.
- "Ravi has 53 stickers. He gives away 3. Mei has 53 stickers. She gives away 30. (a) How many stickers does Ravi have left? (b) How many stickers does Mei have left?" -> "Ravi and Mei each have 53 stickers. Ravi gives away 3. Mei gives away 30. (a) Ravi has ___ stickers left. (b) Mei has ___ stickers left.". shorter; answer frames end with the noun.
- "Add 36 + 23 in two ways. (a) Jump first, then hop. (b) Hop first, then jump. (c) Is the answer the same? Circle Yes or No." -> "Add 36 + 23 in two ways: (a) jump first, then hop; (b) hop first, then jump. Is the answer the same? Circle Yes or No.". shorter.
- "(a) Draw a number line from 0 to 100 with a mark every 10. Put a coin on 7. Jump it in tens and write each number. (b) What stays the same?" -> "(a) Draw a number line from 0 to 100. Put a coin on 7 and jump it in tens. Write each number. (b) What stays the same?". shorter.
