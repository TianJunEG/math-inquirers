# Wording guide for Level 1 (ages 6-8) questions and child-facing text

The owner reports that some question wording is strange. Use this guide to review and rewrite every piece of text a child reads or hears: workbook questions, exit tickets, the end-of-term check, slide text, home practice, practice items, and tutor lines that are read to children.

1. **Short and plain.** One idea per sentence, usually 6 to 12 words. No more than three short sentences before the first question. Words a Primary 1 child can read aloud or understand when read aloud. Avoid: 'compensation', 'represent', 'determine', 'respectively', 'each time' chains, passive voice, long 'if ... then' sentences.
2. **Say what to do first.** Start with the command ('Fill in', 'Write', 'Circle', 'Draw', 'Count', 'Colour'), then the data, or put the story first and the command last. Never leave the command to be guessed. Use the same command words for the same jobs across lessons.
3. **Natural Singapore English.** Use $ and cents, 'sweets', 'stickers', 'pencils', 'marbles', 'straws', 'coins', 'a grown-up'. Names: Ali, Mei, Siti, Ben, Kumar, Aisha, Wei (keep the cast small and consistent). No American-only words (candy, eraser: say 'rubber').
4. **No trick wording and no hidden second question.** If a sentence has two parts, number them a, b. A 'Spot the mistake' question must show the wrong working clearly and say which part to check; the mistake must be exactly one thing.
5. **Numbers and words match.** Never say 'ten' in the text and show a different count in the picture; blanks have a unit after them ('___ tens and ___ ones', '___ sticks').
6. **One right answer unless the question says 'Choose' or 'Show how you know'.** Open questions must say so ('Many answers are right.').
7. **Read it aloud test.** Read each question in a calm tutor voice: if you stumble, it is too long or unnatural. Rewrite.
8. **Grammar and tense.** Present tense for puzzles ('Mei has 5 sweets'), past tense only for stories that happened ('Ben ate 2'). Consistent plurals ('1 ten', '2 tens', '1 one', '5 ones').
9. **Sentence frames beat explanations.** Where a child must explain, give a frame ('I know because ___.').
10. **Do not change the maths or the answers** unless the original was wrong or ambiguous; if you change an answer, update the answer checks (verify/lNN_checks.py), the key, the guide's answer lines and the practice templates, and say so in your report.

## How Singapore school papers word questions

Learned from a real P1 quiz (Henry Park, multiplication and division) and a real P2 revision paper (Rosyth). These are patterns, not questions to copy. Full catalogue and our comparison: `docs/books/school_paper_wording_patterns.md`. The rules above still apply; use these to make our wording sound like a school paper.

11. **Story first, then the question; picture between story and answer.** A word problem is 1 to 3 short sentences, then one question, then the picture, then the answer line. Repeat the noun in the question ('How many bags of pencils does Ravi have?'), not 'How many does he have?'.
12. **Fixed question words.** Totals: 'How many ... altogether?' or 'in all'. Gap: 'How many more ... than ...?'. What is left: 'How many ... are left?'. Equal groups: 'each', 'equal', 'groups of'. Money: 'How much ...?'. Use these every time; do not invent new ways to ask the same thing.
13. **Compare with 'greater/greatest, smaller/smallest, more, fewer/less, longer'.** Do not write 'bigger/biggest' for numbers. Ordering: 'Arrange the numbers in order. Begin with the smallest.' (not 'Write these numbers in order. Start with ...'). Number patterns: 'What are the next two numbers?'.
14. **Command phrasings to use.** 'Fill in the blanks.' / 'Fill in the missing numbers.' / 'Circle the ...' / 'Draw ...' / 'Count and write ...' / 'Tick (✓) True or False.' / 'Write a number sentence ...' / 'Find the total ...' / 'Complete the ...'. Add, subtract: 'Add.' / 'Subtract.' (say 'Find the value of', not 'Work out'). Lesson words like 'jump', 'hop', 'open a bundle' stay in the lesson; exit tickets and home practice should say Add or Subtract.
15. **Number format.** Write every quantity in a sentence as a numeral ('Ali has 18 pencils', '4 bags'); never spell out a quantity as a word in a story. Use number words only when the question is about number words. Two-digit and larger numbers have no commas under 1000 (800 g). Times: 6:45 a.m.
16. **Answer frames end with the noun or unit.** Number in a box or `___`, noun after it: 'There are ___ jars.' 'Each jar has ___ cookies.' 'She needs ___ more sweets.' Full sentence, full stop at the end. Units after the blank: '___ cm', '___ g', '___ m'; the dollar sign goes before: '$___'. Use a box or `[]` for a single missing number in a number sentence, and `___` for words or a number plus a unit. Do not mix both in one question.
17. **Number-sentence boxes.** For a number sentence, give 'box, sign, box = box' ([ ] + [ ] = [ ]). When the sign is part of the answer, draw it as a circle between the boxes. Then ask for the answer in a sentence ('Jenny has ___ balls in all.'). Missing-number items: '8 + [ ] = 15' or '5 + 3 = 4 + [ ]' with the instruction 'Fill in the missing number.'.
18. **Multi-part items: a short stem, then (a), (b).** Facts first in the stem, one question per part, part (b) builds on (a). Never two questions in one sentence ('Who has more? How many more?' becomes (a) and (b)). Keep how-to instructions and hints out of the stem; they go in the tutor hint. 'Show your working.' goes at the end, with space.
19. **Choice questions use circle or tick, not 'write why'.** To check understanding use 'Is Siti correct? Circle Yes or No.' or a True/False tick table, then one short frame ('I know because ___.') only if needed. Do not state a child's wrong reasoning inside the stem ('Ravi says 19 is bigger because 9 > 2').
20. **Riddle and clue questions are one short paragraph.** 'I am a number between 30 and 50. My ones digit is 6. What number am I?' Not 'Mystery number. Clue 1: ... Clue 2: ...'.
21. **Names.** Keep the cast, and add variety from real papers: Ali, Mei, Siti, Ben, Kumar, Aisha, Wei, plus Ravi, Jenny, Lily, Jamie, Ray, Amelia, Jason. First name only. Introduce a name once, then 'he/she' in the next sentence, then the name in the question if it is a new idea. Mix girls and boys as the main actor. A pair of children can be drawn once with names under them.
22. **Contexts and things.** Sweets, cookies, doughnuts, lollipops, pencils, notebooks, erasers or rubbers, balloons, balls, marbles, jars, bags, cakes, stickers, books, buses. Everyday, countable, drawable. Money is dollars (whole dollars at this age, e.g. '$2 each', 'gave the cashier $10', 'How much change?'). No brands, no fantasy, no big numbers.
23. **Section and instruction wording.** If a worksheet has parts, give each a one-line instruction in the paper style: 'Fill in the correct answers in the spaces provided.' / 'Show all your workings clearly.' / 'Write your answers in the boxes provided.' / 'Study the picture below and answer Questions 5 to 7.' Label options (1), (2), (3), (4), never A, B, C, D, with brackets at the right for the answer. Label figures 'Figure A', 'Jug B', 'Box C'.
24. **Do not teach inside the question.** A paper question never contains a lesson sentence ('That is one ten less.', 'The left card counts the hundreds.') or a method order ('Take the tens first, then the ones'). Put teaching in the slide or tutor script; keep the question a question.
25. **Keep to what children are tested on.** These papers test numbers to about 40 in a P1 quiz and numbers under 1000 only for g in P2; they test exact (not 'nearest') lengths, 1 or 2 steps in a problem sum, and give True/False or MCQ for reasoning. Our rounding ('nearer ten', 'nearest cm'), 3-digit digit-card items, 3-step stories and 'say why' prompts are enrichment: keep them in Think harder and Go further, not in start questions, exit tickets or the end-of-term check.
