# Wording guide for Level 1 (ages 6-8) questions and child-facing text

The owner reports that some question wording is strange. Use this guide to review and rewrite every piece of text a child reads or hears: workbook questions, exit tickets, the end-of-term check, slide text, home practice, practice items, and tutor lines that are read to children.

1. **Short and plain.** One idea per sentence, usually 6 to 12 words. No more than three short sentences before the first question. Words a Primary 1 child can read aloud or understand when read aloud. Avoid: 'compensation', 'represent', 'determine', 'respectively', 'each time' chains, passive voice, long 'if ... then' sentences.
2. **Say what to do first.** Start with the command ('Fill in', 'Write', 'Circle', 'Draw', 'Count', 'Colour'), then the data, or put the story first and the command last. Never leave the command to be guessed. Use the same command words for the same jobs across lessons.
3. **Natural Singapore English.** Use $ and cents, 'sweets', 'stickers', 'pencils', 'marbles', 'straws', 'coins', 'a grown-up'. Names: Ali, Mei, Siti, Ben, Kumar, Aisha, Wei (keep the cast small and consistent). No American-only words (candy, eraser: say 'rubber').
4. **No trick wording and no hidden second question.** If a sentence has two parts, number them a, b. A 'Spot the mistake' question must show the wrong working clearly and say which part to check; the mistake must be exactly one thing.
5. **Numbers and words match.** Never say 'ten' in the text and show a different count in the picture; blanks have a unit after them ('___ tens and ___ ones', '___ sticks').
6. **One right answer unless the question says 'Choose' or 'Show how you know'.** Open questions must say so ('Many answers are right.').
7. **Read it aloud test.** Read each question in a calm tutor voice: if you stumble, it is too long or unnatural. Rewrite.
8. **Grammar and tense.** Present tense for puzzles ('Mei has 5 sweets'), past tense only for stories that happened ('Ben ate 2'). Consistent plurals ('1 ten', '2 tens', '1 one', '5 ones').
9. **Sentence frames beat explanations.** Where a child must explain, give a frame ('I know because ___.').
10. **Do not change the maths or the answers** unless the original was wrong or ambiguous; if you change an answer, update the answer checks (verify/lNN_checks.py), the key, the guide's answer lines and the practice templates, and say so in your report.
