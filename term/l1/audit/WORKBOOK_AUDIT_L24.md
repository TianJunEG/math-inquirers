## Verdict: Ready after minor fixes

The maths is right everywhere: counting mixed coins and notes, swapping a coin for coins, change by counting up, two prices, the ways to make 50¢ with two kinds of coin, change from a $1 coin in cents, the fewest notes and coins (5 for $73, 3 for $13) and how many amounts three pieces can make (7). Money notation follows the school-paper file exactly (50¢ never 50c, $5, no $1.50, no amount that mixes dollars and cents except the $1 = 100¢ stories, no $20 note, and the page says so). Start here stays inside school Primary 1 money (coins to 100¢, notes within $30, change from $5). The named beyond item "Fewest Notes and Coins" sits at Go further. The faults are in the kit and the pictures, which this lesson depends on more than any other in the term: the play-money pieces are about 40% of the planned size with cut lines 1.5 mm apart, the same coin is drawn at different sizes on different questions, the kit supplies fewer coins than the tasks imply, and one assigned non-routine type (work backwards: paid and change given, find the price) is missing. The outputs also predate the no-calculator Mi.

## B. Age and syllabus fit

Start here is school Primary 1 money (counting coins to 100¢, notes within $30, exchange a coin for coins, circle coins for an amount, compare a note with a coin, change from $5): placed correctly and, per the 23 Oct amendment, higher than the Pre-flight's $20. Think harder is P1 level with bigger numbers (two items and change from $10, a $1 coin in cents) plus T2 which is a listing puzzle beyond the syllabus, correctly at Think harder (owner decision 6). Go further is beyond P1 (the fewest notes and coins for $73 using $10 and $50; how many amounts; how many stickers for $1, a division-like idea carried out by counting on). Named beyond item present in `moe.note` and `beyond`. No MOE-aligned wording.

## C. Tier principle

Start here approachable and explored (Explore: make 60¢ two ways, count two handfuls, change twice; Mini: count the biggest first, $1 = 100¢, count up). No contest item at Start here. T4 (more coins is not more money) is the lesson's main misconception placed at Think harder: good. S3 asks 50¢ in ten 5¢ coins; the class showed $1 = two 50¢ = ten 10¢, so ten 5¢ is a small extension via counting in 5s; acceptable at Start here, noted.

Non-routine assignments (preflight section 13, lesson 24): "GC T: coins for an exact amount; the fewest coins" is present (T2, G1, Investigation, contest_moves). "BACK T: paid and change given: find the price" is **absent** from the workbook, home sheet and online set (searched every string for "how much did it cost", "price of", change-given questions: all ask for change, none for the price). The pre-flight lists BACK among the four priority types.

## D. Wording

Plain, short, command first, (a)/(b), units on the blank (`____¢`, `$____`). Money words are right: "fifty-cent coin", "ten-dollar note", "play money" hyphenated in stems, symbols in pictures. S4 gives the target in the stem and then asks "The coins I circled are worth ____¢": the blank repeats the number (55¢) that the stem has just given; a pure copy exercise (Low; finding 8). S1 and S2 share the identical stem "Count the money. Fill in the blank." for coins and notes: a child at $ may write $ in the cents blank; the blank for S2 has "$" printed before it, which helps. G1 stem "You may use each one as many times as you like." is good plain English for unlimited pieces; but the kit cannot model it (finding 3). No hints in stems.

## E. Layout and usability for a 6-year-old (A4)

Pages 1 to 5 flow; every page starts with a line of instruction; Name and Date on every page; Mi on page 1. Page 3 is 75% full. Page 5 (Investigation, exit ticket and look-back) is full but cramped, as in L23 (finding 7).

**Pictures.** Money is drawn from a shared figure with a `size` parameter that scales to fit the cell: measured on 200 dpi renders (coin face, border excluded) the 50¢ coin is 19.7 mm in S1, 11.8 mm in S3, 13.8 mm in S4 and 9.9 mm in T4; the 20¢ coin is 17.9 mm in S1, 12.4 mm in S4, 8.9 mm in T4 Set A and 7.0 mm in T4 Set B; the $1 coin is 11 mm in G1 and G3 but 17.7 mm in G2. In T4 Set B the 20¢ and 10¢ coins are visibly smaller than the same coins in Set A, which makes Set B look like "lots of little coins" and Set A "fewer, bigger" - the opposite of the idea the question teaches, which is that size does not decide value (finding 2). The pictures are also up to 1.5 times larger than the kit pieces a child actually holds (S1 50¢ face 19.7 mm against a 13 mm kit coin). Digits on the smallest coins (S3 single 50¢, S4, T4 Set B, G1 row) are about 2 to 2.5 mm tall and the words PLAY MONEY under each note are under 1.5 mm: legible at arm's length but not comfortable for a six-year-old.

**Kit pages (6 and 7).** Cut and handle test: the 16 coins are rounded squares 9.9 mm (5¢), 10.8 mm (10¢), 12 mm (20¢) and 13.3 mm (50¢) across inside cut cells 15 mm wide, and the 13 notes are 25 to 29 mm by 11 to 13 mm. The pre-flight contract says coins 24 mm and notes 70 x 35 mm, and the CUTOUT audit rule F10 says cut lines at least 6 mm apart; here the dashed cut cells are 1.5 mm apart (measured at 200 dpi), so a grown-up cutting along one line risks the next piece. Two coins differ by under 1 mm of diameter, so size is no cue (fine for the idea, but the 5¢ and 10¢ labels are the only difference at a glance). Page 6 is 35% full and page 7 about 30% full (rubric item 14 asks 40%); the pieces could be doubled in size on the same pages. Both pages state "A grown-up cuts" and "Print this page on its own sheet" and keep the "play money, not real money" label. There is no "no printer" line on the kit page itself (the guide has it).

**Kit contract against tasks.** Supplied: 5¢ x 4, 10¢ x 6, 20¢ x 4, 50¢ x 2; $2 x 5, $5 x 4, $10 x 3, $50 x 1; the $1 coin is a Term 2 token. The tasks and script ask for more than the coins supplied: S3b (ten 5¢ coins), the Mini-lesson line "Ten ten-cent coins make a dollar. Check it on your table", Sneak-peek 2 (10 ten-cent coins), the slide `s-m3` (ten 10¢ coins drawn) and Remember (3) all need ten of a coin the kit supplies four or six of; the contract's "max_used" says 3 and 6 for these. Notes and tokens are fine (the most any task uses is $2 x 2, $10 x 2, $5 x 1, tokens x 5).

The `$1` coin is a round token in Lesson 20 and a rounded-square beige coin in every picture here; fine, and the guide says "tokens are the $1 coins".

## F. Pedagogy

Progression: count coins, count notes, equal values, circle coins for an amount, compare, change; then two items, ways, cents change, sets; then fewest pieces, number of amounts, stickers. The exit ticket matches the objective (count, change as MCQ, count notes). Timing: Start here 16.5 minutes (page 1 8.0, page 2 8.5) against 8:00 + 9:00: fits. Think harder and Go further are genuinely different. Misconceptions targeted: counting pieces not value (S1 and S2), bigger coin worth more (T4, S5), 50¢ more than $1 (talk time), adding instead of counting up (S6, E2), forgetting a piece. Class numbers are not repeated (Explore used 60¢, 70¢, 65¢, 40¢ to 50¢, $6 to $10; Mini used 75¢, 50¢ and 50¢, $8, 35¢; the workbook uses other amounts; the ruler is $6 in Explore and $4 in T1).

## G. Safeguarding and inclusion

Names (Ali, Ravi, Mei, Siti, Ben, Kumar) are spread; no wrong working is attached to a name; "play money, not real money" is on every kit page; the guide lets real coins be used only if a grown-up is present and keeps small pieces away from small children and mouths. Money contexts (bun, ruler, toy car, sticker) are neutral. No labels like "gifted" or "weak". Paper is private. The "I am still stuck" route is the WhatsApp photo (page text: "my grown-up will photograph this page for my tutor").
