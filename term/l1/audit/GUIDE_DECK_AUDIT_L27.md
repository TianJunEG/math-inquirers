## What works

One idea at a time and a clean order: one of each in a row (rows only, with tiles), then columns, then both, then "check every row and every column". The warm-up re-teaches the missing one in a row and a digit row with objects, so a child who joins cold follows (the guide says so and says this is the safest lesson of the term to skip). Explore is kit-led and the IF STUCK lines are concrete ("read down the column aloud", "keep the top row and ask where the circle can go", "use C, S and T on paper"). `s-m3` and `s-m5` hold the answers back and the answer slides follow; the check slides follow each workbook page; the exit ticket is read with all four options. The sentence frame ("the row has ... and ..., so the box must be ...") is given once and used throughout; the guide forbids "always start in a corner". The safeguarding block is consistent with the script (small tiles, nothing in mouth or ears, away from younger children, nobody cuts, no paper to the camera, gallery view, no private chat, WhatsApp-only route, no names with answers, passes allowed). Recording and greeting wording is word for word that of L24. The beyond-P1 statement is in the strand note, the "Plain statement" NOTE, `beyond` and tutor_prep and is honest (the missing one in a row is early Primary 1; elimination, the 4 by 4 and a larger grid are beyond on purpose). No MOE-aligned claim. The hand-off (strip page, 14 strips, no matches or toothpicks) is true against `l28.json`; the previous hand-off (tiles from L26) is true.

## Timing and tutor load

Script 67:52 of 90, slow class 80:16, last 5 minutes untouched (flex 0:44 of 5:00, so the lesson has about 22 minutes of slack in a quick class: that is the spare-time menu's job and the menu is there). Workbook allotment (page 1 7:30, page 2 9:00, choice 4:00 plus the 30-second stretch) fits the guide's estimates (6.0 and 8.5 minutes for a middle child). One tutor can run it. One practical point: Explore asks each child to build a grid with tiles that the tutor cannot see on a video call; the guide's remedy ("children say what they have made and may tilt the camera to the table", `l27.json` NOTE at Explore) is sensible but is the only place in the guide that lets a child turn the camera, while the opening line and the safeguarding block say "so I can see you" and "nobody holds paper to the camera": say plainly that tilting the camera to the table is allowed and that the child's face need not be in view (finding 6).

## Slide audit (28 slides)

Text height measured on every slide: the smallest body text in the whole deck is 16 pt or more on the 1440 x 810 pt page (about 21 px at 1920 wide): the text is legible. Clean: slides 1 to 9, 14 to 22, 24 to 27 (title, warm-ups, sneak-peek, Explore, the rule, Your turn, check slides, break, workbook slides, choice, talk, exit, look-back, end, optional). Mi appears on the title, two small bubbles and the end slide with no numbers in the bubbles, and Mi holds the book, not the calculator (the deck was built after the no-calculator artwork arrived). Concerns:

- Slide 12 (`s-m4`, "Is the grid finished?") draws a grid whose rows are fine and whose first column (circle, square, circle) and second column (square, triangle, triangle) both have a repeat. The script asks "Which column has two of the same?" (singular); two children will name two different columns and both are right (finding 2).
- Slide 10 (`s-m2`, "Look along the row. Look down the column.") draws the row as three picture tiles, but the column as a bullet list ("? / circle / triangle") of text: the "column" the tutor says ("a mark, then a circle, then a triangle, going down") is shown as a list, not a column of picture boxes, so the picture of a column is lost on the slide that teaches columns (finding 3).
- Slide 11 (`s-m3`, the box "a" in the middle): the letter a is drawn about 2 mm tall under the box and is not in the PDF text layer; at 1080p it is a dot. The tutor's words carry it ("box a, in the middle"), but the slide should show a large "a" or a highlighted box (finding 4).
- Slide 13 (`s-m5`, "Your turn"): the pill "Write both answers on your paper." overlaps the footer text at the bottom left ("...plain why." is visible behind it).
- Slide 27 (`s-opt`, the optional "three in a row" grid): the picture shows the diagonal 1, 2, 3 and the instruction says exactly that; no answer shown. Good. The title "Go further: three in a row" uses a tier label for an optional spare-time slide that the guide says is not announced by tier: harmless.
- Slide 28 (`s-menu`, "If we have time: pick one"): the first line reads "Is it always true? Two circles can never be next to each other. Test it." This is not the activity in the guide and the JSON (Is it always true that if every row has one of each then every column does? answer: no, three identical rows). It is also a bad claim: in a correct grid two circles can touch at a corner (for example the circle in the top left and the circle in the centre of a valid 3 by 3 grid), so "never next to each other" is false or ambiguous depending on what "next to" means (finding 5).
- Slides 16 and 18 (the workbook pages as slides): grids and shape rows are about 1 cm at 1080p; the guide tells the tutor to "read every question, every option of E2 and the box labels aloud: the shapes on the slides are small": acceptable as the child works from the book.
- Exit slide 23: E2's four options are not on the slide (only "Write its number in the bracket"); the tutor reads them (guide line "read all four options") and the child has them on page 5: fine for a live class, but a child watching the recording hears the options only as speech.
