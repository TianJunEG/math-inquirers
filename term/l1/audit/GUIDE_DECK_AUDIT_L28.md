## What works

One idea at a time and a clean order: build, count, table, how it grows, use it to go on, why the sticks are shared, the rule in words. The warm-up (count in 2s, doubles, read a two-row table) is exactly what the lesson needs, so a child who joins cold follows. Explore is kit-led with a guess before each build and an explicit "do not build now" round whose answer (11) is withheld until the Mini-lesson. The IF STUCK lines are concrete ("look at the strip the two triangles share", "ask what the number for four triangles was"). The slides keep answers off while asking (`s-explore3`, the table slides with blank boxes, "Your turn" then "Check: your turn"); the check slides follow each workbook page; the exit ticket is read with its four options. The guide forbids letters and the word "formula" and says "sticks in the table, strips on the desk". The safeguarding block is consistent with the script (paper strips, never matches or toothpicks, nothing in mouth or ears, away from younger children, nobody cuts, no paper to the camera, gallery view, no private chat, WhatsApp-only route, no names with answers, passes allowed). Recording and greeting wording is word for word that of L24 and L27. The beyond-P1 statement is in the strand note, the "Plain statement" NOTE, `beyond` and tutor_prep and is honest (equal-group tables are Primary 1; the stick pattern, the 10th, 20th and 100th picture and working back are beyond on purpose). No MOE-aligned claim. The hand-offs are true: the strips from Lesson 27 (14, no matches) and the 8 mistake cards for Lesson 29.

## Timing and tutor load

Script 67:26 of 90, slow class 79:56, last 5 minutes untouched (flex 0:40 of 5:00: about 22 minutes of slack for a quick class, which the spare-time menu fills). Workbook allotment fits the guide's per-question estimates (7.5 minutes for page 1 and 10.5 for page 2: 18 minutes against a note of "about 16"; finding 8). One tutor can run it. One realism point: Explore gives 2:30 for children to build 1, 2 and 3 triangles in a row with 7 loose strips and write the three numbers, and 1:55 to guess, build the fourth triangle (9 strips) and count. A six-year-old chaining equilateral triangles from paper strips on a table (strips slip; "a second triangle that joins the first" does not say that the two triangles share a strip) will need more than 1:55 for a fourth triangle; the guide's own IF STUCK ("A child who has too few strips draws the sticks as lines") is the right remedy, and the skip order (shorten Explore round 3) is explicit, but round 2 is where a slow class loses time (Low, finding 6). The tutor cannot see the strips on a video call: the guide's remedy is the same camera-tilt line as L27 (finding 7).
