## B. Age and syllabus fit

Start here (finding the one missing picture in a row, a column with two shapes showing, spotting a repeat, one circle per row) is school Primary 1 "missing one in a row" thinking placed correctly; Think harder (elimination with two clues, how many shapes could go in a box, what keeps a grid correct) and Go further (4 by 4 digits, 5 by 5 counting) are beyond Primary 1 on purpose and sit at the right tiers. The named beyond item "Four by Four" is in `moe.note`, `beyond`, the guide, the parent page and tutor_prep; no MOE-aligned wording. The Term 3 risk "beyond-P1 statements present": present and honest in all four places. The lesson has no paper evidence (the 23 Oct study found no grid item): the author says so and the guide marks the wording unverified.

## C. Tier principle

Start here: S1 to S4 are explored in class (Explore rows and columns, Mini rows, columns, row-and-column, "is the grid finished") and S3 is the same box-with-row-and-column move as the Mini-lesson `s-m3`, so approachable. S5 ("one circle in every row and every column: how many in the whole grid?") is an inference that class never states (Explore uses 3 circles, 3 squares, 3 triangles on mat 1, so a child who counted the tiles can read it off, but the class does not say "one per row, so three"). G3 asks exactly the same thing for 3s in a 5 by 5 grid and the online practice has both (c5 and g2). Either S5 is Think harder or G3 is not Go further (finding 7). No contest-style item at Start here; the non-routine LOG type (elimination in a 3 by 3 grid with clues, preflight section 13) is at Think harder (T1) as required; `contest_moves` names it ("logic: elimination in a 3 by 3 grid with clues"). S3 is a two-line elimination at Start here and is the nearest to a LOG item below Think harder: acceptable because the column alone decides.

## D. Wording

Plain, short, command first, (a)/(b), Singapore English ("box", "circle one", "tick"), no hints in stems, no ÷ or x anywhere. Remaining problems: S1 asks "the box with the ? mark" while S1 draws one ? per row (fine). S4 gives the whole grid in words: "Row 1 is circle, square, triangle. Row 2 is square, triangle, circle. Row 3 is circle, square, triangle." (finding 6). T2 is words only: "Circle in its row, circle in its column: [] shapes." reads like a coded phrase to a six-year-old; a two-box picture would be clearer (Low, finding 9). G2 puts "?" in the unknown boxes and also uses the letters a, b and c: "Which digit goes in each box marked a, b and c?" while seven other boxes are marked "?" too; "?" here means "unknown, not asked" whereas in S1 and T1 "?" means "asked": the same symbol has two meanings in one lesson (Low, finding 10). The word "box" (not "cell") is used consistently and "row" and "column" are defined in class.

## F. Pedagogy

Progression: one missing in a row; true or false for rows; row and column together; check a grid; one per row; then two clues, how many could go in, swaps; then 4 by 4 and 5 by 5; then every grid with a 2 in the middle. The exit ticket matches the objective (missing in a row, which row has one of each, a box with a row clue and a column clue). Timing: Start here 14.5 minutes (page 1: 6.0, page 2: 8.5) against 8:00 + 9:00 allotted in the 32-minute workbook part: fits, with a slow-class margin; the guide's note "about 16 minutes" in two places disagrees with its own per-question estimates (14.5: finding 12). Think harder and Go further are different (T3 swaps versus G1 digits). Misconceptions targeted: checking rows only (S4, E-items, Talk), repeating a shape in a column, thinking one clue is enough. Class numbers are not reused: Explore uses 3 circles/squares/triangles in free form and the top row circle-square-triangle, the Mini-lesson uses `s-m1` square-circle-triangle, `s-m3` a box with square in row and circle in column, `s-m4` a failing grid and `s-m5` a row and a grid; the workbook uses other grids, but E3 repeats the structure of `s-m3` (a circle in its row and a square in its column against a square in its row and a circle in its column): same reasoning, new shapes (acceptable). The Investigation answer says each grid is "the same grid as another one turned upside down or swapped left to right" yet counts them as four different grids and lists "counting the same grid twice, turned round" as a mistake: a child who treats turned grids as the same will say 1 or 2 and be marked wrong (finding 3).
