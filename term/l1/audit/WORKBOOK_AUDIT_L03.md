Auditor: independent desk review by a model that did not write the lesson. Sources: `out/l03/L03_Workbook.pdf` (7 pages) and `L03_Workbook_Answer_Key.pdf` (3 pages), rendered with pdftoppm, pages 1-5 and 7 viewed (page 6 is the same tens-and-ones kit as L01, text checked); `lessons/l03.json` (hints, notes, minutes) read for context. Instruments: gifted-style rubric (12 criteria x 0-2, max 24, `reaudit_p1.md` instrument A) on workbook evidence; strict tier compliance per printed item. Student-journey instrument not used (out of scope for this brief).

## Verdict: Ready after minor fixes. Rubric 16/24. Tier compliance 13/15 (87%). 0 High, 4 Medium, 8 Low.
Fix S4 and E2 before use (exit ticket ambiguity); decide on G1/G3 (three-digit numbers).

## (A) Maths correctness
Independent answers: S1 35, 53, 53; S2 72, 48, 65, 30; S3 Mei 38 (3 bars + 8 squares, counted in the picture), Ben 41, Ben; S4 62, 26; S5 14, 25, 41, 52; S6 No, 21; S7 82, 28; S8 Ravi (52 vs 25); T1 85, 35; T2 30, 73; T3 51, 53, 71, 73, 75 (enumerated all 12 two-card numbers: 57 fails clue 2; nothing starts with 1 or 3 passes "more than 40"); T4 6 numbers; G1 940, 409; G2 12 and 16; G3 528 (528 and 582 are in 500-599; only 528 has ones > tens); E1 41; E2 73, 37; E3 36, 60, 63. All match the key. **Ambiguity (Medium): S4 and E2 read "Use the cards 6 and 2. The greatest number is ___ / The smallest number is ___".** There is no command to make a number, and no "use both cards": 6 and 2 themselves are numbers (and 66, 62, 622 are all "numbers using the cards"). The key wants 62 and 26. Compare S7, which correctly says "Use both cards each time." T1 "Use two cards" assumes one card each; the kit holds two sets, so a child could lay two 8s and write 88 (the key lists this as a mistake, but the wording does not forbid it).

## (B) Age and syllabus fit
Comparing and ordering two-digit numbers and comparing sets are P1 (numbers to 100, 1.4-1.5). S1-S8, E1-E3, T1-T4 are P1 content. **G1 and G3 use three-digit numbers (940, 409, 5xx), which are P2 (numbers to 1000).** The lesson note says "hundreds are explained in the question"; they are not: neither the stem nor the page explains hundreds, only a tutor-side hint ("The left card counts the hundreds"). Two of the three Go further items therefore need next-year content nobody has taught; that is acceleration, not extension. Placement at Go further (never at Start here or in the exit) is correct per the plan and wording guide rule 25, but see finding 2.

## (D) Wording
Good: short stems, command words, "Arrange these in order. Begin with the smallest number." follows rule 13. Problems: S4/E2 missing command (above); "greater" is used for two numbers in S1/S2/E1 but "greatest/smallest" for two cards in S4/E2 (rule 13: greater for two); S3 "Look at the picture for Mei. Ben has 4 bars and 1 square." is the facts out of order and Ben has no picture; T1 and G1 open with "You have the cards ..." then two commands in one sentence; G3 is 38 words and G1 asks two things in one stem (rule 18); S6 "Circle Yes or No" has nothing to circle. "Then write how you know" in the page 3 hint has only one dotted line per item.

## (E) Layout and usability
Good: large type, clear boxes, drawing box on S8, digit cards on a printable page with a heavy font, nothing cut off, Mi only on page 1. Problems: the S3 picture is small (cells about 16 px; the child must count 30 + 8 cells) and shows only Mei; page 1 about 85% full but this picture is the weakest visual in the three lessons; the Investigation has no writing area; T3, T4 and G2 give one or three dotted lines for listing 5 or 12 numbers (G2b has none for 16 numbers); kit page 2 is usable (two sets of 0 to 9, 20 cards, easy rectangles) and kit page 1 is the L01 page again (fine); page 7 is 75% full.

## (F) Pedagogy
Progression is strong: make two numbers, compare, order, then cases. Targets the right misconception (compare the ones first; 13 vs 30; 25 vs 52; same digits, different numbers). Variety is good (cards, drawing, true/false, listing). Exit ticket matches the objective (compare, make greatest/smallest, order) except ambiguity above. Investigation "Is the greater card always first?" is a good open task but needs writing room. Problem posing is absent (no "make your own" item); L02 G3 shows how.

## (G) Safeguarding and inclusion
No labels, no ranking, pass option in look-back. Ravi is the child who makes the error in S6 (Siti in L01 and L02): rotate. Cast otherwise mixed (Mei, Ben, Siti, Ravi, Ali in sneak-peek).

## (H) Consistency
Same visual system as L01 and L02. L03 has the cleanest header. "Circle Yes or No" lacks options in all three. The kit pages are rectangular like L01.
