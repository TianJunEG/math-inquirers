## Verdict: Ready after minor fixes

The maths is right everywhere: every table, every rule ("double the number of triangles, then add 1"; "3 sticks for the first square and 3 more each time"), the "none left over" cases, the odd/even claim and the 14-strip Investigation were re-derived and agree with the key, and the beyond statement is honest. Start here is approachable (equal-group tables, a bead ratio table, pages read per day) and Think harder carries the non-routine items the preflight assigns (TAB, PAT, and BACK at Go further are all present and named in `contest_moves`). The faults are in the picture and the supports around the puzzles: the one stick picture in the workbook (S1) draws a row of squares as a single thick outline in which the individual sticks cannot be told apart, the exit ticket asks a question (E3, odd or even sticks) that the class never teaches, a wrong table is attributed to a named child (T3, "Ben made a table"), the kit cut lines are 3.7 mm apart on a page that is 40% full, and two Start here questions ask past the end of the printed table.

## E. Layout and usability for a 6-year-old (A4)

Pages 1 to 4 flow; every page starts with an instruction line; Name and Date on every page; Mi on page 1 with the book (no calculator: the workbook was built at 03:26, after the no-calculator artwork at 03:08); header "Term 3 • Lesson 8" and footer "Lesson 8" correct. **The stick picture (page 1, S1).** The only picture in the workbook shows three squares in a row as one thick outline (about 55 mm by 18 mm) with two inner vertical lines. At 200 dpi the top and the bottom edge are single unbroken lines; no stick ends, gaps or joins are drawn, so the child cannot see how many sticks make the top edge.
