## E. Layout and usability (6-year-old, A4)

Answer boxes are large rounded squares, two joined for two digits. The strips are clear and large on pages 1 and 2 (boxes about 18 mm wide with 5 mm bold numerals). Page 3 is cramped (finding 3). Yes/No is bold words to circle. Every page opens with an instruction line, and page 1 has the "After question 3" banner. Nothing is cut off. The kit page prints edge to edge on a grid background; the strips alternate yellow and blue so a child can see where one ends.

## F. Pedagogy

Progression: cups, then boxes, then a strip choice, then an extra step, then draw the strip, then take away, then twice as many: sound and the strips are used the way the lesson says. Start here is feasible only slowly (finding 5). Think harder differs from Go further (twice as many and a transfer versus hidden unit and 3 times as many). The exit ticket matches objectives 1 and 2 (E1 number sentence, E2 add a step, E3 take away a step); it does not test "twice as many", which is the Think harder idea, correctly. Misconceptions are named in the key (twice as many = 2 more, 3 groups of 4, forgetting the loose ones, stopping at 26 in T1).

## G. Safeguarding and inclusion

Private paper; no ranking; look-back may be passed ("I would like to pass"); "I am still stuck (my grown-up will photograph this page for my tutor)" is the approved route; names Mei, Ali, Ben, Ravi, Jenny, Wei, Siti, Kumar, Lily: mixed. Counters are never food (sweets and pens are story objects). Pass.
