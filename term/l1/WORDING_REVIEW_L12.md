# Wording review, Lesson 12 (Bar Strips: More and Fewer)

Reviewed against `WORDING_GUIDE.md` rules 1 to 25 and `docs/books/school_paper_wording_patterns.md`: every workbook question, exit ticket, investigation, look-back, slide text, script line read to children (SAY, READ, ASK), home practice, parent page, online practice items and templates, spare-time menu. Answers are unchanged by the review (65 answer checks, 180 practice variants pass).

## Rules applied and what changed (draft -> final)

- Rules 11, 12 (story first, fixed question words): each story is 1 to 3 short sentences ("Ali has 12 stickers. Mei has 8 stickers."), then the frame. Fixed words every time: "How many more ... than ...?" for the gap, "more ... than" for adding the extra part, "altogether" for the whole, "fewer ... than" for the shorter strip. The noun is repeated in the frame ("Ali has [] more stickers than Mei.").
- Rule 14 (command phrasing): the stems carry no command; the frame is the question. S4 says "Draw a strip for Mei and a strip for Ravi." (names the strips, one command). S7 follows the paper MCQ pattern: "Which number sentence finds how many more beads Aisha has than Lily? Write its number in the brackets." with options (1) to (4).
- Rule 15 (numerals): every quantity is a numeral; "more", "fewer" and "altogether" are the only quantity words.
- Rule 16, 17 (answer frames): a box for a number, the noun after it; two-digit answers use two linked boxes; "Ali gives Mei [] stickers." A box is used for every answer in the workbook, never a mix of box and line in one question.
- Rule 18 (parts): S3, S5, T1, T3, G1, G2 use (a), (b) with the facts in the stem; (b) builds on (a) ("Dev has [2] beads." then "They have [2] beads altogether."). Hints and methods are not in stems.
- Rule 19 (circle, not write why): no reason is asked in Start here; the talk question asks "Is her picture a good picture?" and the model answer gives the reason.
- Rule 21 (names): cast spread over Ali, Mei, Ben, Kumar, Siti, Dev, Ravi, Wei, Jenny, Aisha, Lily. The error-check character is Aisha (talk), not Siti. Wrong working is attached only to Aisha's strip picture, not to a number.
- Rule 22 (contexts): stickers, marbles, beads, cards, stamps, books on shelves; no money, no brands.
- Rule 24 (do not teach inside the question): stems never say "take the extra away" or "share the rest"; those are in hints and the mini-lesson. T1 keeps "take the extra 8 away" in the hint only.
- Rule 25 (keep to what is tested): total-and-difference, transfers and "fewer than" with the unknown person first are Think harder or Go further only. Start here uses "more than" with the known person first.
- Rule 7 (read aloud test): each READ was read aloud; the page 2 READ gives each question as a short unit ("Question 4: ... Question 5: ..."). "Fewer" is always said as "fewer stickers", never "less".
- Rule 6 (one right answer): the sum-and-difference and transfer items all have a whole-number answer (checked by trying every split); totals are even where halves are needed. The open puzzle items say many answers are right.

## Slides and script read to children

- Remember: "I think of a number", "count up", "a strip is a number" use one fixed phrase each; "the whole strip", "a part", "the gap" are named with a picture before they are used.
- "Hello, inquirers!" and the recording statement are the same words as every lesson; no first names on the recording; the four-things reminder card is identical across the term.
- "Side by side for how many more, end to end for altogether" is said the same way in Explore, the mini-lesson and the notes.
- Parent page: "strips (called bar models at school)"; "the gap" and "altogether" are in the words list; the beyond-P1 sentence says which questions go past school.

## Read-aloud stumbles fixed

- "Mei has 4 fewer stickers than Ali" is said with a picture of the taken-off cells ("take 4 cells off the end of Ali's strip").
- "Share the total between two equal strips" replaced "halve the total", which a 6-year-old may not have heard.
- "The gap" is introduced as "the extra part" first, then named.

## Left as is (with reason)

- "Wei has 18 fewer marbles than Ravi" puts the unknown person first on purpose: this is the reversed wording the plan asks for at Think harder.
- The slide titles say "Bar Strips" while the lesson title is "Bar Strips: More and Fewer": the full title ran into the title-slide decoration.
