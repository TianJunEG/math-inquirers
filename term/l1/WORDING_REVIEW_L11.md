# Wording review, Lesson 11 (Who Is Where?)

Reviewed against `WORDING_GUIDE.md` rules 1 to 25 and `docs/books/school_paper_wording_patterns.md`: every workbook question, exit ticket, investigation, look-back, slide text, script line read to children (SAY, READ, ASK), home practice, parent page, online practice items and templates, spare-time menu. Answers are unchanged by the review (82 answer checks, 62 practice variants pass).

## Rules applied and what changed (draft -> final)

- Rule 11, 12 (story first, fixed question words): all stories are 1 to 3 short sentences, then the blank. "How many children are in front of Ben?" always names the noun. Between and in front always use "How many children ...". S7 uses the frame "There are [] children in front of Ben."
- Rule 13 (comparison words): no "bigger/biggest" anywhere; places use "from the front" and "from the back" every time ("from the other end" appears only in the tutor script as a spoken explanation).
- Rule 14, 23 (command phrasing): "Study the picture.", "Fill in the places of the children.", "Count the children.", "Put a tick in the correct box." (printed by the template with the True/False table), "Circle Yes or No." The script for S1 matches the printed table.
- Rule 15 (numerals): every quantity is a numeral ("13 children", "6 children"); ordinals are written 1st, 2nd; "first, second, third" appear only where the lesson is about the words (Explore, mini-lesson, script).
- Rule 16, 17 (answer frames): boxes for a number ("There are [] children between Ali and Mei."), a line for a place with its unit ("Jenny is ___ from the back.", "Kumar is ___ in the line."), never both in one question. S3 uses two linked boxes for the two-digit answer 10.
- Rule 18 (parts): T1, S3, G1, G2, G3, E2 use (a), (b), (c); draft G2(b) "The child in the middle of the line is ___" became "The middle child is ___ from the front." (also cleared a 7-word overlap with a reference text). Hints and methods stay out of the stems.
- Rule 19 (circle, not "write why"): T1(a) "Kumar says there are 10 children in the line. Is Kumar correct? Circle Yes or No." states a wrong conclusion but not wrong reasoning, then (b) asks for the number. S1 is a True/False tick table.
- Rule 20 (clue questions): Go further G3 became a short paragraph of three statements ("Siti was 1st. Ben beat Ali. Ali beat Mei.") with plain "beat" instead of "finished before", which a 6-year-old reads as the opposite.
- Rule 21 (names): cast spread over Aisha, Kumar, Wei, Jenny, Lily, Mei, Ben, Ali, Siti, Ravi, Jason. The error-check name is Kumar (workbook) and Mei (talk, a true claim); not Siti. Names are first names only; the next sentence uses the name again where a new idea starts.
- Rule 22 (contexts): lines of children, a table, a race. No money, no brands.
- Rule 24 (do not teach inside the question): stems never say "Count from the back" or explain "right behind"; those are in the hint and in the mini-lesson slide.
- Rule 25 (keep to what is tested): "how many between" and counting from both ends are Think harder and Go further only; no rounding, no 3-step stories in Start here or the exit ticket.
- Rule 7 (read aloud test): each READ line was read aloud; the READ of page 2 is four short sentences, one per question; "right behind" is said the same way every time.
- Rule 6 (one right answer): G1 "sits between" means next to both, and exactly one seating fits (checked by trying every arrangement); G3 exactly one order fits; the open home and spare-time puzzles say so.

## Slides and script read to children

- Remember: "Touch each one once" and "The last number tells how many" are fixed phrases; "the door" defined once with a picture. "Say it on three" replaces "show me" so nothing is shown to the camera.
- Sneak-peek and exit: the exit ticket READ is a single paragraph of three questions, each with the full sentence; the answers slide reads each answer with its unit ("3 children", "2nd from the back").
- "Hello, inquirers!" replaces "Good morning"; recording statement in the privacy-notice words; no first names on the recording.
- Parent page: plain, no jargon; "the very next place after" is explained in the words list; the beyond-P1 sentence says which questions go past school.

## Read-aloud stumbles fixed

- "Kumar is 3rd" and "Kumar is ___ in the line" kept parallel so the frame does not repeat the stem.
- "Ben beat Ali" used instead of "Ben finished in front of Ali" (two meanings of "front" in a lesson about front and back).
- "From the front" and "from the back" never shortened to "from front".

## Left as is (with reason)

- "Study the picture. The door is on the left." is repeated on several questions: it is a fixed instruction, the same each time, which the rules prefer.
- The tutor script uses "first, second, third" as words while the pages use 1st, 2nd, 3rd: this is the lesson's topic (reading the short form aloud).
