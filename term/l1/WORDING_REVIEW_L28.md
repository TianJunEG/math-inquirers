# Wording review, Term 3 Lesson 8 (Make a Table)

Every child-facing sentence was read aloud against WORDING_GUIDE rules 1-25 and `docs/books/p1_school_paper_patterns.md` (section 6.5: the two-row table of equal groups and the day-by-day table are Primary 1 items; the stick pattern has no paper evidence and its wording is unverified). Maths unchanged by the review; 115 answer checks and 63 variants pass.

## Changes made during review (old -> new, reason)
1. "Sticks" is the word in every table and picture; "strips" is the paper we hold. The class and the kit say strips, the books say sticks, and the tutor says so once (rule 5, 7).
2. Fixed question words: "Fill in the table.", "How many sticks make 100 triangles?" became "100 triangles in a row need ____ sticks.", "Tick True or False for each statement.", "Circle Yes or No." (rules 2, 12, 14).
3. Rules are said in words ("Double the number of triangles, then add ____ ."); no letters, no "formula", no "n" (rule 1).
4. Table questions state the story first and the command second: "For every 3 red beads there are 2 blue beads. Fill in the table." (rule 11).
5. Every quantity is a numeral; number words only in tutor lines (rule 15). "Each tray holds 7 cups" and "Mei reads 3 pages each day" carry the unit after each blank (rule 16).
6. The wrong claim in Talk time is given to "a child", and Ben's table in T3 is a made-up working, not a claim about a real child (rule 4, D18).
7. Teaching sentences removed from questions (rule 24): "the new triangle shares a stick" lives in the slides and hints, not in S1 or T1.
8. "Work back" is never printed on a page; G2 says "How many triangles?" after the total (rule 24).
9. Odd and even are written as words with the choice printed: "odd or even? Circle one." (rule 19).
