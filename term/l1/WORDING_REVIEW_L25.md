## Changes made during review (old -> new, reason)
1. Money notation: whole dollars as $12, "$____" blanks with the sign first, number-sentence boxes with a dollar sign in each ("$[] + $[] = $[]", rule 17 and the P1 pattern). No decimals, no mixed amounts, no cents needed.
2. Fixed question words (rule 12): "How much more does ... cost than ...?", "How much ... altogether?", "How much change ...?", "How much more money does she need?", "Which costs more / the most / the least?". The same words on every page, exit ticket and home sheet.
3. "Cheaper / dearer" avoided in child text; the papers use "costs more", "costs the least", "most expensive". "Cashier" and "got back"/"change" used in the change items (patterns doc, section 3.6).
4. S1 and S4: the choice words are the item names (book, bag), printed as choices, because a price tag has no letter label; "Circle one." is its own part (rule 19).
5. S2, S6: the story and the question in one stem, then one answer frame ("The bag costs $____ more.", rule 16); an empty first part was removed.
6. S5: "pays with two ten-dollar notes" (there is no $20 note); the money picture shows two notes.
7. T2, G1: "costs $6 more than", "cost $16 together" in separate short sentences; the method (try a price, take the extra away first) is in the hint, never in the question (rule 24).
8. T3: three parts on one story (total, change from a fifty-dollar note, "Is $40 enough? Circle Yes or No.").
9. T4: stem shortened to "Look at the five price tags. Ben spends all his money each time. There are two ways each time."; each part says how many things and shows the blanks for both ways.
10. Talk and Mini: the wrong claim belongs to "one child" and "another child", not a cast name (D18).
11. H1 rewritten with "she" so it does not repeat a Primary 1 paper phrase (7-word check).
12. Banned words: "stretch" replaced by "moves" in the break; no "challenge" outside the program name.
13. Investigation text trimmed (the seven prices are in the sentence, readable, under the reading-load limit).
14. Mi's lines contain no numbers or answers.
