# Wording review: Lesson 32 (Build the Figure, Term 4 Lesson 2)

Every child-facing sentence (workbook, exit ticket, home sheet, slides, online practice, parent page) was read against WORDING_GUIDE rules 1 to 25 and the Primary 1 paper patterns (`docs/books/p1_school_paper_patterns.md`).

- Commands: Count each kind; Fill in the blanks; Use your ruler. Draw the line; Build the boat ...; Circle one word each time; Circle Yes or No; Tick True or False; Draw a figure. Same command for the same job across the workbook, exit ticket and home sheet (rules 2, 14).
- Fixed question words (rule 12): 'How many squares? []', 'How many more squares than triangles? []' (the school-paper frame 'How many more ... than ...?'), 'There are [] triangles in all.' Plurals are kept consistent: the blanks sit before the noun ('[] squares in each row') so '1 circles' never appears.
- Shape words: triangle, square, rectangle, circle, half circle, quarter circle, piece, figure, whole side. 'Piece' means one kit shape and 'figure' a picture made of shapes; both are taught in Explore and the Mini-lesson before Start here uses them. 'Whole side' is shown with the kit (the full length of a side), never 'edge to edge'. No 'trapezoid', 'parallelogram' or 'hexagon' on any child page: the slanted four-sided shape is described as 'a slanted four-sided shape'.
- Numbers as numerals ('2 triangles', '8 shapes', '6 squares'); number words only for shape names. Pictures and words match: S1 says 8 shapes and the panel has 8 tiles; S4 says squares in rows and shows 3 rows of 4; G1 says 2 rows of 3 squares and the picture has 6 squares (all checked in the verify script by counting).
- Wrong working: no child is named for a mistake. The Talk statement ('2 triangles always make a square') is given as 'A child says' and the exit ticket asks 'Can 2 triangles make a square?' with no wrong reasoning in the stem (rules 4, 19, D18). Mei appears only as an actor ('Mei makes a picture ...', optional slide).
- Rule 24 (do not teach inside the question): stems give facts only ('The square is turned on its corner. It has 2 lines from corner to corner.'); the hints carry 'Touch each shape as you count it' and 'Put the 2 longest sides together'. 'Every Triangle' is the puzzle name, not an instruction.
- Rule 25 (keep to what children are tested on): counting and naming shapes in a picture, cutting a square into 2 triangles and what 2 shapes make are Primary 1. Counting every triangle of every size, the fewest pieces and which sets of pieces make a figure are enrichment: they sit at Think harder and Go further only, never in Start here, the exit ticket or the home sheet slots 1 and 2.
- Choice words are printed on the answer line ('rectangle triangle circle'); no 'say why' prompt in Start here; G2 is a True or False table (rule 19). The riddle-style item is not used; G3 is open and says 'Many answers are right.' (rule 6).
- Reading load: no child-facing string over 40 words (validator). The longest, the T3 stem, is 29 words in five short sentences.
- Changed during review: S1 parts first read 'There are [] circle.' (a plural problem for 1) and now read 'How many circles? []'; T3 first said 'The square has 2 lines' (the picture is turned like a diamond) and now says 'The square is turned on its corner'; the Investigation first step lost a sentence so page 5 fits; E1 changed from 3 squares, 2 triangles and 1 circle (6 shapes, the same total as the class example) to 3, 2 and 2 (7); H1 changed from 4 squares and 3 triangles (the S1 counts) to 5 circles and 2 squares.
- Open: no Primary 1 paper in the sample asks a child to build a figure from pieces, so the building wording ('Build the boat with squares and triangles. Use as few triangles as you can.') is unverified (D22). The count-a-shape-in-a-picture items follow the paper frame ('How many triangles are there?').
